Quarter Report · Lent through Eastertide MMXXV

Fabric & Works
Conservation of the South Transept

Since the lifting of the south rose on the Feast of the Conversion of Saint Paul, the Office has supervised concurrent works upon the glass, the stone, and the timber-and-pipe scaffold that wraps the transept to the cill of the clerestory. The present account, laid before the Chapter on the second Tuesday of March, sets out the condition of the panels returned to frame, the progress of the buttress pinnacle replacement at the south-eastern respond, and the projected charge upon the Fabric Fund through the octave of Easter. It is published, by long custom, that the burgesses of Saint-Évrard may know what is being spent, and upon what.

Panels seven through fourteen have been re-leaded with milled cames of 7 mm flat profile, cemented in linseed putty, and returned to their iron armatures on the second of March. Their reinstallation under the cusps of the rose proceeded without incident over four working days; the cobalt of the apostle borders, washed for the first time in nine decades, has come up remarkably clear.

Panels fifteen through twenty-two remain in the workshop. Six of these required new border pieces, blown to match by Mme Estienne of Carcassonne; the work is patient and cannot be hurried. We expect their return by Passion Sunday. Panels twenty-three and twenty-four — the Christ and pendant — await the delivery of the heavier 9 mm cames ordered from Hartley & Wood of Sunderland.

The original pinnacle, weathered to a soft sugar through fourteen winters of acid rain and the freeze of 1985, was lowered in three sections on the morning of the eleventh of February. The crocket courses had parted from the spirelet at the lower fillet; one crocket fell into the south aisle gutter on St Brigid's Day and was the immediate occasion for the lifting.

New stone has been won from the Belmont quarry, six leagues upriver, where the same bed was opened in 1247 for the fabric of the chapter house. Two blocks of 1.4 cubic metres each were drawn down by river-barge to the works yard at Quai du Chapitre and are now under the banker masons. The carving — eight crockets, finial, and four gabled lucarnes — proceeds at a deliberate pace; we will not set false economies in stone that must stand five hundred years.

A Charge upon the Fabric Fund

The two campaigns above — the glazing in workshop and the stone upon the buttress — together compose the largest single charge upon the Fund since the re-roofing of the north aisle in 2011. The Office set the budget, with the consent of the Chapter and the approval of the Diocesan Commission for Sacred Art, at fifty-eight thousand pounds, inclusive of scaffold hire, banker labour, glazing, freight from Sunderland and Carcassonne, and the costs of public ceremony at the unveiling.

Of this sum, forty-two thousand one hundred and eighty pounds have been raised by Christmastide subscription, the long-standing covenants of the Guild of Vère Burgesses, and a single anonymous benefaction received in Advent. A shortfall of fifteen thousand eight hundred and twenty pounds remains to be met before the striking of the scaffold at the Vigil of Pentecost.
