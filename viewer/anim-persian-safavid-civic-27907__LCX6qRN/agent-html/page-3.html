Standards of Weight & Measure

As fixed in the time of Shah Tahmasp, observed by the muhtasib.

Man
The principal weight of the bazaar, equal to two hundred and forty mithqāl, or roughly three of the Frankish kilo. The man-i Tabriz is the smaller and is used for spices and metals.
Sang
A lesser weight reserved for dyestuffs, silks, and precious commodities. Equal to four hundred mithqāl. Each kalāntar carries a stamped iron sang for verification.
Gaz
The cloth-measure of the realm, of the length of the king's gaz from elbow and clenched in the cubits of the elbow. The gaz-i royal exceeds the gaz-i shar'i by one finger.
Mithqāl
The smallest unit, by which the weight of incense, drugs, and the like, are measured. Twenty-four nakhud compose one mithqāl.

Inspections of the Muhtasib

By appointment of His Honourable the kalāntar of His Majesty's Ministers

The honoured Muhtasib, accompanied by the scribe and four attendants of the watch, shall pass through the arcades on the appointed days, weighing the merchant's stock against the stamped iron of the Office.
