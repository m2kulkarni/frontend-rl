History of the
Cloister

A century and a quarter of stewardship over the sacred arts of the cathedral building age — glass, vellum, and stone gathered in the rooms of the Priory of Saint-Olivier-des-Aulnes.

[motif: quatrefoil]

Three Foundations

1897

The Antiquarian's Vow

Édouard de Saint-Pierre, antiquarian of Rouen, undertakes the burden of order from the dispossessions of Saint-Jean-des-Vignes during the 1972 restoration campaign. His prayer, recorded in his deed of foundation: Lux et lapidem custodire — to keep the light, and the stone.

1923

The Glass Bequest

Upon the antiquarian's death his hen-aided the addition to the conservation, the conservatory of light to the conservation prevention, the eight-window dome of mediaeval stained glass — eighty-six panels of sky, blue, and twelve woven blue work.

1946–1962

The Rebuilding

After hostilities damaged the four roof joists, the cloister of the new chapter and house was rebuilt stone-by-stone by the apprentices of the Compagnons du Devoir. The original Limousine foundation became a permanent institutional in 1947, and the museum opened to the visiting in 1962.

[motif: trefoil]

An Account of the Cloister

The Priory of Saint-Olivier-des-Aulnes was the seventh foundation of the Order of Cluny in northern France, dedicated by Innocent II in 1138 and dissolved in 1791 in the long, dispossessive years of the Revolution. Its stone shell stood in the field for a hundred and six years before Édouard de Saint-Pierre acquired the cloister-walk and the chapel ruin in 1897. By the time of his death in 1923, the chapel had been re-roofed, the lapidary garden laid out among the surviving column-shafts, and the first hundred panes of glass installed in their painted lead. The rebuilding after 1946 was a monument of slow patience; the apprentices of the Compagnons du Devoir worked block by block to a 1789 elevation, the only known elevation.
