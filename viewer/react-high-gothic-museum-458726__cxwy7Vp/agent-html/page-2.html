Galleries &
Permanent
Collection

Seven galleries housing the cathedral arts of northern France, arranged along a single processional axis from Lancet Hall to the open Mason's Yard.

[motif: quatrefoil]

The Cloister Museum's permanent collection comprises one thousand four hundred and seventy-two catalogued objects, of which two hundred and sixteen are on continuous public display across the seven galleries described below. The remaining works are accessible to credentialed researchers through the Reading Room by appointment, and a rotating selection appears in the museum's biennial loan exhibition. All accession numbers follow the museum standard adopted in 1962: prefix CMS, gallery code, and an acquisition sequence.

The Seven Galleries

In processional order, west to east

I

The Lancet Hall

— Anno MCMXCVII —

Twenty-four panels of thirteenth-century French stained glass, arranged in three superposed registers across the east wall and lit from behind by a calibrated daylight rig held at 2,700 K.

Provenance
Long-term loan from the Diocese of Soissons, contracted 2011, renewed 2024 for a further fifteen years. Removed from the dismboriations of Saint-Jean-des-Vignes during the 1972 restoration campaign.
Conservation
Lead-cames replaced 1989 (Atelier Lorin, Chartres). Vidimus film applied 2014. Annual deflection survey shows ⌇ 1mm at panel I.4 since installation; within tolerance.
II

The Tympanum Court

— Anno MMI —

A single carved Last Judgment fragment, limestone, 2.4 m across, depicting the weighing of souls and the mouth of Leviathan. Mounted at original portal height on a steel armature designed by Bernier + Pickering.

Provenance
Antoine workshop, circa 1240. Recovered from the rubble of a demolished parish church near Rouen in 1908, held by the Musée de Picardie and permanent transfer to the Cloister Museum on 2010.
Conservation
Consolidated with ethyl silicate (Wacker OH 100), 2003. No biological growth recorded since installation in climate-controlled court (RH 50 ± 2%, 19 °C).
III

The Scriptorium

— Anno MMIII —

Twelve illuminated leaves displayed under reduced lighting at 50 lux, including a Beatus initial in deep ultramarine and miniam from the Abbey of Saint-Sever (folio 11Ab; Apocalypse cycle).

Provenance
Mixed: leaves loaned from the Cloister family (eight); the Cluny Society duplicate, 1968. Eleven prayer fragments donated by the Marchand family, 1964.
Conservation
Leaves rotated on a 90-day cycle to limit cumulative light exposure (target 0.4 megalux-hours per annum). Mylar interleaving renewed 2022.
IV

The Reliquary Chamber

— Anno MMV —

Seventeen reliquaries in champlevé enamel, gilt copper, and rock crystal. The centerpiece is a Limoges chasse, c. 1190, depicting the martyrdom of Saint Thomas Becket.

Provenance
Bequest of Mrs. Eleanor Whitcombe, 1979, with subsequent acquisitions from the Du Bois collection (1991) and the Lethier-Bourgeois of restitution to civilian custodians (2007).
Conservation
Enamel surfaces examined under raking light biennially. The Becket chasse underwent gold-leaf reintegration on the V&A conservation studios, 2018.
V

The Capitals Cloister

— Anno MMVII —

Forty-one carved capitals on freestanding limestone columns, arranged in a square plan around a central well. Subjects include foliate, monstrous, and Eucharistic programmes.

Provenance
Drawn from the demolished cloister of the Priory of Saint-Pons-de-Thomières (Hérault), dispersed 1791, reassembled through stone trade auctions 1903 and 2015.
Conservation
Salt efflorescence on capitals II.7 and IV.3 (gypsum) treated by poultice 2023; positive drainage installed in alcove since.
VI

The Rose Window Study

— Anno MMX —

A scholarly gallery of six full-scale cartoon reconstructions of the great rose windows of Chartres, Notre-Dame de Paris, and Reims, prepared by the Atelier Simon-Marq, 1998–2014.

Provenance
Commissioned directly by the Cloister Museum thanks to a Lefèvre family bequest. The cartoons are themselves now considered conservation-reference documents.
Conservation
Pigment stability tested annually. The Notre-Dame de Paris cartoon was photographed at high resolution in 2009 and now serves the post-fire restoration committee.
VII

The Mason's Yard

— Anno MMXIV —

An open-air gallery of eighty-eight architectural fragments — moments, finials, gargoyles, rosary elements, and one full surviving spire pinnacle — laid out as a working archaeological grid.

Provenance
Assembled from the salvage records of nineteen French and English cathedral fabric offices, 19th onward. Each piece bears its original site mark in chalk or yellow chalk.
Conservation
Exposed to ambient weather by curatorial intent; lichen growth recorded as part of the long-term study (Veron in Open Air 1994–present).
