A STUDIO VISIT
Indigo at Aizome-an

The fifth-
generation
dyer who refused
synthetic blue

Naoko Furusawa works barefoot beside a 200-year-old earthenware vat sunk into the floor of a Nishijin machiya. She is the fifth Furusawa to do so. The vat, she explains, has not been emptied since 1834.

執筆 · REPORTED BYMariko Endō
写真 · PHOTOGRAPHSKentarō Aoyama
訪問 · VISITED22 FEB 2024
読了 · READ9 MIN
I

That Naoko mixes water and her grandfather's bread starter, that her sukumo arrives in twelve-kilo bales pressed by a man whose grandfather once dyed for Hosokawa daimyo, and that she keeps her pH between four and five — these are the parts most easily reported.

What is not is gesture, the precise lift of cotton from the brown crust of the vat to the air, where the cloth turns over six seconds from a mossy yellow-green to the unmistakable blue-grey of an indigo dawn. The colour is in the oxygen, not the dye.

"The colour finds you, more than you find it. The vat does what the vat does. I only have to listen, and not interrupt." — Naoko Furusawa, 2024

A Conversation, Transcribed

— You took over from your father in 2003. Did you ever consider not taking over?

"I did. I was a graphic designer in Osaka for four years before I came back. The vat was kept by my father with one apprentice. He was sixty-eight then. If I had not come back, the vat would have ended."

— What is the hardest thing to teach?

"Patience. The blue you take out at three minutes is not the blue you take out at six minutes. People want to take out at three minutes because they want a result. But the cloth has its own time."

— You have refused synthetic indigo throughout your career. Why?

"It is not refusal — it is just that the synthetic colour is not the same colour. They share a name, that is all."

— What of your daughter?

"She is twenty-three. She is in Berlin. The vat is patient. We will see what the vat decides."

"The bath has been fermenting since 1834. My job is not to dye cloth. My job is to keep something alive long enough to hand it to whoever comes next."
