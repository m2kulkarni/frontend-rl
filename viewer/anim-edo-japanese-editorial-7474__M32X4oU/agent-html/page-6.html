§ 1 · STATEMENTOn the matter of why we print.

The Tōkaidō Review is a quarterly journal devoted to the material culture of the Edo period — the years between Tokugawa Ieyasu's first arrival on the eastern marshes and the abdication of his fifteenth despot. We publish slowly, four issues a year, on uncoated paper from the Echizen mills.

Our remit is narrow. The artisans who pulled indigo from a dye-vat in the morning. The printers who carved twelve and fourteen blocks for a single sheet. The road-keepers who emptied lanterns at dawn. We are interested in lives that left small marks but indelible ones.

Founded in 2019 by Mariko Sato (until recently of a former century) from the Edo–Tokyo Museum, the Review remains independent, reader-funded, and unaffiliated with any university press.
