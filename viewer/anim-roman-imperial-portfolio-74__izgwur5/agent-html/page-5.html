PARS  V ·  LIBER SCRIPTORUM

SCRIPTA

A chronological register of essays, lectures and field notes, kept since the founding of the practice. Reproduction by permission of the patrons named where applicable.

INDEX SCRIPTORUM

AUGUST · MMXXIV

VIAUG.MMXXIV
ESSAY · III PAGES

“Concrete Without Apology”

A modest defence of opus caementicium as a structural and ethical material, against the present habit of pretending it is something else. Written for the journal Casabella and reprinted with permission.

XIVAUG.MMXXIV
FIELD NOTE · OSTIA

“The Threshold is the Argument”

Three afternoons spent re-measuring the principal threshold mosaics at Ostia Antica with apprentices: every doorway is a question of two architectures meeting, and the floor is where they sign their names against each other.

SEPTEMBER · MMXXIV

IISEP.MMXXIV
LECTURE · ROMA III

“On the Coffer, as Structural Honesty”

Delivered to the fourth-year students at the Facoltà di Architettura, Sapienza, on the cassettone as a means of making weight visible. The Pantheon offered, twice, as the only argument worth bringing.

XIXSEP.MMXXIV
ESSAY · IV PAGES

“Travertine is a Slow Material”

On the patience the stone enforces on those who cut it. Notes from a fortnight at Tivoli, where the family who runs the third-from-last working quarry has agreed to teach the studio one wedge per season.

XXVIISEP.MMXXIV
SHORT NOTE · ROMA

“To Walk is Rome”

A walking-route description of the Via Sacra, written for the apprentices — the eleven things to look for, the four to ignore, and the place where Augustus stops and Hadrian begins, if you let your foot be honest about it.

OCTOBER · MMXXIV

IIIOCT.MMXXIV
FIELD NOTE · CARRARA

“The Pediment Returns”

An afternoon among the master Filippo Zambelli at the saw-house in Carrara — a megalomaniacal project to fit a serlian pediment to a private apartment in Genova. The argument for the pediment is, as always, an argument about the eye finding the keystone first.

XVOCT.MMXXIV
LECTURE · AQUILEIA

“Against the Frameless Window”

The plate-glass curtain wall offered as the architectural equivalent of the missing frontispiece in a printed book. Delivered at the Aquileia summer school of conservation. Forty pages of notes, of which six were spoken aloud.

NOVEMBER · MMXXIV

VIIINOV.MMXXIV
ESSAY · II PAGES

“Mosaic, and the Patient Hand”

On the slowness imposed by the gem-cutter’s wheel and the small hammer; the studio bench is itself an argument, against the tools that will not let it be slow. Written for the journal L’Erma di Bretschneider.

XXNOV.MMXXIV
SHORT NOTE · VENEZIA

“A Catalogue of Verb”

The verbs of architecture as opposed to the nouns. To stand, to lean, to bear, to span, to enclose, to cast a shadow against the wall, to admit the rain after long rain. The list of nouns is short by comparison.

DECEMBER · MMXXIV

IVDEC.MMXXIV
ESSAY · II PAGES

“Notes from a Closed City”

A walk through Pompeii in late November, between the cypresses and the shuttered bookstall: a city kept in a state of pause. The mosaic floor of the Casa del Fauno carries forty years of footfall on its half-restored terrace at noon.

MARCH · MMXXV

VMAR.MMXXV
ESSAY · III PAGES

“Why We Still Draw the Order”

An apologia for measured drawing as the basis of architectural pedagogy. The five orders are not nostalgia: they are the longest-tested grammar humanity has agreed upon, and to draw them by hand is to learn to listen.

APRIL · MMXXV

XIIAPR.MMXXV
LECTURE · FIRENZE

“Vitruvius at the Hardware Store”

A lecture for the Sovrintendenza in Firenze on what the Vitruvian triad asks of a 21st-century practitioner who sources his copper from the same shop as everyone else. The argument: the difference is the patience, not the catalogue.

ENVOI · CORRESPONDENCE

Reading is its own discipline; correspondence is its instrument. Letters in any of the four working languages are answered, in their own language, within the fortnight.
