Folio III · The Bazaar Register

Posted Prices & Weights

of the Qaysarieh and the Caravanserais Adjoining
Week of the 12th — 18th of Rabi'-al-Awwal

Affixed by the muhtasib at the gate of the Qaysarieh, that no merchant may plead ignorance.

By the order of His Majesty Shah ‘Abbas, Refuge of the World, and under the seal of the Dīvānbegi of Isfahan, the following prices are fixed for the week beginning this twelfth day of Rabi'-al-Awwal of the year one thousand thirty and seven of the Hijra. Coin reckoned in 'abbāsī of full silver weight, and where lesser sums are noted, in shahi and dinār. No merchant may demand more.

Standards of Weight & Measure

The principal weight of the bazaar, equal to two hundred and forty mithqāls, or roughly three of the Frankish kilo. The mann shāh is twice the weight and used in coppersmithy.

Mann (mann-i Tabriz)3 mithqāls
Sang (literally "stone")A lesser weight reserved for slim, silk-bearing materials
Gaz (measure)The cloth-measure of the realm, of the length of the king's gaz, set down and chained at the entrance of the Qaysarieh
Mithqāl (its 4¾-th)The smallest unit weight; for silver, saffron, and the other of the items so noted. Twenty-four sūbāzī to the mithqāl
By appointment of the Dīvānbegi, confirmed in person

Inspections of the Muhtasib

The honoured Muhtasib, accompanied by two scribes and four attendants of the watch, shall pass through the arcades on the appointed days, weighing the merchant's stock against the stamped iron of the Office.

1st  &  15th

Of Each Month

Short weight is fined at four times the deficit. A second offence is referred to the Dīvānbegi for trial, and a third committed in measure for permanent expulsion from the markets.
