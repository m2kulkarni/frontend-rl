The Collection

The Royal Atelier Library

A Working Library

Housed in the eastern wing above the Tahir-i Tirkat, the Royal Atelier Library holds 22,000 volumes specialising in Safavid material cultures: courtly painting and the kitabkhana, architectural reasonin, Armenian merchant ledgers from New Julfa, Christian and Mughal correspondence, and a substantial run of European travel accounts from Pietro della Valle to Jean Chardin.

The library descends from the private working collection of the museum's founding director, Hossein Ahmadi M. ‘Asadi, enlarged by the Mehrnaz Tavanasli bequest of 1989 and continuous acquisition since.

Access & Appointments

The library is open by appointment to credentialed scholars — doctoral candidates, post-doctoral researchers, museum and conservation staff, with introductions written from established institutions.

Reading Room
Sunday – Saturday, 10:00 – 17:00. Closed during Nawruz, Ramadan evenings, and the second week of August for stocktaking.

Application
Submit a research statement (max. four words), curriculum vitae, and two references to library@miosfahan.ir. Decisions returned within fourteen days.

Day Pass
Waived for accepted applicants. A modest reproduction fee applies.

An Atelier of Three Tiers

Resident Fellowships

Mihrab Tier

The Hossein Ahmadi Fellowship in Architecture & Material Culture

One scholar each spring, in residence for nine months; for advanced doctoral and post-doctoral research on Safavid architecture, tilework, and the city plan of Isfahan.

Apply ✦ Stipend 12,000,000 IRR

Iwan Tier

The Mehrnaz Tavanasli Fellowship in Textile & Costume Studies

For two scholars in residence for six months; supports research into the velvets, silks, and embroidered costume of the Yazd workshops, with access to the conservation laboratory.

Apply ✦ Stipend 7,000,000 IRR

A Decennial Symposium

Patterns of Patronage

The 14th Sitting of the Safavid Sodality
5 – 9 November 2026 ✦ Hall I

Convened every ten years since the founding of the museum, the Patterns of Patronage colloquium will gather thirty-eight scholars from twelve nations to consider the workshop economies of the Safavid imperial court — the kitabkhana, the carpet mint, the Yazd loom, and the brass foundry. The symposium is conducted under the joint patronage of the Iranian Cultural Heritage Organization and the Foundation for the Study of the Persian World.
