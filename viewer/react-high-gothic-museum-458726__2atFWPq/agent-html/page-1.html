The Cobalt of Chartres

Forty-two panels and medallions, including those known to attend the rose of MCXX, set against the workshop's signature deep cobalt — the pigment whose recipe was, for a generation, kept under oath.

Exhibition · I
Ribs & Buttresses

Thirty architectural drawings rescued from a vault, scale-tracing flying buttresses, drawing how Bernard de Soissons and Jean d'Orbais resolved the upper choirs — including the working sketch of a flying buttress that was never built.

[motif: arch-pointed]Gallery III
Exhibition · I
Folios from the Très Riches Heures

A loan from the Musée Condé. The original folios of the Limbourg brothers' calendar cycle, displayed for sixty days only under controlled lux. The April folio appears benignly for the first time outside the Château de Chantilly since its last colour facsimile printed in 1904.

A Word from the Director

Mlle Geneviève d'Esperandieu · Directrice, The Cloister Museum

It is my joy to welcome you, in this last month of Michaelmas, 2024, to the opening of our north wing — a chamber long imagined and at last built, whose seven lancets bear the same compass-points as those at Reims and Chartres, that the morning light might pass through coloured glass before it touches any visitor's eye.

We are a small institution, and an unfashionable one. We collect cathedrals in fragments — a medallion, a quatrefoil, a single foot of cobalt sky — and we ask of our visitors only what the cathedrals themselves once asked: patience, attention, and the willingness to look upward.

The new wing was made possible by a bequest from the Confraternity of Saint Bernard and by twelve years of stonework by the apprentices of the Compagnons du Devoir. To them, and to you, our thanks.

— Geneviève d'Esperandieu
