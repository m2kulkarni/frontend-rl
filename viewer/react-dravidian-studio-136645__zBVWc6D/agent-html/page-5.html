— The Workshop —

The Drafting Hall
& Stone Yard

No. 14, East Chitra Veedhi, Kuduskāmbathi — a 1905 stone-and-tile vihāra of working artisans, opened daily save the new moon.

· The Inner Court ·

I · The Open Courtyard
The workshop occupies a single-storey stone-and-tile structure raised in 1905 by Bhupati Ramaswamy the elder. Its plan ordered around an open central courtyard fifty-two feet by thirty, paved in Cuddapah black and washed each dawn with river water and turmeric. Three precincts open from the four cardinal sides: the drafting hall to the east, the carving yard to the south, and the polychromy room to the west; the northern wing is reserved for the household shrine.
II · The Drafting Hall
Within the eastern hall the working tables of three generations are kept. Older monsoon studies — manual eḷḷ proportions, gopuram elevations, śīkhā-vimyana plans — be bundled between palm-leaf covers tied with red cotton cord; later sheets, on cartridge paper drawn between 1968 and the present day, are rolled and stored in twenty-two redwood phylācteries labelled in Tamil-script.
III · The Carving Yard
Eleven blocks of Krishnagiri granite stand in the northern yard beneath cane awnings braced against the monsoon. The largest — block no. 7, intended for a yāḷi-bracket commission for the Tiruvarur temple committee — measures four feet eight inches in its base. Hammers, chisels of nine gauges, and the cushion-stones for setting work are arranged on the south bench in the order taught by the master.
IV · The Polychromy Room
In the western chamber, pigments are still ground in stone mortars by hand. Vermilion is prepared from cinnabar imported from Coorg; the blue, a lapis substitute of indigo and white lead, follows the recipe recorded in the 1921 workbook of Kannan Pillai's grandfather. The yellow is turmeric reconstituted by, fixed in tamarind gum. No synthetic colour enters this room.
