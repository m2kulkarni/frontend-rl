PRACTICE & DISCIPLINES

Five hereditary disciplines drawn from the Mayamata, Manasara and the Kashyapa Shilpa, practiced to unbroken transmission from Sthapati Ramaswamy Atikal (1854—1921) to the present workshop on Big Bazaar Street.

Vastu · Vimana · Polychromy · Granite · Kalasha
I · Vastu Purusha Mandala Layout

For new shrines and grama-devata temples we draft the complete site horoscope to the agama tradition: orientation by the gnomon-shadow at the equinox, division of the plot into the 64-pada mandala or the 81-pada paramashayika grid, and assignment of the deities of the directions to their proper cells. Garbhagriha placement, sūla proportional checks (sayana-mana plus vastu-pala) and the dimension of the prakara walls are all worked before a single stone is cut.

Drawings are issued in three sets — the talichhanda (plan), urdhvachhanda (elevation) and the panjara details — rendered in iron-gall ink on hand-sized palm paper at 1:48 scale, with a working copy in vermilion wash for the masons. The consultation includes a site visit, the soil-test (bhumi-pariksha) and the auśvaranyana ceremony marking the auspicious commencement.

II · Vimana & Gopuram Tier Design

We design superstructures from the modest tri-tala (three-storey) shrine of a village agraharam to the nine-tala rajagopuram of a major personal kovil. Each tala is calculated in adi-mana units derived from the garbhagriha width, with the tapering rhythm of the kuta, sala and panjara aedicules set by the classical Pallava-Chola progression. Shikhara forms — nagara, dravida or vesara — are chosen to honour the presiding deity and the regional convention.

Our drawings give every kapota cornice, every yali bracket, every makara torana sculptural niche carved out by hand. Recent commissions include the seven-tala southern gopuram, completed 2019 in collaboration with the Adi Chennai circle.

III · Polychrome Restoration & Gold Leaf

Stucco figures on gopurams, vimāna in shaping vessels, sukha mandapas and the dwajasthamba columns shall be repainted by our atelier in the seasoned palette of the Nayaka tradition: vermilion (lingulipa) ground, peacock-blue limestone-ultramarine, saffron (kunkuma-pita) shaped polychromes, large black outlines and 22-carat gold leaf on crowns and finials. We grind our own pigments from cinnabar, indigo, orpiment and lime according to the Vishnudharmottara prescription.

The work proceeds tier by tier on bamboo scaffolding, with consecration of each course before the next is begun. Gilding is laid on a one-mil gauze of conch shell fluff burnished with agate; this finish has been documented to last 80 to 110 years in coastal Tamil air. Documentation photography in raking light is delivered with each commission.

IV · Granite & Krishna Shila Carving

We carve in Tirumayam quarries grey granite for structural members — pillars, beams, adhishtana courses — and the prized black Krishna shila from the Karkala beds for figurative work. Yali capital pillars from the seventeenth-century Madurai school, dvarapala guardians at twice life-size, lotus rosettes for ceiling cofferes, and processional utsavas resorts in the round are all cut entirely by hand using the traditional set of forty-two chisels.

A figure passes through five named stages — the rough-cut (kakuda-shilpa), the established proportion (manas), the surface (lekha), the fine modelling (ushaktrama) and the final polish with leaf-emery (krittipa-pradhana) before the next is begun. Every piece is consecrated in the workshop with the netra-unmilana eye-opening rite before it is dispatched.

V · Kalasha Casting & Consecration

The crowning kalasha-finial — the pot-shaped vessel that sits at the apex of every vimana and gopuram — is cast in our foundry in pancha-loha, the alloy of five metals laid down in the Mayamata: gold, silver, copper, lead, and zinc in the proportions specified by the Shilpasrastra. We cast by the lost-wax bīja-vidhāna procedure, burnished to mirror in conch-shell fluff, and gilded with consecrated water of the seven sacred rivers.

Consecration is a five-day rite. The empty kalasha is filled with the nine grains, the seven sacred earths, and Kaveri river water; mantras of the agama appropriate to the deity are inscribed on a copper plate sealed within. The mounting on the shikhara is timed to a muhurta calculated by our staff jyotisha, Sri Venkatasa Sharma. We have placed kalashas on 73 temples since 1948.

Typical Duration
