Vamsavali — Genealogy of the Workshop

recorded at East Chitra Veedhi, Madurai · by hand of the present principal

GENI
Sthapati Vaidyanatha Ramaswamy
1854 — 1921
Pupil of the Tanjore court guild under Sri Subbaraya Bhagavti, Vaidyanatha received the silā and chisel investiture in 1872 and removed to Madurai the following monsoon. The earliest extant drawing in our chest — a longitudinal section of the Pudu Mandapam buttress — bears his cipher and the date Vaikāsi 1879. He fathered four sons: the second, Muthuswami, alone took up the rule and the gānita.
GENII
Sthapati Muthuswami Ramaswamy
1881 — 1953
Commissioned in 1923 for the fine renewal of the western gopuram at the Andal Temple, Srivilliputhur — a campaign of seven seasons in which he dressed by hand the kalasha-finial and re-set three of the apsaras corner figures. His ledger of that work, bound in red buckram, remains the workshop's single most-consulted reference.
GENIII
Sthapati Arunachalam Ramaswamy
1916 — 1991
Awarded the Silver Medal at the Madras Presidency Crafts Exhibition of 1948 for a half-scale model of a Dravida vimana cut from a single block of Krishnagiri granite. He served as consulting sthapati to nine renovations between 1952 and 1976, and corresponded at length with the epigraphist K. R. Srinivasan on the proportions given in the Mayamata.
GENIV
Sthapati Subramaniam Ramaswamy
1944 — 2013
Trained from his eleventh year, Subramaniam inherited the workshop in 1985 and was the first of the line to keep its measured drawings in true cyanotype paper rather than palm leaf. Under him the present drafting hall was extended by one bay (1988) and the north-facing stone yard was paved.
GENV
Sthapati R. Gahela Ramaswamy
b. 1968 — Principal, Present
The current principal received the silā in 1996 from his father and now directs the atelier's three working departments — vastu consultation, gopuram restoration drawings, and stone-carving commissions. He continues the daily morning recitation from the Mānasāra before the chest of pattern-books.

The Canon

Mānasāra · Mayamata

All drawings issued from this workshop are set out in conformity with the two principal Sanskrit treatises on temple architecture — the Mānasāra of the Cōḻa province and the Mayamata of the Pāṇḍya. Master pupil-tested editions of both these texts, struck under the imprimatur of the Mysore Oriental Library, sit in the working chest behind the principal's drafting board.

Where the master drawings — most notably on the heights to width ratio of the door-jamb — diverge from the canon, the workshop has, since the time of Arunachalam Sthapati, followed the Mayamata as the more recent.
