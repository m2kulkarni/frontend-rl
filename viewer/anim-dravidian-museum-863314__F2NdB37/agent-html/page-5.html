SCHOLARSHIP & THE PĀLĀRU JOURNAL

Since 1971, the Trust has nominated a programme of original research into the temple architecture, bronze-casting, ritual painting, and literary tradition of South India, with the result that some thirty-eight monographs and the quarterly Pālāru Journal of Dravidian Temple Studies have appeared under the imprint of its Reading Press.

THE PĀLĀRU JOURNAL

A quarterly review of Dravidian temple architecture & iconography

FOUR ISSUES ANNUALLY · PRINT & DIGITAL

Each issue contains six to eight peer-reviewed essays, a programme of inscriptional notes, and a chronicle of conservation and excavation across the Tamiḷ country. The Journal is sent free to all Patron members of the Trust and to a list of partner libraries world-wide.

THE VISITING SUMMER FELLOWSHIP

An eight-week residence open to scholars at any career stage

ELIGIBILITYA practicing scholar holding a doctoral degree (or equivalent professional standing) in art history, archaeology, religious studies, Tamiḷ literature, or a cognate field. We welcome independent researchers and curators alongside the academy.
STIPEND₹ 1,30,000 with on-site accommodation in the Reading House, full library and gallery access. The Trust covers one return rail or air ticket within India.
RESIDENCEEight weeks in residence, June 1 — July 31. Two open colloquia and a public lecture are required of the fellow during the term.
OUTPUTOne peer-reviewed essay submitted to the Journal within twelve months of the residence; the Trust holds first-publication right.
APPLICATIONSOpen 1 September; close 30 November. A two-stage process: written proposal, then interview at the Trust offices in Tanjavur (or by video for international applicants).
NEXT INTAKEThe 2026 Fellow will be announced in February 2026. Twenty-three Fellows have been hosted since 1971.

To apply, write to the Curator of Studies at fellows@palaru.museum.in with a 1,500-word proposal, a current curriculum vitae, and a writing sample.

CURRENT RESEARCH PROJECTS

ESTABLISHED · 2022

THE EPIGRAPHIC SURVEY OF THE LOWER KAVĒRI BANK

A four-year survey, in association with the Archaeological Survey of India, of all undocumented inscriptions on temple compound walls in the Tanjavur, Pudukkottai, and Sivagaṅga districts.

ESTABLISHED · 2023

THE NATIVE COLOUR PROJECT

A pigment-chemistry study of the painted ceiling-cloths of the Mīṇākṣi-Sundareśvarar shrine, in partnership with the National Gallery, London, and the Sarabhai Foundation.

ESTABLISHED · 2025

THE NĀYAK ESTAMPAGE PROJECT

A digital corpus of the some 2,800 estampages held in the Reference Library, with high-resolution scanning and a public-facing portal — first phase live in late 2026.

FORTHCOMING FROM THE MUSEUM PRESS

  • SEPTEMBER 2026BLAZE OF THE BRONZE: THE ENERGETICS OF THE IMPERIAL CHŌḶA WORKSHOP — by R. Kalyāṇi.
    The first length-study of the energetics of the Imperial Chōḷa atelier — temperature, alloy, gesture — drawn from a decade of metallurgical analysis.
  • DECEMBER 2026THE LITANY OF AIYĀVAḶAM: SELECTED INSCRIPTIONS, EDITED & TRANSLATED — by K. Naṅgammai & R. Sundararājan.
    A critical edition of the Aiyāvaḷam corpus — 137 inscriptions of the Pāṇḍya country, in Old Tamiḷ with English and Tamiḷ-modern translations facing.
  • FEBRUARY 2027THE BODY OF THE GOPURAM: A PHOTOGRAPHIC ATLAS OF THE NĀYAK PYLONS — by Padma Subrahmaṇyam & Raghu Ranjanan.
    A four-volume atlas, photographed in the eight-by-ten format, of the seven greatest Nāyak gopurams of South India — Madurai, Cidambaram, Tiruvaṇṇāmalai, Sricittapur.
