Lucia Marciana

Founder of Atelier Vesperia, working in the lineage of Roman imperial geometry — axis, rhythm, and the quiet authority of stone.

[motif: meander-greek-key]

Lucia Marciana was born in Naples in 1984, raised within sight of the buried colonnades of Pompeii. She read architecture at the Sapienza Università di Roma, completing the Master of Architecture in 2010 with a thesis on the proportional systems of the Markets of Trajan. Her early years in practice were spent in apprenticeship to Paolo Vespasiani, the conservator and draughtsman, with whom she worked between 2010 and 2014 on restoration drawings for the Domus Aurea and a private chapel in Tivoli.

She founded Atelier Vesperia in 2016, in a tufa-walled studio on the Via dei Coronari. The practice is small — three architects, two draughtsmen, a single mosaicist — and accepts roughly four commissions each year: a villa, a public room, a courtyard, an ornament. Lucia draws every plan herself, by hand, on tracing paper with a 0.3 millimetre lead.

Teaching

Since 2019, Lucia has held the chair in Composition & Ornament at the Accademia di Belle Arti di Roma, Via di Ripetta 222. She conducts a single graduate atelier each autumn, limited to twelve students, devoted to the geometry of Roman imperial plans and the discipline of the hand-drawn line. Past visiting positions include the Cooper Union (2022) and the British School at Rome (2017, 2020).

Credo

"An architecture begins with one line drawn straight through the site — and everything that follows must answer to it. Restraint in material, generosity in proportion: the room owes its dignity to what is left out. Travertine, brick, lime, and the slow tessera. No more."

Lucia Marciana
