Anno Domini · MMXXIV

ABOUT THE MUSEUM & HISTORY OF THE CLOISTER

A century and a quarter of stewardship over the sacred arts of the cathedral building age — glass, vellum, and stone gathered in the name of the Priory of Saint-Albain-des-Apâtre.

[motif: trefoil]

THREE FOUNDATIONS

1897

THE ANTIQUARIAN'S VOW

Édouard de Saint-Pierre, antiquarian of Reims and resident of the rue Cardinal-Saint-Pierre, makes the foundational pledge to assemble all the cathedral debris recovered from the bombardments of the prior generation, that they might endure beyond him. He buys back two crates of stained glass from a wagonwright at half their weight in iron.

1923

THE GLASS BEQUEST

Upon the antiquarian's death, the bequest of two thousand panels of stained glass — gathered through the work of three priests, two stone-cutters, and one rural curate — is given over to the Diocese of Reims, on condition that they be displayed under daylight, in unbroken sequence. The first formal galleries open in the choir of the closed Église Saint-Médard.

1944—1962

THE REBUILDING

After incendiary damage in the war years, the cloister itself and the lancet hall undergo full re-glazing. The reconstruction is overseen by the Master Glazier of the Atelier Lorin, whose family had supplied the original Saint-Médard panels. The institution is incorporated as the Cloister Museum on the Feast of the Translation of the Body of Saint Aldhelm, in 1962.

AN ACCOUNT OF THE CLOISTER

[motif: trefoil]

The Priory of Saint-Albain-des-Apâtre stood empty from the suppressions of 1791 until Édouard de Saint-Pierre, then fifty-four years of age and lately retired from his commerce in ecclesiastical antiquities of Reims, took possession of its ruined chapter house and west tribune in the autumn of 1897. He installed his private collection — manuscript leaves, capital fragments, and a set of three rose-window medallions attributed to the workshop of Saint-Denis — in the surviving south range, opening one room to scholars by appointment.

Following the bequest of 1923 the trust acquired contiguous parcels to the north and east, regularising the precinct to its present 4.6 hectares. The new lancet hall, completed under the Foster atelier in 1962, runs ninety-three metres along the former rectorial nave; its twenty-two paired lancets house the rose of the de Sainte-Pierre glass at the original sill height. The Rose Window Study wing, dedicated in May 2018, completes the north-west angle of the cloister and provides climate-controlled vitrines for the medallion fragments and a small chapel for the conservation of pigments and lead cames.
