HALL II · THE PERMANENT COLLECTION

Galleries of the
Safavid Court

Seven halls trace two centuries of imperial patronage — from the cobalt tilework of the Maydan-i Naqsh-i Jahan to the inlaid steel of the arsenal of Shah Sultan Husayn. Each gallery is curated as a chapter in a single argument: that under the Safavids, Isfahan became, as the saying goes, half of the world.

THE SEVEN GALLERIES

GALLERY ONE

TILEWORK OF THE MAYDAN

144 OBJECTS · 8′6″

Cobalt and turquoise spandrels lifted from the south arcade of the Maydan-i Naqsh-i Jahan, exhibited in the orientation in which they would have stood.

CURATOR · M. DASTAN
GALLERY TWO

MANUSCRIPTS OF THE ROYAL KITABKHANA

88 OBJECTS · FOLIO ROOM

Illuminated leaves of the Shahnameh of Shah Tahmasp and pages from the lacquered bindings of Mir ‘Imad.

CURATOR · F. NEZHAD
GALLERY THREE

SILKS & TEXTILES OF YAZD

62 OBJECTS · LOOM ROOM

Silver- and gold-thread silks and velvets in seasonal rotation, with weave-mounted explanations of court practice.

CURATOR · B. ARJOMAND
GALLERY FOUR

METALWORK & INLAID STEEL

213 OBJECTS · THE ARSENAL

Damascened helmets, prayer-rail keys, and astrolabes from the workshops of Mashhad and Isfahan’s armoury.

CURATOR · H. LAJEVARDI
GALLERY FIVE

COINAGE OF SHAH ‘ABBAS I

410 OBJECTS · CABINET ROOM

Silver abbasi, mahmudi, and earlier dirhams of the mints of Tabriz, Kashan, Hamadan and Isfahan’s royal mint.

CURATOR · A. TABATABAEI
GALLERY SIX

PORTRAITURE: NOBLES & EUROPEANS

39 OBJECTS · SECOND COURT

Drawings after Riza ‘Abbasi and pen-portraits of the Augustinian Friars and Carmelites who entered Shah ‘Abbas’s court.

CURATOR · D. PIRBASTI
A SCHOLAR’S NOTE

THE RECONSTRUCTED IWAN

A full-scale tile-wall replica of the iwan of the Lutfullah, in the proportions calculated by the early measurements of A. Godard. The original was lost in 1873 to fire and now exists only here.
