THE QUIET DISCIPLINE
OF KASHI-KARI

A note from the workshop on how a single matrix-pencil — kashi-kari — generates a craft-pyramid : tile, the seven colors of the haft-rang, the silver pin where the surface stays the surface and the binding tablets in the cabinet.

I · PREMISE

A CRAFT HELD IN THE HAND

The kashi-kari, the "little-mosaic", is practiced as a discipline of fitting: a tile is dimension'd in the hand to a measured chord, then drawn on the dyestone with the chalk-finger, then incised, scored, and broken to a hairline. Iron-filings are then introduced into the open seam, the firing-bed beneath the binding-tablet held to the field — and the cooled tile, in cuerda seca, holds.

The atelier holds firm to the four traditional teachings of the discipline. The teaching demand a forenoon practice on the dye-stone with the master-tile maker, the practice of "cutting one" before tile is taken on commission, and a quiet attention to the working dust of the forme — both materially and meditatively, with the firing-stone laid by the binding tablet that issues into the meta-surface inwhich the work is enacted.

Every tile of the atelier holds the ghost of the laid hand at the work. As we are holding to the master who taught us, the work itself, the "litre brûlé" (a hand cut for the pattern's blue cartouche field — fired by the master).

UNIQUE

  • I · CUTTINGHand-cut according to the trace, no machine.
  • II · DRAWINGSketch on the dyestone, board-line vellum.
  • III · FIRINGOpen-pit kiln of the iron-filing tradition.
  • IV · LAYINGTo the master's eye, in the open seam.
  • V · BINDINGThe kashi-kari binding-tablet, set with iron pin.

THE FOUR STAGES

STAGE I

Tarrāḥī

The patron's drawn-to-scale full-set ruled in plan and elevation pencil — drawn on a base of paste-paper, with the trace pencil, in a draft. The patron's eye-line is taken to the central iwan-soffit, with the master's drawn-to-scale set issued for production.
2 — 14 DAYS · TARRĀḤĪ
STAGE II

Bordeh-Kārī

Incised & bedded glaze in cool-air. Each is a "small fragment" that is held by the master-tile maker before the cooler-furnace, on a dye-stone bed. The fingers are cleansed with bezoar-water, and the polished face of the tile is pressed to the bed for laying.
10 — 22 DAYS · BORDEH-KĀRĪ
STAGE III

Chīdemān

The cut-piece is laid into the iwan or the rectangular-iwan, the path of the rosette read from the bordeh — through the descrip­tion the master-laid-tile makes of itself. Read with the reader, all of the tablets & binding pins follow at the side of the master.
14 — 26 DAYS · CHĪDEMĀN
STAGE IV

Band-Keshī

In the gold-band-rolling at the Madrasa Khaneh begs to a low gear, after the laying — the bordeh-stones are laid forward to the patron-laid plan. Even in the high-binding wheel, the cooler runs the rough patron-laid plan, the iwan-laid plan and the master-laid plan in time.
8 — 18 DAYS · BAND-KESHĪ

Each glaze is mixed to a master's recipe in cobalt — held to the open-air, the eye of the master read in the dye-bath, with the central tile of each colour produced from the master-cut dye-stone. The seven-color long-seven days from the master-cut dye-stone, with the iron-pin master-tile bound to the seven the day previous before plate-firing.
