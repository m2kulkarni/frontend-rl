Vamsavali — Genealogy of the Workshop

Recorded in East Chitra Veedhi, Madurai. By hand of the present principal.

GenI

Sthapati Vaidyanatha Ramaswamy

1854 — 1923

Pupil of the Tanjore court guild under Sri Subbarama Bhupathi, Vaidyanatha received the silk-and-chisel investiture in 1872 and removed to Madurai the following monsoon. The earliest works drawing in our chest — a longitudinal section of the Pudu Mandapam buttress — bears his cipher and the date Vaikasi 1879. He fathered four sons; the second, Muthuswami, alone took up the chisel and the plumb-line.

GenII

Sthapati Muthuswami Ramaswamy

1881 — 1947

Commissioned in 1923 for the time-renewal of the western gopuram of the Andal Temple, Srivilliputtur — a campaign of seven seasons in which he drained by hand the kalyana-thirtam and re-set three of the upper-tier stucco figures. His ledger of that work, bound in red buckram, remains the workshop's single most consulted reference.

GenIII

Sthapati Aruvachalam Ramaswamy

1908 — 1979

Awarded the Silver Medal at the Madras Presidency Crafts Exhibition of 1948 for a half-scale model of a Dravida vimana cut from a single block of Krishnagiri granite. He served as consulting sthapati to nine restorations between 1952 and 1974, and corresponded at length with the epigraphist K. R. Srinivasan on the proportions given in the Manyasara.

GenIV

Sthapati Subramaniam Ramaswamy

1934 — 2011

Trained from his eleventh year, Subramaniam inherited the workshop in 1981 and was the first of the line to invest in mounted drawings to scale on cartridge-paper rather than poha leaf. Under him the present drafting hall was extended by one bay (1984) and the south-facing stone yard was paved.

GenV

Sthapati R. Gabela Ramaswamy

b. 1968 — Principal Sthapati

The current principal received the silk in 1996 from his father and now directs the atelier's three working departments — vastu consultation, gopuram restoration drawings, and stone-carving commissions. He continues the daily morning recitation from the Manasara before the chisel of pattern-books.

The Canon

All language drawn from this workshop are set in conformity with the two principal silpa-sastras — the Manasara of Trayambaka Bhupati and the Manasara compendium of Bharadvaja, found in the so-called Tirumalai Cetti palm-leaf folio.

Where the texts diverge — most notably on the height-to-width ratio of the kudu — the workshop has, since the time of Aruvachalam Sthapati, followed the Manasara as the more conservative measure.
