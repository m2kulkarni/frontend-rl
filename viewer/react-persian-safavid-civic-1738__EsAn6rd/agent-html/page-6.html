Visit the Dīvān · Hours & Direction

Petitioners, scribes, and curious travellers are received at the western arcade of the Maydān-i Naqsh-e Jahān. What follows is the practical guidance our doorkeepers most often give — committed here to paper so that none arrive unprepared.

Notes for Those Who Come

i.On the manner of dress

The chancery is a place of public record. Visitors of all stations are asked to come modestly attired — long sleeves, covered head for women in the inner halls, and clean footwear left on the second threshold. Robes of state are not expected.

ii.For the elderly & infirm

A shaded waiting bench of carved walnut stands in the inner courtyard, beside the cypress. Petitioners who cannot stand for the queue may signal the doorkeeper, who will summon a clerk to attend them where they sit.

iii.Public scribes

Directly opposite our entrance, beneath the eastern arcade, sit the registered public scribes (kātibān-i sirrutāntī). They draft petitions, certify translations, and read documents aloud for a head fee posted on their boards.

iv.The afternoon recess

At the mu'adhdhin's call for ẓuhr the registers are closed and sealed for the day. Matters arriving late will be received the following morning; no exception is made save for the Mayor's own warrant.

Approaching the Bureau

Enter the Maydān from its southern arch and keep the great pool to your right. The western arcade runs the length of the square; our doors are the third you will pass after the Qaysariyya Gate, marked by twin cobalt tiles bearing the city's cypress device. Should you lose your bearing, any of the kabāb-merchants under the arcade will point you to the Dīvān-khāna without charge — it is a kindness customary to the quarter.

Travellers arriving from the caravanserai may find it shorter to approach by the Chahār Bāgh and turn east at the Madrasa of the Mother of the Shāh; the walk takes roughly the time of two short ṣūras. Mounts and pack-animals are tethered at the public picket-line on the square's northern flank, never within the arcade itself.

The door of the Dīvān is open to whomsoever brings a clean matter and a quiet voice.
— Naqī-i Kūh, Keeper of the Gate
