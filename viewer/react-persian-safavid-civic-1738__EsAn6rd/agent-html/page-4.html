Register IV  ·  Of the Bureau

[motif: arch-ogee]

The Bureau & Its Officers

Established by royal firmān in the year 1006 of the Hijra, the Dīvān-i Shahr keeps the rolls of the residents of Naqsh-e Jahān, the measure of the markets, and the courses of the canals.

Bureau ✶ Founding
Of the Founding under Shāh ʿAbbās

In the year 1006 A.H. — being the year of the resolve of the royal seat from Qazvīn to Iṣfahān — His Majesty Shāh ʿAbbās, called the Great, did command that a Bureau be raised within the new city. Entering the masjids of Naqsh-e Jahān, that no measure should be false in the bazaars, no canal stopped against its neighbour, and no household untrue toward upon the rolls. The seal was struck in cobalt and gold and pressed upon the founding ledger by the hand of the wazīr Ḥātim Beg Urdūbādī.

From that day forward the Dīvān has kept its watch from the chamber beside the Qaysariyya gate, its officers four in number; its scribes nine, its couriers six, and its lamps trimmed each evening by the porter Ḥusayn Khān ʿAlī, son of Murād, in the manner appointed.

Roster  ·  The Pillars of Office

The Present Roster of Officers

[motif: arabesque-filet-divider]

Kalāntar — Mayor

Mīrzā Ḥusayn Khān

Lord Mayor of the City and chief warden of the Bureau. He sits in counsel each Saturday after the dawn prayer and receives prominent burdens the second and fourth hours.

Mustaufīʿ — Inspector of Markets

Āqā Ṣādiq Tabrīzī

Keeper of the just weight and the iron press. He walks the bāzār daily; the loaf of bread is fixed at one-shahī of nān-i sangak and the ney shall be straight and unrent in his measure.

Sirr-Dīr — Master of Waters

Ustād Ibrāhīm of Yazd

Apportionment of the Zāyande-rūd among the seven mādiī-canals. He keeps the schedule of the turn-tower (chargazū-tower) and audits the bridge of the Allāhverdī Khān and Khājū.

Munshī al-Mamalīk — Chief Scribe

Mīr Muḥammad-Bāqir

Chief of the writing-chamber, keeper of the great register and the seal of record. Under his hand pass all decrees of the Bureau; nine scribes copy fair under his watch.

Charge  ·  The Charter

The Threefold Duty of the Bureau

The keeping of true records — that no name be lost, no birth unwritten, no deed unwitnessed.

The measure of bread — that the loaf weigh full, the price hold steady, the poor not be wronged at the oven.

The proper repair of canals and bridges — that water touch every garden in its turn, and the spans of the river stand firm against flood.

Sealed at Iṣfahān, in the chamber beside the Qaysariyya gate. — On the Register of Quarters: Notices in Hearing.
