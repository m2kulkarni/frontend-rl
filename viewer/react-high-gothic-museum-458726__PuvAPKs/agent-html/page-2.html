Galleries &
Permanent
Collection

Seven galleries housing the cathedral arts of northern France, arranged along a single processional axis from Lancet Hall to the open Mason's Yard.

The Cloister Museum's permanent collection comprises one thousand four hundred and twenty-two catalogued objects, of which two hundred and sixteen are on continuous public display across the seven galleries described below. The remainder is studied by appointment, and a rotating selection appears in the museum's biennial loan exhibitions.

All accession numbers follow the museum standard adopted in 1962: prefix CMG, gallery code, and acquisition sequence. Provenance citations appearing in the entries that follow are abbreviated; the full record is available, on request, in the reading room at no fee — even from the unobserved.

The Seven Galleries

In processional order, west to east

I
[motif: arch-pointed]

The Lancet Hall

Circa 1180 – 1215

Twenty-four panels of thirteenth-century French stained glass, arranged in three superimposed registers across the east wall and lit from below by a calibrated daylight rig held at 2,700 K.

Provenance
Long-term loan from the Diocese of Soissons, contracted MM, renewed MMXIV for a further fifteen years. Removed from the dismembered choir of Saint-Jean-des-Vignes during the 1793 inventaire campagne.
Conservation
Lead came replaced 1989 (Atelier Loire, Chartres). Vidimus film applied 2014. Annual deflection survey; current lean to panel LH-014 measured 1.2 mm, within tolerance.
II
[motif: arch-pointed]

The Tympanum Court

Circa 1140 – 1190

A single carved Last Judgment fragment, limestone, 2.4 m across, depicting the weighing of souls and the mouth of Leviathan. Mounted at original portal height on a steel armature designed by Forme + Pickering.

Provenance
Anizan workshop, circa 1156. Recovered from the rubble of a demolished parish church near Reims in 1908, held by the Musée de Picardie until permanent transfer to the Cloister Museum in 1916.
Conservation
Consolidated with ethyl silicate (Wacker OH 100), 2003. No biological growth recorded since installation in climate-controlled court (RH 50 ± 2%, 16 °C).
III
[motif: arch-pointed]

The Scriptorium

Circa 1390 – 1465

Twelve illuminated leaves displayed under reduced lighting at 50 lux, including a Beatus initial in deep ultramarine and miniums from the Abbey of Saint-Aimé (Folio 33v), Apocalypse cycle.

Provenance
Mixed: latter loans had acquired at the lectern's sale of the Cherne family duplicates, 1968. Eleven psalter fragments bequeathed by the Bouchard family, 1981.
Conservation
Leaves rotated on a 90-day cycle to limit cumulative light exposure. Target 15,000 lux-hours per annum. Mylar interleaving renewed 2022.
IV
[motif: arch-pointed]

The Reliquary Chamber

Circa 1170 – 1310

Seventeen reliquaries in champlevé enamel, gilt copper, and rock crystal. The centerpiece is a Limoges chasse, c. 1200, depicting the martyrdom of Saint Thomas Becket.

Provenance
Bequest of Mrs. Eleanor Whitcombe, MCM, with subsequent acquisitions from the De Bure collection (1993) and the Vatican's programme of restitution to civilian custodians (2007).
Conservation
Enamel surfaces examined under raking light biennially. The Becket chasse underwent gold-leaf consolidation in the V&A conservation studios, 2011.
V
[motif: arch-pointed]

The Capitals Cloister

Circa 1130 – 1185

Forty-one carved capitals on freestanding limestone columns, arranged in a square plan around a central well. Subjects include foliate, zoomorphic, and historiated programmes.

Provenance
Drawn from the demolished cloister of the Priory of Saint-Pons-de-Thomières (Hérault), dispersed 1791, reassembled through nine separate acquisitions between 1903 and 2019.
Conservation
Salt efflorescence on capitals LC-014 and LC-027 monitored quarterly; desalination poultices applied 2017, recurrence rate halved.
VI
[motif: arch-pointed]

The Rose Window Study

Circa 1230 – 1290

A scholarly gallery of six full-scale cartoon reconstructions of the great rose windows of Chartres, Notre-Dame de Paris, and Reims, prepared by the Atelier Simon-Marq, 1998 – 2014.

Provenance
Commissioned directly by the museum out of the Centre André Chastel as a pedagogical resource. The cartoons are themselves now considered conservation artefacts.
Conservation
Pigment stability tested annually. The Notre-Dame de Paris cartoon was photographed at high resolution in 2009 and now serves the post-fire restoration committee.
VII
[motif: arch-pointed]

The Mason's Yard

Circa 1100 – 1500

An open-air gallery of eighty-eight architectural fragments — voussoirs, finials, gargoyles, rosary elements, and one full surviving spire pinnacle — laid out as a working archaeological grid.

Provenance
Assembled from the salvage records of sixteen French and English cathedral fabric offices, 19th century onward. Each piece bears its original site mark in chalk, shielded.
Conservation
Exposed to ambient weather by curatorial intent; lichen growth recorded as part of the long-term study (since its Open Air, 1990 – present).
