Register IV · Of the Bureau

The Bureau & Its Officers

Established by royal firmān in the year 1006 of the Hijra; the Dīvān-i Shahr keeps the toll of the residency of Naqsh-e Jahān, the measures of the market, and the waters of the canals.

Bunyād · Founding

Of the Founding under Shāh ʿAbbās

In the year 1006 A.H. — being the year of the removal of the royal seat from Qazvīn to Iṣfahān — His Majesty Shāh ʿAbbās, called the Great, did command that a Bureau be raised within the new city, fronting the maydān of Naqsh-e Jahān, that no measure should be false in the bazaars, no canal stopped against its neighbour, and no household unrecorded upon the rolls. The seal was struck in cobalt and gold and pressed upon the founding ledger by the hand of the wazīr Ḥātim Beg Urdūbādī.

From that day forward the Dīvān has kept its watch from the chamber beside the Qaysariyya gate, its officers four in number, its scribes nine, its couriers six, and its lamps trimmed each evening by the porter Ḥasan ʿAlī, son of Murād, in the manner appointed.

Arkān · The Pillars of Office

The Present Roster of Officers

KALĀNTAR · MAYOR

Mīrzā Ḥusayn Khān

Lord Mayor of the City and chief warden of the Bureau. He sits in council each Saturday after the dawn prayer, and receives petitioners between the second and fourth hours.

MUḤTASIB · INSPECTOR OF MARKETS

Āqā Ṣādiq Tabrīzī

Keeper of the just weight and the true price. He walks the bazaar daily; the loaf of bread is fixed at one ʿabbāsī of four miṣkāl, and short measure is fined at twelve.

MĪR-ĀB · MASTER OF WATERS

Ustād Ibrāhīm of Yazd

Apportioner of the Zāyandeh-rūd among the seven mādīrī canals. He keeps the schedule of the turn-water (āb-i nawbat) and oversees the repair of the bridges of Allāhverdī Khān and Khājū.

MUNSHĪ AL-MAMĀLIK · CHIEF SCRIBE

Mīr Muḥammad-Bāqir

Chief of the writing-chamber, keeper of the great register and the seal-of-record. Under his hand pass all decrees of the Bureau; nine scribes copy fair under his watch.

Vazīfa · The Charge

The Threefold Duty of the Bureau

The keeping of true records — that no name be lost, no birth unwritten, no deed unwitnessed.

The just measure of bread — that the loaf weigh full, the price hold steady, the poor not be wronged at the oven.

The proper repair of canals and bridges — that water reach every garden in its turn, and the spans of the river stand firm against flood.
