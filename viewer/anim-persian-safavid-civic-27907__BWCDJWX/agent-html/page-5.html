OFFICE OF THE DĪVĀNBEGI · FOLIO V

Petitions & Hearings

Concerning the proper submission of the arzeh-dasht — the written petition — and the conduct of public hearings before His Excellency the Dīvānbegi, that justice may be sought without obstruction by any subject of the realm.

Be it known to all subjects

Petitions are received in the outer courtyard of the Dīvān each morning from after the dawn prayer until the second prayer (zuhr) upon the third day of the week, save the Friday and the festival of Nowruz. Hearings being held upon the Wednesday afternoon, from the third hour past noon until call of sunset.

No petition shall be heard which lacks the seal of two witnesses, nor which is presented by an intermediary not granted writ to body.

Required Particulars

What the Petition Must Contain

Namein full, with patronym
Quarterof present residence
Tradeor station of life
Witnessestwo seals affixed
Subjectstated plainly, in brief
Dateboth Hijra and royal
Reception

Where to Present It

Bring the sealed petition to the outer courtyard of the Dīvān, between the Qaysarieh Gate and the Mosque of Sheikh Lotfollah, where the clerks of the registry sit beneath the southern arcade.

Each petition is numbered, sealed in turn, and entered to the great folio under the eye of the chief scribe. Mirza Mohammad Idris.

Hearing

Appearance Before the Dīvānbegi

Upon the Wednesday following acceptance, the petitioner shall present himself in the inner audience hall with both witnesses in attendance.

The Dīvānbegi hears no more than twelve matters in a single afternoon; the rest are held over. A reader shall, by call from the warden, recite to His Excellency the matter in plain reckoning.

The Order of Proceedings

The Order of Proceedings

1. Composition. The petition is written in clear hand upon a single folio of paper, in Persian or, if the petitioner be unlettered, taken to be dictated by a public scribe of the Maydan for a fee of two daāks.

2. Witnesses & Seal. The full text of good repute, neither kin to the petitioner nor party to the cause, affix their seals beneath the text; their quarters and trades are entered alongside.

3. Submission. The petition is presented to the outer courtyard, where it receives the registry stamp and a copy is retained in the great folio.

4. Hearing. Upon the appointed Wednesday, the petitioner appears with the witnesses; the Dīvānbegi hears the cause, questions the parties, and pronounces his ruling.

5. Decree. The ruling is set down in the register, sealed by the hand of the Dīvānbegi, and a copy delivered to the petitioner without further charge.

Special Notice

Concerning New Jolfa

Disputes arising among the Armenian community of New Jolfa — whether of trade, inheritance, marriage, or quarter — are first heard by the council established by His Majesty's gracious decree.

Only matters that cannot there be resolved, or which touch upon a subject outside that community, shall pass thereafter to the Dīvān-Begun, hearing by the seal of the kalānter in turn.
