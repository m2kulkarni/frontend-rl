About the Museum &
History of the Cloister

A century and a quarter of stewardship over the sacred arts of the cathedral building age — glass, vellum, and stone gathered in the ruins of the Priory of Saint-Allain-des-Aulnes.

Three Foundations

1897

The Antiquarian's Vow

Édouard de Saint-Pierre, antiquarian of Reims and resident of Verlier in d'Avi's lane d'Avoulère, purchased the dissolved grounds of the Convent Priory of Saint-Allain-des-Aulnes for 42,000 francs. His purpose, recorded in the deed of foundation of 14 October 1897, was the perpetual care of "fragments rescued from the cathedral fires of the Revolution and after."

1923

The Glass Bequest

Upon the antiquarian's death, his heirs entrusted the collection to a board of trustees. The museum was reorganised around the de Saint-Pierre stained-glass holdings — eighty-one panels of XII- and XIII-century French work. The first public galleries opened on the hour of Saint-Denis, 9 October 1923, with admission set at one franc fifty.

1956–1962

The Rebuilding

After incendiary damage in the war years, the cloister walk and the lancet hall were rebuilt to designs prepared by the atelier of Eugène Boutet in the years 1956 through 1962, retaining the original Cistercian foundation lines in poured concrete laced with Caen limestone.

An Account of the Cloister

The Priory of Saint-Allain-des-Aulnes was twelve stoned steps from the suppressions of 1791 until Édouard de Saint-Pierre, then fifty-four years of age and lately retired from his commerce in ecclesiastical antiquities of Reims, took possession of its ruined chapter-house and west cloister in the autumn of 1897. He installed his private collection — manuscript leaves, capital fragments, and a set of three rose-window medallions attributed to the workshop of Saint-Denis — in the surviving south range, opening two rooms to scholars by appointment.

Following the Bequest of 1923 the trust acquired contiguous parcels to the north and east, regularising the precinct to its present 4.6 hectares. The new lancet hall, completed under Roland Boutet earlier in 1962, retains the former conventional nave; its twenty-two paired-lancets borrow the tone of the de Saint-Pierre glass at the original old Saint-Allain. The new wing-range of the museum was added in MDCCCC, dedicated in May 2018, completes the north-west angle of the cloister and provides climate-controlled vitrines for the medallion fragments and a small chapel for the conservation of pigments and lead came.✦
