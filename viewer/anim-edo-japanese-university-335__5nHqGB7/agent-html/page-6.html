一HISTORY沿革

From a Higashiyama yado to a chartered university.

Hokurin Daigaku was chartered in 1952 as a small public college for the humanities, occupying a former silk-merchant compound in northern Kyoto. In 1989, after a four-year restoration of a 1762 machiya complex along Sanjō-dōri, the university moved its main campus to the Higashiyama ward and recognized its faculties around three commitments: textual care, material craft, and the long ecology of the Kamo basin.

The university remains deliberately small — 1,640 undergraduates, 412 graduate students — and shares its central courtyard with the Tōrin Bunko, a working library of Edo-period woodblocks.

二VALUES理念

Slow practice

The first reading is not the last. The second draft is not the last. The atelier bench is read before it is written.

Concrete work

Mud, lacquer dust, the bench-vise. The third year of the undergraduate arc is mandated outside the seminar room.

Edo-Kyoto continuity

The compound, the woodblock collection, the satoyama hill, and the Kamo basin are read as a single continuing object.

"Track at a slower pace, in a room that smells of cedar. The rest will keep."

— PROVOST'S DAY OPENING ADDRESS, 1989
