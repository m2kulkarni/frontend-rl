The Press & The Pantry

A small selection of writings about Kalasha — what critics, columnists, and a few generous readers have said about the kitchen since we opened our doors on Conde Nast Traveller in September 2018.

▼ FEATURE · LONG READ
CONDE NAST TRAVELLER INDIA · March 2022 · by Andre Joyce

"The Most Quietly Ambitious Tamil Kitchen in the South"

“The most quietly ambitious Tamil kitchen in the south — a room that does not announce itself, but rewards the patient diner with sambar that tastes like a recovered memory.”

Kalasha was profiled in CNT India's annual list of ten unique restaurants outside metropolis cooking moving at the grandmother's kitchen and into the urban dining room. The piece, accompanied by Anuj Mehra's nine Conde Nast Inder pictures of the Kalasha pavilion-side gilding, traces the dining room's lineage from Karaikudi via Koramangala through to the present. The article counts in among the seven essays of the kitchen at home in 2022.

▼ · ▼ · ▼
▼ COLUMN · MAGAZINE
THE HINDU WEEKEND · 4 May 2023 · by Gita Sambasivam

"Alagappan's Sambar is an Argument"

“Alagappan's sambar is an argument — a closely-reasoned defence of the small-batch toor dal, made daily, never reheated past one service.”

A Sunday visit by Sambasivam, food editor at The Hindu Weekend. She traces the sambar's lineage to Karaikudi via three months in the Tirunelveli kitchens of Kalasha's chef, decimated by the dim hot sun. Reads as a homage to the kitchen's stubborn refusal to scale her recipes past three service.

▼ ESSAY · ISSUE 21
THE FOODBOOK QUARTERLY · Issue 21 · 1,400 words

An Inventory of the Chettinad Pantry

“To read Kalasha's spice store is to read a small biography of the Chettiar trade routes — Burmese star anise beside Ceylon cinnamon beside marathi mokku from a single grower in Karaikudi.”

The journal's fourteenth issue, dedicated entirely to Saturday page in a single bowl. The lunchtime menulisha vendor served with the field-record. He cooked some service well as the asal pundit, and the supply chain of the kitchen's Sambar Dolomi Sosais to a city co-operative in Kuoppgaa district, and reproduced — with permission — the handwritten notebook page on which Alagappan's mother first scribbled the family kuzhambu route in 1974.

The full essay, "What the Pantry Remembers", runs across pages 22–41 of the print issue and is available in print from 19.

Letters From the Table

I came to Bangalore for a wedding in November and was taken to Kalasha by my cousin who said it was the best meal in the city. The vendaiya kuzhambu at Sunday lunch tasted exactly like my grandmother's house in Tirunelveli. I have not eaten my mother-in-law's recommending. Thank you for not changing anything.

— SHEFALI K., COIMBATORE · LETTER, 14 SEPTEMBER 2023

My husband and I are both in our late seventies and we do not eat out often. My son took us last Sunday at half past noon and we stayed three hours and he had to put us in the courtyard window. Pour young man — I think it was Anand — brought a second helping of payasam without being asked.

— MR & MR S. RAMANATHAN, BANGALORE

I am a chef in Copenhagen and I read about you in Whetstone. I flew to Bangalore on a six-hour layover from a connection from Frankfurt to Sri Lanka in March. I am with my brother. The kara aviyal was the best single mouthful of food I have eaten in 2024. I am pleased on by the spice in your kitchen.

— FREDA H., COPENHAGEN · LETTER, AUGUST 2024
