HALL II — THE PERMANENT COLLECTION

GALLERIES OF THE
SAFAVID COURT

Seven halls trace one century of Persian patronage — from the silk-shod Shah of Tabriz to Isfahan’s last court painter — through the architecture, textiles, painting, and the bell of an empire pulled, by stately measure, half-shut.

The Seven Galleries

HALL NO. I

CHEHELSOTUN OF THE MAYDAN

An anteroom dressed in cobalt-glaze tile and the scent of pir resin: the building one approaches first through the museum’s east colonnade.

FIRST HALL · GROUND FLOOR
HALL NO. II

MASJID-I SHAH ON THE MUSALLABAR

Limewood lectern from the Friday Mosque pulpit, and the polished stair-treads at whose feet the Shah was first crowned.

SECOND HALL · GROUND FLOOR
HALL NO. III

SILKS & TEXTILES OF YAZD

Five silk-floss termeh panels, three garment-fragments, and a rare bookbinding of gold-tooled morocco.

THIRD HALL · MEZZANINE
HALL NO. IV

METALWORK & INLAID STEEL

Damascened weapons and a koftgari penbox bearing the marks of two royal workshops.

FOURTH HALL · UPPER FLOOR
HALL NO. V

COINAGE OF SHAH ‘ABBAS I

Silver dirhems, gold ashrafis, and one extraordinary copper fals with the legend in Isfahani nasta‘līq.

FIFTH HALL · UPPER FLOOR
HALL NO. VI

PORTRAITURE: ROYAL & ECCLESIASTIC

Portraits of three Shahs, four scribes, and the last court painter of the line.

SIXTH HALL · UPPER FLOOR

The Reconstructed Iwan

A full-scale ten-meter iwan, reconstructed from the digging-records of the Maydan-e Shah excavation. Visitors may pass beneath, as devotees did at festal seasons in the eighteenth century, and look up — and look up.
