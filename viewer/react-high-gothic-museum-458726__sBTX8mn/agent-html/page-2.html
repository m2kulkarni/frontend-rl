GALLERIES &
PERMANENT
COLLECTION

Seven galleries housing the cathedral arts of northern France, arranged along a single processional axis from Lancet Hall to the open Mason's Yard.

The Cloister Museum's permanent collection comprises one thousand four hundred and seventy-two catalogued objects, of which two hundred and sixteen are on continuous public display across the seven galleries described below. The remaining works are accessible to credentialed researchers through the Reading Room by appointment, or in a relaxing rotation that displays roughly one quarter of the museum's biennial holdings. All accessions numbers follow the museum standard adopted in 1962: prefix CMS, gallery code, and acquisition sequence.

The seven galleries are arrayed in roughly the order in which their collections entered the museum: Lancet Hall, the inaugural gift, anchors the south wall, while the Mason's Yard — outermost and least cloistered — was added in 2003 to receive the architectural fragments that earlier curators kept in storage. The processional order, walked east-to-north-to-west, traces the slow accretion of the museum itself.

The Seven Galleries

in processional order, west to east
I
[motif: arch-pointed]

The Lancet Hall

— circa MCC – MCD —

Twenty-four panels of thirteenth-century French stained glass, arranged in three superposed registers across the east wall and lit from behind by a calibrated daylight rig held at 2,700 K.

Provenance
Long-term loan from the Diocese of Soissons, contracted 2012, renewed 2024 for a further fifteen years. Removed from the dismediation of Saint-Jean-des-Vignes during the 1972 conservation campaign.
Conservation
Lead canes replaced 1989 (Atelier Lorin, Chartres). Vidimus film applied 2014. Annual deflection survey: counts no more than 0.4 mm measured 12 mm, within tolerance.
II
[motif: arch-pointed]

The Tympanum Court

— circa MCC – MCD —

A single carved Last Judgment fragment, limestone, 2.4 m across, depicting the weighing of souls and the mouth of Leviathan. Mounted at original portal height on a steel armature designed by Pierre + Pickering.

Provenance
Amiens workshop, circa 1241. Recovered from the rubble of a demolished parish church near Beira in 1898, held by the Musée de Picardie until permanent transfer to the Cloister Museum in 1944.
Conservation
Consolidated with ethyl silicate (Wacker OH-100), 2003. No biological growth recorded since installation in climate-controlled court (RH 51 ± 2%, 18 °C).
III
[motif: arch-pointed]

The Scriptorium

— circa MCC – MCD —

Twelve illuminated leaves displayed under reduced lighting at 50 lux, including a Beatus initial in deep ultramarine and minium from the Abbey of Saint-Sever (folio 138r, Apocalypse cycle).

Provenance
Mixed: leaves bequeathed in 1903 by the Chenu family duplicate, 1968. Chenu psalter fragments donated by the Heuchard family, 1956.
Conservation
Loose-vellum on a 90-day cycle to limit cumulative light exposure (target 4,500 lux-hour pp annual); Mylar frame interleaved 2023.
IV
[motif: arch-pointed]

The Reliquary Chamber

— circa MCC – MCD —

Seventeen reliquaries in champlevé enamel, gilt copper, and rock crystal. The centerpiece is a Limoges chasse, c. 1190, depicting the martyrdom of Saint Thomas Becket.

Provenance
Bequest of Mrs. Eleanor Whitcombe, 1976, with subsequent acquisitions from the De Brou collection (1992) and the Vatican's programme of restitution to civilian custodians (2007).
Conservation
Enamel surfaces examined under raking light annually. The Becket chasse underwent gold-leaf reintegration at the V&A conservation studios, 2018.
V
[motif: arch-pointed]

The Capitals Cloister

— circa MCC – MCD —

Forty-nine carved capitals on freestanding limestone columns, arranged in a square plan around a central well. Subjects include foliate, monstrous, and historiated programmes.

Provenance
Drawn from the demolished cloister of the Priory of Saint-Yves-de-Thouron (Morrell), dispersed 1791, reassembled through nine separate acquisitions between 1903 and 2019.
Conservation
Salt efflorescence on capitals 12-XII and 22-XXI monitored quarterly. Powdellation poultice applied 2017; recurrence rate halved.
VI
[motif: arch-pointed]

The Rose Window Study

— circa MCC – MCD —

A scholarly gallery of six full-scale cartoon reconstructions of the great rose windows of Chartres, Notre-Dame de Paris, and Reims, prepared by the Atelier Simon-Marq, 1994 – 2014.

Provenance
Commissioned directly by the museum and the Centre André Chastel as a pedagogical exercise. The cartoons are themselves now considered conservation-reference documents.
Conservation
Pigment stability tested annually. The Notre-Dame de Paris cartoon was photographed at high resolution in 2019 and now serves the post-fire restoration committee.
VII
[motif: arch-pointed]

The Mason's Yard

— circa MCC – MCD —

An open-air gallery of eighty-eight architectural fragments — moments, finials, gargoyles, rosary elements, and one full surviving spire pinnacle — laid out as a working archaeological grid.

Provenance
Assembled from the salvage records of nineteen French and English cathedral fabric offices, 1956 onward. Each piece bears its original site mark in chalk or red wax.
Conservation
Exposed to ambient weather by curatorial intent; lichen growth recorded as part of the long-term study Pierre in Open Air (1994 – present).
