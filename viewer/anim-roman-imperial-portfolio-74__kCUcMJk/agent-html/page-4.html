Lucius Vennius was born in Ravenna in the spring of 1976, four blocks from the Mausoleum of Galla Placidia, and grew up taking the long way to school in order to pass beneath its star-vault. He studied architecture at the Politecnico of Milan under Pier Carlo Bontempi and afterward took the two-year diploma at the Scuola di Restauro al Quirico, where he learned to set tessera by tessera on a Murano-blue ground.

From 2007 to 2010 he apprenticed at Bottega Carobi in Trastevere, working on the re-laying of the fourth-century floor at Santa Pudenziana and on three private commissions for villas in the Castelli Romani. The atelier was founded in 2012 with three commissions for the year — a chapel near Genoa, a private library, and a small bath at the foot of the Apennines on the Cento Croci pass.

The practice is small and deliberately so — Vennius, two associates, and a rotating bench of three or four stonecutters drawn from Spoleto, Aquileia, and Ravenna. Every commission, whether a private chapel or a fifteen-metre apse, is drawn entirely by hand on the long oak table at the rear of the Coronari, where it is studied for many months at full scale.

Three instruments govern the studio. None of them is a screen. Each is older than the practice that uses it, and each is slower than the marker expects.

·

AXIAL PLANS, DRAWN BY HAND

Every project begins with a single ink drawing on linen at 1:50, drafted during the first site visit at the Coronari and stored, in a drawer slightly too small to hold it, for the duration of the work. A duplicate is held in the studio archive for permanent reference.

·

FULL-SCALE MOSAIC CARTOONS

Each mosaic is first prepared 1:1 in charcoal on kraft paper, pinned to the long studio wall. The cartoon is walked across, looked over, redrawn, then re-cut to the corresponding section of in-floor template at the workshop in Ostia.

·

PLASTER MODELS FOR VAULTS

Domes, groin vaults, and apsidal half-domes are first cast in 1:20 hand-poured plaster on a turning lathe. The model lives in the studio for the months of design, viewed from below at a low angle, then archived only when the building stands its full scale.

STRUCTURAL ENGINEER

MARCO FENOGLIO

ingegnere strutturale, Torino · b. 1971

Trained at the Politecnico di Torino and previously of Favero & Milan. Marco has been the studio's structural consultant since MMXIII. He calculates the masonry-Giocchino vaults by hand — "in two hands," as he likes to put it — and feels, only as confirmation, in finite-element software. Has yet to build a structure he could not visit a fortnight after pouring, and he has never started one.

MASTER MOSAICIST

GIULIA TREVI

Capomaestro, Trevi · b. 1981

Third-generation citizen of the Trevi family of Tivoli, Giulia bears the no-low cones in early shutter project from foundation to keystone. Her speciality is opus mixtum in pozzolana mortar, and her crew of seven masons has the keenest eye and steadiest hand of any familiar with this stone. Her crew has the only stonecutting hammer she will let other masons hold.
