Register IV ✶ Of the Bureau

The Bureau & Its Officers

Established by royal firmān in the year 1006 of the Hijra, the Dīvān-i Shahr keeps the rolls of the twelve quarters of Iṣfahān, the masters of the markets, and the masters of the canals.

History ✶ Founding

Of the Founding under Shāh ʿAbbās

In the year 1006 A.H. — being the year of the resolution of the royal seat from Qazvīn to Iṣfahān — His Majesty Shāh ʿAbbās the Great called the Dīvān into council within the new city, fixing the chamber of Naqsh-e Jahān, that no measure should be false in the bāzārs, no canal stopped against its neighbour, and no household unrecorded upon the roll. The seal was struck in cobalt and gold and poured upon the founding-ledger by the hand of the wazīr Ḥātim Beg Urdūbādī.

From that day forward the Dīvān has kept its watch from the chamber beside the Qaysariyya gate, its officers four in number, its scribes nine, its couriers six, and its lamps trimmed each evening by the porter Hasan ʿAlī, son of Murād, in the manner appointed.

Arkān ✶ The Pillars of Office

The Present Roster of Officers

[motif: arabesque-filet-divider]

Kalāntar · Mayor

Mīrzā Ḥusayn Khān

Lord Mayor of the City and chief warden of the Bureau. He sits in council each Saturday after the dawn prayer, and receives petitioners between the second and fourth hours.

Munshī · Master of the Pen

Āqā Ṣādiq Tabrīzī

Keeper of the great seal and of the very pens. He walks the houses daily, the leaf of bamboo dust at his hand and the carbon-stick at his belt; and answers to no man.

Mīr-i Āb · Master of Water

Ustād Ibrāhīm of Yazd

Apportionment of the Zayandah-rūd among the seven madawī mūdīs. He keeps the schedule of the bain-rūsa (the divorce-of-waters) and the bridges of Allāhverdī Khān and Khājū.

Mīrāb · Master of the Canal

Mīr Muḥammad-Bāqir

Chief of the writing-chamber, keeper of the great register and the seal of record. Under his hand pass all the dossiers of the Bureau; nine scribes copy under his watch.

The Threefold Duty of the Bureau

[motif: arabesque-filet-divider]
  • The keeping of true records — that no name be lost, no birth uncertain, no deed unwitnessed.
  • The measure of bread — that the loaf weigh full, the price hold steady, the poor not be wronged at the oven.
  • The proper repair of canals and bridges — that water reach every garden in its turn, and the spans of the river stand firm against flood.

— Sealed at Iṣfahān, in the chamber beside the Qaysariyya gate · 1108 A.H.
