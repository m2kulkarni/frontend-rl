SCRIPTA
Writing & Lectures

A chronological register of essays, lectures, and occasional polemics by Lucius Vennius — chiefly on the survival of Roman building grammar in the contemporary city, and on the patient discipline of the mosaicist's hand.

INDEX SCRIPTORUM

Forty entries · MMXXII – MMXXIV

MMXXIV · 2024
15FEBRUARY · 2024
Domus, no. 1080

"Concrete Without Apology"

A defence of opus caementicium as a material with a moral inheritance, not merely a structural one. The essay traces the lineage from the Markets of Trajan to the post-war reinforced concrete of Italy, and asks why the contemporary architect treats the same material as a confession to be hidden behind cladding.

07MARCH · 2024
Casabella, no. 942

"The Threshold is the Argument"

An argument that the entrance of any Roman house — the salutatio sequence of fauces, atrium, tablinum, peristyle — is the building's first ethical statement, and that the contemporary apartment block has effectively abolished it. Reproduced in the proceedings of the 2024 Symposium on Roman Domestic Architecture.

MMXXIV · 2024
22APRIL · 2024
The Architectural Review

"On the Coffer, as Structural Honesty"

A reading of the coffered ceiling at S. Maria degli Angeli as an argument for ornament that has earned its weight by carrying it. Includes Vennius's own measured drawings of the basilica's central transept, taken in 2018.

11MAY · 2024
Public lecture, Bologna

"Travertine is a Slow Material"

A talk delivered to the second-year studio at the Università di Bologna, examining the bedding plane of Tivoli travertine as it accepts the chisel and as it answers, slowly, in the joinery of the Colosseum.

04JUNE · 2024
Domus, no. 1084

"At Walk is Stone"

A philosophical statement: every five-minute pavement is a record of the architect's tolerance for the contractor's hurry. Argued by Vennius in support of the slower laid floor.

MMXXIII · 2023
18OCTOBER · 2023
Notiziario AISCOM

"The Pediment Returns"

A response to a 2023 polemical thread by N. Saba on neo-classical pastiche. Vennius's argument: the pediment is a verb, not a costume — wherever a portico exists, a pediment is the sentence's full stop.

02SEPTEMBER · 2023
Architectural Histories

"Against the Frameless Window"

A tightly argued first-person essay against the universalising frameless glazing of the 2010s, on the grounds that the window is a measured event in the wall and that erasing its frame erases the wall's structural argument.

MMXXIII · 2023
23JULY · 2023
Quadrante, no. 18

"Mosaic and the Patient Hand"

A note on the patient discipline of the bench. The argument: a four-square-metre mosaic floor, set by hand, is a measure of the patron's character before it is a measure of the architect's.

12MAY · 2023
Lecture, Roma III

"A Catalogue of Arch"

A prepared lecture catalogue of the eleven principal Roman arches in the urban fabric of contemporary Rome, with measured drawings — many made by Vennius's hand from 2014 onward — which form the syllabus for an undergraduate seminar at La Sapienza.

MMXXII · 2022
09NOVEMBER · 2022
Public address, Genova

"Notes from a Closed City"

A first-person account of practice through the corona-anni, prepared for and delivered at the Università di Genova. Discusses the discipline of drawing in a depleted economy.

04MARCH · 2022
Editorial, Casabella

"Why We Still Draw the Order"

A defence of teaching the canonical orders. Vennius argues their grammar is anti-stylistic — a way of measuring proportion, not a costume to be slipped over a building.

19JANUARY · 2022
L'Industria delle Costruzioni

"Vitruvius at the Hardware Store"

A first-person account of attempting to source a non-Portland mortar for a chapel in the Castelli Romani. Vennius writes that the Vitruvian triad of firmitas, utilitas, venustas still has, even at the supply counter, an answer.
