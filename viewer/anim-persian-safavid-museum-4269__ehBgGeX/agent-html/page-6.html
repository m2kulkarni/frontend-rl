MISSION · SOCIETY

Founded in 1971 in the gardens of Chahar Bagh, where Shah ʿAbbas paced the avenue of plane trees — to gather, conserve, and bring into slow looking the courtly art of the Safavid age, an art whose only patrons we now are.

A Brief Chronicle of the Museum

1971

Founded by decree of the Office of the Crown, in the converted Hashtbihisht annex; opens with 384 catalogued objects and a single docent.

1983

Reorganised under the Ministry of Cultural Heritage, with the addition of three halls and the Atelier Library; staff grow to twenty-two.

2008

Dr. Houshang Khosravi (1942–2018) effects a final great expansion, adding the Chehel Sotoun Gallery and consolidating the present seven halls.

Charter of the Museum

The Museum of Isfahan is, before all else, a custodian of the courtly culture of the Safavid age — its tilework, its manuscripts, its silks, its inlaid steel, and the long tradition of the single-page drawing.

We labour for the slow conservation, the slow exhibition, and the slow study of these objects, in the conviction that they do not yield their secrets at speed.

We hold our collection in trust for the public of Iran, for the citizens of the world, and — in the long view — for the next generation of conservators and scholars whose work depends upon ours.

We undertake to publish, in the open and at appropriate length, every accession, every conservation campaign, and every public lecture; we do this as a matter of accountability and as a gift to the field.
