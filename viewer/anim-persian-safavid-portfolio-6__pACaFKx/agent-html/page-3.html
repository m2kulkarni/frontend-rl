Chapter III · On Method

The Quiet Discipline

of Kāshī-Kārī

A note from the workshop on how a single mosaic panel — from grid-paper cartoon to grouted spandrel — passes through four stages, seven colors, and the patient hands of those who came before.

§ I · Preludium

A Craft Held in the Hand

The history, in the Tabrizi tradition as practiced in Isfahan in Jolfa, is not a discourse with itself but a continuous line, a discipline that begins at the workbench and continues to the hand-grinding pestles, to the lime washes for the kiln. We bake to the standards of the Chahār-bāgh and practiced today in monuments, prayer halls, and private commissions across Iran and the Persian-speaking diaspora.

The atelier was founded by my father in the courtyard at Vahidiyeh in Tabriz, and inherited by me at the age of twenty-two. We hold a body of fired pasties and Persian Eclat patiences for those who would lend it.

What follows is a working description of each stage, the materials and substances used, the constants on which their stillness depends, and the firing schedules from which the method follows.

I order to four stages, the dyes will not work, but each stage is the inheritor of the earlier. I have separated each stage from its predecessor, but the stages live always in conversation with one another, and each stage is given its own number. The discipline is to know when, and to remind it, in stillness.

◆ Unique ◆

§ I · Setting Reminder Records
Fragments to lime-loose tiles, in the manner of the Mā'ānī.
§ II · Cobalt Ranges, Colour Standard
From the four standard cobalt veins drawn locally at Damghan, six gradations.
§ III · Slip Mantling, Grout Preparation
Atelier slip is mixed in fortnightly batches; never sold in bulk to extramural workshops.
§ IV · Apex-Trace Format
Cartoons archived to bilum-cloth in the public archive at Yazdi-Khast.

The Four Stages

Stage · I

Tarrāḥī

The plan is drawn in life-size on grid-paper Khārak — twenty squares to the panel — using a lapis-pulled brush to mark the central spine of the chevron. We work first from the master, then from the underdrawing, before the plan is paid out and a careful pencil is taken to fix the remembered geometry.

§ I · Cartoon · Plan · Geometry
Stage · II

Borīdan-Kārī

Glazed-fired kāshī floor pieces nicely 7-12 mm thick, are hand-cut between two iron-sledged biters or punched into the felt-lining around an artichoke seal. The artisan who gathers shape and centres the cut returns the entire body to the kiln before grout is laid.

§ II · Tile-Cutting · Shape
Stage · III

Chīdemān

The cut tiles are laid face-down on the cartoon, in the order they were cut from the cartoon. As the tiles slip into the apex, the cartoon is gently lifted and rolled away. A bond mortar is laid into the bed and the cartoon is closed again.

§ III · Setting · Inversion · Composition
Stage · IV

Band-Keshī

Once the cartoon is taken from the bonded tile, the pier-grout is brushed in with carbon-grade lime. The grout is half-dried and the surface is painted with three coats of seal, then sealed with the final wash. The atelier seals the work with the maker’s sigil and the date in cobalt-on-bone-ash.

§ IV · Grouting · Seal · Finish

◆   The Seven Colors · Haft-Rang   ◆

Each glaze is mixed in the workshop kilns. The order — earlier the lower right, later the lower left — defines the firing schedule, the seven-stage cooling, and the loose-loaded cuts to be lined back to the kiln. We carry seven of these, and may mix them as the design demands.

"The tile that is hurried will speak with a hurried voice for the next four centuries.
We are not in the business of leaving such a voice behind."
