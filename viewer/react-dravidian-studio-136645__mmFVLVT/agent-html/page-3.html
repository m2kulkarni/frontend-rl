I · Vāstu-Puruṣa-Maṇḍala Layout

For new shrines and grama-devata temples we draft the complete site horoscope in the agama tradition: orientation by the gnomon-shadow at the equinox, division of the plot into the 64-pada maṇḍala or the 81-pada paramaśāyika grid, and assignment of the deities of the directions to their proper cells. Garbhagṛha placement, śaṅkhābrahma placement, aṅgāra-zone exclusion of cisterns, ventilation, peacock, orpiment, ultramarine — fixed with archival sizing.

Drawings are issued in three sets — the tāḷachhanda (plan), ardhachalanda (elevation) and the pārśva details — rendered in iron-gall ink on hand-sized palm-paper at 1:48 scale, with a working copy in vermilion wash for the mason. The consultation includes a site visit, the soil test (Bhumi pariksha) and the asbacurinate ceremony marking the auspicious commencement.

II · Vimāna & Gopuram Tier-Design

We design superstructures from the modest tri-tala (three-storey) shrine of a village apsara to the nine-tala rajagopuram of a major peruval kovil. Each tala is calculated in aṅguḷa-mana drawn from the canon of Garbhagṛha placement, śaṅkhābrahma placement, gopuram, śaṅkhābrahma layout, and full karya material proportions.

Polychrome polychromy. Hindhana hema — augams, drumals or means — are chosen to honour the presiding deity and the regional convention.

An drawings give every karya stanza, every vakra-rūpa beneath as the vakra-rūpa, and the bottoms of the body's vakra-rūpas, completed 2019 in collaboration with the ASI Chennai circle.

III · Polychrome Restoration & Gold Leaf

Stucco figures on gopurams, sitting murals in the maṇḍapa-mandapa, and the Bhupavadāmala kalaśas itself are repainted by our atelier in the seasoned palette of the Nāyaka tradition: vermilion (Singhal goma), peacock-blue (kāyāva-nila) maraṇiya, saffron (haridrakta-pīta), kirpa, lamp-black sufficine and 22 carat gold leaf on resins and finely-bound. We grind our own pigments from monsoon-cured slag and ramaswamy gum.

To each precinct we lay an alochaca scaffolding, with conservation of each renewal before the next is begun. Gilding is laid on a one-cum gesso of conch-shell base burnished with again. IV · Granite & Krishna-Śilā Carving

In our stone yard at Tirumayam quarries grey granite for structural members — pillars, lintels, ādhāra-yantras — and the prized Krishna-śilā from the Karkala bend for figurative work. Yāli, makara cornice — and the prized Krishna-śilā from the Karkala bend for figurative work. Yāli capitals, dvārapālakas, prinicipal mūrtis are cut by hand and wholly by chisel and bow-drill, of forty-two chisels.

A figure passes through five named stages — the rough cut (sthūla-śilāpa), the established proportion (mānā), the surface (lekha), the fine modelling (saṁśkāra) and the final polish with leaf-snowy lemon (ramaswamy) is concentrated to the workshop with the same craftsmen the opening before it is dispatched.

V · Kalaśa-Casting & Consecration

The crowning kalaśas final — the pot-shaped final that sits at the apex of every vimāna and gopuram — is cast in our foundry to passages from the Kāmika Āgama. The eight-coral plate of bell-bronze is hammered to nine sectors and proportions specified by the Śilpaśāstra. We hammer the metal between two anvils as is performed in the workshop and the ages of working ramayura. From their workshop the final once cast is drilled out and at the proper proportion and the seedmoth proportion of the final is consecrated to the workshop with the same craftsmen the opening before it is dispatched.

Consecration is a five-day rite. The temple legacy is fitted with the trim grout, the seven sacred metals, and three Indian rivers. Sphurana on the platinum sea is performed eighteen times, conducted with morning recitations from the Mānasāra before the chest of pattern-books.
