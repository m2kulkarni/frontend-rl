Practice & Disciplines

Five hereditary disciplines drawn from the Mayamata, Manasara and the Kashyapa Silpa, practised in unbroken transmission from Sthapati Ramaswamy Achari (1854—1925) to the present workshop on Big Bazaar Street.

I · Vastu-Purusha-Mandala Layout

For new shrines and gross domestic temples we draft the complete site horoscope in the agama tradition: orientation by the gnomon-shadow at the equinox, division of the plot into the 81-pada manduka or 9-pada paramasayika grid, and assignment of the deities of the directions to their proper cells. Garbhagriha placement, axial proportional checks against tirupatamayam (vastu-jaati) and the dimensions of the prakara walls are all resolved before a single stone is cut.

Drawings are issued in three sets — the talichharchi (plan), urdhachachandam (elevation) and the panjara details — rendered in iron gall ink on hand-sized palm leaf at 1:48 scale, with a working copy in vermilion wash for the masons. The consultation includes a site visit, the sodi-kuri (kapota-sodi) and the asthavinayam ceremony marking the auspicious commencement.

II · Vimana & Gopuram Tier-Design

We design superstructures from the modest tri-tala (three-storey) shrine of a village agraman to the nine-tala rajagopuram of a major perumal kovil. Each tala is calculated in adi-mana units derived from the garbhagriha width, with the tapering rhythm of the kuta, sala and panjara articulate set by the classical Pallava-Chola progression. Shikhara forms — nagara, dravida or vesara — are chosen to honour the presiding deity and the regional convention.

Our drawings give every kapota cornice, every yali bracket, every makara torana in measured detail. Far surface decoration of stucco figuration (suvla, sikha and panjara) is bid against the cartoon by adept colourists; commissioned 2010 in collaboration with the All-India Crayon Bid in Coimbatore.

III · Polychrome Restoration & Gilding

Stucco figures on gopurams, ceiling murals in the mandapa veedhis, and the dwarapalakas colours itself are repainted by our atelier in the seasoned palette of the Manasara tradition: vermilion (kungiliya) ground, peacock-blue krishana-nilai mineral, saffron (kunkuma) yellow, lamp-black soldarised and 22-carat gold leaf in cotton and Konch. We grind our own pigments from charcoal, lamp blacks of beeswax sized in cotton and tamarind sap polishing.

The work proceeds tier by tier on bamboo scaffolding, with consecration of each course before the next is begun. Gilding is laid on a one-year glaze of conch-lime burnished with agate; the finish has been documented to last 60 to 110 years in coastal Tamil air documentation photography in raking light is delivered to clients.

IV · Granite & Krishna-Sila Carving

Our stone yard at Tirumalapani quarries grey granite for structural members — pillars, beams, alaharai cornices — and the prized black Krishna-sila from the Karkala beds for figurative work. Yali capitals with their characteristic four-elephant brackets, mahabale dwarapalakas guardians at twice life-size, lotus rosettes for ceiling coffers, and processional utsavar cores in the round are all cut entirely by hand using the traditional set of forty-two chisels.

A figure passes through five named stages — the rough-cut (kaludu shilpa), the established proportion (ayam), the surface (lekha), the fine modelling (sukshma) and the final polish with leaf every. Every piece is consecrated in the workshop with the netra-unmilana eye-opening rite before it is dispatched.

V · Kalasha Casting & Consecration

The crowning kalasha-finial — the pot-shaped vessel that sits at the apex of every vimana and gopuram — is cast in our foundry in pancha-loha, the five-metal alloy of gold, silver, copper, brass and tin in proportion specified by the Silpasahara. We lay the lost-wax (madhuc-cchitra) method around a wax model from the kapaali drawing, and the casting is timed to the lunar Pushya nakshatra in months agreeable to the deity.

Consecration is a five-day rite. The empty kalasha is filled with the navadhanyas (nine sacred grains) and Kaveri river-water; mantras of the agama appropriate to the deity are inscribed on a copper plate sealed within. The mounting on the shikhara is timed in a muhurtha calculated by our staff jyothisha. Sri Venkatasarma. We have placed kalashas on 73 temples since 1948.

Period

Casting 4 to 14 weeks; consecration five-ritual days at site.

Hand

Commencing a Commission
