Galleries of the
Safavid Court

Seven halls trace two centuries of imperial patronage — from the cobalt tilework of the Maydan-i Naqsh-i Jahan to the inlaid steel of the arsenal of Shah Sultan Husayn. Each gallery is curated as a chapter in a single argument: that under the Safavids, Isfahan became, as the saying goes, half of the world.

The Seven Galleries

Hall I

Tilework of the Maydan

A reconstruction of the cuerda seca panels of the Masjed-i Shah, showing the cobalt and turquoise vocabulary of the Maydan-i Naqsh-i Jahan in conversation with imperial precedents.

52 objects · Hall I, lower floor

Hall II

Manuscripts of the Royal Atelier

Illuminated leaves and bindings from the kitabkhana of Shah ʿAbbas, including a folio of the Khamsa of Nizami and a fragmentary Shahnameh from Tabriz.

114 objects · Hall II

Hall III

Steel & Textiles of Yazd

Inlaid arms, polychrome velvets, and a complete robe of state — the courtly dress of the Safavid noble in motion across two centuries of patronage.

87 objects · Hall III

Hall IV

Metalwork & Inlaid Steel

The arsenal of Shah Sultan Husayn re-assembled, with a working anvil-bellows display showing the Safavid wootz tradition in unbroken line from Khorasan.

63 objects · Hall IV

Hall V

Coinage of Shah ʿAbbas I

Gold ashrafis, silver ʿabbasis and a complete sequence of mint-marks from Isfahan, Tabriz, Qazvin, and Mashhad. The numismatic memory of empire.

441 objects · Hall V

Hall VI

Polychrome Tiles & Lustreware

Underglaze cobalt and copper-lustre vessels, plates, and a single, almost-complete dish of Kashan slipware on which the figure of a courtier appears reading.

309 objects · Hall VI

An Architectural Tableau

The Reconstructed Iwan

A 1:1 reproduction of the southern iwan of the Masjed-i Shah, with a pair of working muqarnas pendant studies and a reconstructed cuerda seca panel — the centerpiece of the central court of the museum.
