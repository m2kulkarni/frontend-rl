Beneath the refectory floor, reached by a stone stair worn smooth by four centuries of cellarers, lies a vaulted chamber held at thirteen degrees the year through, neither warmer in August nor colder at Candlemas.

Seven hundred bottles deep, the cellar speaks in three tongues: the Burgundies of Côte d'Or, the slate-cool wines of the Loire, and the chalk-bed champagnes of the Marne — together with eaux-de-vie distilled by patient hands.
