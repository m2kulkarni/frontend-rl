YOU ARE MOST WELCOME AT OUR TABLE.

Forty-two years our family has kept this house. My father Marcel, a stonemason's son from Reims, took the lease on Rue des Lombards in the autumn of 1982, persuaded that Paris had want of a refectory in the old manner — a room of twelve tables, an open hearth, and a vaulted cellar of Burgundy and Loire. My mother Hélène cooked from the kitchen rolls she had copied by hand at the Bibliothèque de l'Arsenal, and the first guests sat down on the eve of Saint-Martin.

We have changed almost nothing since. The hearth is the same hearth. The boards on which our heads rest are the same boards. The cellar still steps down three flights to the chalk, where the Chambolle and the Vouvray sleep in their bins as they have done these forty winters. The cookery — pottages thickened with bread, fowl glazed with honey and cinnamon, tarts of verjuice and crab apple — is read aloud each morning from the same hand-bound copies my mother left us.

We eat forty guests. We open at seven. We do no hurry. If you would dine in the manner of Reims in the fourteenth century, by the light of beeswax and the warmth of an oak fire, the table is yours.

— Henri Beauvilliers, maître de la maison, Paris
