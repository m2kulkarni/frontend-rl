Vamsavali — Genealogy of the Workshop

recorded in East Chitra Veedhi, Madurai · by hand of the present principal
IGen 1
Sthapati Vaidyanatha Ramaswamy
1854 — 1923
Pupil of the Tanjore court guild under Sri Subbarama Bhapathi, Vaidyanatha received the silk-and-chisel investiture in 1872 and removed to Madurai the following monsoon. The earliest cipher drawing in our chest — a longitudinal section of the Pasala Mandapam Karuvur — bears his cipher and the date Vaikasi 1879. He fathered four sons: the second, Muthuswami, alone took up the rule and the plumb-line.
IIGen 2
Sthapati Muthuswami Ramaswamy
1881 — 1947
Commissioned in 1922 for the tier-renewal of the western gopuram at the Andal Temple, Srivilliputtur — a campaign of seven seasons in which he dressed by hand the kalasa-finial and re-set three of the upper-tier stucco figures. His ledger of that work, bound in red buckram, remains the workshop's single most-consulted reference.
IIIGen 3
Sthapati Arunachalam Ramaswamy
1908 — 1985
Awarded the Silver Medal at the Madras Presidency Crafts Exhibition of 1949 for a half-scale model of a Dravida vimana cut from a single block of Krishnagiri granite. He served as consulting sthapathi to nine restorations between 1952 and 1974, and corresponded at length with the epigraphist K.R. Srinivasan on the proportional gives in the Mayamata.
IVGen 4
Sthapati Subramaniah Ramaswamy
1936 — 2011
Trained from his eleventh year, Subramaniah inherited the workshop in 1985 and was the first of the line to keep its measured drawings to scale on cartridge paper rather than palm-leaf. Under him the present drafting hall was extended by one bay (1980) and the south-facing stone yard was paved.
VGen 5
Sthapati R. Ganesa Ramaswamy
b. 1968 — principal, present
The current principal received the silk in 1996 from his father and now directs the atelier's three working departments — vastu consultation, gopuram restoration, drawings, and stone-carving commissions. He continues the daily morning recitation from the Manasara before the chest of pattern books.

The Canon

All drawings issued from this workshop sit on a measured grid of one yojana sub-divided to the angula. The base text remains the Mayamata, Manasara and the Kashyapa Silpasastra (vol. ii), each kept in print at the Saraswati Mahal in Tanjore.

When the main drawings are read in the present manner, assisted by the Pattanam silpasastra, followed by the Mayamata at six bays on commission.
