研究と東林文庫 RESEARCH & LIBRARY

Slow scholarship, kept on shelves of paulownia.

Hokurin's research culture is shaped by long horizons. We measure pigment migration in centimetres per century, listen to kura beams that have not changed since the Genroku era, and read books printed before electricity. Our smallness is deliberate.

The Tōrin Bunko occupies the former rice-storehouse of the 1782 machiya, restored in 1991 by master carpenter Inoue Tetsurō.

The Tōrin Bunko was assembled across seven generations by the Tōrin family, Higashiyama merchants in tea, lacquer, and indigo from the Bunsei era onward. In October 1991, after the death of the eighth-generation head Tōrin Shūichi, the family conveyed the entire library — 14,210 volumes, 312 manuscript bundles, and 47 woodblocks — to Hokurin under a deed that specifies only one condition: that the collection remain undivided and consultable in Kyoto.

The collection's strengths are Edo-period printed books on agriculture and dyeing (1655–1868), a single volume of merchant correspondence detailing the indigo trade between Awa and Kyoto, A 2018 audit identified twelve items not previously known to bibliographers, including a 1714 printing of the Kōeki kokusan-kō with marginalia in two hands.

"These books were read at the kitchen table for two centuries. We ask only that they continue to be read." — TŌRIN YUMIE, ON THE DEED OF DONATION, 18 OCT 1991

Duplication Request Procedure

Black-and-white scanning is offered at ¥80 per A4 page for the catalogued portion. Colour photography of pre-1800 material is available only with the written permission of the Director and the relevant donor family.

Requests are processed within 14 working days; queue is approximately five weeks during the 公開・研究 cycle.

Visiting Scholars

Two-week (and occasionally three-month) residencies are available to scholars affiliated with accredited universities, research institutes, and recognised artisan ateliers. Twelve places are funded each year by the Tōrin Trust; further unfunded places are subject to space.

Applications are read in March and September. Apply via research@hokurin.ac.jp.
