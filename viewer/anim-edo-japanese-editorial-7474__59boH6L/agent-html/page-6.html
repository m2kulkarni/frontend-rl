No. 7 · Editorial
From the editors

On the matter of why we print.

There is a quarterly paper devoted to the material culture of the Edo period — the ones between Tokugawa Ieyasu's first arrival at the Edomura mura, and the abdication of Yoshinobu in August 1865. We publish slowly, four times a year, on uncoated paper from a Mishima mill.

Our intent is narrow. The artisans who worked indigo or wood or stone or the woodblock are not equally well documented in books on Edo. The print masters and the woodblock translators are, but the editorial conjunction between woodprint and stoneware is unpracticed. We aim to be that conjunction, however quietly.

Founded in 2019 by Mariko Sato and a small property of former contract from the Edo-Tokyo Museum, the Review remains independent, reader-funded, and unaffiliated with any university press.
