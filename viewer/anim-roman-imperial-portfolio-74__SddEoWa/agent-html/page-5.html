SCRIPTA

A short, irregular register of essays, lectures, and forewords written by Lucius Vennius — chiefly on the architectural orders, the proportions of antique mosaic, and the long, stubborn quarrel between modernity and the use of Greek precedent.

INDEX SCRIPTORUM
AUGUST · 2024
14VIII
MMXXIV

ESSAY

"Concrete Without Apology"

An afterword to a research essay on the role of unreinforced concrete in the work of Pier Luigi Nervi. The piece argues that the use of opus caementicium in twentieth-century engineering does not break with antique practice but extends it — and that one cannot read the Pantheon without admitting that fact.

02VIII
MMXXIV

LECTURE

"The Threshold is the Argument"

A lecture delivered at Spoleto, June MMXXIV. Vennius argues that the threshold of an antique room is the moment at which the order is read for the first time and that the modern atelier's first task is to recover the threshold as a category of architectural argument.

MARTIUS · 2024
22III
MMXXIV

FOREWORD

"On the Coffer, as Structural Honesty"

Foreword to the Karlsruhe University reprint of Marc-Antoine Laugier's Essai sur l'architecture. Vennius takes the coffer as a measure of how the Roman builders accepted the load path of the soffit and rendered it visible at the same time it permitted them to lighten the dome.

07III
MMXXIV

ESSAY

"Travertine is a Slow Material"

Published in Domus, March MMXXIV. A reflection on travertine as the most patient material in the architect's vocabulary, and the conclusion that no contemporary building made of it has ever been finished too early.

DECEMBRIS · 2023
19XII
MMXXIII

LECTURE

"As Walls in Rome"

A delivered lecture at the spirit-of-the-place colloquy of the American Academy in Rome, December MMXXIII. The wall is read as a writing surface that records its own correctness, and that loss of correctness over time is read as the building's deepest measure of its own age.

OCTOBRIS · 2023
12X
MMXXIII

ESSAY

"The Pediment Returns"

Published in Casabella, October MMXXIII — a defence of the pediment as an active proposition for contemporary practice. The essay argues that the pediment does not survive on Roman buildings as nostalgia but as a workable proposition for finishing a horizontal cornice.

04X
MMXXIII

FOREWORD

"Against the Frameless Window"

An argument that a window without a frame is not a building element but a hole left in a building. The essay argues for the moulded surround, the frame, and the architrave, against the modern fashion of the unrelieved opening.

IULII · 2023
28VII
MMXXIII

ESSAY

"Mosaic and the Patient Hand"

Vennius's longest essay yet, written for the studio's tenth anniversary. The piece argues that the mosaic is a patient art — that the architect's hand is a slow medium, and that no atelier should take on more work than its hand can finish in proper time.

11VII
MMXXIII

FOREWORD

"A Catalogue of Reds"

Foreword to the Aquileia reprint of a 1923 catalogue of red lithic stones used in Roman opus sectile. Vennius reads the catalogue as a manual rather than a museum: every stone is a working stone, with a name and a quarry that have not been forgotten.

APRILIS · 2023
18IV
MMXXIII

ESSAY

"Notes from a Closed City"

A short notebook entry written in lockdown, reading the silent city centres of Rome, Aquileia, and Trieste as a single instrument of the architectural imagination. Reprinted in Architectural Review, May MMXXIII.

JANUARII · 2023
22I
MMXXIII

LECTURE

"Why We Still Draw the Order"

A defence of the imposed exercise of drawing the orders by hand at full scale, delivered to the first-year students of the Politecnico di Milano. The text argues that a student who has not drawn the order has not yet seen it.

MARTIUS · 2022
04III
MMXXII

ESSAY

"Vitruvius at the Hardware Store"

An affectionate observation that everything Vitruvius prescribes for the practising architect can be assembled today out of off-the-shelf hardware in any northern Italian provincial town — and that nothing in the kit has changed in two thousand years.
