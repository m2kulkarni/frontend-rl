OPUS
TESSELLATUM

The conservation, restoration, and original making of mosaic floors and walls — from the Roman second-century white-on-black through the Byzantine gold ground.

THE SOURCING OF TESSERAE

I

MARBLE OF CARRARA

For the white grounds and outlines — cut at our quarry on the Apuan ridges and graded by hand into ten classes of pearl, ivory, and lily-white.

II

PORPHYRIES

Imperial reds salvaged from late-empire fragments and recut to the original tessera proportions, used sparingly for ornament fields and for the figured borders.

III

SMALTI

Glass tesserae blown in our small furnace at Murano, including the gilt-leaf gold-grounds for vault and apse work, in the manner of the Ravenna ateliers.

CONSERVATION METHOD

VII

Seven steps from the inspection of the original surface to the final consolidation of the relaid bedding. Each step is set down by Vennius in his Manuale del Tessellatore and followed by every artisan in the studio. We do not improvise on antique work; we follow the maker's hand.

Lifting is by indirect tracing onto Japanese mulberry paper. Cleaning is dry-brush only until the bedding is mapped; consolidation is in slaked lime. No epoxy resin enters the studio.

THREE CASE STUDIES

I
SITEVilla Quintilia, Roma
PERIODII saec. p.Chr.n.
WORKThreshold reintegration

A radiate sun-medallion at the threshold of the great hall — the original survived in roughly two-thirds of its surface, with the central rosette intact but the outer ring almost entirely lost. The reintegrated rays were cut at the original 7-millimetre tessera scale and laid in lime in the manner attested in the surviving fragment.

II
SITEDomus Lomelinia, Genova
PERIODNew work · MMXXIII
WORKCubiculum floor

An original composition for the small bedchamber of a private house: the Pompeian-red and ivory opus reticulatum pattern in stone, set on the diagonal in the antique manner. The work was laid by three apprentices over fourteen weeks under the architect's supervision.

III
SITESacrarium privatum
PERIODNew work · MMXXIV
WORKApse-vault medallion

A small private oratory: a gilt-glass medallion at the centre of a violet ring-vault, in the manner of the late Ravenna ateliers. The central gold-leaf was struck and laid by Vennius himself; the rings were cut in concentric cassettes by the studio's senior glass-maker.
