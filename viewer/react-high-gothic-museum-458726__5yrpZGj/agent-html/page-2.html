GALLERIES & PERMANENT COLLECTION

Seven galleries housing the cathedral arts of northern France, arranged along a single processional axis from Lancet Hall to the open Mason's Yard.

The Cloister Museum's permanent collection comprises one thousand four hundred and seventy-two catalogued objects, of which two hundred and sixteen are on continuous public display across the seven galleries described below. The remaining works are accessible to credentialled researchers through the Reading Room by appointment, and a rotating selection appears in the museum's biennial loan exhibitions. All accession numbers follow the museum standard adopted in 1962: prefix CMA, gallery code, and acquisition sequence.

The Seven Galleries

In processional order, west to east

I[motif: arch-pointed]

The Lancet Hall

Circa MCC – MCD

Twenty-four panels of thirteenth-century French stained glass, arranged in three superposed registers across the east wall and lit from behind by a calibrated daylight rig held at 2,750 K.

Provenance
Long-term loan from the Diocese of Soissons, contracted 2012, renewed 2024 for a further fifteen years. Removed from the dismembered ruins of the cloister of Saint-Jean-des-Vignes during the 1972 restoration campaign.
Conservation
Lead cames replaced 2009 (Atelier Loire, Chartres). Vidimus film applied 2014. Annual deflection survey; counter bow at panel L8-IV measured 0.4 mm, within tolerance.
II[motif: arch-pointed]

The Tympanum Court

Circa MCL – MCC

A single carved Last Judgement fragment, limestone, 2.4 m across, depicting the weighing of souls and the mouth of Leviathan. Mounted at original portal height on a steel armature designed by Foster + Pickering.

Provenance
Anima workshop, circa 1184. Recovered from the rubble of a demolished parish church near Beuve in 1908, held by the Musée de Picardie until permanent transfer to the Cloister Museum in 1968.
Conservation
Consolidated with ethyl silicate (Wacker OH-100), 2003. No biological growth recorded since installation in climate-controlled court (RH 50 ± 3%, 18 °C).
III[motif: arch-pointed]

The Scriptorium

Circa MCC – MCD

Twelve illuminated leaves displayed under reduced lighting at 50 lux, including a Beatus initial in deep ultramarine and minium from the Abbey of Saint-Sever (folio 13b), Apocalypse cycle.

Provenance
Mixed: latter loose leaf acquired in the bidder's sale of the Cloutu-Beatty duplicates, 1968. Choeur psalter fragments donated by the Maréchal family, 1903.
Conservation
Leaves rotated on a 90-day cycle to limit cumulative light exposure (target ≤ 50,000 lux-hours per annum). Mylar interleaving renewed 2022.
IV[motif: arch-pointed]

The Reliquary Chamber

Circa MCC – MCD

Seventeen reliquaries in champlevé enamel, gilt copper, and rock crystal. The centerpiece is a Limoges chasse, c. 1200, depicting the martyrdom of Saint Thomas Becket.

Provenance
Bequest of Mrs. Eleanor Whitcombe, 1974, with subsequent acquisitions from the Le Roux collection (1992) and the Vatican's programme of restitution to civilian custodians (2017).
Conservation
Enamel surfaces examined under raking light biennially. The Becket chasse underwent gold leaf reintegration at the V&A conservation studios, 2019.
V[motif: arch-pointed]

The Capitals Cloister

Circa MCC – MCD

Forty-one carved capitals on freestanding limestone columns, arranged in a square plan around a central well. Subjects include foliate, monstrum, and historiated programmes.

Provenance
Drawn from the demolished cloister of the Priory of Saint-Yves de Thouroitte (Mons-de-Bois), dispersed 1791, reassembled through nine separate acquisitions between 1903 and 2007.
Conservation
Salt efflorescence on capitals C-12 and C-31 monitored quarterly; desalination poultices applied 2017, recurrence rate halved.
VI[motif: arch-pointed]

The Rose Window Study

Circa MCC – MCD

A scholarly gallery of six full-scale cartoon reconstructions of the great rose windows of Chartres, Notre-Dame de Paris, and Reims, prepared by the Atelier Simon-Marq, 1996–2014.

Provenance
Commissioned directly by the Cloister Museum under Charest as a pedagogical resource. The cartoons are themselves now considered conservation reference documents.
Conservation
Pigment stability tested annually. The Notre-Dame de Paris cartoon was photographed at high resolution in 2019 and now serves the post-fire restoration committee.
VII[motif: arch-pointed]

The Mason's Yard

Circa MCC – MCD

An open-air gallery of eighty-eight architectural fragments — voussoirs, finials, gargoyles, rosary elements, and one full surviving spire pinnacle — laid out as a working archaeological grid.

Provenance
Assembled from the salvage records of sixteen French and English cathedral fabric offices, 1956 onward. Each piece bears its original site mark in the form of an iron-gall masonry letter.
Conservation
Exposed to ambient weather by curatorial intent; lichen growth recorded as part of the long-term study (Stone in Open Air 1978–present).
