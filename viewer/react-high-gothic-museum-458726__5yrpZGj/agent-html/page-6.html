ABOUT THE MUSEUM & HISTORY OF THE CLOISTER

A century and a quarter of stewardship over the sacred arts of the cathedral-building age — glass, vellum, and stone gathered to the ruins of the Priory of Saint-Hilaire-des-Aubes.

[motif: cross-flory]

Three Foundations

1897

The Antiquarian's Vow

Édouard de Saint-Pierre, antiquarian of Reims and resident of Verdun-le-Cher near Sénoches, purchased the dissolved grounds of the Cistercian Priory of Saint-Hilaire-des-Aubes for €2,000 francs. His purpose, recorded in the deed of foundation of 14 October 1897, was the perpetual care of "fragments rescued from the cathedral fires of the Revolution and after."

1923

The Glass Bequest

Upon the antiquarian's death, his heirs entrusted the collection to a board of trustees. The museum was reorganised around the de Saint-Pierre stained-glass holdings — eighty-one panels of 12th- and 13th-century French work. The first public galleries opened on the feast of Saint-Yves, 9 October 1923, with admission set at one franc fifty.

1956–1962

The Rebuilding

After incendiary damage in the war years, the cloister walk and the lancet hall were rebuilt to designs prepared by the Atelier Loire of Eure-et-Loir, executed by the master masons of the Compagnons du Devoir. The works were dedicated by the bishop of Sens at the Easter vigil, 1962, retaining the original Cistercian foundation lines in poured concrete faced with Caen limestone.

[motif: trefoil]

An Account of the Cloister

The Priory of Saint-Hilaire-des-Aubes was nearly stripped from the suppression of 1791 and would have been wholly lost had not Édouard de Saint-Pierre, then thirty-five and a wool-merchant of considerable means, walked the ground in the Easter snow of 1879 and resolved upon its purchase. Documentation of his pace-by-pace survey, in his hand and unsigned, survives in the museum archives. Following his bequest in 1923, the trust acquired contiguous parcels to the south and east, regularising the precinct to its present 4.7 hectares. The west lancet hall, raised over the foundations of the priory's chapter house, retains six hundred and ninety lights — the largest contiguous panel of glass in private French ownership. The 1956 wing was made possible by a bequest from the Confraternity of Saint Bernard and by twelve years of stonework by the apprentices of the Compagnons du Devoir. The painted glass installation programme was completed in stages between 1962 and 2014. Annual visitor numbers since 1992 have stabilised at between forty and forty-five thousand, with research consultations standing apart at roughly six hundred per annum.
