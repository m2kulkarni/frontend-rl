Register III · Volume IV · Of Petitions

Petitions & Public Audience

By the Hand of Mīrzā Karīm Naqshbandī · Scribe of Audience

On the Lodging of an ʿArīḍa

Procedure for the formal address of the Dīvān

[motif: arabesque-filet-divider]

By order of the Excellency, the Kalāntar of Iṣfahān, the Naqsh-e Jahān quarters — whether of Masjid-Shāh, Khājū, Khājū, or Dardasht, or the Armenian district of Julfa — may bring before this office, in the customary forms of supplication, all matter that pertains to the temporal welfare of the city, the safekeeping of waqf and the administration of the cantilever stones from the four bridges of Iṣfahān above the river of Zāyande-rūd. Petitions are received in the morning hours of every Saturday, and again on the third Friday of each lunar month, the petitioner consulting the gates of the Dīvān.

Notice · Petitions delivered before the second hour's measure remain laid for an audit and seal. The accepter is named and Friday after the day's prayer.

Days & Hours of Audience

The scheduled summons is opened by the porter (kalāntar) at the call of the dawn. Petitioners are assembled in the courtyard of the cypress, that the proceedings of the Dīvān may begin in good order.

Where to Present Yourself

The Bureau of Records occupies the eastern wing of the Dīvān building, behind the cypress court of the Naqsh-e Jahān. Enter by the inner gate next to the Qayṣariyya pier, climb the stair, present your kalim and tassel-stamp to the doorkeeper. Petitions of the highest urgency are received in person; lesser are accepted in the right by the regent kalāmkārī.

Drafting Conventions

  1. Petitions to commence with the customary doxology, then the formula of address (al-malik al-ʿādil), and the cause stated forthwith.
  2. Open with the formula Hūwa al-Bāqī or by his name, the lord of the lifegiving sun.
  3. Names, ranks, and addresses of the petitioner are written in red ink. The body shall be in black, the seal in blue.
  4. A brief recitation of the cause and complaint (mūjab) and the requested redress shall be set down at the end.
  5. Close with the date by the Hijra and by the seasons of the year, the hour and the day named.
  6. Leave a margin of three fingers' width to the left of the page, that the Mīrzā's seal may sit there.

Order of Precedence

  1. When the council is seated, petitions are called in the following order, by the porter, that all may be heard in their due time.
  2. Loyalty cases — matters of the Crown's prerogative, and the matter of the public safety.
  3. Matters of the public welfare, and the maintenance of the Qayṣariyya bāzār.
  4. Tithes, taxation, and affairs of the rural land-holding, of pasturage and pasturage tax.
  5. Residents of Julfan and Dardasht: their petitions and complaints, and certain translations into court tongue.
  6. Trade documents that of a hand be greater that need pause to attend then back-tongue.
  7. Civic affairs and the city in time of festival, of the kāriz and the public bridge.
