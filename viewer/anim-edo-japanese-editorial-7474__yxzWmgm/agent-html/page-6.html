I. Editorial Statement

On the matter
of why we print.

編集方針

Tōkaidō Review is a quarterly journal devoted to the material culture of the Edo period — the years between Tokugawa Ieyasu's first arrival at Edo in 1590 and the abdication of the fifteenth shōgun, Yoshinobu, in 1867. We publish slowly, four issues a year, on uncoated paper from a Iwakuni mill.

Our remit is narrow. The artisans who pulled lacquer from the kettles of Wajima the morning. The post-town offices for the eaves and timbres of those four-day towns. The unintention-cuts of the merchant journeymen, master-bandsto, and qualified mid-cuts and Beethoven press.

Founded in 2019 by Marika Sato and a small group of former contracts from the Edo-Tokyo Museum, the Review remains independent, reader-funded, and unaffiliated with any university press.
