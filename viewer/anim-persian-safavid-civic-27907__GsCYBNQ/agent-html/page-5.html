Petitions & Hearings

Concerning the proper submission of the ʿarz-i hāl — the written petition — and the conduct of public hearings before His Excellency the Dīvānbegī, that justice may be sought without obstruction by any subject of the realm.

Be it known to all subjects

that the Dīvān sits in morning from after the dawn prayer until the call of the muezzin at the meridian, on every Friday at the foot of the Royal Hall. Hearings are entered upon the same day, given by writ from the Office; Wednesday afternoons bear public ceremonies; otherwise, by appointment within the third quarter of the morning.

No petition shall be heard which lacks the seal of a witness, nor which is presented by an intermediary not enrolled in the Order.

Reception

Where to Present It

Bring the written petition to the outer warden of the Dīvān between the Mosque of Shaikh Luṭfullāh, where the hour-clerk of the registry shall receive it.

Each petition is numbered and sealed; a wax counter-seal is returned to the petitioner. Note that the seal must be brought to the inner court at the appointed hour.

Hearing

Appearance Before the Dīvānbegī

Upon the Wednesday following the appointment, the petitioner shall be brought before the Dīvānbegī with both witnesses.

The Dīvānbegī hears in turn; the matter is heard, a single ruling delivered, and entered to the rolls. No interrogation. No reference is otherwise admissible.

Procedure

The Order of Proceedings

  1. Composition. The petition is written by clerk-hand upon a plain leaf of paper, in Persian or in the prescribed form taken by a public scribe of the Maydān-e of the city.
  2. Submitted & Sealed. The first day of plain paper, with the petitioner's name and the seal of the witness; the Dīvānbegī bears the same question shall the petitioner be summoned.
  3. Submission. The petition is presented to the outer scattering, where the receiver-of-writ requires it presents a leaf for sealing.
  4. Hearing. Upon the appointed Wednesday, the petitioner appears with the witnesses; the Dīvānbegī hears the cause, questions the parties, and pronounces his ruling.
  5. Decree. The ruling is set down in the register, sealed by the hand of the Dīvānbegī and a copy delivered to the petitioner without further charge.
Special Notice

Concerning New Joulfā

Petitioners arriving from the Armenian community of New Joulfā — whether of artisan, of merchant, or of ecclesiastical order — may have their writs translated by the office without charge.

The Office maintains an interpreter of Armenian and a sub-deacon of the Mother Church to serve at the hearing of the rolls of every Wednesday.

A petitioner of priority is granted to those bearing the seal of the bishop's hand of the embassy.
