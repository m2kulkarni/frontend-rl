Four buildings,
one quiet block of
Higashiyama.

The historic campus occupies a single Higashiyama block of fewer than 200 m. Around the courtyard, four small structures: the Sanjō gate, the seminar room, the Tōrin Bunko, and the conservation studio. A fifth, the residency house, sits across the lane.

Walking directions

三条通
Sanjō Gate

Two routes by foot

From Higashiyama Station — Subway Tōzai Line, exit 2. Walk south along Higashiōji-dōri for 5 min, turn right at the bookbinder's window. The Sanjō gate is the second machiya on the left.

From Sanjō Keihan — Walk east along Sanjō-dōri for 9 min, crossing the Kamo. The campus is the third gate beyond the kimono shop.

By city bus — Lines 5 and 100 stop at Higashiyama-Sanjō; the Sanjō gate is 3 min south.

Open-campus days

The four-year MA in Woodblock & Paper Conservation accepts a cohort of fourteen students each autumn. Portfolio and a letter from a working conservator are required. Open campus is one Saturday in March and one in May, run by the student council; portfolio review is informal.

Hokurin Open-Campus Saturdays

Twice a year the campus opens its doors to prospective students, conservation studios, and rare-book buffs to the public. To registration is required: arrive at the Sanjō gate any time after 10. Tea is served by the second-year cohort in the courtyard. The Tōrin Bunko is open with a guided tour at 11 and 14.

  • 10:00 · Sanjō Gate opens; tea in the courtyard
  • 11:00 · Bunko tour with Provost Yamamoto
  • 12:30 · Lunch (machiya bento, ¥800)
  • 14:00 · Bunko tour, second sitting
  • 16:00 · Studio open · informal portfolio review
  • 17:30 · Gate closes
Twice a year the campus opens for hours: courtyard, conservation studios, and rare-book vault to the public.
No registration is required.
