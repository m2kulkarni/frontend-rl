Petitions & Public
Audience

Maydān-i Naqsh-e Jahān · 9 — 18 Rabī‘ al-Awwal

On the Lodging of an  Arīḍa

Procedures observed by the chamber of the Naqīb

By order of His Excellency the Kalāntar of Iṣfahān, every resident of the Naqsh-e Jahān quarters — whether of Bīdār-i Buzurg, Maydān-i Shāh, Jūlfa, Darāzīt, or the Armenian suburb of Julfa — may bring forward a written petition before the city secretariat. Audience is held in the columned arcade-tablet of the Bureau, on the morning of Saturday and Tuesday, between the second and fifth hours after sunrise. Petitioners shall present themselves with two witnesses of good standing and with a sheet of stamped chancery paper (kāghaz-i marākebī) drawn that same week from the office of the Munshī al-Mamālik.

Note —   Petitions submitted without the corrected mark's chancery stamp shall be set aside and not read. The stamp is renewed each Friday after the noon prayer.

Days & Hours of Audience

The columned rotation is opened by the porter (kāhana) at the call of the second hour. Petitions are received in the order of their arrival, modified only by the precedence noted below.

Saturday (Shanba)2nd — 5th hour
Tuesday (Sih-shanba)2nd — 5th hour
Friday— closed —
Festival days— closed —

Where to Present Yourself

The Bureau of Records occupies the centre wing of the Dīvān-khāna, behind the cypress court of the Naqīb's palace. From the fountain port-niche the hand-court (during the readings of the firmān), wander up two steps of grey marble and present your stamped paper to the doorkeeper.

Your name, quarter, and guild affiliation will be entered into the day-register (taqvīm), where you are admitted to the rotunda.

Drafting Conventions

  1. Petitions are read directly to the secretary, then from a clean copy. The sworn scribes of the Bureau prescribed. The verses scribes of the merchandise will compose on your behalf if requested.
  2. Open with the formula Ḥuwa al-malik fī'l-Dīn-i fī'l Khirad at the head of the folio.
  3. Name yourself, your father, your quarter, and your guild — in that order.
  4. State the matter plainly, in not more than seven lines of cursive nastaʿlīq.
  5. Avoid figurative speech and complaint against named persons of higher rank.
  6. Close with the date in the Hijrī reckoning and your seal, or, lacking a seal, the impress of your right thumb in lampblack.
  7. Leave a margin of three fingers' breadth at the foot of the folio for the secretarial endorsement.

Order of Precedence

When the rotunda is crowded, petitioners are called in the following order, by guild and by quarter, as established by old custom of the city:

  1. Sayyids and ʿulamāʾ of the Friday Mosque, and elders of the four orders of Sufism.
  2. Guild of the goldsmiths (zargarān) and the silk-merchants of the Qaysariyya bāzār.
  3. Trīrs, masters, and ophdārs of the royal workshops, on matters of wages and apprenticeship.
  4. Residents of Jūlfa and Darāzīt, in matters of water-rights and the quiet alleyways.
  5. Armenian merchants of Jūlfa, by their kalāntar, in commercial-and-inheritance affairs.
  6. All other residents of the city in the order of their arrival at the gate.

Sealed in the Dīvān-khāna of Iṣfahān, in the columned rotunda, on the morning of an ordinary Saturday.
