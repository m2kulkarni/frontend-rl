Vamsavali · Genealogy of the Workshop

recorded in East Chitra Veedhi, Madurai, on Aippasi pournami

1887FOUNDER

Sthapati Govindaraju Ramaswamy

1849 — 1924

Pupil of the Tanjore court guild under Sri Subbaraya Sthapati. Established the Madurai workshop on grant of land from the Pandyan zamindar; carved the surviving brass kalasha for the eastern gopuram of Sri Meenakshi.

1924SUCCESSOR

Sthapati Subramanian Ramaswamy

1881 — 1958

Codified the proportional cubit and the modified hasta-mana; redrew the village amman pattern book in cotton paper. Master of the polychromy mineral series.

1958SUCCESSOR

Sthapati Murugan Ramaswamy

1912 — 1989

Restored the western mandapa of the Madurai Andal temple in 1962 — the workshop's first dated and signed work since the founder. Trained a generation of granite carvers in Sadarahalli grey.

1989SUCCESSOR

Sthapati Selvaraj Ramaswamy

1948 — 2017

Took the workshop into the eastern Pandya country: directed the Tirumayam Sri Aanjaneya commission in 1991 and the Pudukottai Saturday-day mandapa.

2017PRESENT

Sthapati R. Ganesh Ramaswamy

b. 1976 · current sthapati

The current principal carries the kosa of the workshop, two senior silpis, and four mandalin draughtsmen. The workshop receives commissions year-round, weighed against the mineral lunar calendar and the family priest.

The five-and-a-half generations of stone are set into the lintel of the workshop chamber.

The Tools

  • Hasta-mana cubit · 18 angula
  • Cotton-fibre charcoal-pencil sets
  • Bronze kosa-square & plumb
  • Iron tankis (eight grades, sized in vidra)
  • Patrachalam mineral palette — twelve fixings

The Vamsavali

Five sthapatis, each named for the temple precinct they finished. The names are inscribed on the lintel of the carving room, and offered each Aippasi pournami before work resumes.

Visiting Scholars

Tuesday through Saturday by appointment. The vamsavali pothi is open at the discretion of the sthapati.
