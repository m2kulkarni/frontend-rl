Galleries of the
Safavid Court

Seven halls trace one century of imperial patronage — from the abdah Khwasht of the Maydan-Naqsh ʿAbbasi to the silken loom of the Royal Atelier-Yazd, each gallery is a chapter in a single argument: the made world, lifted in homage to the made world's maker, takes the fully of the world.

The Seven Galleries

— Gallery I —

Tilework of the Maydān

1598–161178 objects

Cuerda-seca panels and faience cuts of haftrang technique recovered from the Mosque of Shaykh Lutfullah and from the spandrels of the ʿAli Qapu — three sequences in cobalt, turquoise, and the saffron yellow.

curated by Dr. Nāhid Hashemi
— Gallery II —

Manuscripts of the Royal Kitabkhana

1502–160554 objects

Illuminated leaves from the great Shahnameh of Shah Tahmasp, the Khamsa of Shah Ismaʿil II, and a clutch of single-page paintings.

curated by Dr. Hossein Tabatabaie
— Gallery III —

Silks & Velvets of Yazd

1550–1690112 objects

Brocade chasubles, voided velvets, and the famed lampas weave of the Royal Atelier-Yazd, including a complete cape lining once worn by Shah ʿAbbasi at the Maydān during the procession of Eid.

curated by Dr. Mariam Sherazi
— Gallery IV —

Metalwork & Inlaid Steel

1525–170096 objects

Damascened and gold-inlaid sword-furniture from the workshops of Yazd and Tabriz, an astrolabe of Najaf-Ali, and the long-handled bowl of the Hajj — the latter signed and dated 1614.

curated by Dr. Yusuf al-Ghamdi
— Gallery V —

Carpets of Ardabil & Tabriz

1539–170023 objects

Medallion carpets, vase-and-cartouche pieces, and a handful of garden-plot carpets restored under the Ekbatan-workshop terms — laid out, in this hall alone, beneath their proper north light.

curated by Dr. Pardis Keramati
— Gallery VI —

Porcelain Wares & Lustreware

1580–1700180 objects

Imitations of Ming, blue-and-white wares of Mashhad and Kerman, and a long case of Saljuk-revival lustreware bowls — these last from the gift of Mr. Iqbal of Tehran.

curated by Dr. Yasaman Tabbati

The Reconstructed Īwān

A full-scale reconstruction of the south-facing iwān of the Mosque-e Shah, executed at half-scale by master tile-cutters Reza Esfahani and Hassan Naderi over four winters with the cooperation of the Cultural Heritage Organization. Visitors may pass beneath the arch and stand within the cuerda-seca cycle. The hall closes at 16.30 daily.
