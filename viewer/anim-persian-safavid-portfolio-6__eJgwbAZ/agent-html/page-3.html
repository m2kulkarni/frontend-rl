THE QUIET DISCIPLINE
OF KĀSHĪ-KĀRĪ

A note from the workshop on how a single mosaic panel — from grid-paper cartoon to grouted spandrel — passes through four stages, seven colors, and the patient hands of those who came before. |>

✦   I   ·   PREMISE   ✦

A CRAFT HELD IN THE HAND

The lesson, in the Tabrīzī manner, is given on a slab of khākī brick: a charcoal cartoon of motif size, a kettle of beeswax-fast paint, and a quartet of curved tile-knives kept beside a washing scissor. Two-thirds of the studio's tile work is done by hand; the rest is the considered placement of forty tiles around a sermon, hand-cut, set on grass. The geometric is set on the cup, the calligraphic is set on a bowl. Both have already been set in their place.

The atelier was founded by sub-three of the women's training in the principle that no fixed work in tile is finished and no other can be the work of a tile. As a tile is to a stone, restoration is the necessary form: it is in the recovery and stewardship of long-faded craft, with the broken hand and the seven-colored ornament that dipped into the soil shall be made fresh again.

Within a few weeks, on a chair given to him last year by Master Iman Yousefi, our visiting Master spoke of how this craft is held in the hand.

THE FOUR STAGES

STAGE I

JARRAḤĪ

The patient cartoon: a full grid in graphite offset — one square equals one tile of mu‘arraq Indian-cotton paper, dampened to be hung on the studio wall for the inspection of the patron, the master, and the apprentice.

12 — 28 DAYS
STAGE II

BORĪDEN-KĀRĪ

Glazed Yazd-fired tile pieces — handcut to a 5-mm tolerance with the twenty curved tile-knives, sorted into the seven canonical colors after Yazd-fired tile, in their place under the steel skirt.

14 — 30 DAYS
STAGE III

CHĪDEMĀN

Set in place at the same time as the cartoon. The pith-glue of cellulose holds the tiles fast for transport. Adjustments to the design are negotiated by the master and the apprentice — and made on the chair in the room.

8 — 16 DAYS
STAGE IV

BAND-KĒSHĪ

At last, the slab is offered to wall. Border banditing follows — and a final lime-mortar grout, slow-set, is rubbed in by the patron's son or another fortunate apprentice. Ten days then to set the glaze.

10 — 14 DAYS

Each glaze is mixed in a ceramic mortar with a fixed water-mass; the lapis is the rarest, the white the most demanding to fire. The seven canonical colors were laid down in the late Safavid khorne, and have not changed since.

"The tile that is hurried will speak with a hurried voice for the next four centuries.
We are not in the business of leaving such a voice behind."
