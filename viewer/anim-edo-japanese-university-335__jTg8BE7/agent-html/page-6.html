沿革と概要

Seventy-three
years of quiet
practice.

Hokurin Daigaku has, since its first lectures in 1952, kept itself purposefully small. We open the gate to the city in two ways: through the third-year residencies our students take elsewhere, and through the open-campus days that bring the city back in.

HISTORY · 歴史

From a Higashiyama jūku to a chartered university

沿革

The first session of what would become Hokurin Daigaku met in October 1952 in a hired room above a kimono merchant on the north side of Sanjō-dōri. Twenty-six students sat on borrowed cushions to hear a lecture on Heian poetics. The university was, in those years, a private gathering of unusual scholars: a former Imperial archivist, a Kyōto silk-merchant's daughter who taught calligraphy without speaking, and the woodblock printer Inoue Tetsurō who later restored the Bunko's storehouse with his own hands.

In 1962 the university received a public charter. By 1989 the original rented rooms had become unsustainable; trustees moved to the present machiya block, restoring the four buildings over four years. The Tōrin Bunko was given to the university in 1991 by a descendant of the original collector and remains the largest of its three formal departments.

Since 1996 Hokurin has admitted a small graduate institute. We do not run an athletics program or maintain a parking deck; we do maintain a kiln, a working noborigama, and one of the country's larger conservation libraries.
