A · STUDIO & PRACTICE

Lucia Marciana

Founder of Atelier Vesperia, working in the lineage of Roman imperial geometry — axis, rhythm, and the quiet authority of stone.

ucia Marciana was born in Naples in 1984, raised within sight of the buried colonnades of Pompeii. She read architecture at the Sapienza Università di Roma, completing the Master of Architecture in 2010 with a thesis on the proportional systems of the Markets of Trajan. Her early years in practice were spent in apprenticeship to Paolo Vespasiani, the conservator and draughtsman, with whom she worked between 2010 and 2014 on restoration drawings for the Domus Aurea and a private chapel in Tivoli.

She founded Atelier Vesperia in 2016, in a tufa-walled studio on the Via dei Coronari. The practice is small — three architects, two draughtsmen, a single mosaicist — and accepts roughly four commissions each year: a villa, a public room, a courtyard, an ornament. Lucia draws every plan herself, by hand, on tracing paper with a 0.3 millimetre lead.

Teaching

Since 2018, Lucia has held the chair in Linguaggio & Ornamento at the Accademia di Belle Arti di Roma, where she teaches a single graduate-only seminar of twelve students each autumn term. The seminar takes a single Roman imperial plan and the geometry of its hand-drawn line, from cutting tools to bone burnishers. Past students have completed monographs on the Casper marbles of the Casa di Augusto and the British School at Rome's pavement of 1924.

"The architecture begins with one line drawn straight through the air — and everything that follows must answer to it. Restraint in material, generosity in proportion: the room cuts its dignity by what it left out. Travertine, brick, lime, and the slow tessera. No more."

— LUCIA MARCIANA · ROMA · MMXXIV
