工房を訪ねる Visit the
Studio.

The hangiten opens its sliding door once each month. The machiya is small, the workbench narrower still, and the carved cherry-blocks rest where the eighth proprietor left them at nightfall. Tea will be offered. Please arrive on foot, and please write ahead.

町家の作法 A note on the
machiya.

The building is timber, paper, and packed earth, three hundred years older than any of us. It asks for slowness. Three small courtesies make the visit possible.

Shoes at the genkan

Step out of your shoes at the sunken stone threshold and place them, toes outward, on the right-hand flagstone. House slippers are provided; socks are welcome.

The blocks are not to be touched

The carved cherry blocks resting on the workbench — some inked, some still drying from a morning's pulling — are alive in their fibres. Skin oil ruins them. Look closely; do not lay a finger.

Tea will be offered

Bancha, brewed in an iron kettle on the irori. Receiving the cup with both hands is the only ceremony asked. Conversation in low voices is encouraged; the printers next door will be working.

御来訪を心よりお待ち申し上げます We await your
visit with quiet care.
— Tomé Yashiro, eighth proprietor, written from the back room of the hangiten on a damp evening of the early autumn rains.
