Catalogue Raisonné · MMXXIV

The Permanent Collection

Four halls trace the medieval ornamental arts of Lower Normandy and the wider Île-de-France: stonework recovered from suppressed abbeys, three Chartres-school medallions, a single illuminated leaf from a Norman psalter, and the gilt-copper reliquary of Saint Évroult himself.

— Hall I —

Stonework & Tracery

Galerie nord
MS-Pierre 003 / Cat. 14

Capital with Acanthus & Beasts

Abbaye de Jumièges · c. 1080–1100

A Norman-Romanesque cushion capital in oolitic limestone, carved with deeply undercut acanthus volutes flanking a pair of confronted lions. Recovered from the south transept of Jumièges following the suppression of 1791 and conserved here since 1894.

Limestone 11th c.
MS-Pierre 011 / Cat. 22

Voussoir with Foliate Mask

Saint-Lô, église Notre-Dame · c. 1230

A keystone voussoir from the destroyed western portal of Notre-Dame de Saint-Lô, struck during the bombardment of 1944 and salvaged the following spring. The carving shows a green-man emitting trefoil oak from the corners of his mouth.

Calcaire 13th c.
MS-Pierre 027 / Cat. 38

Engaged Colonnette & Base

Saint-Évroult-en-Ouche · c. 1175

In situ from the priory's own south arcade. The base shows a typical griffe in the western corner; the shaft retains traces of its original red ochre underpaint, last touched during the campaigns of Abbot Roger du Sap (d. 1126's successor lineage).

Limestone 12th c.
MS-Pierre 034 / Cat. 41

Tracery Fragment, Quatrefoil

Bayeux, deanery rebuild · c. 1265

A bar-tracery quatrefoil from a clerestory window of the Bayeux deanery, replaced during the 1865 Viollet-le-Duc campaign and acquired at the Caen sale of 1903. The cusps preserve sharp lancet-edged tooling marks.

Limestone 13th c.
— Hall II —

Glass

Galerie est
MS-Verre 001 / Cat. 56

Medallion: The Vintage of Noah

Atelier de Chartres · c. 1215

A circular medallion of pot-metal glass leaded in original twelfth-century strapwork. The cobalt ground has the deep "Chartres blue" indistinguishable from the cathedral's western lancets; figural drawing in vitreous brown grisaille.

Pot-metal 13th c.
MS-Verre 002 / Cat. 57

Medallion: The Sower at Dawn

Atelier de Chartres · c. 1215

Companion to Cat. 56, depicting a hooded peasant casting seed across a furrowed field rendered in three bands of green. The borderwork employs the rare "knotted-vine" pattern found also at Bourges and on three panels at Sens.

Pot-metal 13th c.
MS-Verre 003 / Cat. 58

Medallion: The Threshing Floor

Atelier de Chartres · c. 1215

Third of the surviving labours-of-the-months sequence acquired from the abbey of Saint-Père-en-Vallée before its dispersal. A flailing figure in red separates grain from chaff beneath an arched cobalt sky; lead came is largely original.

Pot-metal 13th c.
MS-Verre 014 / Cat. 71

Border Strip with Vine Scroll

Norman atelier · c. 1240

A vertical border strip salvaged from a destroyed lancet at Saint-Pierre-sur-Dives. The vine winds in continuous serpentine through twelve tendrils; small grisaille rosettes mark each turn. Mounted in a contemporary bronze frame.

Grisaille 13th c.
— Hall III —

Manuscript & Illumination

Galerie sud
MS-Évr. 44 / fol. 27v

Beatus Initial & King David

Norman psalter · c. 1280

A single illuminated leaf, vellum, 244 × 168 mm. The historiated B encloses David tuning his harp; line-endings in burnished gold and lapis. The catalogue identifies the hand as that of the so-called Master of Évreux, active c. 1275–1290.

Vellum 13th c.
MS-Évr. 17 / fol. 4r

Calendar Page, July

Bec-Hellouin scriptorium · c. 1310

A calendar leaf showing the labour of harvesting wheat with a curved sickle, paired with the zodiacal sign of Leo. Saints' days are inscribed in three inks; the KL monogram is rendered in blue and rose with white-vine penwork.

Vellum 14th c.
MS-Évr. 09 / bifolium

Antiphonal Bifolium

Saint-Évroult priory · c. 1340

A liturgical bifolium with square notation on a four-line red staff. The chants for the Feast of Saint Évroult (29 December) are preserved here in their unique rhymed-office form, a survival of considerable musicological importance.

Vellum 14th c.
MS-Évr. 51 / fol. 1r

Charter of Robert de Grandmesnil

Saint-Évroult priory · 1066

The earliest object in the museum: a foundation charter on a single sheet of vellum, with the wax seal of Robert de Grandmesnil partly intact. Confirms the priory's dependencies and grants of woodland in the forest of La Trappe.

Vellum 11th c.
— Hall IV —

Reliquary Metalwork

Trésor
MS-Métal 001 / Cat. 102

Châsse de Saint-Évroult

Limoges atelier · c. 1230

The principal reliquary of the priory: gilt-copper over an oak core, with eight champlevé enamel medallions on a vermicule ground. The roof bears applied figures of Christ in Majesty and the four Evangelist symbols in cobalt and turquoise.

Gilt-copper 13th c.
MS-Métal 008 / Cat. 109

Processional Cross

Norman workshop · c. 1190

A double-armed processional cross of beaten silver over a wooden core, with engraved foliate terminals. The corpus is cast separately and shows the four-nail iconography characteristic of the period preceding the Crucifixion type.

Silver 12th c.
MS-Métal 014 / Cat. 116

Pyx with Standing Saints

Limoges atelier · c. 1250

A small cylindrical pyx for the reserved host, the lid surmounted by a knop in the form of a fleur-de-lis. The body is decorated with four standing saints under arcades — Peter, Paul, John the Evangelist, and Évroult — in champlevé.

Champlevé 13th c.
MS-Métal 022 / Cat. 124

Aquamanile in the form of a Lion

Lower Saxony or Mosan · c. 1280

A vessel for the priest's lavabo, cast in leaded bronze, the lion's tail forming the handle and the open mouth the spout. Imported into Normandy by way of the Champagne fairs and recorded in the priory inventory of 1432.

Bronze 13th c.
