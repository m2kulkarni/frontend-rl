Scholarship & the Library
Robert-de-Glanville

Behind the cloister's open arcade lies the working heart of the museum: a research library of 12,400 volumes, a fellowship programme of two stipends, an annual scholarly journal, and the long, patient work of digitising the priory's surviving manuscripts. We welcome by appointment readers whose work touches Norman monasticism, the conservation of stained glass, or the codicology of the Évroult corpus.

The Bibliothèque Robert-de-Glanville

A working library, founded 1947

The library occupies the upper range of the surviving cloister-wing, where the lay-brothers' dormitory once stood. Its collection — 12,400 catalogued volumes, of which approximately 940 are pre-1800 — is named for the architect-canon Robert de Glanville (1881–1953), whose bequest of his personal working library in 1947 founded the present holdings. The collection specialises narrowly: Norman monasticism, particularly the houses of the diocese of Lisieux from the eleventh through the fifteenth centuries; and the conservation of medieval stained glass, with strong holdings in twentieth-century French and German technical literature.

Notable strengths include a near-complete run of the Bulletin monumental from 1834, the personal offprint files of the glass-conservator Jean Lafond, and a working reference set of cartulary editions for the dioceses of Lisieux, Évreux, Bayeux and Sées. The library does not lend; all consultation is on-site, in the reading-room beneath the rib-vaulted bay overlooking the south walk of the cloister.

Three workstations are reserved for visiting researchers; a fourth is fitted for the consultation of large-format material and the priory's own manuscripts. Photography is permitted under the standard conditions of the Direction régionale des affaires culturelles. The reading-room seats fourteen and is open Tuesday through Friday, 9 h 30 – 17 h 30, with prior application to the conservator of manuscripts.

The library catalogue is partially available through the Sudoc union catalogue; the pre-1800 holdings and the Évroult manuscripts are described only in the printed Catalogue raisonné (1971, with supplements 1989 and 2014), copies of which may be consulted in the reading-room or purchased from the museum shop for 38 €.

Visiting-Fellowship Programme

Stipendium Roberti de Glanville

Two fellowships are awarded each year — one in the Lent term, one in Michaelmas — to scholars whose work depends substantively on the library's holdings or on the Évroult manuscripts. Fellows are housed in the converted infirmary range and are required to give one public seminar during the tenure of the award.

Applications open on 15 January and 15 June; decisions are communicated within ten weeks. The selection committee comprises the conservator of manuscripts, the director of the museum, and two external members nominated by the Société des Antiquaires de Normandie.

Cahiers de Saint-Évroult

Annales & commentarii

Founded in 1968, the Cahiers appear annually in November. Each volume runs to 180–220 pages and is distributed by subscription to libraries and learned societies, and through the museum shop. Articles are accepted in French, English, and Latin; abstracts are provided in all three.

The current editor is the conservator of manuscripts. Submissions for the forthcoming volume close on 1 March; the editorial board sits in May. A complete back-run is maintained in the reading-room and may be consulted on request.

The Digitisation of MS Évr. 1–88

opus in progressu · 2019–

The systematic digitisation of the eighty-eight surviving manuscripts of the Évroult corpus was begun in 2019, in partnership with the Institut de recherche et d'histoire des textes. Each codex is photographed at 600 dpi in raking and direct light, with full collation diagrams and updated codicological descriptions; results are released to the public domain volume by volume on the museum's repository.

Work proceeds at a deliberate rhythm of eight to twelve manuscripts per year. The cartulary (MS Évr. 41–42) and the Norman customary (MS Évr. 17) have been treated first, on account of demand; the calendar fragments and pen-trial leaves of MS Évr. 71–88 will close the campaign, projected for completion in late 2027. Researchers are warmly invited to consult the working files in advance of publication, by appointment.
