The Light of Chartres

Glass Fragments, 1194–1260

Forty-seven panels and shards recovered from the Atelier Lorin archive trace the cobalt and ruby palette that defined the High Gothic. The exhibition closes 14 March; admission is included with the priory ticket.

Permanent Galleries

Stones of the Priory

Salles I–VI, eleventh to fifteenth centuries

Capitals from the demolished chapter house, a near-complete sequence of Norman tympana, polychrome wood saints, and the Carrouges reliquary cross. Six rooms run the length of the surviving north walk.

A word from the director

The priory of Saint-Évroult was never one of the great names — Bec, Jumièges, Mont-Saint-Michel — and that is, I think, why it has so much to teach us. What survived here is the daily fabric of a Norman religious house: the cloister walk a community circled four times a day for five hundred years, the corbels carved by craftsmen whose names we will never know, the glass that lit the chapter house when the brothers gathered to read the Rule. Our task, since 1873, has been to keep that fabric legible. We hope you will find, in these galleries, something of the patience the stones themselves require.

Mme Aliénor CarrougesDirector, since 2019
