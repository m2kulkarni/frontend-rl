Atelier Practice
Drawing · Modelling · Construction

From the first stroke of the reed pen upon linen paper, through the patient labour of the plaster knife, to the rising of dressed stone upon the site — the studio holds to one method, observed in three stages, since its founding in the year MMXIV.

Tribus Gradibus · The Three Stages

Each commission, without exception, follows the prescribed sequence.
· I ·
Drawing

Ink Elevations on Linen

Every project begins at the long oak boards of the drawing room, where the chief draughtsman, Tomaso Conti, prepares ink elevations upon Belgian linen paper at scales of 1:100 and 1:50. We use lampblack and gall-iron inks ground at the studio each Monday, and the line-weights are governed by the rules set down in our copy of Vignola, Rome 1562.

A first sheet of plan, section, and principal elevation is delivered to the client within forty days of the commission's signature. A complete folio of working drawings — never fewer than fourteen plates — follows within six months.

Folio · Lin. Belg. · 580 × 820 mm
· II ·
Modelling

Plaster Maquettes at 1:50

From the approved drawings, our two model-makers — Lucia Bertolozzi and Andrea Manzini — translate the project into plaster of Paris at the scale of one to fifty. The model is built upon a poplar armature, faced in fine gypsum, and tooled with steel rasps to produce the precise relief of mouldings, triglyphs, and dentils.

The maquette is presented to the client by candlelight, raked from the west, that the play of shadow upon the cornice may be judged as it shall appear in the afternoon sun. No commission has ever proceeded to construction without this examination.

· III ·
Construction

Supervision in Tunic-Grey Coats

The third stage carries the studio onto the site itself. Our principal, Marcus Aurelius Salvi, and one assistant attend each commission in the studio's plain tunic-grey work coats — woven by the textile guild of Prato, fastened at the shoulder with a bronze fibula bearing the studio's mark — for no fewer than sixty days of inspection per fabric.

Stones are checked against the templates; mortar joints are measured to the half-millimetre; capitals are set only after the abacus has been levelled by water in a bronze trough. Construction is held to be the slowest and the most truthful of the three labours.

Sodales · Standing Collaborations

Two houses with which the studio has worked, without interruption, since MMXVII.

Cave di Marmo · Carrara

Apuan Alps · Toscana

The studio holds a standing covenant with the marble quarries of Carrara, by which a senior pair of cavatori — Pietro Falcone and his son Niccolò — reserve for our exclusive use the white-veined Statuario block, drawn from the Polvaccio face. Our cornices and the shafts of our columns are cut by their hands and arrive at Roma by rail, sealed in oiled jute, on the third Friday of each month.

Bottega Pellegrini · Napoli

Vico San Domenico · Napoli

For programmes of fresco, vault decoration, and grotesque ornament, the studio engages the workshop of Giovanna Pellegrini, occupying the ground floor of a sixteenth-century palazzo in Vico San Domenico. Signora Pellegrini and her four assistants prepare their own pigments from the earths of Pozzuoli and Vesuvius, and work only in true buon fresco upon the wet intonaco.
