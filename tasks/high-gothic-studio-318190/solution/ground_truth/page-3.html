Chapter I

A Brief History of the House

The atelier was founded in the spring of 1986 by Hélène Vaucresson, who had taken her diploma from the École de Versailles in 1981 and spent the intervening decade in service to the Monuments Historiques — three years at Senlis under Pierre Lablaude, four at the central bureau in Paris, and three on the long campaign at Saint-Remi.

Her decision to leave the State and open a small bureau on Rue de Vesle was, at the time, regarded as eccentric. The practice was deliberately situated within a half-hour's walk of the cathedral. Her first commission, the consolidation of the south transept window of Notre-Dame de l'Épine, arrived within four months.

"To work upon a cathedral is to enter a conversation already nine centuries old. One does not begin; one continues." — H.V., Carnet d'atelier, 1992.

For its first decade the atelier worked almost exclusively on stone — vaulting, tracery, the reset of fallen voussoirs after the storms of '99. The historian Mathilde Renouard joined in 1994; the resident stone-cutters Joachim Ferré and Anselme Doré followed in 1997 and 2003 respectively, both trained at the Compagnons du Devoir.

Civic and contemporary commissions entered the practice from 2008, beginning with the new mediathèque at Châlons. The studio today does not regard these as a departure but as the same discipline applied to younger walls.

Since 2011 the practice has been led collegially by four senior architects: Vaucresson herself, Jean-Baptiste Coursier, Inès Mahieu, and Théo Brassart. Three associates — Camille Lavergne, Olivier Pinson, and Naïma El Ghali — sit beneath them. Two stone-cutters work in residence; one historian, one accountant complete the household.

The atelier accepts at most six commissions in any given year. Three of those are conserved for sacred or heritage fabric. The remaining three may be civic, scholarly, or domestic, provided the work bears upon the older question.

The studio is not a partnership in the legal sense. It is, in Vaucresson's phrase, une maison — a house, with rooms set aside for those who live in it.

Chapter II

The Present Composition

Eleven, including the cat

The studio numbers eleven souls — four senior architects, three associates, two stone-cutters in residence, one historian, and one accountant. A black cat, Athanase, has been on the books in an unofficial capacity since 2014.

The atelier occupies the first and second floors of a 17th-century hôtel particulier on Rue de Vesle in central Reims. The drawing room is on the second floor, the stone yard at the rear court.

Chapter III

The Senior Architects

Hélène Vaucresson

Founder  ·  DPLG Versailles, 1981  ·  Stone fabric, Gothic vaulting

A decade at the Service des Monuments Historiques, including the long campaign at Saint-Remi (1983–86), preceded the founding of the atelier. Specialism: the consolidation and rebedding of high-Gothic vault webs and flying buttresses. Drawing master to all who pass through the studio.

Selected Papers

  • "Notre-Dame de l'Épine: South Transept", AMH, 2001
  • "Carnet d'atelier I–III" (privately printed, 1992–2018)

Jean-Baptiste Coursier

Senior Architect  ·  HMONP Belleville, 1996  ·  Civic commissions, mediathèques

Joined the practice in 2002 from the Atelier Castro-Denissof. Coursier directs the studio's contemporary civic work — the Châlons mediathèque (2011), the Senlis tribunal extension (2017), the Reims music conservatoire (in progress, completion expected 2026). Reads Pugin every winter.

Selected Papers
  • "The Civic Arch: Notes from Châlons", D'A, 2013
  • "Stone in Public Buildings, 1990–2020", Le Moniteur, 2021

Inès Mahieu

Senior Architect  ·  ENSA Paris-Malaquais, 1999  ·  Glazing & daylight, sacred interiors

Trained additionally at the CRMH workshop in Champs-sur-Marne (DSA Patrimoine, 2002). Mahieu is the studio's lead on stained-glass campaigns and the geometry of daylight. Directed the cleaning and re-leading of the western lancets at Saint-Remi (2015–19) and the new dalles de verre programme at the chapelle de Sept-Saulx.

Selected Papers
  • "Daylight in the Romanesque Choir", Cahiers de Saint-Michel, 2016
  • "Re-Leading: A Manual for Practitioners" (co-authored), 2020

Théo Brassart

Senior Architect  ·  ENSA Strasbourg, 2004  ·  Survey, photogrammetry, digital model

Joined in 2009. Brassart oversees the survey department: laser scanning, photogrammetry, and the construction of the studio's three-dimensional models from the hand-measured drawings. Holds that the digital model exists to confirm the eye, not to replace it. Lectures at ENSA Nancy each spring.

Selected Papers

Chapter IV

On Working Method

"Measured drawing first.
Digital model second.
Always."
— House Rule of the Atelier, 1986 / 2011
i. The Hand

Every commission begins with the survey: the building measured by hand, in pencil, on cartridge paper at 1:50 or 1:20. The senior architect of the project goes onto the scaffold with two assistants, a folding rule, and a plumb-line. No commission proceeds until this drawing is complete and signed at the bottom.

The hand-survey is filed in the studio's drawing-room, in flat oak boxes ordered by year. There are now thirty-eight such boxes.

ii. The Model

The hand-survey is then translated into a digital model — not before. Brassart's department photographs and laser-scans only after the pencil drawing is in. The model is used for tolerance-checking, for fabrication drawings sent to the stone yard, and for coordination with engineers. It is not used to think with.

This sequence is the single non-negotiable rule of the practice. It is older than any of the partners. It will, we hope, outlast them.
