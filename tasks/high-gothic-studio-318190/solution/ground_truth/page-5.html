Essay · On Geometry

On the geometry of plate tracery at Soissons

Hélène Vaucresson, principal architect

The clerestory at Soissons offers a rare unaltered passage from plate to bar tracery, and its proportions still resist easy diagramming after eight centuries. Drawing from the chapter archive and three weeks of rope-access survey, this essay reconstructs the masons' compass-and-string method and considers what its slight imprecisions tell us about workshop authority.

Quarry-fresh limestone: sourcing notes from Saint-Maximin

Jérôme Berthier, materials lead

A morning at the Saint-Maximin quarry, where the upper bed is again being worked after a forty-year pause, and a stone of remarkable consistency emerges in slabs the colour of cold cream. We discuss matching, weathering rates, and the small heresy of preferring quarry-fresh stone to careful salvage.

Polemic · Conservation Ethics

A short defence of the unfinished west front

Margaux Aubrac, partner

There is a quiet movement to "complete" the western towers of half-a-dozen French cathedrals, and we should resist it. The unfinished west front is not a wound; it is a document, and the impulse to plaster it over is the impulse to forget the centuries that left it that way.

Field Note · Survey

A photogrammetric survey of the chapter house at Noyon

Étienne Lambert, surveyor

Six days, 3,840 photographs, and a point cloud of 1.2 billion vertices later, we have a model of the Noyon chapter house accurate to within 4 mm. The drawings reveal a barely perceptible bowing of the south wall that the eye had been quietly correcting for years.

Essay · Sacred Space

On the patience of cold churches

Hélène Vaucresson, principal architect

The case against heating a medieval church is, briefly: the stone does not want it, the timber does not want it, and the windows will tell you so within a winter. A meditation on under-floor air systems, hand-warmers, and the dignity of a building permitted to remain itself.
