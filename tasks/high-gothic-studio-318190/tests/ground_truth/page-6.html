— The Three Offices of the Studio —

Forms of Engagement

The atelier accepts commissions under three distinct rubrics. Each is governed by its own protocol, fee structure, and expected duration. We do not undertake speculative work, competition entries without honoraria, or commissions whose programme conflicts with the conservation of consecrated fabric.

— I —

Full Architectural Service

From feasibility to handover · 18–60 months · ecclesiastical & civic

The complete architectural commission, undertaken from initial feasibility through detailed design, tender, contract administration, and the issuing of the final certificate. Reserved chiefly for sacred fabric, civic interiors, and projects involving the conservation of listed stone.

Camille Roussel coordinates programme; Father-Architect Henri Vernier signs the drawings.

  • RIBA / OAR full stages 1–6
  • Stone, lime & lead schedules
  • Glazing & liturgical furnishings
  • Site presence: weekly
— Fee: 9 – 12 % of works value —
— II —

Conservation Reports & Condition Surveys

Standalone document · 8–14 weeks · single fixed fee

A bound conservation report, drawn from on-site survey, archival research, and structural-stone assay. Suitable for parishes preparing diocesan returns, owners of monuments historiques, and trustees commissioning quinquennial inspections.

Reports are delivered in two leather-bound copies and a digital archive, with a defects schedule, photographic plates, and a ten-year fabric forecast.

  • Quinquennial & decennial surveys
  • Stone petrography commissioned externally
  • Mortar analysis (Reims, Châlons)
  • Photogrammetric record included
— Fee: € 14,500 – € 38,000 —
— III —

Expert-Witness & Tribunal Work

Testimony & written opinion · 4–20 weeks · per instruction

Expert testimony before ecclesiastical chapters, the Commission Régionale du Patrimoine, and civil heritage tribunals concerned with disputed conservation works, demolition orders, or contested attributions of medieval fabric.

The studio gives evidence in French and English. Three matters per annum are accepted; further requests are placed on a waiting list reviewed each Michaelmas.

  • Written expert opinion (rapport)
  • Cross-examination at hearing
  • Site re-inspection where required
  • Bilingual: FR / EN
— Fee: € 480 / hour, day-rate € 3,200 —

The studio reads new enquiries on the first Monday of each month.

Letters and emails received between Mondays are gathered, read in chapter, and distributed amongst the architects on the morning of the first Monday. A reply is dispatched within three weeks of that reading.

If your enquiry is urgent — a structural collapse, a stone fall, a flood within consecrated fabric — telephone the studio directly and say so to Mme. Roussel.

How to Address the Atelier

— a brief protocol for those writing to the studio for the first time —

— STEP I —

Name the fabric

Identify the building, its dedication, its parish or commune, and — if known — its protection status (classé, inscrit, MH, or none).

e.g. St-Étienne-le-Vieux, classé 1875
— STEP II —

State the office

Indicate which of the three offices applies: full service, conservation report, or expert witness. If you are uncertain, say so plainly.

Office I, II or III
— STEP III —

Enclose the matter

Brief description, anticipated timetable, and a single contact. Photographs welcome but not required; we shall ask for what we need.

One letter, one matter
— STEP IV —

Await the chapter

The first Monday after receipt brings your letter to chapter. A written reply follows within three weeks, accepting, declining, or proposing terms.

Reply within XXI days

What the Studio Does Not Undertake

For honesty's sake — to spare both your time and ours.

Speculative competitionsEntries without honoraria or paid second stages.
New-build housingDomestic dwellings, save those attached to existing sacred or civic complexes.
Retail interiorsIncluding hospitality, food & beverage, and short-let conversion of monuments.
Demolition consentsThe atelier will not author petitions for the removal of medieval fabric.
Replica workPastiche reconstruction in the absence of documentary or archaeological evidence.
Cement-based repairPortland-cement renders or pointing on stone of pre-1900 date.
