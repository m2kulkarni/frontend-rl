Petitions & Audience

Of the manner by which a citizen, a freedman, or a peregrinus resident in Ostia may bring a complaint, a request, or a suit before the magistracy of the Aediles.

Locus Curiae

The office of the Aediles stands upon the Decumanus Maximus, opposite the Temple of Rome and Augustus and adjacent to the Basilica. Petitioners enter through the southern portico, where the lictor on duty receives the tessera and announces the suit.

Insula III, Regio I — three hundred paces eastward from the Porta Romana, beneath the standard of the fasces.

Horae Audientiae

Audience is held from the third hour through the ninth on every day, save the nundinae of the macellum and the public feriae inscribed upon the Fasti. The aediles sit in pairs; one upon the curule chair, one in attendance.

Petitioners arriving after the eighth hour are recorded for the morrow. No suit is heard upon the Kalends of January nor during the Saturnalia.

Quae Ferenda Sunt

Each petitioner shall present his tessera of citizenship or residency to the scriba at the threshold, together with a written request inscribed upon a wax tablet or sheet of papyrus, sealed and signed before two witnesses of standing.

Suits concerning weights, prices, water rights, and public order are heard first; matters of damage and trespass thereafter, in the order of the register.

Forma Petitionis

The Form of Petition

For Citizens of Rome

The Roman citizen presents the tessera bearing his tribe and praenomen. The petition is read aloud by the praeco, and the aediles render judgement at the third or fourth hour following. Suits may be lodged on behalf of household, estate, or freedman of the citizen's manus.

For Freedmen

Libertini approach with the brand or token of manumission and the name of the patronus. A patronus may not be summoned by his own freedman save in matters of the public weight and measure, where all stand equal before the curule chair.

For Resident Peregrini

Foreigners resident in Ostia — those of Alexandria, Carthage, Corinth, and the harbours of Hispania — must present the tessera hospitalis stamped at the Porta Marina, accompanied by a sponsor of citizen rank. Petitions in tongues other than Latin shall be translated by the scriba at a fee of two asses.

Of the Tablet

Tablets shall be of beech or fir, not exceeding one foot in length. Papyrus rolls shall bear the seal of the petitioner pressed in red wax. No suit shall be heard if the writing be illegible or the seal broken before the curia.
