Aquae et Thermae

De Aquis · De Thermis · De Fontibus Publicis

Of the waters that descend from the Anio Vetus into our port, the fountains that nourish the wards, and the four bathing complexes held under the inspection of the Aediles.

De Aquaeductu Ostiensi

The Branch of the Anio & the Public Fountains

Our city is fed by a subsidiary branch of the Aqua Anio Vetus, taken at the eleventh milestone from the parent channel and conducted by arcade and substruction across the Laurentine plain to the castellum aquae at the Porta Romana. From the great castellum the flow is divided into three orders: the first to the public fountains and the basins of the wards (vici); the second to the four imperial baths and the palaestrae; and the third, by lease and tally, to the houses of those who hold a license under the tabula plumbea stamped by this office.

Each branch is gauged by the bronze calix set in the wall of the castellum and witnessed twice yearly, on the Kalends of March and the Kalends of September, by the senior aedilis curulis in person. Tampering with a calix or piercing of the public mains carries a fine of two thousand sesterces and the confiscation of the offending lead pipe.

Thermarum Ostiae Conspectus

Thermae Neptuni

Built by the divine Hadrian and dedicated in the year of the consuls Bruttius Praesens and Iunius Rusticus. Famed for the great mosaic of Neptune in the frigidarium; the caldarium faces the south-west and is heated from the corner praefurnium.

Address
Regio II, Insula IV, on the Decumanus.
Hours
Women: hour II to VI · Men: hour VII to sunset.
Entry
¼ as · women ½ as

Thermae Mithrae

So called from the Mithraeum cut beneath the service corridor. A modest establishment of seven chambers, suited to the laborers of the salt-pans and the wool-fullers of the Vicus Augustanus. Inspected for soundness of vault every fifth year.

Address
Regio I, Insula XVII, behind the Mill of Silvanus.
Hours
Open hour III to sunset · No women's interval.
Entry
⅛ as · sailors free

Thermae Septem Sapientium

The Baths of the Seven Sages, named for the painted exedra in the round hall depicting Solon, Thales, Bias, and their fellows with their inscribed maxims. The largest cold-pool in the city, eighteen paces across.

Address
Regio III, Insula X, by the Insula of the Charioteers.
Hours
Women: hour I to V · Men: hour VI to first lamp.
Entry
½ as · children ¼ as

Thermae Fori

Adjoining the Forum on its eastern flank; restored at the cost of the senator C. Cartilius Poplicola in the consulship of Aelius Caesar. Reserved on the Ides of each month for the magistracy and the tribunes of the corn-fleet.

Address
Regio I, Insula XII, east side of the Forum.
Hours
Women: hour II to V · Men: hour V to ninth.
Entry
1 as · gratis on Ides

Edictum de Aquae Parsimonia

Standing Advisory · Months of Sextilis & September

Whereas the flow of the Anio is observed each year to fall by some two-fifths during the dry months of Sextilis (August) and September, and whereas the public bathing complexes consume in summer above six hundred quinariae daily, this office, by authority of the senior aedile Q. Asconius Marcellus, decrees the following observances from the Kalends of Sextilis to the Kalends of October:

The fountains of the wards shall be drawn from only between the second and tenth hours; the watering of private gardens by leased pipe shall cease entirely; the Baths of the Forum shall close their natatio on the days following each nundinae; and householders are reminded that to fill a private cistern from the public spout is theft of public water and shall be punished with the lash for slaves and a fine of fifty sesterces for the freeborn.

Citizens observing breach of these provisions are urged to lodge complaint at the office of the curule aedile, in the chamber adjoining the Forum Baths, before the eleventh hour of any working day.

Given at Ostia · A.D. III Kal. Aug. · Q. Asconius Marcellus, Aed. Cur.
