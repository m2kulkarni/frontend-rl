I

De Pretio Farris

Concerning the just price of emmer-wheat in the public market

Datum · A.D. III Kal. Mai.· Forum Ostiense· Album II · Tabula VII

Be it known to all dealers, measurers, and householders trading at the public horrea: the ceiling price for one sextarius of cleansed far shall not exceed three asses and two semunciae of bronze coin until the next Kalends of September. No vendor, whether Roman, Latin, or peregrine, shall demand of buyer nor purser a sum beyond this measure.

The aedile’s mensores shall daily set forth the bronze sextarius beside the table of weights at the Forum, and any grain found cut with chaff, sand, or barley dust shall be confiscated to the public granary at Horrea Epagathiana. The penalty for first transgression is a fine of fifty denarii; for the second, the public posting of the dealer’s name upon the album; for the third, exclusion from the market for the remainder of the consular year.

This edict supersedes the price-notice of the previous spring and shall be cried at the gate of the Macellum each market-day at the second hour.

Scriba · Marcus Vibius Quietus · Sigillum Curiae · Pridie Non. April.
II

De Saccariis Portus

On the regulation of dockside porters at the Trajanic Hexagon

Datum · Kal. Iun.· Portus Traiani Felicis· Album II · Tabula IX

Whereas the saccarii of the hexagonal basin have, contrary to the agreement struck under the Praefectus Annonae, raised their wage above eight asses for each amphora borne from quay to horreum, the College of Aediles has heard the complaint of the navicularii of Africa and Hispania and now decrees as follows.

No porter inscribed upon the roll of the Schola Saccariorum shall demand more than seven asses for the carriage of a full amphora, nor more than three asses for a half-measure of oil or salted fish. Carriage from the inner mole to the granaries of Severus shall be reckoned a single stage; no second fee may be claimed for passage through the Decumanus Maximus.

Officers of the College shall daily inspect the wage-table nailed beside the lighthouse, and any porter found exacting beyond the lawful sum shall forfeit his bronze tessera and be barred from the basin for thirty days. Disputes between porter and shipmaster are to be carried, without delay, to the tribunal of the Aedilis Curulis sitting in the Piazzale delle Corporazioni.

Scriba · Lucius Caecilius Hermeros · Sigillum Portus · Kal. Iun.
III

De Popinis ad Theatrum

A prohibition of unlicensed cookshops within twenty paces of the theatre

Datum · A.D. VI Id. Iul.· Theatrum Ostiense· Album III · Tabula II

By the authority vested in the Aediles by the Ordo Decurionum, no popina, taberna, nor itinerant brazier shall be set up within twenty Roman paces of the outer wall of the theatre, measured from the steps of the cavea outward to the Cardo. The smoke of frying tripe and the cries of the wine-criers have hindered the orderly conduct of the ludi scaenici and have offended both citizen and visitor.

Cookshops already standing within the prohibited measure shall be removed before the Ides of August. Those bearing a valid licentia from the previous aedileship may apply, in person and with their tessera, for a new station along the Via dei Molini or beside the Caseggiato dei Triclinii, where ample custom remains.

Any innkeeper, sausage-seller, or vendor of hot chickpeas found in violation shall pay a fine of twenty-five denarii, and his hearth shall be quenched by the urban vigiles. A second offence shall draw the loss of the licentia and the public breaking of his oven.

Scriba · Quintus Annius Festus · Sigillum Theatri · A.D. VI Id. Iul.
IV

De Pondere Iusto

On the inspection of weights and measures in the Macellum

Datum · A.D. IV Non. Aug.· Macellum Magnum· Album III · Tabula V

Forasmuch as several fishmongers and butchers were found, in the audit of the previous month, to keep balances weighted with lead beneath the pan, the Aediles command that every libra and uncia in use within the Macellum, the Forum Vinarium, and the stalls of the Decumanus be brought, before the Nones of September, to the public ponderarium beside the Curia.

There the bronze standards, sealed with the mark of the Divus Traianus, shall be applied, and a leaden tessera affixed to each balance found true. Any merchant trading without the year’s tessera shall be presumed fraudulent and his stock confiscate to the poor of the wards.

Scriba · Titus Flavius Philocalus · Sigillum Macelli · A.D. IV Non. Aug.
V

De Custodia Aquae Nocturna

Concerning the night-watch upon the public fountains and baths

Datum · Id. Sept.· Thermae Forenses· Album III · Tabula XI

Reports brought by the curatores aquarum show that, in the dark hours, certain householders have bored clandestine pipes into the public mains feeding the Thermae Forenses and the basin of Neptune, drawing off water without payment of the vectigal. This is a theft from the people of Ostia and an injury to the bathers who find the calidarium cool at the second hour.

From this day, two slaves of the city shall walk the line of the aqueduct from the Porta Romana to the cisterns of the Caseggiato del Serapide, between the eleventh hour of night and the first of day, bearing torches and the bronze sounding-rod. Any unauthorised tap discovered shall be sealed with lead at the householder’s cost, and the offender summoned to answer before the Aedilis Plebeius.

Scriba · Gaius Sempronius Verus · Sigillum Aquarum · Id. Sept.
