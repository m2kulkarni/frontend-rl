De Cursu Honorum

The Path of Public Honour

The Roman magistracy is climbed in fixed order, each office a step upon the ladder by which a citizen of standing offers his years to the Republic. The would-be statesman first serves ten campaigns at arms, or their equivalent in civil duty, before he may stand for the quaestorship at the age of thirty. From the quaestorship — which entrusts him with public coin and the keeping of the treasury at Rome or in a province — he may, after the prescribed interval of two full years, present his name to the Comitia Tributa for the aedileship.

The aedile is the magistrate of the city itself: of its markets and its weights, its waters and its baths, its games and its fires. At Ostia, where the grain of Egypt and Africa is unloaded for the City, the office carries weight beyond its rank, and is not lightly granted. Two are elected each year by the curiate assembly of the colony; both must have held the quaestorship and neither may be less than thirty-six years of age.

Having served his year as aedile and rendered his accounts before the duoviri, the magistrate may at last present himself for the praetorship, and from there, in the fullness of years and with the favour of the Senate and the People, for the consulship itself. Such is the cursus established by the lex Villia annalis and confirmed by the divine Augustus.

Aediles Curules

The Two Serving Aediles · Anno MMDCCLXXVII

M·C·R

M. Caecilius Rufinus

Aedilis Senior · Curule Aedile

Of the gens Caecilia, Rufinus served as quaestor of Sicily under the proconsul Sextus Quinctilius and now presides over the markets of Ostia, the customs at Portus, and the public order of the colony. He bears the curule chair and the right of the toga praetexta, and may pronounce edicts within his sphere of competence.

T. Annius Severus

Aedilis Iunior · Plebeian Aedile

A son of the Annii of Lavinium and former military tribune of the Legio II Adiutrix, Severus is charged with the aqueducts, the four public bath-houses of the colony, and the watch against fires. He sits as colleague to Rufinus and may exercise full magisterial power in his absence from the city.

Scriba Princeps

Chief Scribe of the Curia

PHI

Philocrates Puteolanus

Scriba Princeps · Chief Scribe of Puteoli

A freedman of the Calpurnii of Puteoli, manumitted in his thirty-second year and granted the citizenship by the patron's testament, Philocrates supervises the tabularium of the Curia and the keeping of the registers — of grain, of taxes harbour-paid, of weights tested, and of edicts pronounced. Under him serve six junior scribae, four messengers (viatores), and the recorder of the Album Decurionum. He receives no curule honour, but holds the salaried rank of apparitor primae classis at sixteen hundred sesterces per annum.
