Liber Secundus · Of the Six Schools

Faculties &
Schools

Six learned schools compose the body of the University, each presided over by a Dean and bound to the others by the common discipline of the seven liberal arts. The Triennium prepares the citizen of letters; the Quadriennium Maius forms the magister and the philosopher.

Tres Anni · The Three Foundational Years
Pars Prima

Schools of the Lower Course

Three schools admit candidates to the Triennium. Each requires of the student a written disputation in Latin, an oral examination upon the canon, and the formal pledge taken before the Praetor of Studies.

Schola I

Classical Letters

Dean
Prof. Livia Cornelia Acuti

Reading begins with Homer in the original Ionic and proceeds through Virgil, Cicero, Tacitus, and Augustine. Candidates compose verse in Latin and Greek hexameter, prepare a paleographic edition from the University manuscripts, and defend a thesis upon a single chosen author before the Collegium of Letters in their third year.

Founded MCMVIII72 Fellows
Schola II

Roman & Civil Law

Dean
Prof. Marcus Tullius Vergani

The school inherits the lecture-hall of the Pandects, founded upon the Corpus Iuris Civilis of Justinian and the Twelve Tables. Students argue moots in the reconstructed basilica, study the praetorian edicts, and follow the descent of Roman jurisprudence into the modern codes of Italy, France, and Louisiana. Final disputation is held in open forum.

Founded MCMXII118 Advocates
Schola III

Philosophy

Dean
Prof. Helena Aurelia Ridolfi

Three years of close instruction in the Platonic dialogues, the Nicomachean Ethics, the Stoic fragments of Epictetus, and the meditations of Marcus Aurelius. The school holds a weekly symposium in the Peripatetic colonnade and requires of every Triennium candidate a written commentary upon a single book of the Metaphysics, defended in person before the Praeceptor.

Quattuor Anni · The Greater Course of Study
Pars Secunda

Schools of the Higher Course

Admitted only upon completion of the Triennium or its equivalent abroad, the Quadriennium Maius leads to the magisterial laurel and, in time, to the doctorate conferred under the seal of the Curia.

Schola IV

History of Architecture

Dean
Prof. Quintus Vitruvius Bensa

Founded by direct lineage from the Vitruvian academies, the school treats the De Architectura as living instruction. Students measure the orders in situ at Tivoli and Ostia, draft by hand the elevations of the Pantheon and the Maison Carrée, and in their fourth year prepare a full design — pediment, entablature, plan — for the public examination held in the Aula Magna.

Founded MCMXXII54 Magistri
Schola V

Pure Mathematics

Dean
Prof. Aulus Sergius Maffei

Beginning with the thirteen books of Euclid in the Heiberg edition and continuing through Archimedes, Apollonius, and the Diophantine arithmetic, the school cultivates demonstration in the geometric manner. Modern analysis enters by way of Cauchy and Weierstrass; algebraic structures are studied under Bourbaki. Final examination requires an original theorem.

Founded MCMXVI43 Magistri
Schola VI

Astronomy & Natural Philosophy

Dean
Prof. Octavia Manlia Cassiani

From the Almagest of Ptolemy to the celestial mechanics of Laplace, the school joins the astronomical tradition to modern physics. Observations are conducted from the rooftop meridian of the Specola Aquilana, founded MCMXXXI; theoretical seminars treat Newtonian gravitation, Maxwellian fields, and the Aristotelian doctrine of natural place. Thesis required.
