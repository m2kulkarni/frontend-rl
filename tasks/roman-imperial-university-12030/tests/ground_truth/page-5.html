Four institutes of the Universitas Aquilana sustain a continuous tradition of inquiry into antiquity, law, the heavens, and the architecture of the Mediterranean world. Litterae nostrae per saecula florent.

Since its founding in MCMXII, the Universitas Aquilana has organised its scholarly life around enduring institutes — communities of philologists, jurists, astronomers and architectural historians whose work proceeds at the slow, considered pace proper to the disciplines of antiquity. In the academic year 2024–2025, the four institutes collectively administer 47 active research projects, edit eleven peer-reviewed series, and host visiting fellows from twenty-three nations. The figures below do not tell the whole story, yet they sketch the lineaments of a working scholarly community.

The Four Institutes

Quattuor Instituta Universitatis

Institutum Epigraphicum

Founded MCMXX · Latin Inscriptions

Heir to the great epigraphic tradition of the central Apennines, the Institute maintains the standing edition of the Inscriptiones Italiae Centralis and a digital concordance of more than 38,000 published lapidary texts from Latium, Samnium and Picenum.

Director Prof. Lucia Vespasiana leads a permanent staff of nine and rotating cohorts of eighteen doctoral candidates engaged in autopsy fieldwork at sites from Sulmo to Alba Fucens.

Centrum Iuris Romani

Founded MCMXXVII · Comparative Roman Law

The Centrum is among the most consulted seats in Europe for the comparative study of Roman private law and its survival in the modern ius commune. Faculty maintain the running commentary on Justinian's Digest now in its forty-third bound volume.

Director Prof. Tiberio Cassano coordinates collaborative seminars with the universities of Salamanca, Heidelberg and Coimbra, and supervises the Centrum's biannual journal Acta Iuridica Aquilana.

Observatorium Aquilanum

Founded MCMXXXIV · Astronomy

Established in 1934 on the slopes of Monte Cefalone, the Observatory operates a 1.2-metre Cassegrain reflector and a permanent meridian circle. Its long photometric record of variable stars, unbroken save during the war years, is among the longest in continental Europe.

Director Prof. Camilla Orsini leads the spectroscopic programme on Cepheid variables and supervises the Observatory's contribution to the European Time-Domain Survey.

Officina Architecturae

Workshop of Architectural History

Reconstituted in 1978 from the older Cabinet of Antiquities, the Workshop investigates the building cultures of the Italic peninsula from the third century BC through the Carolingian renewal. Drawings produced here have furnished the standard reference plates for six current handbooks.

Director Prof. Marco di Lauro directs the survey programme of imperial public bath complexes and the ongoing photogrammetric campaign at Alba Fucens.

Featured Project · Officina Architecturae

Corpus Picturarum Pompeianarum

A Digital Corpus of Pompeian Wall-Painting
led by Prof. Aurelio di Sangro

Begun in October 2023 and projected to run through August 2027, the Corpus Picturarum Pompeianarum is the most ambitious documentary undertaking ever mounted by the Officina Architecturae. The project will produce a fully calibrated, multispectral and stratigraphic record of every accessible painted surface in Regiones I, V and IX of the buried city — comprising an estimated 14,800 square metres of decorated wall.

Field campaigns are conducted in collaboration with the Parco Archeologico di Pompei under a formal convention signed in March 2024. The published corpus will appear in twelve folio volumes and as an open-access digital edition, mirroring the structure of the institute's earlier Tabulae Romanae series.

The project is sustained by a €2.4 million grant from the Consilium Europaeum Investigationis (CEI/Horizon-Antiquitas, contract no. 101-2023-87) awarded in September 2023, with supplementary support from the Soprintendenza Speciale and the Fondazione Aquilana.

Project II · Institutum Epigraphicum

Tabulae Sulmonenses

Dr. Flavia Mancini, Principal Investigator

A four-year programme to produce the first comprehensive critical edition of the bronze magistrate-tablets recovered from the territory of Sulmo between 1898 and 2019, comprising 217 fragments now divided between Sulmona, the Museo Nazionale d'Abruzzo, and the Vatican collections.

Each tablet is being recorded by reflectance transformation imaging at the Officina's photogrammetric station and collated against archival drawings. Funded by the Ministero della Cultura (€420,000) with co-funding from the Fondazione Aquilana.

Project III · Observatorium Aquilanum

Apenninus Time-Domain Survey

Prof. Camilla Orsini & Dr. Hadrian Petraeus

Inaugurated in January 2023, the survey is the Observatory's contribution to the European pan-spectral monitoring consortium. Each clear night, the 1.2-metre Cassegrain reflector at Monte Cefalone records a fixed set of 312 fields covering 84 square degrees of the northern sky, with photometric precision sufficient to detect Cepheid micro-variability.

The campaign has already enabled three peer-reviewed publications and one short-period eclipsing binary catalogue (Petraeus et al., 2024). Funded by the Italian Astronomical Society and ESO infrastructure programme.
