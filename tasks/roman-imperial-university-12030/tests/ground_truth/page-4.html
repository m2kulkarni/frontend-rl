The Collections

— Custodia librorum, ordo et lumen —

Reserva Aurelia

The university's celebrated collection of editiones principes — the first printed editions of the Roman canon. Cicero's De Officiis in the 1465 Subiaco printing; Tacitus from Vindelinus de Spira, Venice 1470; Vitruvius's De Architectura from Sulpicius, Rome 1486.

Access is granted by signed petition to the Head Librarian. Volumes are consulted only within the Sala Aurelia under the supervision of a custos.

Codices Manuscripti

Three hundred and eleven manuscripts ranging from the ninth to the seventeenth century: Carolingian gospel-fragments, glossed legal codices from Bologna, and the celebrated Codex Aquilanus — a tenth-century miscellany of Boethius and Macrobius.

The manuscript catalogue (Catalogus Codicum Aquilanus, ed. Marenco-Severi, 2019) is held at the entry desk of the Sala Manuscriptorum.

Architectonica

A dedicated cabinet of architectural folios and treatises — Alberti's De Re Aedificatoria, Palladio, Vignola, and the recent acquisition of Sebastiano Serlio's Tutte l'Opere d'Architettura, Venice 1551, in original calf binding.

Drawings, plates, and modern reproductions are held in the climate-controlled Cubiculum Vitruvii on the third floor.

Manuscript Request Procedure
  1. Address a written petition (petitio consultandi) to the Head Librarian, citing the shelfmark from the Catalogus.
  2. Submit a letter of standing from a recognised faculty or institution, no older than twelve months.
  3. Await confirmation by post or signed electronic notice; allow seven working days.
  4. Present the confirmation, photographic identification, and a clean cotton apparatus on the day of consultation.
  5. All notes must be taken in pencil. Photography is permitted only with the seal of the Custos Codicum.
  6. Volumes are returned to the desk before the eighteenth hour for cataloguing and inspection.

— Recent Acquisition · Anno MMXXIV —

The Serlio
Folio of 1551

In the autumn of last year the Bibliotheca received, by gift of the Marenco family, a complete copy of Sebastiano Serlio's Tutte l'Opere d'Architettura et Prospetiva, printed at Venice in 1551 by Francesco Marcolini. The volume is bound in original tooled calf with brass clasps, the gilt of the spine still legible: SERLIO · ARCHITETTVRA.

All seven books are present, including the rarely-surviving Libro Estraordinario with its thirty plates of grotesque portals. Three folios bear marginal annotations in a sixteenth-century hand, identified by Dottoressa Marenco-Severi as those of the Aquilan stone-mason Pietro di Calvi, who is recorded in the cathedral fabric-rolls of 1568.

The folio is now on permanent display in the Cubiculum Vitruvii, third floor, and may be consulted by appointment for graduate research in architectural history.
