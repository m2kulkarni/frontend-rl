Admissiones
& Scholarum Pretia

The path to enrolment at the Universitas proceeds by three measured steps, in the manner of the Roman cursus: a written examination, a dossier laid before the College of Lectors, and a viva voce held in the Sala dei Padri Antichi.

Examina · Dies VIII Martii · MMXXV

Tres Gradus

The Three Steps of Admission
Gradus Primus

Examen Scriptum

A written examination in Latin composition, or a modern equivalent paper in French, German, or Italian. Candidates are seated in the Aula Maxima and given four hours to compose a discursive essay upon a classical proposition drawn by lot.

XV Februarii MMXXV
Gradus Secundus

Recensio Dossier

The College of Lectors reviews each candidate's portfolio: prior transcripts, two sealed letters of commendatio, a writing specimen of no fewer than three thousand words, and a brief autobiographical statement upon the candidate's intellectual formation.

XX Februarii — VI Martii
Gradus Tertius

Viva Voce

The final disputation is held in the Sala dei Padri Antichi before three professors of the candidate's chosen faculty. The viva lasts forty minutes and addresses the writing specimen, the examination paper, and a single question drawn from the chosen course of study.

VIII · Octo Beneficia

The Trajan Scholarships

Eight Trajan Scholarships are conferred annually, each covering the full tuition of one student drawn from beyond the Italian peninsula. Awards are made on the joint judgement of the Praefectus Admissionum and the Council of Deans, weighed upon merit shown in the written examination and demonstrable need. Notification follows the viva voce by no later than the Kalends of May.

De Examine

On the Structure of the Entrance Examination

The entrance examination of the Universitas Aquilana is a single sitting of four hours, conducted in the Aula Maxima under the eye of the Praefectus Examinum. Candidates may compose in classical Latin or, by election declared at registration, in one of three modern tongues: Italian, French, or German. No paper is admitted in any other language without the prior dispensation of the Senate.

The examination opens with the drawing of a sealed proposition from a bronze urn — by tradition the same urn that has stood in the Aula since the founding year of MCMXIV. Three propositions are drawn; the candidate elects one and composes a sustained essay of between two thousand and four thousand words, defending a thesis with reference to at least three authorities of the candidate's choosing.

Papers are read blind by two Lectors and, where their judgements diverge by more than a single grade, adjudicated by a third. Marks are not disclosed, but the substance of the reading is conveyed in the viva voce of the eighth of March.

Partes Examinis

  1. Propositio Drawing of three sealed propositions; election of one. (15 minutes)
  2. Compositio Sustained discursive essay of 2,000–4,000 words. (3 hours)
  3. Lectio Brevis Translation of a short passage from a classical author, drawn by the Praefectus on the morning of the examination. (30 minutes)
  4. Quaestio Logica One short proof or syllogistic exercise, for candidates of the Schola Mathematica. (15 minutes)
