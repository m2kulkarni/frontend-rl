Veritas · Disciplina · Memoria

Founded
MCMXII

A modern classical university dedicated to the study of antiquity, philosophy, law, and the mathematical sciences in the Roman tradition — where the lecture hall, the cloister, and the forum still meet.

Tres Facultates

The Three Faculties

Facultas I

Litterae Humanae

Letters & the Human Studies

The reading of Latin and Greek authors, the composition of prose and verse, the history of the Mediterranean, archaeology, and the philology of the ancient and patristic worlds. Seminars are conducted in the Cloister of Saint Augustine; weekly recitation is required of every undergraduate.

Dean · Prof. M. Aurelius Verro
Facultas II

Ius et Philosophia

Law & Philosophy

From the Twelve Tables to the Justinianic Code, and from Heraclitus to the schoolmen — the faculty trains jurists and metaphysicians alike. Disputations are held publicly each Friday in the Hall of the Rostra, where the student must defend a thesis viva voce before the masters.

Dean · Prof. L. Domitia Crispina
Facultas III

Scientiae Mathematicae

The Mathematical Sciences

Geometry after Euclid and Apollonius, astronomy after Ptolemy and Kepler, and the modern apparatus of analysis, mechanics, and number theory. The Observatory of the Aventine is open nightly to the senior cohort, and the calculating room admits visiting fellows by appointment.

Dean · Prof. C. Severus Gallo
“ Non scholae sed vitae discimus ” — inscribed above the great door of the Atrium Minervae
Address from the Curia

From the Rector Magnificus

Rector Magnificus · MMXXIII–MMXXVIII

Dr. Helena Cassini-Auriga

D.Phil. (Oxon.), Professor Ordinarius of Roman Law

Welcome, postulant, scholar, and friend, to the one hundred and thirteenth academic year of our foundation. To enter Universitas Aquilana is to accept a discipline older than any nation now living: to read the texts as the ancients wrote them, to argue as the Forum argued, and to measure the heavens with the instruments of geometry and patience. We are, by our charter, neither monastery nor academy of fashion — we are a school of memory, where what was thought well once is held to be worth thinking again.

This Michaelmas we admit two hundred and forty new auditors across our three faculties. They will share the colonnades of the Aventine campus with one thousand four hundred enrolled students, ninety-two tenured masters, and the largest collection of pre-1600 manuscripts in private hands south of the Alps. The cost of attendance — €11,400 per annum for citizens of the Union, €17,200 for guests from elsewhere — has been held constant for a third successive year by order of the Curia, and forty-four scholarships of full remission remain open at the time of this writing.

I urge those who feel the call to read Cicero in the original, or to derive Kepler's second law on a slate, to set down their hands and apply. The gates of the Atrium Minervae are open from the third hour after sunrise.

H. Cassini-Auriga Rector Magnificus, written from the Curia, Kalends of November
