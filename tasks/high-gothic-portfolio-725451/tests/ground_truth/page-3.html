Commissions & Completed Works

A catalogue of twelve panels and ensembles delivered from this workshop between the years two thousand and seventeen and two thousand and twenty‑three, each entry recorded in the manner of the cathedral fabric‑books: title, place, year, dimension, and a brief account of the iconographic programme.
I
The Tree of Jesse
North Transept · Saint‑Pierre de Beauvais
Anno MMXIX · 4·2 × 1·8 m

Eleven crowned ancestors of David rise upon a single curling vine of madder against cobalt ground. The lunette above is closed by the dove of the Holy Spirit set in a quatrefoil of gold pot‑metal.

II
Three Lancets of the Virgin's Childhood
Private Chapel · Domaine de Loches
Anno MMXXII · 2·6 × 0·7 m, each

Anne teaching the child Mary to read; the Presentation in the Temple; the Marriage at the Golden Gate. Each lancet carries a grisaille border of vine‑leaf and acorn after a fragment recovered at Saint‑Urbain de Troyes.

III
The Martyrdom of Saint Eustace
South Aisle · Église Saint‑Eustache de Rambouillet
Anno MMXVIII · 3·1 × 1·1 m

A medallion programme of nine scenes in cobalt and ruby, ranged in two columns of quatrefoils after the Chartres model. The crowning oculus shows the brazen bull within a circle of gold flames.

IV
Liberal Arts Window
Bibliothèque Municipale · Hôtel de Ville d'Orléans
Anno MMXX · 2·4 × 2·4 m, square

The seven Liberal Arts personified as crowned women, set in roundels around a central figure of Philosophia. A secular commission, but treated in the iconographic register of the cathedral schools.

V
The Crucifixion with Mary & John
Chevet · Abbaye Saint‑Wandrille‑Rançon
Anno MMXXIII · 3·8 × 1·4 m

The Crucified is shown alive and crowned, after the Romanesque convention preserved at Le Mans. The grieving figures wear robes of madder and ultramarine; the suppedaneum is golden, the ground a deep cobalt night.

VI
The Labours of the Months
Refectory · Prieuré de Saint‑Cosme, La Riche
Anno MMXXI · 1·9 × 0·9 m, twelve panels

Twelve small medallions arranged in a single calendar band: pruning vines, sowing grain, the slaughter of the pig, the warming hearth. Each labour is set against a clear quarry of grisaille leafwork.

VII
The Annunciation, Lily & Lectern
Lady Chapel · Cathédrale Notre‑Dame de Sées
Anno MMXVII · 2·2 × 0·8 m

Gabriel kneels at left, the Virgin reads at her lectern at right; between them rises a stem of three lilies in gold pot‑metal. The dais beneath is paved in a quatrefoil‑in‑square diaper, painted in trace‑line and silver stain.

VIII
Rose of the Apocalypse
West Façade · Église Saint‑Julien de Pithiviers
Anno MMXXII · 3·6 m, diameter

The Lamb at the centre, encircled by the four creatures and the four‑and‑twenty elders crowned with gold. The outermost ring carries angels with censers, executed in deep cobalt and madder against a parchment‑white field.

IX
Heraldic Lights for the House of Cherbon
Manoir de la Bourdaisière · Indre‑et‑Loire
Anno MMXIX · 1·4 × 0·6 m, four panels

Four shields blazoned in pot‑metal — gules, azure, or, and argent — each surmounted by a helm and supported by a pair of vine‑leaf consoles. A domestic commission, set into the stair‑hall windows of the south range.

X
Saint Martin Dividing his Cloak
North Aisle · Basilique Saint‑Martin de Tours
Anno MMXX · 2·9 × 1·0 m

The young soldier on horseback parts his red mantle for the beggar at the city gate of Amiens. Three medallions stacked vertically: the gift, the dream of Christ in the cloak, and the vesting of the bishop.

XI
The Good Samaritan & the Fall of Adam
Ambulatory · Collégiale Notre‑Dame de Mantes‑la‑Jolie
Anno MMXXIII · 4·6 × 1·6 m

A typological window in seven registers, pairing the parable of the wounded traveller with the expulsion from Eden, after the celebrated thirteenth‑century programme at Bourges and Sens. The borders carry castle and tower in gold.

XII
Notre‑Dame de la Belle Verrière (after the model)
Oratoire des Sœurs · Saint‑Loup‑de‑Naud
Anno MMXXI · 3·2 × 1·2 m

A faithful reduction of the great twelfth‑century Virgin of Chartres, made at the request of the community after the loss of their nineteenth‑century glass to a tempest. Cobalt of the old recipe; the throne in gold and madder.

Of the Marks Below the Arch

Each panel in this catalogue is sealed in its lower spandrel by a quatrefoil — the customary mark of the workshop of Sancerre.

Quatrefoil of Gold — Consecrated Work Plain Quatrefoil — Secular or Domestic

Enquiries concerning new commissions, copies, or the restoration of existing ensembles are received at the workshop in Chartres at any season of the year save the fortnight of the feast of the Assumption.
