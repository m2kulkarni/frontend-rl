Conservancy of the Old Glass

These pages set apart, from new commissions, the works of repair — the cathedral medallions and seigneurial quarries entrusted to the atelier for stabilisation, re-leading, and continued care. Each intervention is undertaken under the principle of minimum intervention, that the hand of the thirteenth-century glazier remain legible to the thirteenth century after our own.

Workshop accredited · Direction Régionale des Affaires Culturelles · Centre-Val de Loire · No. 2014/MH-VV/0317

Of Minimum Intervention

Nothing is undone that has held; nothing is renewed that the panel itself does not require to remain a panel. The old lead is preserved where its strap still bears the glass; the old glass is preserved even where it has clouded, for the cloud is itself five centuries of weather. Where new glass is admitted, it is matched in pot-metal and thickness, and is set apart by a discreet engraved date in the came so that no future hand mistakes our work for the work of the medieval glazier.

Three Works under our Care

Sainte-Radegonde · Poitiers

Three thirteenth-century medallions of the apsidal chapel — the Vie de Sainte Radegonde — were assessed in March of 2017. The leading was found exhausted, with strap-fatigue along the upper trefoils and ten cracked quarries held only by a century-old copper-foil repair of indifferent quality. Eight original heads survived intact; the cobalt grounds were sound.

Interventions

  • Full re-leading in 6mm and 9mm cames cast to the medieval profile.
  • Edge-bonding of fourteen fractures with conservation-grade silicone resin.
  • Three small lacunae filled with new mouth-blown pot-metal, dated 2017 in the came.
  • Removal of 1903 cement infills incompatible with the surrounding glass.

Saint-Étienne · Auxerre

The Arbre de Jessé of the north ambulatory was found in serious mechanical distress in the autumn of 2019: the central panel had bowed nearly forty millimetres from the plane, and Jesse's recumbent figure was held only by the residual strength of three saddle-bars. The campaign of 2020 was therefore one of stabilisation rather than restoration.

Interventions

  • Removal in toto, panel by panel, with full photogrammetric record.
  • Installation of new stainless saddle-bars and copper tie-wires.
  • Edge-bonding of forty-one fractures; no replacement glass introduced.
  • Re-installation behind a ventilated isothermal protective glazing.

Château de Sully

A private collection of fourteenth-century grisaille quarries — armorial, foliate, and one bestiary panel showing a hare and lurcher — has been in the atelier's continuing care since 2015. The panels were dispersed during the Revolution and reunited by the present family in 1872; their leading dates from that nineteenth-century re-assembly and is now itself of considerable historical interest.

Interventions, Ongoing

  • Biennial inspection and dust-removal in atelier; September visits.
  • Selective edge-bonding as small fractures appear; no re-leading undertaken.
  • Replacement of three failed saddle-bars (2018, 2021, 2023).
  • Climate monitoring of the south gallery on behalf of the proprietors.

Accreditation & Habilitation

The Atelier de Sancerre is accredited by the Direction Régionale des Affaires Culturelles for intervention upon stained glass classified Monument Historique, under registration No. 2014/MH-VV/0317. The workshop maintains the qualifications Qualibat 7142 (vitrail d'art, niveau confirmé) and MPF 2019-V of the Groupement des Entreprises de Restauration des Monuments Historiques, and is a member of the Corpus Vitrearum — France.

All campaigns are conducted under the supervision of the architecte en chef des Monuments Historiques of the relevant circumscription, with documentary record deposited at the Médiathèque du Patrimoine et de la Photographie, Charenton-le-Pont.
