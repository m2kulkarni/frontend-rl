Of the Master & His Schooling

Guillaume de Sancerre, born at Bourges in the year 1981, came to the glazier's bench by the longer road. From 1999 until 2006 he served as apprentice and journeyman under the maître-verrier Étienne Deschamps at the Manufacture de Vitraux de Chartres, where the old programme was still kept: two years at the cartoon-table before the cutter was put into the hand, two more at the painter's easel grinding bistre and silver-nitrate, and a final pair tied to the leading-bench, learning the came as a builder learns mortar. Of those seven winters Deschamps gave him only one praise that he remembers — tu coupes enfin comme un homme qui écoute le verre — and that, the master writes, is the one he keeps.

From 2007 to 2011 he was apprenticed in restoration at the Atelier du Vitrail of the Cathédrale de Reims, under Sylvain Pradier, working chiefly upon the thirteenth-century clerestory of the choir and the so-called Verrière des Vignerons. There he learned the slow grammar of conservation: the cold-mending of fractured pot-metal with epoxy of refractive index 1.52, the differential cleaning of corrosion crusts, and the cardinal rule that every intervention must be reversible by the next century's hand.

The atelier was founded in 2013 in the rue au Lin, three streets from the south porch of Notre-Dame de Chartres, in a former cooper's hall whose north light has not been rebuilt since 1842. There the master keeps the bench, the cartoon-loft, and a kiln of his own building — a brick muffle of 1.4 cubic metres, fired with bottled propane to 645° C, after the manner of the old wood-fired kilns but cleaner in its thermal curve.

Six hands work beside him. Each is a craftsman of one trade only; no hand at this bench cuts and paints, none paints and leads. The division is medieval and it is intentional. A single panel of a metre square will pass through every one of the six before it is glazed into its iron armature, and it will bear the tool-marks of each.

The Workshop's Credo

Three things this bench will not do, and one it will not omit. So it has stood since the founding, and so it shall stand.

  1. No machine-rolled glass. Every pane that leaves this atelier is mouth-blown cylinder or crown glass, drawn from the houses of Saint-Just-sur-Loire and Lamberts of Waldsassen. The hand of the blower must be in the glass before the hand of the painter is laid upon it.
  2. No copper foil. The Tiffany method, however ingenious, is not of this tradition. Every join is leaded came of milled or cast lead, soldered with a 60/40 alloy, cemented with linseed-and-whiting putty as Theophilus prescribed in the twelfth century.
  3. No shortcut through the cartoon. The full-scale drawing — the vidimus in small, the carton at the size — is made by hand, in charcoal and red chalk on kraft paper, and approved by the patron in the presence of the master before a single piece of glass is scored.
  4. And: the panel must be signable. If a hand at this bench cannot put his mark to a piece without shame, that piece is broken at the bench and begun again. There has been one such breaking in the past three years.

— Visitors are received at the rue au Lin on Tuesday and Thursday afternoons by appointment; the bench is otherwise kept in silence.
