About the Museum
History & Foundation

Founded under the auspices of the Comune di Roma to safeguard the marbles, mosaics, and inscriptions recovered along the Via dei Fori Imperiali, the Antiquarium Traianum has, for more than nine decades, kept watch over the civic memory of imperial Rome.

Established MCMXXXII · Reopened MCMLI

A Chronicle in Three Acts

— Acta Musei, MCMXXXII–MCMLI —

MCMXXXII · 1932

Foundation

By deliberation of the Comune di Roma, dated the fourth of March 1932, the Antiquarium Traianum was instituted to receive the increasing yield of marbles unearthed during the construction of the Via dell'Impero. The first director, Carlo Anti, professor of archaeology at the University of Padua, oversaw the consignment of three hundred and forty objects from the storerooms of the Forum of Trajan to the new civic galleries on the Via Quattro Novembre.

MCMXLIII–MCMXLV · 1943–45

Wartime Evacuation

Following the bombardment of San Lorenzo in July 1943, the Soprintendenza ordered the immediate transfer of the museum's principal holdings — the porphyry tetrarchs, the Trajanic relief fragments, and the Aurelian inscription — to the casemates of Castel Sant'Angelo. Crates were sealed under the supervision of curator Lavinia Castelli. Not a single object was lost; the keys were returned to the museum on the eighth of June 1945.

MCMLI · 1951

Postwar Reopening

On the twenty-first of April 1951 — the traditional Natalis Romae — the Antiquarium reopened to the public after six years of closure and reinstallation. The architect Mario Ridolfi designed the new travertine vestibule and the rotunda for the colossal head of Trajan. Mayor Salvatore Rebecchini delivered the inaugural address to a gathering of nine hundred citizens, scholars, and pontifical delegates.

From the Founding Charter, IV March MCMXXXII

Ut Memoria Imperii Custodiatur “That the memory of the Imperium be kept, and the marbles of the Forum, broken though they are, may yet speak with one voice to the city and to the ages — this museum is established by the People and Senate of Rome.”
— Deliberation of the Consiglio Comunale, signed by Mayor Francesco Boncompagni Ludovisi and Director Carlo Anti

The Director

AF

Dr. Aurelia Fabrini

Direttrice del Museo, since 2019

Dr. Fabrini, formerly Vice-Soprintendente of the Capitoline Museums and a specialist in Trajanic state relief, succeeded Prof. Enzo Marcellino in the autumn of 2019. Under her direction the museum has undertaken the digitisation of the inscription archive, the conservation of the Aula del Trionfo mosaics, and a renewed programme of public scholarship in collaboration with the Sapienza Università di Roma.

Mission & Mandate

The Antiquarium operates as an autonomous civic institute under the Assessorato alla Cultura of the Comune di Roma. Its statute charges it with the conservation, study, and exhibition of imperial-period material drawn from the Imperial Fora, and with the publication of the annual Acta Antiquarii Traiani.

The museum is open to scholars by appointment in the storeroom and reading-room on the lower level, and admits the public Tuesday through Sunday. Admission is free on the first Sunday of each month, by long-standing concession of the Sindaco.
