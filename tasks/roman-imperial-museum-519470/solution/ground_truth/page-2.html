Galleries I — IV · Catalogus Permanens

Permanent Collection

Twelve principal works, drawn from the strata of the Trajanic and Antonine forums, document the public face of imperial Rome — its rulers in armour, its citizens in stone, its gods underfoot in tessera. The catalogue below records each object as it appears in the galleries, arranged by acquisition number AT-101 through AT-212.

Sculpture · Mosaic · Inscription

Galleries I through IV — opened to the public 14 March 1962, reordered 2019.

Inv. AT-114

Cuirassed Torso of Trajan

Pentelic marble · c. 108 CE

Recovered in 1932 from the southern exedra of the Forum of Trajan, the torso preserves a relief programme of paired Victories flanking a central gorgoneion — a standard imperial trope here rendered with unusual attention to the lorica's leather pteryges. The head and lower limbs are lost; the figure stood approximately 2.4 m at completion.

Provenance Forum Traiani, southern exedra Dimensions H. 138 cm · W. 84 cm Acquired 1932, soprintendenza transfer

Inv. AT-127

Mosaic Pavement with Marine Thiasos

Opus tessellatum · Ostia · 2nd c. CE

Lifted in nine sections from a frigidarium adjacent to the Baths of Neptune, this 6.2-metre pavement renders Tritons, hippocamps, and Nereids riding sea-monsters in a tonal palette restricted to four marbles. The tesserae average 8 mm and the central emblema preserves an inscribed signature, FELIX FECIT, in the lower right border.

Provenance Ostia Antica, Reg. II Ins. IV Dimensions 6.20 × 4.05 m Acquired 1968, on loan from Ostia

Inv. AT-131

Funerary Altar of Lucius Aelius Niger

Travertine · 96 CE

A square altar with garlanded bucrania on three faces and a tabula ansata bearing the dedication of a freedman of the Aelian gens, set up by his wife Cornelia Salvia and three named children. The opening line — D · M · L · AELIO · NIGRO — preserves the early Flavian-Trajanic transitional letter forms, with strongly serifed apices.

Provenance Via Latina, tomb XII Dimensions H. 92 cm · W. 64 cm Acquired 1908, Boncompagni bequest

Inv. AT-142

Portrait Head of Plotina

Lunense marble · c. 112 CE

An over-life-size head of the empress wearing the high diadem-fronted coiffure introduced during her husband's Dacian triumph. The drilling of the upper hair-mass is restrained compared to later Hadrianic practice, and the brow's slight asymmetry suggests the piece may have been a workshop replica rather than a court original.

Provenance Forum Augustum, north portico Dimensions H. 41 cm Acquired 1954, excavation deposit

Inv. AT-156

Relief Panel with Sacrificial Procession

White marble · c. 138–161 CE

Six togate figures advance toward an unseen altar at the right; the central celebrant lifts a patera, while two camilli bear an acerra and a ewer. The composition recalls the inner frieze of the Ara Pacis without its Augustan crowding, suggesting a public monument of the early Antonine years, perhaps the precinct of Faustina the Elder.

Provenance Forum Romanum, west drain Dimensions H. 78 cm · L. 184 cm Acquired 1899, state purchase

Inv. AT-163

Statuette of Mercury Travelling

Bronze, silver inlay · c. 150 CE

A finely cast lar-shrine bronze, 28 cm in height, depicts the god in mid-stride, caduceus in left hand, marsupium in right; the petasos and winged sandals retain traces of silver inlay. Recovered with two companion lares in a domestic shrine on the Caelian, it stands as one of the museum's most complete household assemblages.

Provenance Caelian, Domus dei Valerii Dimensions H. 28 cm Acquired 1976, scavi municipali

Inv. AT-178

Fragment of the Acta Fratrum Arvalium

Marble inscription · 87 CE

Eleven lines of a Domitianic protocol of the Arval Brethren, recording the vows undertaken pro salute imperatoris on 3 January. The stone was reused in late antiquity as paving in the atrium of San Cesareo de Appia, from which it was extracted in 1923. The dating consul-pair is fully preserved.

Provenance San Cesareo de Appia (reused) Dimensions H. 56 cm · W. 71 cm Acquired 1923, ecclesial deposit

Inv. AT-184

Sarcophagus Lid with Reclining Couple

Proconnesian marble · c. 230 CE

The lid alone survives of what was once a kline-type sarcophagus of the Severan period: a married couple reclines on a banqueting couch, the wife's right hand resting on her husband's shoulder. Her hairstyle — braided into a flat nest at the nape — corresponds closely to portraits of Julia Mamaea, securing the date.

Provenance Via Appia, fifth mile Dimensions L. 218 cm · H. 64 cm Acquired 1888, Torlonia exchange

Inv. AT-191

Capital from the Basilica Ulpia

Pavonazzetto marble · c. 113 CE

A Composite capital of the inner colonnade, preserving three of its four corner volutes and the full crown of acanthus mollis. The deep undercutting and crisp scotia mark the high standard of the workshops dispatched to the Basilica from the imperial quarries; surface tooling indicates final dressing on site.

Provenance Basilica Ulpia, north aisle Dimensions H. 96 cm · W. 124 cm Acquired 1934, Mussolini scavi

Inv. AT-198

Stele of the Cohors III Praetoria

Limestone · 162 CE

A military funerary stele bearing the relief portrait, in shallow niche, of Marcus Antonius Sabinus, optio of the third praetorian cohort, dead at twenty-eight after seven years' service. The inscription records his origo as Aquileia and is dated by reference to the consuls of 162; the workmanship is provincial-trained but accomplished.

Provenance Castra Praetoria, exterior Dimensions H. 162 cm · W. 58 cm Acquired 1911, ministerial deposit

Inv. AT-205

Mosaic Threshold with Guard-Dog

Black-and-white tessellatum · 1st c. CE

From the vestibule of a Pompeian-type domus on the Esquiline, a chained mastiff bares its teeth above the legend CAVE CANEM. The composition, taken up almost word for word in Petronius, exhibits the schematic vigour typical of Flavian black-and-white pavements; the border of crenellated meander frames the panel without crowding it.

Provenance Esquiline, Domus Aureliana Dimensions 1.40 × 0.95 m Acquired 1947, post-war recovery

Inv. AT-212

Votive Hand of Sabazius

Bronze · late 2nd c. CE

A right hand cast in the benedictio latina, encrusted with the symbolic apparatus of the Phrygian god: a snake coiling the wrist, a tortoise and frog at the base, a pine-cone at the thumb's tip, and a small reclining figure of the deity in the palm. Such hands, suspended in domestic shrines, attest the wide diffusion of mystery cults under the Antonines.

Provenance Aventine, mithraeum sub. Dimensions H. 22 cm Acquired 1981, antiquariat purchase

Gallery V · Numismatica

The Coinage of Empire

A study cabinet of forty-seven specimens traces the imperial portrait from the principate of Nerva to the Tetrarchy. Six representative pieces are displayed in the standing cases; the remainder may be consulted by appointment in the print-room on Fridays.

Aureus of Nerva

Rome · 97 CE · 7.32 g

Laureate head right; reverse, Concordia seated holding patera and cornucopia. A scarce issue of the second consulship, struck in the months following the adoption of Trajan. Slight die-shift at the obverse rim; otherwise lustrous fields.

IMP · NERVA · CAES · AVG · P · M

Sestertius of Trajan

Rome · 104 CE · 25.91 g

Reverse, the harbour at Centumcellae viewed in raised aerial perspective — six ships ride at anchor between the curving moles. A celebrated and uncommonly architectural reverse type, here preserved with full legend and brown patina.

PORTVM · TRAIANI

Denarius of Hadrian

Rome · 134 CE · 3.18 g

Travel series; reverse, AEGYPTOS reclining with sistrum and ibis. From the second tour of the empire (130–134), this issue is one of the most legible specimens in the cabinet — the sistrum's three rattles individually distinguishable.

HADRIANVS · AVG · COS · III · P · P

Aureus of Antoninus Pius

Rome · 145 CE · 7.27 g

Reverse, the consecratio pyre of Faustina the Elder, four tiers tall and crowned by a quadriga. The piece commemorates the divinisation of the empress in 141 and reproduces, in miniature, the lost cenotaph of the Campus Martius.

DIVA · FAVSTINA

Antoninianus of Gallienus

Mediolanum · 263 CE · 3.84 g

Radiate bust right; reverse, GERMANICVS · MAX · V, trophy between two captives. The flan is debased and the strike soft, characteristic of the mid-third-century crisis; included as a counterweight to the high-imperial issues above.

GALLIENVS · AVG

Follis of Diocletian

Treveri · 296 CE · 9.55 g

Reverse, GENIO · POPVLI · ROMANI, the Genius standing with patera and cornucopia. The new monetary system of the Tetrarchy, with its broad copper module and standardised Genius reverse, here closes the cabinet's chronological run at the threshold of late antiquity.
