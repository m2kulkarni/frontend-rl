Of the Manner of Commissions

Of Commissions
& Their Manner

Wherein the prospective patron is shown the order & observance by which a work in glass or vellum is brought from first speech to feast-day delivery, with prices set down in livres tournois according to the custom of Soissons.

I · The Five Stages

The Order of a Work

· i ·

The First Interview

The patron is received in the long chamber above the workshop on the rue des Verriers, by appointment sent through the parish clerk of Saint-Gervais. There the maker hears what is wanted — saint, scene, blazon, or text — what wall or chapel shall receive it, and at what light it shall be seen. No pledge is taken at this hour; only earnest counsel given, and a fee of two sous tournois for the parchment of notes.

· ii ·

The Drawing of the Cartoon

Within forty days the cartoon is laid out at full scale upon whitewashed boards in the north studio, that the patron may walk before it as he should before the finished work. The figures are charcoaled, then inked in lampblack; the leadlines numbered; the colours noted in Picard hand at the margin. Master Gautier or Brother Aubert of the scriptorium attends to answer questions of likeness and heraldry.

· iii ·

The Patron's Approval

Approval is given in writing, sealed at the foot of the cartoon-board itself, with a deposit of one third the agreed price. From this hour no figure may be added nor saint exchanged save by a new accord and a second seal. Should the patron repent his choice, the deposit holds the boards in the workshop for one twelvemonth; thereafter they are washed and the timber put to other use.

· iv ·

The Cutting & the Ruling

For glass: the pot-metal is brought from Pierre Lefèvre's furnace at Reims, scored to the cartoon with the hot iron, trimmed at the grozing-pliers, painted with the tracing-black, and fired thrice in the small kiln behind the yew-tree. For books: the calfskin is pumiced, ruled with the leadpoint at twenty-six lines to the page, and the quires gathered before any pigment is set down. A second third of the price falls due at this stage.

· v ·

Delivery upon the Feast Day Agreed

The work is delivered upon the feast day named in the first accord — most often the patronal feast of the chapel that shall house it — wrapped in linen for vellum, or crated in straw and oak between two boards for glass. The final third of the price is rendered upon delivery, in the presence of the parish priest or a notary of the bailliage. A small mass is sung at the workshop on the morning of departure, that the work may travel in good keeping.

Of the Customary Tithe For any work destined to a church, chapel, abbey, or shrine, the workshop renders one tenth part of the agreed price to the parish of Saint-Gervais, where this house was founded and where the bones of Master Renaud, our predecessor, lie before the rood. So has it been kept since the year MCCXIX, and so shall it be kept whilst this atelier endures.

III · Of Feast Days & Delivery

Days Customarily Chosen

II Februarii The Purification For lady-chapel windows and Marian books; candles blessed at delivery.
XXIV Junii Saint John Baptist For baptistery glass and font-side panels; the favoured day of the bishopric.
XV Augusti The Assumption The high summer feast — books bound in red leather are most often delivered now.
XXIX Septembris Michaelmas For accounts settled and rolls illuminated; the workshop's books also closed at this hour.
I Novembris All Saints Apt for memorial windows and books of the dead; candles travel with the work.
XXV Decembris The Nativity Reserved for great commissions; only one work in glass delivered each Christmastide.

To set down a commission, send word by the carrier of Saint-Gervais, addressed to the workshop in the rue des Verriers, sealed and signed by your hand or your priest's. Reply within fifteen days is pledged.

— Gautier de Soissons, master
