The Workshop and Its Hands

An accounting, in his own hand, of the master Gautier de Soissons; of those bound and free who labour beside him; and of the rule by which all work is measured.

Of the Master, His Beginning

In the year of Our Lord one thousand two hundred and fifty-one, upon the eve of the feast of Saint Médard, I, Gautier, son of Aymon the wool-fuller, was born within the walls of Soissons, hard by the abbey-church of Saint-Jean-des-Vignes. My mother set in my hand, before I could grasp it, a fragment of blue glass that had fallen from the workshop next our door; and so the colour cobalt has stood between me and all common sights from that day forth.

At thirteen years, in the autumn of 1264, my father bound me by indenture for seven years to Master Renaud, glazier of Reims, who held his bench within sight of that great cathedral then rising stone by stone. Under his rule I learned to grind the oxide, to score the table, to leaden a quarry, and at last to draw the cartoon. He was a hard man and a just one, and he beat my knuckles with the lathekin when my line wavered. I bless his name nightly.

In the year 1271 I took the rank of journeyman, and walked seven winters between the workshops of Chartres, Beauvais, and Amiens, carrying my satchel of pigments and my small panel of trial-glass. In 1278, by the grace of the Confraternity of Saint Luke and the seal of Master Renaud, I was made free of my apprenticeship and granted leave to set up my own bench. That bench, by the favour of the Chapter of Notre-Dame, I established within the parish of Saint-Pierre-le-Vieux at Soissons, where it stands yet, in the rue des Cordeliers.

I am now in my forty-third year, and the hand has not yet betrayed me. Of what I have learned, the half I owe to Master Renaud; the other half to the saints whose faces I have copied a thousand times, until I came to know their features as those of my own household.

The Hands of This House

Perrin de Laon

Journeyman Glazier · of seven years' service

Perrin came to me in the spring of 1287, freed of his bond at the workshop of Master Aubert of Laon. His hand at the lead-knife is the surest in the house; it is he who sets the came of every great panel before the firing. He keeps the second bench by the south window, and is paid two sous and four deniers the week, with bread and the use of the south room.

Joscelin of Beauvais

Journeyman Glazier · cartoonist of figures

Of all my hands, Joscelin draws the saintly visage with most tenderness; he learned of Master Aimery, whom I knew at Beauvais. He is given charge of all faces, hands, and drapery upon the cartoon, and his wage is the same as Perrin's, with two further deniers in respect of his draughtsmanship. He keeps a small chamber above the parchmenter's room.

Dame Aalis la Brodère

Illuminator · mistress of the gold-leaf

Widow of a goldsmith of Reims, Dame Aalis came to this house in 1284 and has not left it. She lays the leaf upon the bole, burnishes it with the dog's tooth, and there is no finer field of gold within forty leagues. She is paid in silver weekly, takes the table with my own family, and has the keeping of the great press of pigments under lock and key.

Brother Hervé

Illuminator · of historiated initials

Brother Hervé is loaned to us by the abbey of Saint-Médard, four days the week, that he may set the great initial letters and the marginal beasts of our books of hours. He labours in silence, takes neither wage nor bread of us, but a candle is lit before Saint Christopher each Sunday in his name, by the order of his Prior.

Mahaut de Compiègne

Illuminator · of borders and diaper-grounds

Mahaut, daughter of the late Master Eudes, has been three years at our bench. She paints the diaper-grounds in cobalt and madder, the vine-rinceaux of the border, and the rinceau of the bas-de-page. Her wage is one sou the week, and her father's old satchel of brushes is kept for her in the cupboard by the press.

Old Robin

Parchmenter · of the back-yard tannery

Robin came to this craft before I came to my mother's milk. He keeps the lime-pits in the back yard, scrapes the calf-skin and the kid, stretches it upon the herse, and pounces it for our scribes. He says nothing to anyone, but his hand has prepared every leaf of every book that has left this door these eleven years past.
