Liber Operum · Catalogus Codicum

Books & Illuminations

Manuscripts completed in the workshop, in the years of grace MCCXXIV–MCCXLI

Beneath the tall north window of the scriptorium, three desks stand in a row of pointed lecterns, and at each one a journeyman keeps the gathering set before him. Here we record the codices that have left this house — bound, sewn, and given to their patrons — together with the pigments that ran through their pen-knives and brushes, and the names of those whose hands moved the gold. Let the reader know: every leaf here counted was cut from calf or sheep raised within twelve leagues of Soissons, and limed, scraped, and pumiced in the yard behind the chapel of Saint-Crépin.

Psalter for Brother Anselme

Made for the Cistercian House of Vauclair · A.D. MCCXXVIII

Bound in oak boards covered with white tawed leather, this Psalter was set down for Brother Anselme, sub-prior of the abbey of Vauclair, that he might keep the hours through every season of the year. The full one hundred and fifty psalms are written across one hundred and eighty-four leaves of vellum, in a single column of twenty lines per page, with the eight liturgical divisions opened by a historiated initial in burnished gold and lapis. Every psalm without exception begins with a gold-leaf initial laid over a ground of red lead and madder.

Of Pigments

Lapis lazuli ground from a single stone purchased at the fair of Provins, brought thither from Outremer by the merchant Othon de Tyr. Madder lake from the gardens of the Beguines at Laon. Vermilion ground in the workshop from cinnabar of the Almadén road. Gold of Byzantium beaten by Renaud Orfèvre to one-eight-thousandth of an inch.

Hands at the gathering — Quires I–VIII written by Hugues le Jeune; quires IX–XVI by the master himself; gold-laying and burnishing throughout by Aalis la Doreuse, the only woman of our house.

Bestiary of the Schoolroom of Laon

For the Cathedral School of Notre-Dame de Laon · A.D. MCCXXXII

Commissioned by Master Gervais, scholasticus of the cathedral school upon the heights of Laon, that the boys of the choir might learn the natures of the beasts and the signs they bear of the things invisible. The book runs to one hundred and seven leaves, of which forty-two carry full miniatures occupying the upper third of the page: the lion that sleeps with eyes open, the pelican that opens her breast, the unicorn brought low by the maiden, the caladrius that turns its face from the dying.

Of Pigments

Verdigris scraped from copper plates buried in the dung-pile six weeks together. Madder of Picardy from the gardens at Crécy-sur-Serre. Orpiment from the apothecary Master Thibaut at Reims, used sparingly and with great care. Lampblack ground in linseed oil. Lead-white tempered with white of egg and a thread of honey from the abbey of Vauclair.

Hands at the gathering — All script by Perrin de Crépy, journeyman of seven years; the miniatures of the four-footed beasts by the master; the birds and serpents by Jehan Boivin, who keeps still the eastern desk.

Pontifical of the Bishop of Châlons

For His Lordship Pierre de Hans, Bishop of Châlons-en-Champagne · A.D. MCCXXXIX

The greatest book yet to leave this house. A pontifical containing the offices proper to a bishop — the consecration of churches and altars, the blessing of chrism and oils, the conferring of orders from porter through priest, the rite for the coronation of a king should the occasion ever fall. Two hundred and forty-six leaves of the finest uterine vellum, written by the master alone over fourteen months, with sixty-three large historiated initials and four full-page miniatures: the Bishop in cathedra, the consecration of an altar-stone, the ordination of a priest, and the dedication of a church beneath a tall pointed arcade.

Of Pigments

Lapis from Outremer brought up the road from Marseille by the merchant Bernart de Narbonne, three full ounces purchased at twenty sous the dragme — a sum the bishop himself defrayed in advance. Ultramarine of the second washing reserved for the diaper grounds; the first washing kept for the Virgin's mantle in the dedication scene. Madder lake from the gardens of Picardy, deepened with brazilwood from Sainte-Marie-aux-Mines. Vermilion ground in the workshop and bound in glair. Shell-gold burnished with the tooth of a wolf, after the manner of the Greeks.

Hands at the gathering — Script and rubrication entirely by the master, save the canon prayers in quires XXII–XXIV, written by Hugues le Jeune. Miniatures of the consecration and ordination by the master; the diaper grounds and architectural canopies by Perrin de Crépy. Gold-laying by Aalis la Doreuse, fifty-one days at the bench. Burnishing by the apprentice Guiart, son of the carpenter Robert of the Rue des Cordeliers.
