Works in Glass

Lights wrought for choir, chapel, and refectory in the year of Our Lord MCCXC and after

— Liber Vitri · Folio II —

Herein are set down three commissions executed in the painted light, each conceived after the manner of the great cathedral builders, each leaded with English calme and fired in the small kiln above the cloister at Soissons. The cobalt is drawn from the Meuse valley, near Givet, where the cobaltite ore is calcined with sand and potash by Brother Bernard of Dinant; the madder from beds maintained at the priory of Origny; the gold-yellow is silver stain, lately learned from a Picard glazier returning from Chartres.

The Tree of Jesse

North aisle, Collegiate Church of Saint-Yved · Braine · Anno Domini MCCLXXXIX

Set in three lancets above the tomb of the lord Robert of Dreux, the vine springs from the loins of recumbent Jesse and ascends through fourteen crowned kings of Judah to the Virgin enthroned at the apex. The cartoon was drawn upon whitened oak boards in the lower workshop and reduced into eighty-three pieces of pot-metal glass, leaded with English calme of two pieds the rod.

Dimensions
Each lancet: 11 pieds 4 pouces high × 2 pieds 7 pouces wide (du Roi)
Pieces
Two hundred forty-nine, of which forty-six in cobalt
Glaziers' marks
Gautier de Soissons, mag.; Aubert le Verrier
Cobalt
Smalt of Givet, third firing — a deep azure approaching black in the deepest grounds, drawn from the Meuse smelters of Brother Bernard.
Delivered
Feast of Saint Martin, MCCLXXXIX · price LXXX livres parisis

Saint Eligius at the Forge

Single lancet, Chapel of the Goldsmiths' Guild · Noyon · Anno Domini MCCXCI

Commissioned by Master Renaud Boursier, provost of the Confraternity of Saint Eloi at Noyon, this single lancet stands above the eastern altar where the goldsmiths gather upon the kalends of December. The saint is shown at his anvil in the act of striking the chalice of King Clotaire, his tongs holding the metal still ruddy from the coals. The ground is laid in the deep madder reserved for the upper register, with the figure-field in cobalt of Givet.

Dimensions
9 pieds 2 pouces high × 2 pieds 4 pouces wide (du Roi)
Pieces
One hundred eleven, of which the saint's robe alone in seven shades of silver-stain yellow
Glaziers' marks
Gautier de Soissons, mag.; Jehannot d'Orchies, apprentice of the third year
Cobalt
Meuse smalt, second firing — somewhat lighter than that of Braine, suited to the smaller field; bartered at the fair of Provins for one mark of fine silver per livre.
Delivered
Vigil of Saint Eloi, MCCXCI · price XLII livres tournois

The Wheel of Fortune

Oculus, refectory of the Abbey of Longpont · Anno Domini MCCXCIV

Commanded by Dom Hugues de Pierrefonds, abbot of Longpont, to be set in the western wall of the new refectory above the lectern, that the brethren might consider, at every meal, the inconstancy of worldly estate. Four figures turn upon the wheel: Regnabo ascending, Regno crowned at the height, Regnavi cast down, and Sum sine regno ground beneath. The hub is occupied by Lady Fortune herself, blindfolded — though, as it pleases the abbot, the blindfold is rendered here as a band of silver-stain rather than the customary white.

Dimensions
Diameter: 6 pieds 8 pouces (du Roi); set within a stone-traceried oculus of eight foils
Pieces
One hundred ninety-six, all leaded radially from the central hub; the rim alone in twenty-four segments of madder.
Glaziers' marks
Gautier de Soissons, mag.; Pierres li Bouvier, painter of heads; Margot la Plommière, leader
Cobalt
Meuse smalt, first firing — the most costly grade, called azur de Givet supérieur; reserved for the four cardinal grounds because the diurnal light at Longpont, falling steeply westward, would silver any thinner blue.
Delivered
Octave of the Assumption, MCCXCIV · price CXX livres parisis, with twelve sols added for the sworn carter who bore the panels by river from Soissons
Of the cobalt — let it be known that the Meuse valley, between Givet and Dinant, yields an ore which, calcined with potash and fine sand of the Aisne, produces a blue darker by half than that of Saxe; the master prefers it for all grounds in figure-glass, and will accept no commission below twenty livres in which it cannot be used.
