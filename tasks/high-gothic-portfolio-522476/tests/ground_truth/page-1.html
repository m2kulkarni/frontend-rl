Of the Hands now Engaged

Commissions Presently Undertaken

A Cycle of Clerestory Lights
for Our Lady at Senlis

Lady Chapel · seven lancets · begun Pentecost MCCCIV

Commanded by the Chapter of the cathedral church of Senlis, seven tall lancets in the manner of the eastern apse: the Tree of Jesse rising on the dexter side, the Coronation of the Virgin filling the centre, and the Five Joys disposed at the sinister. The cartoons have been laid down upon the whitened table, and the master glazier Jehan le Vert prepares the cobalt pot-metal sent from the Rhine.

Delivery — Saint Michael's Day, MCCCV

A Book of Hours for the Countess of Vermandois

CXLIV folios · vellum · Use of Soissons

Madame Eléonore, Countess of Vermandois, has bespoke a small book to lie open upon her prie-dieu: the Hours of the Virgin in the Use of Soissons, the Penitential Psalms, and an Office of the Dead set after her late lord. Twelve full miniatures shall stand at the great divisions, and the calendar receive a labour of the months in burnished gold against madder grounds. The hand is text-quadrata of Brother Aymeric.

Bound by Candlemas — XL livres tournois

Restoration of the Damaged Rose at Saint-Quentin

North transept · twelve foils · injured by tempest of MCCCIII

In the night of Saint Bartholomew the great hailstorm shattered the tracery of the north rose, and the cobalt ground of the upper foils lies broken upon the pavement. The Chapter of Saint-Quentin has retained the atelier to draw the missing figures anew — three angels, the prophet Isaias, and the lamb of the Apocalypse — and to refire those panels which yet hold their lead. Old glass shall be matched grain-for-grain.

In progress · second year of labour

Of the Crafts We Practise

The atelier accepts work in glass, in vellum, and in painted panel, after the manner of the northern schools and the masters of Chartres. Cartoons for windows are drawn full-scale upon whited oaken tables; the cames are cast in our own foundry behind the rue des Verriers; the colours are ground each morning in oil of walnut and bound with gum of cherry.

For books we keep the calf-skin in lime water through the autumn months and finish vellum to a satin nap by Lent. Gold is laid upon a gesso of bole and burnished with the tooth of a wolf. Pigments are won from lapis lazuli, from madder root, from the verdigris that gathers on copper plates above vinegar.

— Gautier de Soissons,
Magister Vitrearius et Illuminator
