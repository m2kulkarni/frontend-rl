Of the Sixth Folio

To Find
the Atelier

at the Sign of the Blue Lamb
in the parish of Saint-Pierre-au-Parvis

The Sign of
the Blue Lamb

Look for a wooden board hung above the door, painted in cobalt and madder: a lamb couchant bearing the cross-staff, with a banderole reading Agnus inter vitra. The board was repainted at Pentecost in the year of our Lord MCCLXXXVII.

Rue des
Verriers

From the cathedral parvis, take the street descending east toward the river. Pass the chapel of Saint-Crépin (patron of leather-workers, known by its small bell-cote of two arches). The atelier is the third door beyond the chapel, on the south side of the way.

Saint-Pierre
au-Parvis

Our hearth lies within the parish of Saint-Pierre-au-Parvis at Soissons, under the cure of Father Eustache the elder. The parish bell rings the offices, and by it the workshop is opened, closed, and at midday silenced for the breaking of bread.

Of Letters and Their Carriage

Letters and writs may be sent to the master in care of the chapter scriptorium of the cathedral,

— ad scriptorium capituli ecclesiæ Suessionensis —

where the precentor's clerk, brother Aymar of Braine, will deliver them into our hands at the next chapter meeting. A reply may be looked for within a fortnight, and within four days for matters concerning glass already commissioned.

The Approach

for those who come from afar

Travellers arriving from Reims by the eastern road shall enter the city by the Porte Saint-Christophe and bear right at the well of three basins. Those coming from Laon enter by the Porte de Crouy and follow the river-walk until the bell-cote of Saint-Crépin appears above the rooftops. From the abbey of Saint-Médard the way is shorter still: one league by the cart-road, less if the meadow be dry.

The cathedral itself is the surest landmark, its western towers visible from a half-day's ride across the plain. Once within sight of its parvis, no further direction is needed save the asking of any child or chandler in the rue des Verriers, who will know the blue lamb at once.

To Visiting Masters

a particular invitation

Masters of glass, illumination, or kindred crafts, journeying upon the roads of Christendom, are welcomed to lodge a single night within the journeymen's loft above the workshop. The straw is fresh-laid each Saturday after Vespers, and the loft holds three pallets beneath the rafters where the cartoons of past windows are stored.

We ask in return only this: that the guest set his hand to one drawing in our common book, with name, town, and the date in Roman numerals — that the lineage of those who have passed beneath our roof be kept, as is the custom among the workshops of the north.
