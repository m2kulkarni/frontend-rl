Pancha Prakara · The Five Enclosures A Walking Treatise on Stone, River & Light Campus & Heritage Walk Forty-seven Acres on the Vennaru

The Sri Rajaraja Institute occupies a 47-acre site on the southern bank of the Vennaru, a distributary of the Kaveri, two kilometres west of the Brihadeeshwara temple. The plan, drawn in 1979 by the late Sthapati C. Ganapati Iyer, organises the campus as four concentric prakaras — earth, courtyard, garden, and grove — around the central Vidya Mandapa, where the Saraswati shrine and the principal lecture halls converge under a single granite roof.

The Four Prakaras

A Concentric Reading of the Plan
Outer · Vana Prakara Garden · Pushpa Court · Mandapa
Vidya
Mandapa Saraswati Shrine

The Vana Prakara — the outermost ring — is a 4.2 km perimeter walk shaded by tamarind, neem and 380 native palmyra. It marks the boundary with the river embankment and the Mariamman Koil road, and houses the four hostel blocks and the staff quarters.

The Pushpa Prakara holds the gardens — sthala-vriksha groves, a medicinal plot maintained by the School of Vaidya Sastra, and the lotus tank fed by the Vennaru sluice. The Karikalan Hall of Examinations stands at its eastern gate.

The Mandapa Prakara contains the academic quadrangles. Around it cluster the Sembiyan Mahadevi Library, the Nayak Auditorium, and seven teaching pavilions named for Pallava, Chola and Vijayanagara royal patrons.

The Innermost Court opens onto the Vidya Mandapa, a granite-pillared shrine-hall completed in 1983 to a design recovered from the Mayamata. The Saraswati bronze within was cast at Swamimalai by Sri Devasenapathi Sthapathi.

Named Buildings

Halls, Pavilions, Quadrangle

Karikalan Hall of Examinations

East Gate · Pushpa Prakara

A pillared hall of black charnockite, 64 m × 22 m, named for the Early Chola king Karikalan. Twenty-eight rows of stone benches accommodate 1,120 candidates under a single coffered ceiling. The corbels carry portrait-medallions of every Vice-Chancellor since 1981.

Capacity: 1,120 · Built: 1984 · Architect: M. Pichai Sthapati

Sembiyan Mahadevi Library

North Quadrangle · Mandapa Prakara

Named for the queen and patron of the great Chola bronzes, the library holds 412,000 volumes, 11,800 palm-leaf manuscripts, and the Sastri Memorial Collection of South Indian inscriptions. The reading rooms open onto a central pond planted with the thousand-petalled sahasra-dala lotus.

Hours: 06:30–22:00 · Manuscripts wing: by appointment · Founded: 1985

Nayak Auditorium

South Quadrangle · Mandapa Prakara

A 1,400-seat performance hall completed in 1992 in the style of the seventeenth-century Sangeeta Mahal. Twin tiers of viewing galleries rest on sixteen monolithic pillars; the proscenium carries a frieze of the eighteen rasas. The hall hosts the annual Margazhi Bharatanatyam Festival each December.

Capacity: 1,400 · Acoustic: stone-resonant · Festival: Margazhi (Dec 16 – Jan 14)

Yali Quadrangle

Open Air · Mandapa Prakara

An open-air teaching court flanked by 64 yali pillars carved by the third- and fourth-year students of the Department of Stone Craft over twelve seasons. The quadrangle is used for outdoor recitals of Tevaram and Tiruvasagam and for the monthly Pournami new-moon assembly.

Pillars: 64 yali · Granite: Krishnagiri · Carved: 1996–2008

The Heritage Walk Itinerary

A Two-and-a-Half Hour Tour for Visitors · Tuesdays & Saturdays
09:00Assembly

The Vennaru Gate & Outer Tank

Walk begins at the western Vennaru Gate, where the river embankment is faced in cut sandstone laid by the institute's first Sthapati cohort in 1981. The guide, drawn from the senior Sastra students, recites the foundation invocation.

09:35First Stop

Karikalan Hall & the Foundation Inscription

The 1979 copper-plate grant of the founding trust is on permanent display in the eastern vestibule. Note the bilingual Tamil-Sanskrit colophon and the donor list including the Tanjavur Maratha Sarasvati Mahal Library.

10:10Second Stop

Sembiyan Mahadevi Library — Manuscript Wing

Visitors are admitted to the conservation room. The librarian on duty unrolls one of the eleventh-century Tirumurai palm-leaves under cold lamp; photography is not permitted.

10:50Third Stop

The Yali Quadrangle & Stone Workshop

A live demonstration of granite carving by fourth-year apprentices of the Department of Stone Craft. Visitors may try their hand at chiselling soft soapstone under supervision; tools and a small block are provided.

11:25Fourth Stop

Vidya Mandapa & Saraswati Shrine

The innermost hall. Removal of footwear required. The Swamimalai bronze of Saraswati is unveiled briefly; the daily noon recitation of the Sri Suktam follows.

11:30Conclusion

Lotus Tank & Refreshment

The walk ends at the Pushpa Prakara lotus tank with filtered Vennaru water, jaggery and sundal served from the Annapurna kitchen. A donation of ₹150 per visitor supports manuscript conservation.
