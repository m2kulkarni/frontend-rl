VI.  De Itinere — Of the Journey

The University occupies four buildings on the southern flank of the Vézelay hill, immediately below the Basilique Sainte-Marie-Madeleine.

Visitors are received daily; the porter's lodge at 2 Place Saint-Aubain is staffed from seven of the morning to ten of the evening.

Visiting the Hill of Vézelay

A practical guide for prospective students, scholars in residence, parents, and the merely pilgrim. Approach the Morvan in any season; the cloister is open through the Octave.

Tres Viae — Three Approaches

How One May Come

Vézelay is a hill town reached by patience. The pilgrim road, the rail, and the highway each meet at the foot of the basilica; the University lies a hundred paces below.

Visiting by Season

Per annum — the cloister and the lectures.

Quaestiones — A Few Replies

Practical Matters

May I attend a public lecture without booking?

Yes — the Hilary lectures in the Salle Capitulaire are open to all, without charge or reservation, until the hall is filled. Doors open thirty minutes before the hour. The schedule for Hilary 2025 is given on the Calendar page.

Are the buildings accessible to those with limited mobility?

The Bibliothèque and the Collège ground floor are fully accessible by lift; the Cloître Nord, by virtue of its 12th-century stone steps, is regrettably not. The porter's lodge will arrange a private viewing of the cloister gardens upon request.

Is the chapel open for prayer?

The Chapelle Saint-Aubain is open from 07:30 to 18:30 for silent prayer and study; Mass is celebrated by the Fraternités Monastiques de Jérusalem at the basilica above. The University does not maintain a separate liturgy.

May I bring a dog, or a child?

Children are warmly welcomed in the precinct and on the cloister tour from age eight. Dogs on lead are permitted in the outer courtyards but not in the Bibliothèque or the chapel. The University cat, Origène, takes precedence in all matters.
