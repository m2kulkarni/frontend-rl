The Four Faculties

— The Quadrivium — Theology, the queen of sciences;
the Liberal Arts, her handmaids;
Law, both civil and canon;
& Natural Philosophy,
that the works of creation
might be read aright.

In the ordo studiorum of Saint-Aubain, instruction proceeds through four faculties, each housed within its own cloister or tower, each governed by a Doyen elected from among the senior Masters. The faculties hold their schools by common charter of 1247 and meet in plenary congregation at the feast of Saint Mary Magdalene, patroness of the abbey-church beneath which the studium first gathered. What follows is the present roll of chairs, with notes upon their lecture-halls and the masters who hold them in Hilary term, 2025.

I

Sacrae Paginae

Cloître Saint‑Aubain

Facultas Prima · Senior Faculty

The Faculty of Sacred Theology

"Quod studium primum & maximum, fons & origo omnium aliarum scientiarum, hic in monte Vezeliacensi conservetur ad finem saeculi."
— from the foundation charter, 1247

The senior of the four schools, the Faculty of Theology occupies the upper storey of the eastern cloister and the great hall above the chapter-house. Its lectures begin at the third hour of the morning, after Lauds, and conclude with disputation at None. Candidates for the licentiate must hold the Master of Arts and have heard the Sentences read in their entirety; the doctorate is conferred by the Chancellor only upon the public defence of a quaestio in the choir of the Madeleine.

II

Septem Artes Liberales

Tour Bernard · West Range

Facultas Secunda · Propaedeutic

The Faculty of Liberal Arts

"Trivium fundamentum, quadrivium ascensus: per haec septem gradus anima ad sapientiam ducitur."

All matriculands of Saint-Aubain pass first through this faculty, completing the trivium — grammar, dialectic, rhetoric — within two years, before ascending to the quadrivium of arithmetic, geometry, music, and astronomy. The seminar in the quadrivium meets in the upper chamber of the Tour Bernard, where the astronomical instruments cast in 1689 by Le Bègue of Auxerre remain in continuous use; Dr. Étienne Caillet conducts the term's readings on the De Institutione Arithmetica of Boethius and the Theorica Planetarum.

III

Ius Civile & Canonicum

Salle Capitulaire

Facultas Tertia · Utrumque Ius

The Faculty of Civil & Canon Law

"Incorporata fuit haec facultas per bullam apostolicam Innocentii Papae IV, anno Domini millesimo ducentesimo quadragesimo septimo, mense Junii."

Saint-Aubain is among the few studia north of the Alps to retain instruction in both laws under a single Doyen — a privilege confirmed by papal bull of June 1247 and never since revoked. The civilians read the Corpus iuris civilis in the morning hours; the canonists read the Decretals of Gregory IX and the Codex Iuris Canonici of 1983 in the afternoon. Joint disputation on questions of mixed jurisdiction is held the first Friday of each month in the Salle Capitulaire, before the Doyen and assembled Bachelors.

IV

Philosophia Naturalis

Cloître Sud · Laboratories

Facultas Quarta · Newest of the Four

The Faculty of Natural Philosophy

"Liber naturae duobus modis legitur: oculo simplici per observationem, ratione per demonstrationem."

Established in 1782 by lettre patente of Louis XVI and reformed under the Concordat of 1801, the youngest of the faculties retains its original charge: the patient reading of the book of nature, alongside the book of Scripture. Its laboratories — for chymistry, botany, and the cabinet of natural history — occupy the entire south range of the Cloître Sud, where the vaulted undercroft has been fitted since 1873 with copper benches, fume-hoods of stained oak, and the fine collection of Burgundian fossils given by the Comtesse de Montréal. The herbarium contains 18,400 mounted specimens.
