Liber I — Of the Library

Founded in 1247 by the canons of the abbey-school, the Bibliothèque Saint-Aubain occupies the western range of the cloister at Vézelay, its reading-room raised upon the old chapter-house of Abbot Hugues de Poitiers. The collection has grown without rupture for almost eight centuries.

Its holdings comprise codices, incunabula, charters, and the printed apparatus of modern humanistic study; access is open to matriculated students, registered scholars, and — by appointment — to visiting readers from any institution of learning.

Libellus II — Of the Custodian

The library is administered by the Custos Librorum, presently Mme Aliénor Brossard, archivist-paléographe, supported by four conservators and a rotating fellowship of doctoral cataloguers from Paris and Lyon.

Materials acquired since 1804 are kept in the modern stacks beneath the south transept; the medieval and early-modern reserve is shelved in the upper armarium, accessible by petition through the registrar.

A reading desk is reserved each term for visiting fellows of the École nationale des chartes.

Codex Insignis

The Vézelay Psalter, c. 1290

Produced in Paris, in or near the workshop of Master Honoré of the rue Érembourg-de-Brie, and brought to Vézelay before 1318 — when it appears in the inventory of Prior Guillaume de Cluny under the rubric "psalterium pulcherrimum cum kalendario".

The codex contains 248 folios, fully illuminated, with twelve historiated calendar miniatures, eight full-page Beatus initials, and over 340 marginal grotesques painted in lapis, gold-leaf, and the distinctive carmine-red associated with the Honoré atelier. The Litany preserves the unusual invocation of Saint Aubain (10th c., obit. 23 April), patron of the studium.

The manuscript was lent in 1873 to the Bibliothèque nationale de France for the centenary of the École des chartes, and has since left Vézelay only twice: for the 1968 Paris exhibition Les Manuscrits à peintures en France, and for technical imaging at the IRHT in 2019.

Featured Acquisition · Anno MMXXIV

A Fragment of the Speculum Historiale of Vincent de Beauvais

Catalogued in March 2024 and now shelved as MS frag. 312, this bifolium of the Speculum Historiale (Book XXIV, on the Carolingian succession) was donated to the Bibliothèque Saint-Aubain by the Bibliothèque municipale de Sens, as part of an exchange of duplicate medieval material agreed between the two institutions in 2022.

The fragment had been used as a flyleaf in a 1581 Lyonese imprint of Cicero's De Officiis, from which it was lifted during conservation in 2018. The hand is a clear littera textualis of the third quarter of the thirteenth century, very probably North-French, with red-and-blue pen-flourished initials.

"Anno Domini octingentesimo decimo quarto, Karolus Magnus apud Aquasgrani diem clausit extremum…"

The bifolium has been paginated, foliated against the standard Douai edition (1624), and digitised at 600 dpi for inclusion in the consortium catalogue Calames. A printed facsimile, with an introduction by Dr Henri Lavaur (Faculté des Lettres), is to appear in the autumn 2025 number of the Bulletin de la Bibliothèque Saint-Aubain.

Of the Reading-Room

Mores Salae Lectoriae

i.

Of Admission

Readers present a current carte de lecteur, or a letter of accreditation from a recognised institution, at the wicket of the cloister porch.

ii.

Of Materials

Pencil only; ink is forbidden in the reserve. Laptops are permitted at the eastern desks; cameras require the written assent of the Custos.

iii.

Of Quotation

Three volumes may be consulted at once; no more than one manuscript. Petitions for additional items are heard each Wednesday at noon.

iv.

Of Silence

The reading-room observes the rule of silentium claustri; conversation is held in the antechamber, beneath the engraving of the abbey.
