De Admissione · Hilary 2025

Admissions &
the Matriculation Examination

Two stages, in the older manner: a written disputation in the morning, an oral defence before the Faculty in the afternoon. Letters of intent are received until the eleventh of January.

The Order of Examination

Candidates seeking admission to the Université Saint-Aubain are received in the older manner, by examination rather than dossier. The Chancellor's Council holds that a young scholar's fitness is best discerned through the labour of composition and the cut-and-thrust of viva voce disputation; accordingly, every applicant — whether destined for Theology, Arts, Law, or Medicine — sits the same two-stage Matriculation. There are no auxiliary tests, no interviews of conversation, no statements of personal motive submitted in advance. The examination is the application.

I. The Written Examination

Held in the great hall of the Collège des Quatre-Vents on Friday, the fourteenth of February 2025, between the third and ninth hours after Prime. Candidates are given a passage of classical Latin prose — drawn in 2025 from Cicero's Pro Archia — for translation, grammatical analysis, and rhetorical exposition. A second sitting in the afternoon requires an original Latin composition of some four hundred words on a moral or philosophical proposition supplied at the desk. Lexica are not permitted; a single sheet of personal notes may be brought.

14 February 2025 · Salle des Quatre-Vents · 9h00–15h00

II. The Oral Disputation

Those who pass the written paper are recalled within the fortnight to defend a thesis of their own choosing before two members of the relevant Faculty. The disputation runs not less than thirty minutes; the candidate states the proposition, the examiners object, the candidate replies. Faculty Theology requires, in addition, the citation by memory of one Psalm in the Vulgate. The disputation is conducted in French, with technical objections often posed in Latin.

25 February – 7 March 2025 · By summons

Tuition & the Bourse

Fees are set annually by the Chancellor's Council and remain modest by comparison with the universities of Paris or Dijon. The figures below apply to the academic year 2025; they are payable at the beginning of each term to the Bursar's office in the Bureau du Chancelier.

Bourse Saint-Bernard

Endowed in 1879 by a quiet bequest of the Cistercian Abbey of Pontigny, the Bourse Saint-Bernard waives all tuition for twelve students per cohort, chosen on the strength of the Matriculation papers alone. No separate application is required; the prize is conferred at the close of the oral disputation. Recipients are bound only by the modest obligation of attending Vespers in the chapel of Saint-Aubain on the four high feasts of the year.

Letters of intent should be written by hand, in French or Latin, no longer than a single folio. They are to enumerate the candidate's previous studies, the Faculty to which admission is sought, and the proposed subject of the oral thesis. Transcripts and one letter of recommendation, sealed, must be enclosed.
