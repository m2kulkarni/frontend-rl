De Domo Nostra

— Of Our House —

A chronicle of the founders, the building, the kitchen,
and the hand that painted these walls.

The Founding

In the year MMXIX, two classicists turned cooks set down their books and lit a fire on the Aventine.

Triclinium Aureum was opened on the seventeenth of October, MMXIX, by Marcus Vibius Sabinus and his daughter Livia Vibia. Father and daughter had each spent a long apprenticeship among manuscripts before they spent any among saucepans — he as a Latinist at the Collegio Romano, she as a doctoral student of agrarian Rome at the Scuola Normale in Pisa. The kitchen was the late convert; the philology came first.

The notion of a supper-house cooking from the De Re Coquinaria began as a private joke at the family table in Trastevere, and was tested upon friends for the better part of three years before it acquired premises, a licence, and a sign. The sign — a single gilt laurel above an unmarked door at Via di Sant'Anselmo, 14 — is the only announcement the house makes to the street.

The Building

A printing-works, vaulted in brick, gave the house its bones.

The Shell

The premises had stood empty since 2014, when the last of the Aventine printing concerns — Tipografia Pellegrini, founded 1898 — closed its presses. The Pellegrini building is a long, narrow rectangle running back from the street, roofed by three shallow brick groin vaults of the late nineteenth century, themselves built upon foundations of Roman opus reticulatum visible still in the cellar.

It was these vaults that decided the matter. They gave the long room its acoustic and its proportions — twenty-eight metres by nine, with the central vault rising to five and a half — and they accepted lime plaster without complaint. The presses were sold to a museum at Treviso; the iron rails on which they had run are preserved in the floor.

The Triclinium

The dining-room is laid out on the antique plan: three sides of low couches, the fourth open to the service. Twenty-one diners may recline at one sitting, never more. A second, smaller chamber — the cubiculum — seats six at a low marble table, and is reserved for the wine suppers described elsewhere in this book.

The pavement is the original printing-works terrazzo, scrubbed and waxed. The walls were taken back to brick, replastered in seven coats of slaked lime, and given over to the muralist named below.

The Governance of the Kitchen

Three writers preside, dead these nineteen centuries: Apicius, Cato, Columella.

The kitchen is governed by recipes drawn from the ten books of De Re Coquinaria, attributed to Apicius, and by the agricultural writers Cato the Elder (De Agri Cultura) and Columella (De Re Rustica), with reference to the elder Pliny on questions of substance and to Galen where the matter has crossed into medicine. We adjust only where ingredients are no longer to be had — the African silphium being long extinct, we use asafoetida; the Roman liquamen of mackerel viscera being unobtainable in commerce, we ferment our own each spring in glazed jars on the roof, after a recipe communicated by the Gargilius institute at Pompeii.

Patinam de piris: pira elixa et purgata e medio teres cum pipere, cumino, melle, passo, liquamine, oleo modico. — Apicius, IV. ii. 35 (rendered nightly as our Patina Pirorum)

Where a Roman text calls for a measurement we cannot verify — the cyathus, the acetabulum, the loose handful — we cook the dish three times in three weights and decide by argument at the table. The argument is preserved in a black notebook kept by Livia in the pass; it now runs to four hundred and sixty pages and is unlikely to be finished.

Bread, wine, and oil are the three things we do not adapt. The bread is a panis quadratus of stone-milled spelt, baked each morning by Tommaso Ricci in a wood oven on the Via Marmorata. The oil is a single Sabine grove (Casperia, harvest of November) pressed within twelve hours. The wine is treated in a separate book — Vina Nobilia — to which the reader is referred.

The Frescoes

The dining-room is painted, wall to vault, by one hand.

The fresco cycle that surrounds the diner on three sides is the work of Elena Ricci of Naples, conservator and muralist, trained at the Istituto Centrale per il Restauro and for fifteen years a member of the team responsible for the stabilisation of the second-style cycles at Pompeii and Boscoreale. Signora Ricci painted the room over four months in the spring and summer of MMXIX, working in true buon fresco upon damp lime, after the manner — and at full scale — of the megalography in the Villa of the Mysteries.

The figures are not copies. The composition follows the Mysteries cycle in its rhythm of standing forms upon a continuous Pompeian-red ground, divided by slender black pilasters; but the figures themselves are taken from other rooms — the seated lyre-player from the Villa of P. Fannius Synistor, the winged genius from the House of the Vettii, the offering-bearer from a fragment in the Naples archaeological museum, inv. 9106. Signora Ricci has called this her centone, her patchwork; we call it the room.

The pigments are those a Roman painter would have known — cinnabar for the field, Egyptian blue for the pilaster grounds, yellow ochre and madder, lamp black, and a single passage of leaf gold above the entrance, applied a missione. The room is lit by twelve oil lamps and six candles. Electric light is not admitted.

A Brief Chronicle

  • MMXVI The Private Suppers BeginThe first cooked-from-Apicius supper is given for nine friends in the Vibius family flat at Trastevere. The patina cotidiana is judged a success; the boiled flamingo is not attempted.
  • MMXVIII The Aventine Premises AcquiredThe disused Pellegrini printing-works is taken on a fifteen-year lease in March. Demolition of partitions reveals the brick vaulting and a fragment of Roman wall in the cellar.
  • MMXIX Frescoes Completed; House OpenedElena Ricci concludes the dining-room cycle on 4 October. The first paying guests are received on 17 October, a Thursday, at the eleventh hour by the Roman reckoning.
  • MMXXI The Cellar of FalernianA consignment of forty-eight bottles of an experimental Falernum from Mondragone is laid down in the cellar; the first is drawn for a supper in autumn MMXXIV.
  • MMXXIII The Black Notebook FilledLivia Vibia closes the first kitchen notebook (460 pp.) and begins a second. The first is bound in vellum and given a place on the pass.
  • MMXXV The Present YearForty-two covers a week, two services, Tuesday through Saturday. Closed in August, as is the Roman custom.

Colophon

This page set in Cinzel and EB Garamond, after the manner of an inscribed tablet and a humanist book respectively. The borders follow the meander floor of the House of the Faun at Pompeii (Reg. VI, ins. 12). The roundels are after Cleland's ornament n. 10818, the oak wreath being sacred to Jove.
