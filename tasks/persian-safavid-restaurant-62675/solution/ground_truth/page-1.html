— Hall of Forty Columns —

A Safavid table, set nightly in the courtyard tradition of Isfahan.

Tuesday — Sunday · Dusk Until the Eleventh Hour

Three Iwans, One Garden

— the kitchen, the hall, the terrace —

— I —

The Kitchen

Saffron from Qaen, sumac from the foothills of Damavand, and barberries dried on the rooftops of Mashhad. Our chelo is steamed over a charcoal hearth in the Isfahani polo manner and crowned each evening with a gold-leaf tahdig.

— II —

The Hall

Forty slender pillars, cast in cedar and lacquered cobalt, stand in a still pool of water that doubles their number to eighty. Diners are seated on low Tabriz carpets between glazed tile iwans set with verses of Hāfez and Saʿdī.

— III —

The Garden Terrace

Beyond the hall, a chahar bagh of pomegranate and quince, watered by four narrow channels that meet beneath a marble basin. From April to October we set eighteen tables beneath the plane trees, lit only by oil lanterns.

— A Note from the Proprietor —

Yazd, 1962

My grandmother Soraya kept a brass pot, blackened on its underside, that she would set upon the coals before the call to evening prayer. Into it went lamb shank, dried Persian limes, and a long thread of saffron bloomed in rosewater. The khoresh-e gheymeh she made on Friday afternoons in our courtyard in Yazd is the dish I have been chasing for sixty-one years.

Chehel Sotoun is, in the end, an attempt to set her table again — for forty guests at a time, in a hall of forty columns, in a city far from the desert she knew. We will not pretend otherwise.

Darius Khorsandi
Chef-Proprietor · est. Tuesday, the 14th of March
