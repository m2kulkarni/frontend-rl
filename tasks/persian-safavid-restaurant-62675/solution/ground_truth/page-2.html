The Sofreh

Our table is laid as a spread cloth in four iwans — the garden, the long hearth, the rice of the twelve provinces, and the bazaar of sweets. Dishes are drawn from the courtly kitchens of seventeenth-century Isfahan, set to the rhythm of what the orchards and millers send each week.

The Full
Sofreh

For the table of four or more, our kitchen lays the complete sofreh — all four iwans served in sequence, with bread baked to the hour, sharbat of sour cherry and rose, and tea drawn from a samovar of beaten copper. Reservations advised by sundown of the prior day.

A Note on the Sofreh

The menu turns with the season and the post-rider. Where a dish names a farm or a mill, that name was true at the writing of this card; should the rains delay a delivery, the kitchen will speak the substitution at your table. Bread is laid before salt; salt before water; water before tea.
