Three Iwans, One Table

The Hall — Our Rooms

Three rooms framed as iwans face a single courtyard table. Each is built from the materials of the city it remembers — Tehran mirror-work, Isfahani cobalt tile, and the orchard stone of Qazvin.

Iwan I Twenty-four seats

The Mirror HallTālār-e Āyeneh

The principal dining room sits beneath a fragmented-mirror ceiling commissioned in 2019 from the atelier Pirnia of Tehran, whose family has set āyeneh-kāri for ninety-three years. Eleven thousand hand-cut shards reflect the cobalt of the side walls and the small flame of each candle, so that the room is, after dusk, almost entirely made of light.

Twenty-four seats are arranged along a single low cedar table in the Safavid manner, with cushions of indigo wool from Khorasan. Service is paced to four hours; the kitchen does not turn the room.

Iwan II Twelve seats, private

The Cobalt IwanIwan-e Lājvardi

A private dining room for twelve, walled on three sides in glazed seven-colour tile (haft-rang) set after the dome of the Sheikh Lotfollah mosque (Isfahan, 1619). The pattern was redrawn from photographs by the architect Niloofar Sadeghi and fired in 2021 at a kiln outside Yazd; ninety-six tiles were broken in transit and replaced from the same batch.

The room is reserved most often for engagements, anniversaries, and the long autumn dinner of the saffron harvest. A single ogee niche at the head of the table holds a copper bowl of pomegranate, replaced each Thursday.

Iwan III Seasonal, open-air

The Pavilion GardenBāgh-e Kūshk

From the first week of April through the last of October, the rear courtyard opens as an outdoor pavilion set among four quince and two sour-cherry trees, planted in 2014 from cuttings carried from a private orchard near Karaj. A linen canopy is raised at midday and struck before midnight; in May the cherry sets fruit directly above the lamb table.

Service here is more informal: shared platters of kashk-e bademjan, charcoal kebabs from the open hearth, and tea brewed over a samovar that has stood in the family since 1958. Forty seats in good weather, eighteen if the rain comes.

A Note on the Forty Columns

The eight cedar columns that divide the three rooms — and which, doubled in the long mirror of the entry passage, complete the count of forty — were reclaimed in 2018 from a 1930s caravanserai near Qazvin, dismantled when the Tehran–Rasht road was widened. They were planed by hand at the workshop of master Hossein Najjar in Lavasan, oiled with walnut, and raised here in the spring of 2022. Two retain the iron rings to which travellers once tied their horses; we have left these in place.
