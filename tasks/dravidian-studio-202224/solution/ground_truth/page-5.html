Reading, more than drawing, is the true labour of the studio. The texts collected below are working documents — argued in print, delivered from lecterns, or scribbled in the broadsheet that travels with our consignments — through which we test the proposition that the Dravidian grammar still has uses for the contemporary city.

Essay

On the Tala System: Why a Column Is Never Just a Column

Marg Magazine · Vol. 74, No. 3 · pp. 44–61

A long-form essay arguing that the Śilpa Śāstra tala measures — the nine-, ten-, and twelve-part proportional grids that govern image and pillar alike — be read not as antiquarian formulae but as a generative interface: a way to pre-load a building with cultural memory before the section is even drawn. Illustrated with our own tala-overlay studies on the columns of the Mīnākṣī Sundareśvarar maṇḍapa and a comparison set from a 2022 hospitality commission in Karaikudi.

Lecture

Vermilion is a Verb

V&A Jameel Centre · London · 27 October 2022 · 18:30 GMT

The annual Jameel Lecture, delivered by principal R. Ananthanarayanan to a hall of 220. The argument: that kāvi red — the iron-oxide pigment that floods the Nayak temple ceiling — is best understood as an act of marking time, renewed every twelve years by the temple's painters. The studio's broadsheet press and a Madurai-prepared pigment cake were set out on the lectern table; the audience was invited to handle both. A response by Mariam Rosser-Owen followed.

Reading Ranganāthaswāmy as a City

Domus India · Issue 109 · pp. 78–89

Commissioned by editor Kaiwan Mehta, this essay treats the seven concentric prākāra walls of the Srirangam temple-town not as fortification but as urban score — a graduated sequence of thresholds that calibrates the density, sound and light of the surrounding fabric. We propose four contemporary design lessons drawn from a fortnight of field measurements taken with students of the CEPT summer studio, July 2021.

The Mandapa as Slow Software

Indian Institute of Architects · Chennai Chapter · Online

A pandemic-era talk treating the thousand-pillar maṇḍapa as a piece of versioned, patched, locally-forked software whose maintenance contract has never lapsed. The studio's argument was that contemporary practice — accustomed to deprecation — has much to learn from a system designed for unbounded continuity. Q&A with Brinda Somaya followed.

Review

Against the Atrium: A Letter on the New Tirupati Terminal

Architectural Review Asia Pacific · Issue 158

A 1,200-word polemic against the dominance of the climate-controlled glass atrium in temple-town transit architecture, with an alternative sketch proposal — a stepped, open tirumaṇḍapa roof, ventilated by the gopuram-section's own chimney effect, presented at 1:200. The letter generated correspondence from six practices over the following eighteen months, archived herewith.
