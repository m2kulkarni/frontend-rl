The Living Grammar

A statement of practice, in three measures

Tala — Proportion

Sthapati Workshop holds that the tala system — the ancient Dravidian module that orders a temple from plinth to finial — is not an antiquarian curiosity but a working unit of measure. We draw in angula and hasta beside the millimetre, and find that contemporary brief and canonical proportion rarely contradict each other.

Our 2019 dossier on the proportions of the Meenakshi Amman kalyana mandapa remains in continuous use as a teaching document at SAP, Anna University.

Silpa — Ornament as Structure

In the southern tradition the kumbha-panjara, the vyala bracket, the lotus medallion are not applied decoration; they describe how a load is gathered, turned, and let go. We design with that vocabulary intact — even where the building is a tea pavilion or a typeface specimen — because ornament that does no work loses the right to be there.

The studio maintains a working library of 1,140 rubbings and squeezes from temples between Madurai and Kanchipuram.

Varna — Polychrome as Devotion

The Nayak painters of the 16th–18th centuries did not treat colour as decoration but as offering. Sacred white, vermilion, gold-leaf, peacock-blue, ink-black — the five-colour grammar — entered the building because it entered the rite. The Workshop applies the same five colours, in the same proportions, to print, exhibition, and façade.

We do not work in muted palettes. We do not produce greyscale renderings. The drawing is the building is the painting.
