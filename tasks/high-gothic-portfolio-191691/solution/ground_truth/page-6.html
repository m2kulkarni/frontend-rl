To Petition the Atelier

Of Commissions, Visiting Hours, and the Manner Whereby Letters and Couriers May Be Received at the Lodge beside the Cathedral Close

MCCCIV

Of Receiving Petitioners

Whereby prelates, chapters, and burghers may make known their will

To the worshipful reader, peace and the favour of the Most High. The atelier of Guillaume de Sens, master mason and tracery-cutter, doth keep its lodge beside the cathedral close at Sens, in the rue des Tanneurs, where the Yonne runs swift beneath the bishop's mill. Here are received all who would commission work in stone, in glass, or in gilded metalwork, whether for the embellishing of a high altar, the glazing of a clerestory, or the cutting of a tomb-slab for the repose of the faithful.

Petitioners are bidden to come in person upon the days appointed below, or by trusted hand of letter to be entrusted unto the chapter's couriers, who ride from Sens unto Paris, Chartres, Amiens, and Reims at the turning of each fortnight. The master himself receives no man during the silent hours of Matins nor between the bells of Sext and None, that the tracing-floor be not disturbed in its measure.

Estimates and contracts shall be rendered in livres tournois, with terms by feast-day instalment as is customary among the lodges of the Île-de-France — a fourth part at the laying of the contract, a fourth at the rough-hewing, a fourth at the setting, and the residue upon the day of dedication. No work is undertaken save by sealed agreement witnessed before two clerks of the chapter.

Of the Manner of Petition

— Modus petendi —

By personal attendance. Let the petitioner present himself at the gate of the lodge, bearing letter of introduction from his prelate, abbot, or guild-warden. The under-master shall conduct him to the second chamber, where the master keeps his counters, his compasses, and the great parchment of the tracing-floor. Here may the work be discussed, the dimensions taken in hand, and the chosen stone — whether the white of Tonnerre, the blue of Soissons, or the cream of Caen — appointed for the commission.

By letter and courier. Letters may be entrusted to the chapter's couriers at the chancellery of the cathedral, sealed and directed: À Maistre Guillaume de Sens, son atelier en la rue des Tanneurs, jouxte le cloître. The atelier responds within the span of two feast-days following receipt, the answer borne back upon the next courier's saddle.

By writ of the chapter. Where a great commission is intended — the rebuilding of a vault, the glazing of a rose, the cutting of an entire portal — the petition must come under the seal of the chapter or commune, and shall be inscribed in the lodge-book of the atelier, that posterity may know by whom and at what cost the work was raised.
