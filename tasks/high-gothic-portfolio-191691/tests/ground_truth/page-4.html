Folio IV · Liber Tractoris

The Master’s Hand

Drawings from the Tracing-Floor at Sens

In the great loft above the south aisle of the cathedral at Sens, where the floor is laid in a thin coat of plaster fresh-troweled at each new commission, the master sets down with iron stylus and lead-tipped string the figures by which stone shall afterward be cut. These leaves are taken from that floor: scraped, transferred to vellum at one-twelfth the working scale, and bound here for the instruction of those who would learn the discipline of proportion ad triangulum — the geometry of the equilateral triangle, by which Vitruvius and the Lombard masters before us drew every elevation, every section, every moulding of consequence.

What the apprentice must understand is this: nothing in the wall is invented. Every crocket, every voussoir, every moulding profile descends from a figure first laid down by compass and straight-edge upon the tracing-floor. The hand of the cutter follows the line; the line follows the geometry; the geometry follows a single root-square or root-three rectangle proportioned at the very beginning of the work. To depart from this discipline is to ruin the fabric. To keep it is to build for three centuries.

The Four Plates

drawn between Lent of the Year of Our Lord MCCXCVII and the Feast of S. Michael MCCCI

MCCXCVII

Section of the Compound Pier

Sectio columnae fasciculatae · 14 ft. across the choir

Drawn for the third bay of the new choir at S. Étienne, the pier is set out from a single circle of two pieds-de-Sens, divided into eight upon the diameter to give the centres of the engaged shafts. The cardinal four shafts stand a la nue against the cardinal axes; the four lesser, between, take the diagonal rib and wall-rib. The square noyau at the heart receives no thrust but holds the bond — it is for the masonry, not for the eye.

Note that the diagonal shafts are made smaller by one third, lest they crowd the principal four and confound the springing of the vault. — G. de S.
Drawn: Lent, MCCXCVII Vellum: 14 × 22 in. Built: Choir of S. Étienne, Sens, bay III
MCCXCIX

Setting-Out of the Equilateral Arch

Arcus ad triangulum · super spatium 14 pedum

Of all arches the equilateral is the most honest. The radius is the span itself: each compass-foot fixed at A and B sweeps an arc whose meeting at the apex completes a triangle of three equal sides. The rise is then necessarily the half-span multiplied by root-three — for a span of fourteen pieds, the rise is twelve and one-eighth, less the thickness of the keystone. The voussoirs are radiated from A for the dexter half, from B for the sinister; the joints will never want, for they obey the centre.

The apprentice who cannot draw this figure with chalked string in the open air is not yet ready for the chisel. — G. de S.
Drawn: Octave of Easter, MCCXCIX For: Nave arcade, Bourges Lady-Chapel Span: XIV pedes Senonenses
MCCC

Profile of the Crocket and Finial

Crocchetum cum florone · pinaculum boreale

Each crocket grows from a single arc swept by the compass at three successive radii — i, ii, iii — increasing as the pinnacle ascends, that the eye from below shall see them at one apparent size. The leaf-bract is then carved free of the gable to a depth equal to one-third its own height, no more. The finial is laid out as a bud of four lobes upon a sphere; its proportion is taken from the half-diameter of the gable’s base, divided in three. To make the crocket equal at top and bottom is the error of the country mason.

Foreshortening is not vanity — it is a debt the carver pays to the eye that looks up. — G. de S.
Drawn: Feast of S. John, MCCC For: North pinnacle, Sens transept Stone: Tonnerre limestone
MCCCI

Geometry of the Six-foiled Rose

Rosa sexfoliata · ad circulum

Six foils, neither five nor seven, for the six is the perfect figure of nature — the bee’s cell, the snowflake, the lily of the field. From a single great circle of six pieds we strike, with the same opening of the compass, six points upon the circumference; from these we draw inward circles of half the radius, and where each meets its neighbour, a foil is born. The hexagonal star at the centre is gratuitous: it falls of itself out of the construction, and the leadwork follows it without contrivance.

The rose is not designed; it is uncovered. The compass merely lifts the veil. — G. de S.
Drawn: Feast of S. Michael, MCCCI For: West rose, Chapelle de la Vierge Glazier: Robert d’Auxerre

Three Disciplines of the Lodge

Tres regulae loggiae · which the apprentice shall recite at his admission
I

Ad Triangulum

Every elevation of consequence — pier to capital, capital to springing, springing to apex — shall be proportioned by the equilateral triangle. No measure shall be set upon the floor without first being confirmed by the compass against this figure. The eye knows what the rule has refused to invent.

II

Of the Compass

The compass is the master’s authority. It is set down before sunrise upon the floor, lifted only at the bell of nones, and never touched by an apprentice in his first year. Every line not struck from a pricked centre is an opinion, and opinions do not bear stone.

III

Silence in the Loft

While the master draws, no man speaks above the breath. The plaster takes the line in the moment of striking; a jolted hand cannot be unstruck. He who breaks this rule loses his place at the loft and pays five sous to the chapter of works.

The Tracing-Floor at Sens

Pavimentum tractoris · super basilicam Senonensem

The loft above the south-aisle vault was first plastered for our use in the year MCCLXXXIX, by leave of the Chapter of S. Étienne and at the cost of the Bishop, Étienne Bécard. It measures seventy-two pieds in length and twenty-three across, and bears at this day the half-effaced lines of seven full traceries, three pier-sections, and the great wheel of the south transept of Auxerre, drawn there in the summer of MCCXCIV by my own hand and the hand of Master Pierre de Chelles, who came to consult upon the rib-vault.

The plates here published are the first that have been drawn from that floor at reduced scale; the originals remain, and shall remain, where they were struck. To remove them would be to break a chain of testimony that runs from the elder masters of Laon and Reims directly into the present work.
