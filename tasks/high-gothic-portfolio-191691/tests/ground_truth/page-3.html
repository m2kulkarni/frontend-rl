From the great kilns of Picardy come our pots of cobalt-blue, the deep glass of which no Burgundian rival has yet matched; from the Argonne, our murrey-red, struck through with three flashings to deepen the madder; and from the local works at Sens itself, the pale verdure and rough quarrels that fill the grisaille fields. The atelier of Guillaume keeps long covenant with the master glaziers of the rue de la Verrerie — chief among them Pierre Aubri, who has cut for us since the year of grace MCCLXXXIX, and Jehan le Verrier, who succeeded his father at the second forge.

Our windows are drawn first upon the tracing-floor in chalk and red ochre, panel by panel, then transferred to the whitened oak board where the glazier may set out his lead lines. Cobalt is reserved for the mantles of the Virgin and for the deep sky behind the prophets; madder for the wounds of Our Lord and for royal robes; gold-leaf, applied with silver stain after the year MCCC, for haloes, sceptres, and the crowns of the kings of Israel. Of these three commissions, set forth below, the atelier holds the surviving cartoons in its own hand.

MCCXCVI

The Tree of Jesse

Lady Chapel · Auxerre

Begun at the petition of the Chapter of Saint-Étienne in the spring of MCCXCV and set in lead by Michaelmas of the following year, this tall lancet rises through five tiers of crowned ancestors — Jesse recumbent at the root, the Virgin at the apex bearing the Christ-child against a field of pot-metal cobalt sourced wholly from Picardy. Each king is named in white-letter on a banderole; the foliage is cut from a single sheet of flashed green and abraded to reveal the pale yellow beneath.

Glass:
cobalt, madder, flashed verdure
Came:
H-section, Chartrain pattern
Patron:
Eudes de Cicons, dean
MCCCI

The Passion Cycle

Saint-Quiriace · Provins

A great work of five lancets, eighteen scenes, completed in the year of Our Lord MCCCI for the south transept of the collegiate church. The cycle reads from the Entry into Jerusalem at lower left to the Descent into Limbo at upper right; the Crucifixion occupies the central lancet with the figure of Our Lord drawn against a sky of unbroken cobalt — fourteen panes set without dividing rod. The leading patterns are taken directly from the Chartrain master Pierre de Bonneval, who lent us his cartoons in MCCXCIX.

Lancets:
five, of XII pieds high
Sum paid:
cccxx livres tournois
Patron:
Chapter of St-Quiriace
MCCCIII

Oculus of the Four Evangelists

Sens · west clerestory

Set in the western clerestory of the cathedral of Saint-Étienne, this quatrefoil oculus is our first work to employ silver stain throughout — applied to the haloes, the wing-feathers of the four creatures, and the gilt borders of the Gospel-books. The Lion of Mark and Eagle of John face westward; the Bull of Luke and the Man of Matthew turn to the east. Each lobe is bordered by a ring of madder roundels at the came-junctions, after the manner observed at Bourges. Glass cut by Aubri, leading by the master himself.

Diameter:
VIII pieds, IV pouces
Stain:
silver, post-MCCC
Patron:
Archbishop Étienne Bécard

Of Pigments & Practice

A short discourse upon our materials

Pot-Metal Cobalt

Sourced wholly from the Picard kilns at Saint-Quentin, where the cobalt earth is fluxed with potash from beech-ash and cooled in shallow cakes. The depth is unequalled north of the Loire; one pane will hold its colour against the western sun for three generations of canons, by the witness of those who saw the choir at Soissons re-glazed in MCCLXIV.

Silver Stain (post-MCCC)

Brought to our atelier by the journeyman Renaud de Beauvais, who learned the trick of it at Saint-Urbain de Troyes. Applied as a paste of silver-sulphide and ochre to the back of the pane, then fired at low heat: the silver migrates into the glass and yields a true gold without leading. We have used it in every commission since the Evangelists of Sens.

Leading after Chartres

Our lead came is drawn through an iron mill of two passes, in the H-section pattern adopted by the workshops of Chartres in the lifetime of Master Bernard. Joints are soldered with tin-lead at every crossing — never less than four solders to the square pied — and the panel reinforced with iron saddle-bars at intervals of two pieds.
