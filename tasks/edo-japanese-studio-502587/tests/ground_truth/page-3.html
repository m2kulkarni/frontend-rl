参 / 03Practice — How the Studio Works

The room is built twice — once on paper, and again in cedar.

An essay on method, mock-ups, and the five hands
that have shaped Hakubai work since the studio's
founding in Nishijin, 1998.

Filed under Practice
Method
Apprenticeship
Author Aiko Tachibana
Principal, Hakubai
Revised Spring 2024

Every commission begins with a site visit on foot. We arrive without measuring tools on the first day; we sit in the room, and we listen for what the room is already trying to do. Only on the second visit do we bring the steel tape, the moisture meter, and the small notebook of indigo-dyed paper that has become the studio's habit since 2003.

Our drawings are made by hand, in pencil and ink, on rolls of unbleached kōzo washi from the Iwano mill in Echizen. The choice is not romantic. Pencil on washi forgives a slow line; it admits hesitation; it lets a junction be drawn three times before it is decided. CAD enters the work only after the third reviewed sketch, and then only as a record — never as a proposition.

i.Full-scale mock-ups

For every commission above ¥3,000,000, the studio builds a full-scale mock-up of at least one principal joint or screen at the Nishijin workshop on Inokuma-dōri. The mock-up is built in the same species the final piece will use — usually Yoshino sugi or Kiso hinoki — and it is left to stand in the workshop for a minimum of fourteen days under varying humidity. We watch it. We do not photograph it. After two weeks we either approve the detail or we draw it again.

This step is the studio's single most expensive habit, and the one we will not give up. A mortise that looks correct at 1:10 can be wrong in a way the body recognises before the eye does — the shoulder too tight, the reveal a millimetre proud. Paper does not catch this. The hand catches it.

ii.Paper before wood

Washi enters the work very early — earlier than most studios would think reasonable. We sketch lighting on the same paper we intend to use in the finished lantern, so that the weight of the line and the weight of the shadow can be considered together. The paper from Iwano-san's mill is laid down on the bench beside the cedar offcuts, and we look at the two materials in the same north-facing light for several days before any cut is made.

This is also where colour is decided, if colour is to be used at all. Hakubai work is mostly uncoloured; when pigment is introduced, it is almost always sumi, occasionally indigo, and exactly once in twenty-six years a single line of cinnabar — on a tea-room threshold completed in Ōhara in 2017, at the client's request, to mark the date of a death.

iii.The five hands

No commission of any size leaves the studio without passing through at least three of our long-standing collaborators. Their work is described overleaf. We name them because the work is theirs as much as ours; the studio is, in honest accounting, a sixth chair at a table that was already set when we sat down.

§ 04    Collaborators

Five workshops the studio has worked with for longer than ten years.

A note Each commission passes through at minimum three of the five hands listed at right. Fees are billed transparently; the maker's name appears on the final delivery note alongside ours.
壱

Iwano Heizaburō Paper Mill

Washi · Echizen, Fukui

Ninth-generation kōzo washi maker on the Okamoto river. Supplies the studio's drawing rolls and all shoji and andon paper. Working relationship since 2001.

弐

Hidetaka Ōmori

Sashimono-shi · Sakyō-ku

Cabinet and joinery master, trained in the Kyō-sashimono lineage. Cuts every primary joint in the studio's furniture commissions. Working relationship since 2004.

参

Ren Kawabata

Blacksmith · Kameoka

Hand-forges hinges, kugikakushi nail-covers, and lantern frames in low-carbon tatara iron. The studio's only metals supplier since the 2008 Tōji teahouse.

肆

Mio Tsuda

Plasterer · Uji

Earth-and-lime walls in the Kyōto-kabe tradition, including the rare ōtsu polished finish. Has finished every wall in our tea-room work since 2011.

伍

Daisuke Yano

Lighting Engineer · Osaka

Quietly rigorous engineer of low-voltage and dimmable systems behind washi. Designed the bespoke 2700K driver used across our lantern series since 2013.

Apprenticeship
note

One apprentice, every three years.

The studio takes on a single apprentice on a three-year cycle. The cycle is not advertised; we accept letters by post throughout the year before, and we read them in February. The current apprentice, Kana Mori, joined in April 2023 and will complete her term in spring 2026.

The next opening is for the term beginning April 2026. Letters of interest, written by hand, may be sent to the studio address from October 2025 onward. We do not require a portfolio at first; we ask instead for a description of one room you remember well, in not more than four hundred words.
