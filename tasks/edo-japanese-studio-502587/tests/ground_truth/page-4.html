04Materials Reference

Wood, Paper,
and Iron.

The studio holds a small standing palette. Most commissions are built from materials we have used since 1998, sourced from a handful of suppliers we visit each season. We do not specify a material until we have held that batch in the workshop and watched it move for at least a fortnight.

What follows is a working register — current as of the autumn 2024 inventory — naming the timber yards, paper houses, and the single forge in Kameoka that supplies our hardware. Prices on request; lead times reflect what is honest, not what is marketed.

一 · I

WoodKi — Four Standing Species

All timber arrives air-dried at the supplier and is stickered for a further nine to eighteen months in our Nishijin store. We work no kiln-dried stock. Boards are selected board-by-board at the yard; we do not buy by photograph.

Hinoki · Kiso

Kiso Hinoki

Chamaecyparis obtusa

Pale, fragrant cypress from the Akasawa forest reserve in Nagano. Our standing supplier is the Itō yard in Agematsu, who release small quartersawn lots each March and October. Used for shōji frames, tokonoma posts, and the thin lid panels of our stationery boxes.

Allocation released: Mar · Oct
Grade: jōhin (上品)140-yr min.¥ on application
Sugi · Yoshino

Yoshino Sugi

Cryptomeria japonica

Tight-ringed cedar from the Kawakami valley in Nara, supplied by Nakai Mokuzai (est. 1907). The growth rings are exceptionally fine — twenty per centimetre is ordinary — owing to the dense planting tradition of Yoshino. We use it for cabinet carcasses, ceiling battens, and bath surrounds.

Mill cut: Nov–Feb only
Grade: itame & masame80–120 yrLead 6–14 wks
Yakusugi · Yakushima

Yakusugi (Reserved)

Cryptomeria japonica · sub-alpine

Dark, resinous, and irreplaceable. We hold a small reserve of doctored stumpwood — legally salvaged Edo-period felling waste, certified through the Yakushima Ringyō union. Reserved exclusively for screen frames and the framing of singular tea-room commissions. We do not cut it for cabinetry.

No new allocation 2024
Provenance: edo-bokkenCert. YR-2019/04Reserved stock
Kiri · Aizu

Aizu Kiri (Paulownia)

Paulownia tomentosa

The lightest cabinet wood in regular use, hygroscopic and almost odourless. Sourced from the Hasegawa mill in Aizu-Wakamatsu, who still hand-stack their boards under the eaves for two summers. We reserve it for drawers — chest interiors, document drawers, and the inner trays of bridal tansu.

Boards available year-round
Use: drawer liningsAir-dried 24 moLead 4–8 wks
二 · II

PaperKami — Three Standing Sheets

All washi in the studio is hand-formed (tesuki) and bears the maker's signature stamp. We do not work machine-made paper. Sheets are stored flat in cedar drawers at 55–60% relative humidity.

Kōzo · Echizen

Echizen Kōzo

Broussonetia kazinoki — long fibre

The studio's standard lampshade and shōji paper. Made by the workshop of Iwano Ichibei IX in Imadate, Fukui — a household designated Living National Treasure for paper-making. We order the 2.4-momme weight, undyed, in 88×178 cm sheets. The fibre is left long, giving the sheet its quiet cloud when lit.

Vat months: Jan–Mar
Iwano Ichibei kōbōStamp: 岩野市兵衛¥6,400 / sheet
Gampi · Echizen

Echizen Gampi

Diplomorpha sikokiana — wild-harvested

A finer, glassier sheet than kōzo, with the faint sheen of mica. Used for the inner panels of folding screens, conservation linings, and the sliding panels of our document cabinets. Gampi cannot be cultivated; the bast is harvested wild from the hills above Echizen and supply is therefore irregular.

Variable — confirm by season
Iwano Ichibei kōbōWild bast¥11,200 / sheet
Aizome washi · Aizu

Aizu Aizome Washi

Kōzo base · fermented indigo

Indigo-dyed paper from the Hoshina workshop in Aizu-Bange, dipped six to twelve times in a sukumo vat that has been alive in the same shed since the Meiji era. The depth of blue varies by dip count; we record it on the back of every sheet. Used as a backing paper in lighting, and as a singular accent in screen panels.

Dipping: May–Sep
Hoshina kōbō, Aizu6–12 dip grades¥8,800–14,000
三 · III

IronTetsu — A Single Forge

All hardware in the studio's work — every nail-head, pull, hinge plate, and lock-keep — is made by one smith. We have used no other forge since 2006.

Wakugi · Kameoka

Hand-Forged Nail-Heads

Wa-kugi · charcoal-forged

Square-shanked nails forged from tamahagane-grade bar iron at the Kawabata forge in Kameoka, west of Kyoto. Heads are hammered cold to the four standard studio profiles: round, square, chrysanthemum, and flat. We commission roughly 1,400 nails a year; the forge holds our pattern set.

Forge open: Tues–Sat
Ren Kawabata, smithForge est. 19624 head profiles
Hikite · Kameoka

Drawer Pulls & Hikite

Forged plate · waxed black finish

Recessed sliding-door pulls (hikite) and drop-ring drawer pulls. Each is forged to drawing, filed by hand, and finished in beeswax over heat-blackened oxide. Kawabata-san signs the back of every plate with a single chiselled mark — a habit since his apprenticeship under Kuroda Tetsuzō.

Lead time 8–16 wks
