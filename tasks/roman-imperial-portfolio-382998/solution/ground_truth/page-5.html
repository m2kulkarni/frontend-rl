MMXXIII — Two Thousand Twenty-Three

№ I
Essay

Travertine: Notes from a Quarry at Bagni di Tivoli

MMXXIII

The Classicist · No. 19 · Autumn 2023 · pp. 88–103

A field essay composed across three visits to the Sabini quarries northeast of Rome, where the same beds that faced the Colosseum still yield blocks for the studio's contemporary commissions. The piece examines how the stone's vacuoles, banding, and saw-cut orientation register the long timescales of architecture and ought to discipline the way we draw, specify, and detail with it today.

MMXXI — Two Thousand Twenty-One

№ II
Lecture

A Defense of the Axis

MMXXI

Yale School of Architecture · Hastings Hall · 14 October 2021

Delivered as the autumn Brendan Gill Lecture, this address argues that axial planning — long dismissed as authoritarian or merely scenographic — remains the most legible instrument architecture has for ordering ceremony, daylight, and bodily approach. Drawing on Pliny's Laurentine villa and three of the studio's recent houses, Vedrani sets out a working ethics for axes that are felt rather than enforced.

MMXIX — Two Thousand Nineteen

№ III
Essay

Concrete and Memory: Reading the Markets of Trajan

MMXIX

Log · Issue 47 · Autumn 2019 · pp. 142–157

A close reading of Apollodorus's hemicycle on the Quirinal, treating Roman opus caementicium not as a primitive forerunner of modern concrete but as a fully developed structural and mnemonic medium. The essay proposes that the Markets demonstrate how mass, void, and incident light can be composed without recourse to the orders, opening a quieter line of descent for classical practice.

MMXVIII — Two Thousand Eighteen

№ IV
Essay

On the Survival of the Pediment

MMXVIII

Common Edge · 22 March 2018 · online edition

Why does the pediment refuse to die? Vedrani traces its persistence from the temple front through the Federal courthouse to the suburban portico, arguing that the form survives because it answers a structural question about how a roof meets the sky — a question every building must still face whether or not its architect cares to admit it.
