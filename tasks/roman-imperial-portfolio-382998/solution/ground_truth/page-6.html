Studio & Contact

The atelier maintains practice in Rome and New York, in the conviction that classical architecture remains the most generous grammar by which to address the present.

I

Studium

De Architecto et Sociis

The atelier was founded in 2011 by Gaius Vedrani in a first-floor studio off the Via Giulia in Rome, with a stated commitment to the continuity of classical and traditional architecture. Vedrani took his Diploma di Laurea in Architettura at the Università di Roma "La Sapienza" in 2006, and a Master of Architecture at the Yale School of Architecture in 2009, where he studied under Robert A. M. Stern and was awarded the Henry Adams Medal.

The practice now occupies two studios — one beside the Tiber, one above Lafayette Street in lower Manhattan — and works on private houses, institutional commissions, urban infill, and slow restorations of the kind that resist the tempo of contemporary procurement.

We work as a small house, deliberately. Drawing is done at the board before it is done at the screen; mouldings are detailed at full size; and each project is led by a single architect from the first sketch to the last site visit. The studio currently numbers five, named below.
