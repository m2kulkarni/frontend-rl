Liber Quartus · Folio IV

Drawings & Studies

A working archive of measured drawings, comparative plates, and analytical studies produced at the studio. Each sheet is the residue of a long looking — at the antique, at the orders, at the geometry that survives in stone.

— Practica Hebdomadalis —

The studio reserves one full day each week for drawing from the antique. It is, in our view, the only durable form of architectural education: the silent measurement of cornice and column, the patient transcription of profile and proportion. The folios collected here span seven years of such Saturdays, undertaken in Rome, Tivoli, Pompeii, and on the drawing tables of West 22nd Street.

What follows is a small selection. The originals are kept in flat files and may be consulted by appointment.

Method of the Drawing Day

Every Saturday, beginning at the hour of nine, the studio convenes in person — in Rome at the long oak table on Via dei Coronari, in New York in the third-floor room overlooking 22nd Street. No screens are admitted. The week's chosen subject is set on the wall as a folio plate or photograph, and the drawing proceeds in graphite or ink to a fixed module.

The discipline is intentionally slow. A single capital may absorb a full morning; a complete order, two days. We hold that the architect's hand is the instrument by which proportion becomes intelligible — and that no rendered image can substitute for the steady transcription of an antique profile, line by careful line.
