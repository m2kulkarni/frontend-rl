Tending the cobalt and turquoise fabric of Safavid Iran — tile, manuscript, and garden.

Established in Isfahan in the spring of 1971 by the merchant-scholar Hossein Bagh-e Fin and three companions, the Foundation works to conserve the architectural and artistic heritage of the Safavid centuries (1501–1736) across the historic provinces of Isfahan, Kashan, and Ardabil.

The Work of the Foundation

Three medallions: of purpose, of present labour, and of patronage.

Mission

— Charter of the Foundation, 1971 —

To preserve, study, and publish the tilework, illuminated manuscripts, and garden architecture of the Safavid period; to train successive generations of master craftsmen in the techniques of haft-rang and moaraq; and to maintain the four-fold gardens of Iran in their living, watered form.

The Foundation operates without commercial purpose and accepts no government appropriation.

Current Projects

— Sheikh Lotfollah Dome Survey, 2024–2026 —

A two-year photogrammetric and conservation survey of the inner dome of the Masjid-i Shaykh Lutf Allah (1603–1619), conducted in concert with the Cultural Heritage Organisation of Iran and the University of Isfahan. Sixteen tilers and four stuccoists are presently in residence.

Concurrent works: the Ardabil shrine textile cabinet (Phase II); the Fin garden water-channel relining; manuscript conservation at the Chihil Sutun depository.

Patronage

— Founders, Friends & Fellows —

The Foundation is sustained by an endowment of £ 84.2 million, by the annual subscriptions of eleven hundred Friends in Iran, the United Kingdom, France, and Japan, and by the labours of its fellows in residence.

Annual fellowships of £ 18,000 are awarded each Nowruz to four emerging conservators of Persian arts. Application closes 21 March; see Grants & Fellowships.
