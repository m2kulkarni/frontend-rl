II. ✦ Mission & History

An Endowment for the Persian Arts

Founded in Isfahan in 1971 to safeguard the tile, the page, and the garden — three traces of the Safavid hand that endure where the hand itself has gone.

In the late spring of 1971, two scholars met in the courtyard of the Madrasa-ye Chahar Bagh and resolved that the spalling cobalt of Isfahan should not pass uncatalogued into another century. From that quiet conversation, the Bagh-e Fin Foundation was conceived.

Origins, 1971

The Foundation was incorporated on the fourteenth of Khordad 1350 (4 June 1971) by Dr. Manouchehr Sotoudeh, then Reader in Persian Material Culture at the University of Tehran, and Lady Helena Whitcombe-Pirzad, the British-born scholar of Islamic textiles whose 1968 monograph on Ardabil silks remains in print. Their initial endowment, a sum of £42,000 drawn from Lady Whitcombe-Pirzad's family trust and matched by a private grant from the Pahlavi Cultural Office, was deposited at Bank Melli on the corner of Chahar Bagh-e Bala and the proceeds directed at once toward salvage.

The first fieldwork — undertaken between July and October of that founding year — concerned the spandrel tilework of the southern iwan of the Maidan-e Naqsh-e Jahan. A January storm had loosened a section of the seventeenth-century mosaic above the Sheikh Lotfollah portal; thirty-eight kilograms of cobalt-and-turquoise haft-rang fragments were recovered from the scaffolding pans before the municipal restorers swept them to the dump. Sotoudeh personally drove the fragments, in two wooden crates, to a workshop on Hakim Street, where the tilemaster Ustad Reza Kashi-saz spent the following winter consolidating them. Twenty-two of those fragments are now displayed in the Foundation's Reading Hall; nine were returned, conserved, to the Maidan in 1979.

We do not own the tile. We are merely the hand that catches it as it falls. — Dr. Sotoudeh, in a letter to the Trustees, 1973

Method & Mandate

From its founding charter the Foundation has held to three objects, each phrased in the original Persian and English of the 1971 deed: the conservation of tilework of the Safavid and immediately post-Safavid periods (1501–1736 and the workshop continuities thereafter); the cataloguing and physical stabilization of manuscripts in private and waqf collections; and the preservation, where the Foundation is granted standing, of garden architecture — the chahar-bagh and its hydraulic infrastructure — across the cities of Isfahan, Kashan, and Ardabil. The mandate has not been amended in fifty-three years.

The Foundation does not acquire. It does not auction. It does not lend. It conserves in situ where possible, and where not possible, it underwrites the work of others — typically the provincial offices of the Iranian Cultural Heritage, Handicrafts and Tourism Organization, with whom the Foundation signed a standing memorandum in 1994 and renewed in 2019.

The Trustee Council Today

Governance rests with a four-person Trustee Council, drawn in alternating cycles from the academic, conservation, and legal professions. The current Council was seated on 21 March 2022, at the Nowruz convocation. Its members serve seven-year terms, are unpaid, and meet quarterly in the Foundation's offices on Kucheh-ye Bagh-e Fin, Isfahan. The Council is supported by a permanent staff of eleven, including two field conservators, an archivist, a manuscript binder trained at the Chester Beatty, and the Foundation's secretary, Mme. Farideh Rastegar, who has held the post since 1998.

An Axial Chronology, 1971 – 2024

— Five decades, four cities, one mandate —

Foundation & First Salvage

Sotoudeh and Whitcombe-Pirzad sign the founding deed; spandrel-tile recovery commences at the Maidan-e Naqsh-e Jahan within ninety days.

1971
1978

Kashan Office Opened

A second permanent office is established on Alavi Street to oversee work on the gardens of Bagh-e Fin proper, from which the Foundation takes its name.

The Quiet Years

Through the revolutionary period and the war that followed, the Foundation suspends fieldwork and concentrates on the indexing of its manuscript holdings — 1,184 folios catalogued by 1986.

1979–88
1994

Memorandum with ICHHTO

A standing concordat is signed permitting Foundation conservators to work alongside provincial heritage staff at twenty-three named sites across three provinces.

Sheikh Safi Shrine, Ardabil

The Foundation underwrites a four-year stabilization of the lustre-tile dado of the Chini-khaneh, a project completed under master conservator Bahram Yeganeh.

2003
2011

Fortieth-Anniversary Endowment

Principal grows to €18.4 million through the bequest of Lady Whitcombe-Pirzad. The annual fellowship programme is launched in her name.

Digital Folio Project

In partnership with the Bodleian, 2,300 folios from the Foundation's manuscript holdings are photographed in high resolution and made available without restriction.

2018
2024

The Bagh-e Fin Hydraulic Survey

A three-year, on-going study of the qanat that has fed the garden since 1590, undertaken with the Faculty of Architecture, University of Kashan.
