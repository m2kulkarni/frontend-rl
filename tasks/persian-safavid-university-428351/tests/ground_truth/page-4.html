The Kitābkhāna

Library of the Madrasa-yi Naqsh-i Jahān

Established by deed of waqf in MMVII and housed within the eastern arcade of the principal courtyard, the Kitābkhāna preserves approximately fourteen thousand two hundred bound volumes and three hundred and eighty manuscripts in Persian, Arabic, Ottoman Turkish, Armenian, and the European travellers' tongues. Its holdings answer the institute's chartered concern with the arts, sciences, and architecture of seventeenth-century Persia, and are arranged not by accession but by the nine traditional divisions of the Safavid royal library at Iṣfahān.

At the heart of the collection a single leaf — a folio from a Khamsa of Niẓāmī copied at Tabrīz circa 1539 and bearing the seal of the workshop of Sulṭān Muḥammad — is set under glass on permanent display in the apse of the Mirror-Hall. The remainder of the manuscript holdings, drawn principally from the bequests of the de Rossi family (1972) and the Pope-Ackerman estate (1989), are accessible to qualified readers upon application to the Keeper of Manuscripts.

The Mirror-Hall

Tālār-i Āyīna · After Chehel Sotoun

Adjoining the principal reading room, the Tālār-i Āyīna is a reproduction reading hall modelled at three-quarter scale upon the talar of Chehel Sotoun (1647). Eighteen slender wooden columns rise to a painted lacunar ceiling; the side walls bear a programme of mirror-mosaic executed by the studio of M. Behzādī (Iṣfahān, 2011–2014).

Twelve desks, each fitted with a copper hooded lamp and a velvet manuscript cradle, are reserved for the consultation of pre-1800 imprints and facsimile manuscripts. A reader may book a desk for a half-day or a full day; reservations close at 17:00 on the day prior.

Folio from a Khamsa of Niẓāmī

Tabrīz, ca. 1539 · Permanent Display · Apse of the Mirror-Hall

A single illuminated leaf — recto bearing twenty-one couplets in nasta'līq within an interlace cartouche, verso showing the assembly of Khusraw before Shīrīn beneath a turquoise īwān — bears the discreet seal of the royal workshop and is attributed on stylistic grounds to a hand within the orbit of Sulṭān Muḥammad. Acquired through the Pope-Ackerman bequest in 1989; conserved 2012 by the Bibliothèque nationale de France.

Featured Digital Collections

Open to scholars upon credentialed registration · Updated trimesterly
· I ·

Tilework Surveys of Iṣfahān

A photogrammetric record of the haft-rang and mosaic tilework of fourteen Safavid foundations in greater Iṣfahān, including the Masjid-i Shāh, the Lutfullāh, and the Chahār Bāgh madrasa. 6,420 plates at 600 dpi, with measured elevations.

Begun 2011 · 6,420 Plates
· II ·

The Girih Pattern Atlas

A vector catalogue of two hundred and forty-eight strapwork patterns drawn from monuments, manuscripts, and the surviving panj-kitāb of the royal workshops. Each plate is reducible to its underlying polygonal scaffold and rendered to construction lines.

248 Patterns · Constructed Diagrams
· III ·

Royal Workshop Inventories, 1587–1629

Transcriptions and translations of the surviving kārkhāna rolls of the reign of Shāh ʿAbbās I, drawn from the archives of the Āstān-i Quds at Mashhad and the Topkapı Sarayı. Indexed by craftsman, material, and patron.

Access for Visiting Scholars

Apply through the Office of the Keeper of Manuscripts · Reading Room III

I · Application

Submit a letter of introduction from a recognised university or learned society, together with a written research proposal not exceeding six hundred words, no fewer than twenty-eight days in advance.

II · Privileges

The visiting reader is granted four consecutive weeks of access to the principal reading room, the Mirror-Hall, and to all printed reference works. Manuscript consultation is admitted on a folio-by-folio request.

III · Decorum

Pencil only. Photography is permitted of post-1900 imprints; the reproduction of pre-1800 material requires the written consent of the Keeper. White cotton gloves are provided at the manuscript desks.
