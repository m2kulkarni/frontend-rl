Permanent Collection

Gallery Catalogue · Arranged by Dynasty
❋ ✦ ❋ ✦ ❋

The permanent holdings of the Museum, comprising one thousand two hundred and seven catalogued objects, are arranged across nine ground-floor galleries in chronological sequence from the rock-cut Pallava workshop of the seventh century to the polychrome timber-work of the late Nayak court. The catalogue below records the principal works on continuous display. Loan items, study fragments, and the bronze reserve are documented separately in the registrar's handlist, available to readers in the Saraswati Reading Room upon written application.

❖ Standing Notice ❖

The Bronze Gallery (Hall IV — Cholamandalam) is closed every Tuesday for routine conservation, climatic monitoring, and the rotation of textile mounts. Visitors holding Tuesday-dated tickets may exchange them at the front desk without surcharge.

I · Pallava Workshop

Mahabalipuram & Kanchi · 7th–9th Century

Fragment of a Rock-Cut Lion Pilaster

TMS.1962.114
Granite, 84 × 36 × 28 cm. Recovered from the Shore Temple precinct, 1958.
DonorGift of the Archaeological Survey of India, Madras Circle

Carved from coarse Pallava granite, this pilaster fragment preserves the standing yali whose forepaws once flanked a doorway in the Mahishasuramardini cave-complex. The lion's mane is rendered in stylised flame-curls, and traces of red ochre wash survive in the recessed channels — a rare witness to the polychrome finish that Pallava rock-cut work originally bore.

Hall I · Pallava Court · Vitrine A-3

Capital of the Five-Rathas Type

TMS.1965.022
Granite, 52 × 48 × 48 cm. Field-find from the Mahabalipuram littoral, 1962.
DonorBequest of Sir Arthur S. Crawford, K.C.I.E.

A lotus-bell capital with stepped abacus, exhibiting the characteristic Pallava transition from cubical brackets to the cushion-form found on the Dharmaraja Ratha. The undercut petals retain crisp tooling marks from the iron point-chisel; the abacus bears a faint masons' graffito reading Sri Narasimha in early Grantha characters.

II · Imperial Chola

Tanjavur & Gangaikondacholapuram · 9th–13th Century

Granite Base-Moulding, Adhisthana Section

TMS.1971.308
Granite, 116 × 44 × 38 cm. From a dismantled subsidiary shrine, Tiruvaiyaru.
DonorGift of the Tiruvaiyaru Devasthanam, 1969

A complete vertical section through a Chola temple plinth, exhibiting the classical sequence of upapitha, jagati, kumuda, and kantha. The torus moulding is finely dressed, and an inscribed band records a grant of three veli of land for daily lamp-oil. Of exceptional value as a teaching specimen for students of Dravidian moulding-grammar.

Hall III · Chola Stonework

Miniature Vimana Model in Granite

TMS.1968.501
Granite, 64 × 24 × 24 cm. Three diminishing storeys with crowning shikhara.
DonorAcquired from the estate of Pandit V. Subrahmanya Sastri

A votive vimana model, almost certainly a sculptor's exemplar from the Rajaraja-Brihadisvara workshop, carved in three precisely diminishing storeys with all the Chola elements in correct proportion: corner karnakuta, central panjara, and a domical shikhara crowned by an octagonal finial. Surface tooling consistent with the early eleventh century.

Hall III · Vitrine C-2

Bronze-Casting Moulds (Set of Four)

TMS.1974.117
Fired clay with wax residue, largest 38 × 22 cm. Swamimalai workshop find, 1973.
DonorGift of the Sthapati K. Devasenapathi & Family

A rare survival of the cire-perdue process: four investment moulds for an Uma-Sahita bronze, each broken away from the cast image yet retaining the negative impressions of the goddess's coiffure, tribhanga torsion, and lotus pedestal. The carbonised wax-residue has been radiocarbon-dated to AD 1042 ± 35.

III · The Pandya Continuation

Madurai & Tirunelveli · 13th–14th Century

Door-Jamb with Dvarapala Niche

TMS.1979.044
Granite, 192 × 48 × 32 cm. From a dismantled mandapa, Tirunelveli district.
DonorTransferred from the Madurai District Collectorate, 1978

A Pandya jamb of the late thirteenth century, displaying the characteristic deep-cut creeper-scroll along the inner edge and a niched dvarapala carved almost in the round, his trident raised and his lower garment falling in cascading folds. The Pandya manner here is unmistakably more linear and attenuated than its Chola precursor.

Hall V · Pandya Passage

Lintel Fragment with Fish Emblem

TMS.1981.213
Granite, 78 × 36 × 22 cm. Surface find, Madurai-Alagarkoil road, 1980.
DonorGift of Smt. Lakshmi Sundaresan, Madurai

A finely chamfered lintel fragment bearing the twin-fish device of the Pandya house in low relief, flanked by a pair of conventional makara heads disgorging foliate scrolls. The carving has been read by Prof. R. Champakalakshmi as a foundation marker for a minor shrine commissioned under Maravarman Sundara Pandya II.

IV · Vijayanagara at Hampi

Tungabhadra Court · 14th–16th Century

Yali-Pillar Capital

TMS.1984.077
Granite, 142 × 62 × 56 cm. Dated by inscription c. 1520. Hampi region.
DonorOn long loan from the Government of Karnataka

A composite yali-pillar capital from a thousand-pillared mandapa in the Hampi region, exhibiting the rampant leogryph trampling an elephant — a virtuoso conceit of the late Krishnadevaraya workshops. The mounted rider on the yali's back, though weather-worn, retains the characteristic Vijayanagara turban and stirruped foot. Inscription dates the piece to c. 1520.

Hall VI · The Hampi Pillared Court

Composite Pillar with Musical Notes

TMS.1986.092
Granite (single block), 268 × 38 × 38 cm. Cluster-shaft type with seven engaged colonnettes.
DonorGift of the Indian National Trust for Art & Cultural Heritage

A composite musical pillar of the type found at the Vitthala temple precinct, in which the seven engaged colonnettes are tuned to ring at progressive pitches when struck. Acoustic analysis conducted in 1991 confirms the surviving five colonnettes preserve intervals corresponding to the Carnatic Mayamalavagaula mode. Display protected by acoustic baffle.

Hall VI · Vitthala Annex

Carved Ceiling Panel — Garuda Roundel

TMS.1989.155
Schist, 88 × 88 × 14 cm. Dismantled ceiling slab, Hospet vicinity.
DonorGift of the Hospet Heritage Trust, 1988

A square ceiling slab carved with a deeply undercut Garuda within a sixteen-petalled lotus, the mandala framed by a rinceau of swans and creeper-scroll. The composition exemplifies the Vijayanagara taste for centralised mandala-ceilings, in which mythic narrative is subordinated to geometric order. Mounted overhead in the gallery for correct viewing.

Hall VI · Ceiling Vault

V · Nayak Polychromy

Madurai, Tanjavur & Senji · 16th–17th Century

Polychrome Ceiling Rosette

TMS.1992.041
Plastered timber with mineral pigments, diam. 96 cm, depth 12 cm. c. 1640.
DonorRemoved from a collapsed mandapa at Srivilliputtur and conserved in situ, 1991

A spectacular sixteen-petalled ceiling rosette painted in vermilion, peacock-blue, malachite green, and gold-leaf, with a central Vishnu reclining upon Ananta. The Nayak palette here is at its most uncompromising; recent pigment analysis identifies indigo, cinnabar, and orpiment among the original mineral grounds, all bound in tamarind-seed gum.

Hall VIII · The Painted Tiers

Painted Timber Bracket — Krishna Lila

TMS.1995.118
Carved teak with polychrome, 72 × 28 × 18 cm. c. 1640.
DonorGift of Mr & Mrs C. Rajagopalan, in memory of their son

An angled Nayak corbel-bracket of the curved-volute type, carved in teak and polychromed with a continuous frieze of Krishna-lila episodes — the lifting of Govardhana, the butter-theft, and the dance with Radha. The pigment survives at near-eighty percent, an unusually high figure for exposed timber, and the gilding of the figures' jewellery has not been re-applied.

Hall VIII · Bracket Wall

Ivory-Inlaid Sandalwood Doorframe

TMS.2001.063
Sandalwood with ivory and tortoiseshell inlay, 218 × 142 × 14 cm. Late seventeenth century.
DonorAcquired with funds from the Chettinad Heritage Endowment, 2000

A complete Nayak palace doorframe of considerable luxury, the sandalwood ground inlaid with ivory and tortoiseshell in interlaced lotus medallions and parrot-pairs. The lower panels depict a Tanjavur court procession with palanquins, war-elephants, and Portuguese visitors — a rare iconographic record of the cosmopolitan Nayak court at its zenith.
