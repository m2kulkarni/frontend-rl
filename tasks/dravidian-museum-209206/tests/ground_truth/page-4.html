Special Exhibition · East Wing

The Painted TiersNayak Polychromy Reconstructed

14 October 2024  ·  9 March 2025
Galleries IX–XII, East Wing  |  Curated by Dr. Lakshmi Ramaswami

Curatorial Notice

Seven full-scale tier reconstructions, executed in lime plaster on timber armatures and finished in mineral pigment, occupy the full length of the East Wing. The tiers stand to a maximum height of 6.4 metres and are illuminated by raking light to reveal the modelling of plaster ornament beneath the colour.

The exhibition is accompanied by a 240-page bilingual catalogue (Tamil / English), and a Saturday lecture series held in the Chola Reading Room at 16:00.

Dr. Lakshmi Ramaswami Guest Curator · École française d'Extrême-Orient, Pondicherry

From the Curator

The painted gopuram of the Nayak court has reached us, in nearly every surviving instance, as a ghost: weather-stripped, lichen-darkened, repainted by well-meaning hands in synthetic enamels, or — worst — scrubbed back to the bare lime in pursuit of a "purer" stone. The Painted Tiers is an attempt, undertaken jointly with the conservation laboratories of the École française d'Extrême-Orient at Pondicherry, to restore something of what the seventeenth-century pilgrim would have seen at Madurai, at Srivilliputhur, and at our own Brihadisvara complex during the great recommissioning campaigns of the Tirumalai Nayak court.

Seven tiers have been reconstructed in lime plaster at full architectural scale, each modelled directly from cleaned fragments and from the pigment-bearing strata recovered during the 2019 Madurai conservation. The colours were prepared in the museum's pigment laboratory according to documentary recipes preserved in the Silparatna and the Kasyapa Silpa: cinnabar ground in oil for the vermilion grounds, indigo over a copper-mineral underpaint for the deep peacock-blue robes of the celestial ranks, and pure leaf-gold laid in the Tanjore tradition over a red bole prepared from local laterite.

Visitors will find the tiers arranged in ascending order, from the yali-base register at floor level to the kalasha finial suspended in the apse. The accompanying didactic panels, in the south aisle, document the laboratory work: each panel pairs a fragment from the original monument with the matched modern pigment and the binding medium employed.

The Seven Reconstructed Tiers

Galleries IX through XII · in ascending order from yali-base to kalasha

Cat. No. PT–01 · Gallery IX

Yali-Base Register

The lowest tier, set with vyala and elephant figures in the Madurai manner. Lime plaster ground over teak armature; ferric reds and lamp-black, after fragments at the Pudu Mandapa.

Lime plaster · Mineral pigment · 1.8 m
Cat. No. PT–02 · Gallery IX

The Dvarapala Course

Twin guardians flanking the false aedicule. The peacock-blue dhotis are laid in indigo glaze over copper-mineral underpaint, a layered technique characteristic of the Tirumalai workshop after 1640.

Indigo over copper · 2.1 m
Cat. No. PT–03 · Gallery X

The Celestial Procession

A continuous frieze of gandharvas and apsaras above the third cornice. Gold-leaf jewellery is laid in the Tanjore tradition, on a red laterite bole; the figures are outlined in burnt-umber over a vermilion ground.

Gold leaf on red bole · 2.4 m
Cat. No. PT–04 · Gallery X

Narasimha Avatara Panel

The central narrative of the tier: the disembowelling of Hiranyakashipu. Reconstructed from a single surviving plaster fragment recovered at Srivilliputhur, with pigments matched by cross-section microscopy.

Reconstructed from 1 fragment · 2.6 m
Cat. No. PT–05 · Gallery XI

The Sapta-Matrika Tier

The seven mothers seated in shallow niches. Each garment is differentiated by pigment: cinnabar, peacock-blue, terre verte, and yellow ochre — establishing the colour-iconography of the Nayak gopuram.

Iconographic polychromy · 2.4 m
Cat. No. PT–06 · Gallery XII

The Sukanasa Cartouche

The penultimate tier, bearing the great parrot-beak ornament in profile. The flame-like halo is painted in a triple gradation of vermilion, scarlet, and gold-leaf, after a recipe in the Kasyapa Silpa.

Triple-gradation finish · 2.2 m
Cat. No. PT–07 · Gallery XII

The Kalasha Finial

Suspended at 6.4 metres beneath the apse skylight. Entirely gilded, with peacock-blue accents at the lotus throat. The internal armature has been left visible at one quadrant for didactic purposes.

Gilt finial · 6.4 m suspended · 1.4 m diameter
Cat. No. PT–08 · South Aisle

Pigment & Recipe Cabinet

A didactic vitrine displays the source minerals — cinnabar from Almadén, indigo cakes from the Tirunelveli district, and beaten gold-leaf prepared in the Tanjore workshop — alongside the binding media and brushes used for the tiers.

Didactic display · entry by docent
Cat. No. PT–09 · South Aisle

The Madurai Fragment Wall

Twenty-three original plaster fragments recovered during the 2019 conservation, arranged according to their position on the source monument and paired with the matched modern pigment swatch.

Original 17th-c. fragments · loan

The Three Cardinal Pigments

Vermilion

Ground from cinnabar in linseed oil, after the recipe preserved in the Silparatna. The grounds are laid in two thin coats over a chalked plaster.

Peacock-Blue

An indigo glaze drawn down over a copper-mineral underpaint — the layered technique of the Tirumalai Nayak workshop, characteristic of the years after 1640.

Gold-Leaf

Beaten gold laid in the Tanjore tradition, on a red bole prepared from local laterite. The leaf is burnished with an agate after twenty-four hours of curing.

Timed Entry · A Note for Visitors

Admission to The Painted Tiers is by free timed-entry ticket. Tickets are released at the East Wing reception desk each morning at 09:30, in person, on a first-come basis; we are unable to hold or pre-issue tickets by post or telephone. Each ticket admits one visitor to a thirty-minute viewing window. Late arrivals will be admitted at the docent's discretion. Sketching is permitted; flash photography and tripods are not. The exhibition is fully accessible to wheelchair users via the East Wing lift.
