A Founding Statement Upon the Stones

❖   OFFERED IN THE SEVENTY-EIGHTH YEAR   ❖

For seven centuries the artisans of the Cauvery delta — the sthapatis, the silpins, the master-bronzecasters of Swamimalai — laboured under sun and lamp to translate cosmology into granite. The Brihadeshwara at Tanjavur, raised by Rajaraja the Great in the year 1010, is not merely a building; it is the South's most exact instrument for measuring the heavens against the earth. This museum was founded to keep close company with that instrument.

Our collection is small and severe by design. We do not crowd the Chola bronze beside the Vijayanagara stucco; we do not let the painted ceiling of Madurai dilute the silence of a Pallava lintel. Each gallery is a register, each register a tier of the gopuram, and the visitor ascends as a pilgrim ascends — slowly, axially, and at the cost of breath. What is offered here is not spectacle but instruction.

I welcome you, in the name of the Trustees, to the Hall of the Vimana.

— R. SUBRAMANIAN AIYAR Director, the South Indian Antiquities Trust · Tanjavur, this monsoon

CURRENT EXHIBITIONS

Opened to the public the first day of Aippasi · the museum, daily save Mondays

Cat. № I · Gallery I

Granite & Grandeur: The Chola Vimana

Sixty-one objects in granite, schist, and basalt, drawn from the imperial Chola foundations of Tanjavur, Gangaikonda Cholapuram, and Darasuram. The centrepiece is a four-foot replica of the upper vimana register, with original lintel-fragments mounted in axial procession.

CuratorSmt. Lakshmi Venkataraman, F.A.S.I.

ThroughThe festival of Maha Sivaratri, A.D. 2026

Gallery I — The Granite Hall, ground register
Cat. № III · Gallery III

The Painted Tiers of Madurai

Nayak-period polychrome reconstructions assembled by the Trust over twelve seasons of fieldwork at the Meenakshi Sundareshwarar precinct. Eleven tier-segments, restored in vermilion, indigo, lampblack, and gold leaf according to the 1654 painters' manual of Tirumalai Nayak.

ConservatorSri V. Krishnamurthi, Govt. School of Art

ThroughPongal, the fifteenth day of Thai, 2026

Gallery III — The Polychrome Mandapa, upper tier
Cat. № V · Pillar Study Room

Yali, Makara, Hamsa: Beasts of the Mandapa

A study of the rampant yali, the aquatic makara, and the celestial hamsa as they appear upon the carved capitals of the thousand-pillared halls. Twenty-eight capitals in original stone, four in reproduction, and the nine-foot Tiruvarur lion-yali pillar entire.

In conversation withProf. K. R. Srinivasan's archive

A Notice from the Trustees

The Tanjavur Museum of Sacred Stone was instituted under the auspices of The South Indian Antiquities Trust, by deed dated the seventeenth day of August, in the year of grace one thousand nine hundred and forty-seven. The collection is held in perpetuity for the people of the Tamil country and for all students of the Dravidian temple.
