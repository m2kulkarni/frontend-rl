Prākāra · The Outer Enclosure of the Visitor

Visit the Museum

The Museum stands at 14, East Main Street, Tanjavur 613009, opposite the Sivaganga Garden, within the second prākāra of the old palace quarter. The four enclosure-galleries are arranged on the cardinal axes around a central pillared courtyard, after the manner of a Chola devakulika. What follows is the pilgrim's brief: hours of opening, the schedule of admission, the plan of the galleries, and the lesser pavilions for reading and refreshment.

Key to the Enclosure-Galleries

I · N
Gallery of the Granite Cholas. Lintels, dvārapālas and door-jambs from the imperial workshops of Rajaraja I and Rajendra; foundation-deposits from Gangaikondacholapuram.
II · E
Bronze of Swamimalai. Cast images of Naṭarāja, Somāskanda and the Saiva canon, arranged about a central pradakshina path. Lit by oil-lamp at dusk on the new moon.
III · S
The Painted Tiers — Nayak. Polychrome stucco fragments and ceiling-panels detached from the Subramanya shrine; the current special exhibition extends into this gallery.
IV · W
Stucco & Vimāna. The reconstruction of a five-tiered vimāna in plaster, with original Pallava-period brick cores and Vijayanagara overlays.
◉ M
Central Maṇḍapa. Open courtyard with four black-granite columns recovered from Tribhuvani; benches in teak. The visitor is asked to remove footwear before entry.
◐ R
Reading-Room (NW pavilion). By appointment only — see the note opposite.
◑ T
Tea-Pavilion (SE pavilion). Filter coffee, jasmine tea, and the Museum's almond-laddu, served between 11:00 and 17:00.
Register VIII — Notes for the Considerate Visitor
Cat. № V·5

Accessibility

The eastern gateway admits step-free access through a stone ramp of one in fourteen, laid in 2008 to honour the memory of the founding curator. All four enclosure-galleries are level upon a single granite platform; only the West Vimāna reconstruction requires the ascent of three shallow risers.

A wheeled chair of teak and cane, in the Tanjavur pattern, may be borrowed without charge from the Reception. Large-print catalogues in English and Tamil, and a tactile model of the central maṇḍapa cast in bronze, are kept in Gallery I beside the dvārapāla.

Assistance dogs are welcomed; a water-vessel of brass is set for them at the eastern threshold.

Sarvajana-Praveśa · For All
Cat. № V·6

The Reading-Room

Set within the north-west pavilion, the Reading-Room holds some four thousand volumes on Dravidian temple architecture, sculpture and bronze-casting, including the bequest of Professor K. R. Srinivasan and the complete Annual Reports of the Archaeological Survey of India from 1903 onward.

Admission is by appointment only, granted upon written application to the Reader in Residence (reading@tmss.in) at least seven working days in advance. Researchers are admitted Wednesday through Friday, 11:00 to 16:00.

Pencils only. Manuscripts are consulted in the inner cell, under the supervision of the Conservator.

Pustaka-Śālā · By Appointment
Cat. № V·7

The Tea-Pavilion

A small open-sided pavilion in the south-east corner, raised in 1994 upon the foundations of an older kitchen, serves filter coffee, jasmine and pepper-leaf teas, and a daily plate of southern sweets. The almond-laddu, prepared by the family of the late head cook, Smt. Lakshmi Ammal, is the celebrated speciality of the house.

Open from 11:00 to 17:00 on all days that the Museum itself is open. No outside food is permitted within the prākāra; water-flasks are an exception.

The pavilion is a place of rest and conversation — kindly observe a low voice in deference to the bronzes opposite.
