Founding of the Museum

Register the First · The Founding, 1947

An Engineer Among the Silpins

The museum was constituted by a Deed of Trust executed at Tanjavur on the fourteenth day of April, 1947, three months and three days before the Indian Republic itself drew its first sovereign breath. Its founder, the philologist and engineer V. Krishnaswami Pillai (1881–1958), bequeathed in that instrument the entirety of his personal collection — three hundred and fourteen architectural fragments gathered over four decades from the ruined mandapas of the Kaveri delta — together with his working library of two thousand two hundred volumes on Silpa Sastra, manuscript and printed, in Tamil, Sanskrit, English, and French.

Pillai had begun his career in 1903 as an assistant engineer with the Madras Public Works Department, where the survey of irrigation tanks brought him repeatedly into contact with collapsed temple compounds. By 1919 he had abandoned engineering entirely for the comparative study of the Manasara, the Mayamata, and the Kasyapa Silpasastra; by 1931 he was lecturing on the proportional canons of the Chola vimana at the University of Madras. The fragments he saved — capitals, yali brackets, painted stucco, half a dozen unfinished bronzes from the Tiruvarur foundry — were chosen not for their grandeur but for what they could teach.

The deed names three original co-trustees: the painter Maria Tiruvenkatachari of Pondicherry, the epigraphist Dr. R. Subramanian Aiyar, and Pillai's nephew, the bronze-caster S. Ramanathan. The first gallery, a converted granary in the precinct of the Brihadisvara, opened to the public on the morning of 12 October 1947 with an exhibition of forty-one Chola door-jamb fragments arranged according to the proportional system Pillai had reconstructed in his unpublished 1944 monograph.

Register the Second · Successive Directors

The Lineage of Custodians

From the founding to the present day, eight scholars have held the office of Director. Their tenures, with the principal works undertaken under each, are recorded below.

  1. V. Krishnaswami Pillai 1947 – 1958 · Founder-Director Constituted the original catalogue; published Proportional Canons of the Chola Vimana, 1953.
  2. Dr. R. Subramanian Aiyar 1958 – 1966 Acquired the Tiruvarur bronze hoard (1961); inaugurated the epigraphy gallery.
  3. Smt. Lakshmi Visvanathan 1966 – 1979 Oversaw construction of the Sandstone Wing (1972); first systematic conservation laboratory.
  4. Prof. C. Sivaramamurti II 1979 – 1988 Curated Pallava to Pandya: Five Centuries of the South, the museum's first international loan.
  5. Dr. Padma Kaimal-Ranganathan 1988 – 1999 Established the Painted Tiers programme; recovered fourteen Nayak ceiling panels from Madurai.
  6. Sri T. Vasantha Kumar 1999 – 2008 Digitised the manuscript collection; founded the Annual Silpa Lecture in memory of the founder.
  7. Dr. Anuradha Mahalingam 2008 – 2019 Oversaw the seismic retrofitting of the Granite Hall; published the revised general catalogue (2017).
  8. Prof. K. Ramaswamy Naidu 2019 – present · Director Currently directing the Painted Tiers exhibition and the recataloguing of the Pillai Bequest.

Colophon · Patronage & Acknowledgement

Of Patrons & Sustaining Bodies

The museum is sustained, in equal measure, by endowment income from the Pillai Bequest and by the continuing patronage of three institutions, whose contributions of grant, scholarship, and counsel are here recorded with gratitude.

South Indian Antiquities Trust

Madras & Tanjavur · principal endowment partner since 1962, and present underwriter of the conservation laboratory.

Ministry of Culture

Government of India · supports the digitisation of the Silpa Sastra library and the seismic retrofitting programme.

Serfoji Memorial Foundation

Of His Highness the Tanjavur Maharaja Serfoji II · grants the use of the East Pavilion and the Saraswati Mahal documents.
