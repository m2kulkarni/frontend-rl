The Chettinad Hearth

Mandapa Two · Karaikudi Fire-Kitchen

Across the inner courtyard, beyond the temple-tank well, lies our second mandapa — the aduppu adi, the foot of the hearth — where stone mortars crush peppercorn at dawn, seer-fish arrives by 5:14 AM Pondicherry train, and a single recipe ledger from 1962 still governs every spice ratio served. Here we cook only Chettinad.

A Charter of 1962

The Annamalai Compact, Kanadukathan

In the monsoon of 1962, our founder Sri Subbaiyer Krishnamoorthy travelled by bullock-cart from Mylapore to the Chettinad village of Kanadukathan, where he sat for forty-one days at the kitchen of the Annamalai household — a Nattukottai Chettiar lineage whose grand-aunt Smt. Meenakshi Achi was reputed to crush her own peppercorn against a black-granite ammikkal brought from Pillaiyarpatti.

Their compact, signed in palm-leaf and witnessed by the village kalyana mandapam committee, granted Annapurna Bhavan the right — and the obligation — to cook the Annamalai household's recipes faithfully, in their original spice ratios, in any city the kitchen should travel to. The recipes below are kept in a single bound ledger, opened each morning at 4:30 AM by our hearth-keeper, Sri Murugan Annamalai, great-nephew of Smt. Meenakshi.

Because the Chettinad fire is non-vegetarian, it sits in its own mandapa — separate stone, separate copper, separate water-pot, separate cook. The thali kitchen and the hearth do not share a knife.

The Five Dishes of the Hearth

Carried from Karaikudi · Cooked at 11 AM & 7 PM Daily

Kozhi Chettinad

₹ 480

Country chicken simmered in a roasted masala of crushed black peppercorn, star anise, and roasted coconut. Finished with curry-leaf tempering in cold-pressed gingelly oil. Served with two parottas or three idiappams.

Dry-spice profile
Marathi moggu, kalpasi (black stone-flower), anasipoo (star anise), peppercorn ×3
Recipe origin
Karaikudi · Annamalai household, 1962 ledger fol. 4r
Cooked in
Tinned copper uruli, wood-fire only

Meen Kuzhambu

₹ 540

Seer fish (vanjaram) from Rameswaram, slow-stewed in a tamarind and gingelly-oil gravy darkened with shallot, fenugreek, and the small dry red chilli of Ramanathapuram. The gravy thickens overnight; we serve only the second-day pot.

Dry-spice profile
Fenugreek, kalpasi, Marathi moggu, dry chilli (Ramnad gundu)
Recipe origin
Devakottai · received from Smt. Visalakshi Achi, 1968 addendum
Fish arrival
5:14 AM Pondicherry Express, daily except Tuesday

Nandu Masala

₹ 720

Mud-crab from the Pichavaram backwaters, cracked in the shell and smothered in a Karaikudi masala of roasted poppy, cashew, and anasipoo. Eaten by hand with steamed kuzhi paniyaram. Limited to nine portions per service.

Dry-spice profile
Anasipoo, kalpasi, white poppy, fennel, Marathi moggu
Recipe origin
Kanadukathan · Annamalai kitchen, original 1962 ledger fol. 11v
Service note
Pre-order by 4 PM · finger-bowl with lime served
❦ ❖ ❦

Aattukal Paya

₹ 420

Goat-trotter broth, set on the hearth at midnight and drawn at 8 AM the following morning, thickened with roasted coconut milk and a delicate aniseed tempering. Served with appam at breakfast and with idiappam at dinner. The Sunday version is darker, with extra peppercorn.

Dry-spice profile
Fennel, peppercorn, cinnamon, kalpasi (light hand)
Recipe origin
Pallathur · Sri R. Annamalai's grandmother, 1971 transcription
Cook time
Eight hours, slow charcoal, never aluminium
❦ ❖ ❦

The Varuval Platter

₹ 880

A dry-fry tasting board — three meats finished without gravy, plated on a banana leaf with small heaps of Chettinad podis. Kozhi Varuval (chicken, crushed pepper), Aattirachi Varuval (mutton, fennel and curry-leaf), and Eral Varuval (Tuticorin prawn, anasipoo and coconut shavings). Served with a small lacquered bowl of kuzhipaniyaram and a wedge of country lime.

Dry-spice profile
Kalpasi, Marathi moggu, anasipoo, fennel, peppercorn, dry chilli — all six pillars of the Chettinad masala

Composite · Kanadukathan, Devakottai & Pallathur · ledger fol. 19r–20v
Service
One platter feeds two · banana leaf served only after ritual washing of the leaf

The Three Pillars of Karaikudi

A Reader's Note on the Dry-Spice Cabinet

Marathi Moggu

Kapok bud · Bombax ceiba

A small, woody flower-bud, dark and clove-like in appearance but bitterer on the tongue. We receive 4 kg every fortnight from a single supplier in Theni district. Bloomed in hot oil before the onion is added; never powdered.

Theni · Tamil Nadu

Kalpasi

Black stone-flower · Parmotrema perlatum

A papery lichen scraped from boulders in the Eastern Ghats. Smoky and almost charred when toasted, it is the secret behind the deep colour of every Chettinad gravy. The Annamalai ledger calls it "the breath of the rock".

Kolli Hills · Salem district

Anasipoo

Star anise · Illicium verum

Eight-pointed and aromatic, used sparingly — one whole flower per kilogram of meat. The ledger insists on the smaller, darker variety brought down from Coorg, never the large Vietnamese type. Crushed by hand at the moment of tempering.

Madikeri · Coorg / Kodagu
