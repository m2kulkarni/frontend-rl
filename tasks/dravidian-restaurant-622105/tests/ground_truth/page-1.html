A WELCOME FROM THE PROPRIETOR

Smt. Kalyani Subramaniam · Third-generation custodian

Seventy-eight years ago, on the morning India woke to freedom, my grandfather Krishnaswami Iyer set out three brass tiffin-pots on a wooden table behind the Kapaleeshwarar tank. He sold thirty-eight idlis and a pot of milagai-podi before noon, and that little tiffin counter on East Mada Street became the seed of the hall in which you now sit. He cooked, my father Subramaniam ground the masalas, and my mother Lakshmi tied the banana-leaves into bundles before dawn — a household, never a business.

In 1968 we crossed Kutchery Road into the present pillared mandapa, raised by the sthapati Ganapathi Sthapati's first apprentices, with carved teak columns brought from Karaikudi and a granite floor cooled by two stone wells beneath. The hall has fed Carnatic vidwans and tank-bund pilgrims, schoolchildren and Marghazhi devotees, in unbroken succession through three generations. Our recipes have travelled but never changed: the sambar still arachuvitta on the same ammikkal, the rasam tempered in the same blackened iron eeya-chombu, the curd set in the same five clay pots that came with my mother's dowry.

Sit, take leaf, and be at home. The cook in the kitchen is the same cook who fed your grandfathers — only her name has changed.

— Kalyani Subramaniam Proprietor & Head of the Kitchen, since 1991
