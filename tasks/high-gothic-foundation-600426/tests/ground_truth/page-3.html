Of the Third House · Capitulum Tertium

The Almshouse at Rue des Changes

A house of twelve beds, kept by the Foundation since the Feast of Saint Denis in the year of grace MCCLXXXIII, for the comfort of the broken-handed and the widowed of our craft.

Of the Hall & the Manner of Its Keeping

Twelve Beds, One Hearth, One Chapel

The almshouse standeth upon the Rue des Changes, the third house from the corner of the Pont aux Changeurs, hard by the cathedral close. It is a long stone hall of two and forty feet by eighteen, raised from the common stock of the Foundation in the year MCCLXXXIII at a charge of seventy-eight livres tournois, and roofed with timbers of oak felled in the woods of Beauvoir and given freely by the seigneur Hugues de Vaillant, brother of our then warden.

Within the hall stand twelve beds of pine, each furnished with a straw pallet, a sheet of bleached linen, and a coverlet of undyed wool from the looms of the Sisters of Saint Catherine at Pontoise. In the great hearth at the eastern wall, a fire is kept from the first frost of Saint Martin's day until the Octave of Easter; thereunto the matron addeth one log of beech at sunrise and one at vespers.

Twice in the day are bread and pottage set upon the long trestle: at the third hour after sunrise, white bread of two pounds with a soup of leek, oats, and salt-pork; and at the ninth hour, a portion of rye loaf with cheese of the country, or, on fast-days, a pottage of pease and almond-milk. Wine of Auxerre, watered to the third part, is given on the great feasts and to the infirm by order of the matron.

At the western end is the small chapel dedicated to Saint Gervais, holding an altar of stone, two waxen tapers, a panel painting of the Virgin enthroned (the gift of the widow Aleyde of Mantes, MCCXCIV), and a single lancet window glazed by master Renaud of Chartres, fellow of this Foundation, showing the martyrdom of our patron in five medallions of cobalt and madder. Mass is sung therein each Sunday and on the four quarter days by Père Anselme, chaplain of the Foundation, who receiveth six deniers per mass from the common purse.

Of Admittance & the Present Household

The Conditions of Admittance

No soul cometh under our roof save by the seal of the warden and the assent of two brethren of the Chapter, and only of three sorts, set down by the elder statutes of MCCLXIX:

  • Widows of glaziers who died in the good standing of the craft, and who have not since taken a second husband nor entered religion.
  • Infirm masters past their working years, whose hands or eyes have failed them at the bench, and who have served the craft no fewer than thirty winters.
  • Orphaned apprentices under the age of fourteen, whose master and kin alike are dead, until such time as they may be placed at the Foundation's school.

Each new resident bringeth with him only the cloak upon his back; the house provideth all else. None shall be turned forth except for the breaking of the rule of the hall, and that only by sentence of the warden read aloud before the Chapter.

The Matron & Her Attendants

Dame Petronille of Bonneval

Matron of the House · instated upon the feast of Saint Luke, MCCXCI

Dame Petronille, widow of the master glazier Estienne of Bonneval (who furnished the south clerestory of the cathedral of Sens), keepeth the keys, the linen-press, the bread-account, and the discipline of the hall. She receiveth from the Foundation an honorarium of forty-two sous parisis at each of the four quarter days, and the use of the chamber above the chapel. Of her hand are written the daily roll, the sick-bed list, and the weekly accompt of grain.
