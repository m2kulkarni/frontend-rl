Capitulum Secundum

Of Our Charter
& Purpose

Wherein is set forth the founding, the endowment,
and the threefold office of this Foundation.

✿
Sealed at Beauvais
upon the eve of
Pentecost,
Anno Domini
MCCXLVII.

Forasmuch as it pleased Almighty God to gather together, in the cathedral towns of northern France, certain craftsmen learned in the cutting, leading, and painting of glass, and forasmuch as the labour of those craftsmen sustains the windows wherethrough the light of Heaven is figured unto the faithful, this Foundation was instituted to safeguard their persons in age and infirmity, to nourish their apprentices in youth, and to remember their souls when they are gathered to their fathers.

The Foundation was chartered upon the second Sunday after Easter, the seventeenth day of May in the year of grace one thousand two hundred and forty-seven, under the hand and seal of the Reverend Father in God Hugues de Saint-Cher, by the grace of the Apostolic See Bishop of Beauvais and Ordinary of these dioceses. The instrument was witnessed by the Dean of the Chapter, the Provost of the Glaziers' Guild of the Rue des Changes, and four masters of the cathedral lodge.

The original endowment was furnished out of the common chest of the cathedral chapter at the sum of forty livres parisis, payable in two terms at Saint Martin in winter and at the feast of the Ascension; together with the rents of two messuages adjoining the south porch, the use of a small kiln-house behind the chevet, and a yearly tithe of four ells of broadcloth from the chapter's draper. To these were added, in the autumn of the same year, the legacy of the goldsmith Pierre Vaillant of three score livres for the perpetual repair of the almshouse roof.

The Threefold Bond

✿

By the terms of the said charter, the Foundation is bound, under pain of forfeiture and the censure of the Ordinary, to keep and to maintain three offices, neither suspending the one nor neglecting the others, but rendering each in its due season and with such diligence as becomes a charitable house dedicated to Saint Gervais and the holy company of the glasswrights.

The Almshouse

✿

The first office is the keeping of the almshouse upon the rue des Changes, twelve beds in number, for old or maimed glaziers and their widows; bread, pottage, and a daily measure of small beer to be furnished, with a chaplain in attendance.

The School of Glass

✿

The second office is the apprentice school adjoining the south transept workshop, where eight boys, the sons of poor freemen, are bound for seven years to learn the cutting of pot-metal, the leading of cames, and the trace and matt of figured glass.

The Mass of Saint Lucy

✿

The third office is a yearly Mass for departed glaziers, sung upon the feast of Saint Lucy, the thirteenth day of December, the patroness of light. The names of the year's dead are read from the bede-roll, and twelve tapers of beeswax burn before the altar.

Hugo, miseratione divina Belvacensis episcopus, dilectis in Christo filiis universis ad quos praesentes litterae pervenerint, salutem in Domino sempiternam. Notum facimus quod, considerantes inopiam et laborem fidelium artificum vitri, qui per fenestras nostrarum ecclesiarum lucem Dei figurant, fundavimus et ordinavimus, de consilio et assensu capituli nostri, hanc piam Fundationem sub titulo et patrocinio beati Gervasii martyris: ad sustentandum hospitium pauperum vitrearum in vico Cambii, ad nutriendum scholam discipulorum juxta officinam transepti australis, et ad celebrandam annuatim missam pro animabus defunctorum vitrearum in festo beatae Luciae virginis. Quae omnia rata habere et inviolabiliter custodire volumus, sub poena censurae ecclesiasticae. — Datum Belvaci, anno gratiae M.CC.XL.VII.

The Present Council of Three

By statute, the Foundation is governed in temporal matters by three persons: a master of the craft, a widow learned in the trade, and a religious of an approved house. The seats are held for life, or until release by the Ordinary.
