Wherein six youths are bound under the masters of the chapter, that the noble craft of glaziery may not perish from the cathedral towns.

Six at a Time

No more than six apprentices may dwell at once within the school, that each may have proper instruction in the cutting of quarries and the laying of the leads.

Of the School of Glass

✦ Schola Vitrearia Sancti Gervasii ✦

From the founding of this house in the year of grace MCCXLVII, it hath been ordained by the chapter that six apprentices, and no more, shall at one time be bound under the masters of glaziery within our walls. Each youth, upon reaching his twelfth year, may be presented at the feast of Saint Gervais by his father, his guardian, or by a brother of the trade, and if found of sound limb, of sober mien, and of sufficient quickness in the matter of the eye and the hand, shall be received into the indenture of seven years.

The boy is sworn upon the iron-bound Gospel that lieth in the chapel, his right hand laid upon the painted figure of Saint Luke. He is then signed upon the brow with the smoke of the firing-kiln, in token that he hath put off his former estate and entered into the company of the glasswrights. From that day he weareth the russet tunic of the school, eateth at the lower table of the almshouse, and sleepeth in the dorter above the cutting-floor.

His master receiveth, by the constitution of the Foundation, the sum of iiij sols parisis by the quarter for his board and instruction, and a further xij deniers for the provision of lead, of solder, and of the small iron tools that the boy shall break or lose in the first three years. Upon the completion of the seventh year, the apprentice is examined before the master and two wardens of the chapter; and if he hath shewn himself worthy, he shall be given the freedom of the trade and the gift of a journeyman's panel, hereafter described.

It is moreover provided that no apprentice shall be turned away for poverty, neither shall any be received who hath not first heard the Mass of Saint Catherine sung in our chapel; for she is, after our patron, the second protectress of those who labour with the wheel and the diamond.

Of the Curriculum

✦ The Four Hands of the Glazier ✦

Of Cutting Quarries

⚜

The first year is given over wholly to the diamond and the grozing-iron. The boy must learn to score the white glass of Chartres true to the cartoon, to break the lozenge clean, and to nibble the edge to the line of lead.

Of Firing Grisaille

⚜

In the third year he is taught the mixture of iron oxide, of gum, and of wine vinegar; the laying of the brush upon the glass; and the long vigil at the kiln, by which the painted line is fixed unto the panel forever.

Of Leading Panels

⚜

By the fifth year the apprentice is set to the lead-came, the soldering-iron, and the bench of pegs. He learneth the H-section and the U-section, and to draw the lead through the stretcher till it lieth straight as a quill.

Of Reading Cartoons

⚜

In the seventh and final year the journeyman-to-be is taught to read the master's cartoon upon the whitewashed bench, to apportion the colours, and to set the figures of saints and donors in their proper attitudes.

The Journeyman's Panel

✦ Tabula Vitri in Signum Libertatis ✦

Upon the day of his release, which is customarily the morrow of Saint John ante Portam Latinam, the apprentice presenteth before the chapter a panel of his own devising, no smaller than a foot and a half square, in which he must shew the four hands of his craft: a quarry cut and grozed, a figure painted in grisaille and silver-stain, a border of lead led true to the bench, and a small inscription read from a cartoon of the master's setting.

This panel, being approved by the wardens — namely Maistre Aubert, Maistre Renaud, and a third elected from the chapter at large — is then set into a window of the cloister, where it remaineth unto the end of the journeyman's life as witness of his admission to the trade. The Foundation provideth the white glass and the lead at no cost; the colours, the silver-stain, and the iron oxide must the youth procure of his own purse, that he may know the price of his art.

It is further the custom that, at the same hour, the new journeyman maketh gift of two deniers to the almshouse and a candle of one pound's weight to the altar of Saint Gervais; and he is then embraced by his master, by his fellow apprentices, and by the warden of the school, and so dismissed into the wider company of the glaziers of France.
