Of Pilgrimage & Visitation

To Visit the
Maison de Verre

Whoso comes in good faith, whether pilgrim, patron, or apprentice's kin, shall find our doors open at the hours appointed below.

The Way to the Maison

The seat of our Foundation, the Maison de Verre, stands upon the rue des Changes in the burgh of Chartres, two streets due south of the cathedral's Royal Portal. Whosoever approaches by the western gate shall find the cathedral's twin spires for his guide; let him pass beneath the porch of kings, turn to his right hand by the cloister wall, and descend the rue des Changes by the count of two streets.

The house is known by its leaden door inset with a roundel of cobalt glass, above which is graven, in letters of gilt, the cross flory of Saint Gervais and the year of our charter, Anno Domini MCCXLVII. Travellers arriving by the Orléans road may enquire at the sign of the Three Stoups; those by the Paris road, at the wayside chapel of Saint Lubin.

Of Hours & Liberties

Our gates stand open from the ringing of Prime unto the ringing of Sext upon every weekday, that pilgrims and almsgivers may have leisure to make their devotions, deliver their offerings, or entreat with the brethren upon matters of charity. Upon the Lord's Day and upon the great feasts of the Church, the Maison is closed to common visitation, that the household may keep their vigils.

Apprentice Workshop
Wednesdays only

The chapel of Saint Gervais, builded into the south wing of the Maison, remains unbarred to pilgrims at every hour of the day and the night, for the relief of penitents and the prayers of those who travel late. A taper is kept burning before the relic-shrine of the saint's right hand.

Upon Wednesdays alone, by the goodwill of the master glazier, the apprentice workshop may be observed from the south gallery; let visitors keep silence, that the youths' labour at furnace and lead-came be not disturbed.

Of Correspondence & Farewell

For all matters of correspondence — bequests, petitions, the entry of new apprentices, or the lodging of pilgrims of station — let letters be addressed unto our procurator, Frère Othon de Beauvais, who keeps the seal and the rolls of the house. He receives writs daily after the office of Tierce, and dispatches replies by the cathedral's couriers upon Fridays.

Should any goodly soul wish to be remembered in our daily prayers, or to inscribe the name of a departed kinsman upon the obit-roll of the chapel, the procurator shall make provision, for an offering of three deniers tournois.
