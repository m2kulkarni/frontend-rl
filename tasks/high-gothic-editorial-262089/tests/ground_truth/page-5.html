On the Workshop

A statement of intent, set down from rooms above a bookbinder on the Rue du Cloître.

The Lancet Quarterly was founded in the autumn of 2019 by Aldous Renier and Cosima Auerbach — he, latterly of the École des Chartes; she, latterly of the conservation studio at Sens — as, in their own phrase, “a slow review for a slow architecture.” The first number was set on a single Linotype borrowed from a printer in Saint-Mandé, bound in twelve copies, and posted to friends. The eighteenth number, which you now hold, is bound in two thousand four hundred.

We are edited from three rooms on the second floor of No. 9, Rue du Cloître Notre-Dame, in the IVe arrondissement of Paris, above the workshop of M. Étienne Vasseur, bookbinder, who has tolerated our footfall and our cigarette smoke since the first proofs of Vol. I were corrected on his cutting bench. The Quarterly takes its name from the lancet — that narrow pointed window of the early thirteenth century — because we believe a long review, like a long window, is meant to admit slow light.

We do not chase newness. We publish four times a year, on the quarter days, and we devote each number to one cathedral, one craft, or one open question of medieval making. We commission essays of seven thousand words, drawings measured from the stone, and photographs taken in November light. We pay our contributors. We refuse advertising. We are read, so far as we can tell, by glaziers, masons, archivists, parish priests, and a small and steadfast number of readers who have never seen Chartres.

The Editorial Board

Six readers, severally appointed; two-year terms, renewable once.

Editor & Co-Founder

Aldous Renier

historian of fabric & archive

Lately archiviste-paléographe at the École des Chartes; author of The Cutting Yard at Reims, 1211–1274 (Picard, 2017). Reads at his desk by north light.

Editor & Co-Founder

Cosima Auerbach

conservator of glass & pigment

Trained in the conservation studio of the Cathédrale Saint-Étienne de Sens; specialist in cobalt-blue glass of the second half of the twelfth century. Editor of plates.

Consulting Editor

Mathilde Le Goff

paleographer · École des Chartes

Reader in Latin codicology at the École nationale des Chartes, rue de Richelieu; author of the standard catalogue of the Saint-Denis necrologies, 1140–1265.

Consulting Editor

Sir Henry Whitfield

art historian · The Courtauld

Emeritus Professor at the Courtauld Institute, London; biographer of the Master of the Pucelle Hours and editor of the Whitfield Compendium of English Glaziers.

Consulting Editor

Eleanor Faversham

master glazier · the Glaziers' Trust, York

Master glazier and director of the conservation programme of the Glaziers' Trust at York; supervised the 2014–2019 re-leading of the Five Sisters Window in the north transept of York Minster.

Consulting Editor

Father Bertrand Caillou

canon & liturgical scholar, Beauvais

Canon of the Cathedral of Saint-Pierre, Beauvais; reads on the use of Sarum and the choir-rite of the northern French diocese. Writes on the meaning of unfinished vaults.

A Brief Foundation

Set down on the year of our cutting.

2019
MMXIX · Anno I

The Founding

Renier and Auerbach issue Vol. I, No. 1 — twelve copies, sewn by hand. The first essay treats the cobalt of the Belle Verrière at Chartres.

2021
MMXXI · Anno III

The Editorial Board

The Quarterly seats its first board of six. Father Caillou writes on unfinished vaulting; Whitfield delivers the Pucelle lecture.

2023
MMXXIII · Anno V

The Plate Series

The drawings volume is launched; full-size measured elevations of the chapter-house at Wells are given as inserts in Vol. V, No. 2.

2024
MMXXIV · Anno VI

The Present Number

2,400 copies. 184 pages. Subscribers in 27 countries, the most distant being a parish glazier in Hobart, Tasmania.

Colophon

Set in Fraunces (display) and Source Sans 3 (text). Printed letterpress for the cover; offset for the body. Sewn in signatures of sixteen, bound flat-back in walnut cloth over boards. Each number is sealed with the workshop mark in cobalt and gold.
