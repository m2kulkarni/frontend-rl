Wines & Amari of Rome

Beneath the trattoria, in a vaulted travertine room dug into the flank of the Aventine, we keep some four hundred labels — Lazio first, Campania always near at hand, and a long ink-black shelf of amari for after the bill is paid.

A Word from the Sommelier — Marco Bellini

A wine list, properly built, is a small piece of geography. Ours begins, as Roman dinners do, on the volcanic hills around the city: the tufa benches of Frascati, the basalt slopes of the Castelli Romani, and — pushing east — the limestone of Piglio where Cesanese has quietly become the great red of Lazio.

From there it is only a short carriage south to Campania, where Greco and Aglianico keep memory of the Greek and Samnite vineyards Pliny himself catalogued. After dinner, the cellar turns inland: the bittersweet liturgy of amari, distilled by friars, alpine guides and Trastevere chemists. Pour yours over a single ice cube and consider it the last course.

Lazio

The Volcanic Hills of Rome

Frascati Superiore

Castel de Paolis · 2022 €42

Tufa & salt — Malvasia Puntinata leads, lifted by a thread of bitter almond. Drinks like the breeze off Lake Albano in late September.

Cesanese del Piglio

Damiano Ciolli, “Silene” · 2020 €58

Wild herb & iron — the great red of the Ciociaria. Sour cherry, crushed thyme, and the faint smoke of a wood oven. Decant thirty minutes.

Bellone

Marco Carpineti, “Capolemole” · 2021 €48

Yellow plum & chamomile — an old Roman grape rescued from oblivion. Pairs with anchovy crostini and patience.

Trebbiano di Lazio

Sergio Mottura, “Latour” · 2019 €72

Honeyed restraint — aged in barrique without losing its tufa nerve. Our pour for cacio e pepe and long conversations.

Campania

Volcano, Sea, and Apennine

Greco di Tufo

Pietracupa · 2021 €64

Sulphur & pear — grown on the sulfurous tufa of Tufo itself. Mineral, almost saline, with a long bitter-pith finish that anchors fritto misto.

Taurasi

Mastroberardino, “Radici” · 2017 €95

The Barolo of the South — Aglianico from Apennine slate. Tar, leather, dried fig. Built to outlive the meal and most of its drinkers.

Fiano di Avellino

Ciro Picariello, “906” · 2020 €68

Hazelnut & beeswax — high-altitude Fiano with the structure of a white Burgundy. Astonishing with our coda alla vaccinara.

Falanghina del Sannio

La Guardiense · 2022 €38

Citrus zest & broom — the everyday Roman lunch wine of two centuries ago. Cold, bright, ungrudging.

Amari

The Roman Bitter Shelf

Amaro Formidabile

Roma · 34% €11

Citrus & gentian — a young Roman amaro built around bitter orange and rhubarb root. Bright, almost insolent. Take it neat at room temperature.

Nardini Amaro

Bassano del Grappa · 31% €9

The grandfather — distilled since 1779. Mint, peppermint, gentian, a long chestnut-honey finish. Order it with a strip of orange peel.

Amaro Sibilla

Varnelli, Marche · 34% €12

Honey & smoke — made over open flame in copper, sweetened with mountain honey. The most contemplative pour on the shelf.

Fernet-Branca Riserva

Milano · 40% €10

For after the espresso — menthol, myrrh, saffron. The waiter’s drink, and yours if you mean it.

By the Glass · By the Carafe

A rotating selection of fifteen wines is poured by the glass each evening, in measures of 125 ml. The house carafe — a half-litre of whatever the cellar has too much of, and is right for the season — is €14, and remains the most honest order on the list.

Corkage from your own cellar is €25 per bottle, waived if Marco has not heard of the producer.
