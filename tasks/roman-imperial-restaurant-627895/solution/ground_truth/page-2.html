Trattoria Aventina

The Carta

A Roman table, by season. Our carta is rewritten each Friday after the morning at Mercato di Testaccio, where we choose what the week will hold — agretti in March, puntarelle in November, the first artichokes of February. Pasta is hand-rolled before service every day on the marble counter you can see from the dining room. Bread is baked at four; broth is begun at six.

Spring Carta · MMXXV · Chef Lorenzo Fanti

I

Antipasti

Primo passo — small plates to begin, served on warm ceramic.

  • Carciofi alla Giudia I 14

    Twice-fried artichoke — Romanesco artichoke from the Lazio coast, opened like a flower, fried in olive oil from Sabina, finished with sea salt and a single drop of lemon.

  • Suppli al Telefono II,III,IV 9

    Roman rice croquette — arborio cooked in tomato and beef ragù, a heart of fior di latte that pulls into long strings when you break it open.

  • Fiori di Zucca I,II,III,V 12

    Stuffed zucchini blossoms — with mozzarella di bufala and a single anchovy from Cetara, dipped in a beer batter and fried until just translucent.

  • Puntarelle alla Romana I,V 11

    Curled chicory — dressed at the table with garlic, anchovy, white wine vinegar, and Sabina olive oil. Served only while the season holds.

  • Vignarola I 13

    Spring vegetable stew — fava, peas, artichoke and Romaine braised together with mint and spring onion. The dish that signals April has arrived.

  • Tonnarelli di Pane II,III 8

    Bread of the house — sourdough loaf baked at sixteen-hundred each afternoon, served warm with cultured butter and Trapani salt.

II

Primi

La pasta — rolled by hand each morning on the marble counter.

  • Cacio e Pepe II,III,IV 18

    Tonnarelli — Pecorino Romano DOP aged twelve months, Sarawak black pepper toasted to order, finished tableside with the pasta water that gives the sauce its silver sheen.

  • Carbonara II,III,IV 19

    Rigatoni — guanciale from Amatrice rendered slowly, yolks from Az. Agr. Paganelli, Pecorino Romano, no cream — and never any. The way it has always been done.

  • Amatriciana II,III 19

    Bucatini — San Marzano tomato, guanciale, white wine, Pecorino. Cooked in a heavy iron pan until the sauce clings to every strand.

  • Gricia II,III,IV 18

    Mezze maniche — the elder of the Roman pastas: guanciale, Pecorino, pepper. No tomato, no onion. A study in three ingredients.

  • Gnocchi al Sugo di Coda II,III,IV 22

    Thursday gnocchi — potato gnocchi rolled to order, dressed with oxtail braised five hours with celery, cocoa, and pine nut. Served only on Thursdays, by Roman tradition.

  • Tonnarelli ai Ricci di Mare II,IV,V,VI 28

    Sea urchin from Civitavecchia — tossed warm with butter, lemon zest, and a thread of bottarga di muggine. Limited to twelve plates a service.

  • Fettuccine ai Funghi I,II,III,IV 21

    Wild mushroom — egg fettuccine cut wide, porcini and chiodini foraged in the Aniene valley, butter, thyme, a dusting of aged Parmigiano.

  • Minestra di Arzilla II,V 17

    Skate and broccoli broth — the old Roman Friday soup: skate wing, romanesco broccoli, anchovy, broken pasta. Served in a deep bowl with a spoon for the broth.

III

Secondi

Il piatto forte — meat, fish, and the deep cuts of the Roman quinto quarto.

  • Saltimbocca alla Romana III 26

    Veal, sage, prosciutto — three thin escalopes pinned with toothpicks, seared in butter and finished with a splash of Frascati. Served with a wedge of lemon and nothing else.

  • Coda alla Vaccinara III 28

    Oxtail of the butchers — braised five hours in tomato, celery, dark cocoa, and pine nuts, in the manner of the men who once worked the Mattatoio across the river.

  • Abbacchio Scottadito 29

    Spring lamb chops — grilled over wood embers, rosemary, salt, lemon — eaten with the fingers, hot enough to scorch them, as the name promises.

  • Trippa alla Romana III 22

    Saturday tripe — beef honeycomb tripe simmered with tomato, mint, and Pecorino. Served only on Saturdays. Order it once and you understand.

  • Baccalà in Guazzetto V 25

    Salt cod — soaked three days, gently stewed with tomato, raisins, pine nuts, and a single bay leaf. Served with grilled country bread.

  • Pollo coi Peperoni 23

    Ferragosto chicken — free-range bird from Tuscia, browned with garlic, tomato, white wine, and yellow and red peppers stewed until they melt.

  • Cicoria Ripassata I 7

    Side of bitter greens — wild chicory boiled, then taken back through the pan with garlic, chili, and oil. The proper accompaniment to anything from the grill.

  • Patate al Forno I 7

    Roast potatoes — Viterbo potatoes cut in irregular wedges, rosemary, sea salt, roasted on a heavy tray until the corners are dark.

IV

Dolci

L'ultimo — the closing line of the meal, kept short on purpose.

  • Tiramisù della Casa II,III,IV 10

    House tiramisù — savoiardi from Roscioli, mascarpone whipped to order, espresso pulled at the bar, a single dust of cocoa from Modica. No liqueur.

  • Maritozzo con la Panna II,III,IV 8

    Roman cream bun — a sweet bread split through the middle and packed with cold whipped cream from the Apennines. Eaten with both hands.

  • Crostata di Visciole II,III,IV 9

    Sour cherry tart — short pastry filled with ricotta from the Monti Sabini and visciole conserve made by Sora Lella in September. A lattice top, baked dark.

  • Panna Cotta al Miele III,VII 9

    Honey panna cotta — set the night before with chestnut honey from Cassino, finished with a few crushed Avellino hazelnuts and a thread of saba.

  • Sorbetto al Limone 7

    Amalfi lemon sorbet — spun fresh in the morning, served in a chilled coupe with a small pour of cold prosecco for those who want it.

  • Formaggi e Mostarda III,VII 14

    Three Lazio cheeses — Pecorino Romano DOP, Caciofiore della Campagna Romana, Marzolina di capra, with our own quince mostarda and walnut bread.
