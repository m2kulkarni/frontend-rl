The Workshop of the
World, 1501–1736

Beneath cobalt vaults and turquoise spandrels, Dār al-Naqsh gathers the surviving ornament of the Safavid centuries — tilework, lacquer, and the illuminated page — and presents them as one continuous craft of the line.

· Of the Museum's Mission ·

Dār al-Naqsh is devoted to the ornamental arts of Safavid Iran, from the accession of Shah Ismāʿīl in 1501 to the fall of Sultan Husayn in 1736. Across four galleries and a working library, the museum gathers and interprets the tilework of Isfahan's great squares, the lacquer pen-cases and book-bindings of the royal workshops, and the illuminated manuscript arts — among them the dispersed folios of the Shāhnāma-yi Shāhī.

Our holdings are arranged not by reign but by hand — the cutter of cuerda seca tile, the binder, the gilder of margins — so that the visitor may follow a single line as it travels from the spandrel to the page.

— Curatorial Statement, edition of 2024 —

The Cobalt Field

Tile Programs of Isfahan

Cuerda-seca and haft-rang panels lifted from the Masjid-i Shāh and the Lutfullāh cycle, displayed at the height of the original dado. A long bench permits study of the cobalt ground at one arm's reach.

II

Brushes of the Royal Kitābkhāna

Manuscripts & Albums

Folios attributed to Sulṭān Muḥammad, Mīr Sayyid ʿAlī, and the workshop of Riza ʿAbbāsī — gathered with their gilded margins, ruled cartouches, and the single-figure studies that defined a century of looking.

III

Chehel Sotūn

Mirrors & Verandas

A reconstructed corner of the talar — wooden columns, mirrored ceilings, and the great wall-paintings of court receptions — interprets the open pavilion as the Safavid emblem of welcome.

Gallery III1647–1706

Of the Founding

Dār al-Naqsh was established in 1962 by the Aqa-Riza Foundation, formed in memory of the painter and calligrapher Riza ʿAbbāsī, that the ornament of the Safavid centuries might be studied in the city of its making. The first galleries opened in a converted caravanserai on the western flank of the Maydān; the present halls, raised in 1978, follow the proportions of the Lutfullāh dome at half-scale.

The collection numbers some 2,140 objects across tile, paint, paper, lacquer, and textile, of which roughly one third is on continuous view. The remainder rotates by season, by the hand of the curator on duty.

— Aqa-Riza Foundation · Isfahan · Tehran · Paris —
