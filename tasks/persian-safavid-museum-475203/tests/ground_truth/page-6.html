About Dār al-Naqsh
The Museum of Safavid Ornament

Founded in 1962 within the restored merchant house of Khaneh-ye Sarrafian, the museum is devoted to the tilework, manuscripts, and architectural fragments of Iran from the reign of Shāh Ismāʿīl I to the fall of Shāh Sulṭān Ḥusayn.

The Founding

Khaneh-ye Sarrafian · 1962

The museum was inaugurated on the seventh of Khordād, 1341 AH (28 May 1962), in the central courtyard of Khaneh-ye Sarrafian, a late-Safavid merchant residence built between 1698 and 1714 for the moneychanger Mīrzā Ismāʿīl Sarrafian. The building had stood derelict since the 1920s, its tālār roof collapsed and its painted muqarnas blackened by a coal merchant's tenancy.

Restoration was directed by the architect Ostad Isa Bahadori from 1958 to 1962, with masons from the Qazvin workshop of Hassan Tabatabai. The recovered seven-colour haft-rang tile panels of the eastern īwān, dated by inscription to 1108 AH (1696 CE), form the core of what is today Gallery III.

The institution was placed under the joint trusteeship of the Ministry of Culture and the Anjoman-e Āthār-e Mellī (Society for the National Heritage), an arrangement that has continued, in modified form, into the present. Its founding charter — drafted by the historian Mehdi Bayani — restricts acquisition to objects produced in Iran between 907 AH (1501 CE) and 1148 AH (1736 CE), a discipline rare among Iranian museums and one to which the institution still adheres.

"The house was found a ruin and given a second life as a vessel for the ornament of its own century." — Mehdi Bayani, Founding Charter, 1962

Principal Directors

Six custodianships, 1962 to the present
1962 — 1981

Hossein Mirjalali

Founding Director

Calligrapher and archivist, formerly of the Golestan Palace Library. Established the museum's seven founding galleries, the first conservation atelier, and the principle of acquisition by region rather than dynasty.

1981 — 1993

Farideh Sadeghin

Director & Tile Curator

Oversaw the museum through the years of the Iran–Iraq war, securing the collection in the basement vaults of the Sarrafian house and reopening to the public in 1986 with the landmark exhibition Cuerda Seca and Beyond.

1993 — 2002

Dr. Mohsen Ashtiani

Director

Trained at the Sorbonne; introduced the museum's first published catalogue raisonné of kitāb-e khāna manuscripts in 1997, and inaugurated the Naqsh Lecture in 1998 in honour of Ostad Isa Bahadori.

2002 — 2008

Bijan Rouhani

Director

Architect and conservator. Initiated the long partnership with the Aga Khan Trust for Culture in 2004, and supervised the structural reinforcement of the Sarrafian tālār, completed in 2007.

2008 — 2014

Dr. Shirin Neshat-Pour

Director

Manuscript scholar; curated The Painters of Tabriz (2010) and Shāh ʿAbbās & the Frontiers (2013). Established the public reading room of the Royal Kitābkhāna in the second courtyard.

2014 — Present

Dr. Parvaneh Tabatabai

Current Director

Architectural historian, formerly Senior Fellow at the Aga Khan Documentation Center, MIT. Author of The Inner Dome (2019) and curator of the present exhibition. Leads the museum's tenth-decade campaign of digital cataloguing.

Conservation & Partnership

A practice in three workshops

Aga Khan Trust for Culture

Since 2004, the museum has maintained a continuous conservation partnership with the Aga Khan Trust for Culture, formalised by a memorandum signed in Geneva on 14 March of that year. Joint campaigns have stabilised the muqarnas vault of Gallery V, treated 318 lustreware fragments from the Ardabil deposit, and trained twelve Iranian conservators at the Trust's Historic Cities Programme in Cairo and Lahore.

The 2017 — 2021 campaign, co-directed with conservator Hossein Sadr, returned to view the long-buried haft-rang dado of the second courtyard, presented for the first time in the present exhibition.

The Atelier of Tilework

The museum's in-house atelier, on the upper floor of the Sarrafian east wing, employs four full-time conservators and admits two doctoral fellows each year through a joint programme with the University of Tehran's Faculty of Fine Arts. Each fellowship runs eighteen months and culminates in a fragment-by-fragment study deposited in the museum's archive.

Additional support is provided by the Anjoman-e Āthār-e Mellī, by private endowment from the Foroughi family of Isfahan, and, since 2019, by the Iran Heritage Foundation, London.

The Annual Naqsh Lecture

In honour of Ostad Isa Bahadori, established 1998

An evening of architectural reflection

Held each year on the last Thursday of Mehr · since 1998

The Naqsh Lecture was founded by Dr. Mohsen Ashtiani in 1998 to honour the memory of Ostad Isa Bahadori (1894 — 1973), the architect-restorer who returned the Sarrafian house to use and who first taught the museum's masons the discipline of haft-rang tile setting. The lecture is delivered in the central courtyard at sunset and admits one hundred and twenty guests by ballot, free of charge.

It is followed by a reading from a Safavid manuscript chosen by the director and a service of chāy in the second courtyard.
