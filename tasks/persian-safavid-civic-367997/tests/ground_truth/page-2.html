Folio II · About the Bureau

Council & Mandate

Nine seats, four stewardships, and one charter — the form by which Isfahan's quiet preservation is undertaken, debated, and recorded.

The Bureau is a small body by ordinance and by intention. Its work is to keep what Isfahan has already perfected — domes, water-courses, gardens, and the long-standing methods of their care — and to refuse, where the city's fabric is concerned, the impulse toward novelty for its own sake. Membership is fixed at nine: a Chair, two Deputies, and six Stewards drawn in rotation from the four standing chambers of Tilework, Hydraulics, Gardens, and Archives. Each seat carries a four-year term, staggered so that no chamber is ever wholly renewed at once.

Sessions of the full Council are held on the second and fourth Tuesday of each month at the Old Customs House on Ostandari Street, beginning at 14:30 and concluding by sunset. Eight of the nine annual deliberations are open to the public; minutes from all sessions are deposited within ten working days in the Bureau's reading room and republished in the Folio of Notices. The Council does not legislate. It advises the Office of the Governor, issues binding heritage-permits within the historic precincts, and arbitrates restoration disputes between contracting parties and licensed ateliers.

A Brief History

From Charter to Reorganisation

The Bureau traces its present form to the Municipal Charter of 21 Ordibehesht 1313 (May 1934), under which the Office of Heritage Stewardship was first separated from the general Public Works Directorate. The original mandate was narrow: to record and protect the surviving Safavid monuments of the Maydān, the Chahār Bāgh, and the bridges of the Zāyandeh-Rūd. A staff of seven, including two surveyors and a single tile-restorer, was deemed sufficient.

By the late 1970s the Bureau had grown unwieldy and dispersed; restoration files were held in three different buildings and the chain of authority between the Bureau, the Governor's office, and the national antiquities service was no longer legible. In Mehr 1390 (October 2011) the Council was formally reconstituted under Ordinance 1390/IX-44, which established the present nine-seat structure, the four standing chambers, and the rotating Public Liaison seat.

Principal Dates

1934
Municipal Charter establishes the Office of Heritage Stewardship within Isfahan's town hall.
1956
Old Customs House on Ostandari Street is assigned as the Bureau's permanent seat.
1979
The Council suspended for sixteen months; archives sealed and later restored intact.
1994
Naqsh-i Jahān inscribed on the UNESCO list; the Bureau becomes the local liaison body.
2011
Reorganisation under Ordinance 1390/IX-44 — the present nine-seat Council is constituted.
2018
Digitisation of the 1934–1979 ordinance series commences under Steward Hāshemī.
The Bureau's Pledge

Three Commitments

Set down at the close of the 2011 reorganisation and reaffirmed at every fourth-year transfer of the Chair. They are read aloud at the opening of each Council year.

  1. Symmetry

    That works of restoration shall preserve the central axis of every façade, courtyard, and water-channel; that no addition shall be undertaken which the eye cannot read as balanced from the principal vantage.

  2. Durability

    That methods and materials shall favour the lifespan of generations rather than the convenience of a season; that traditional techniques shall be specified wherever a documented precedent exists.

  3. Public Access

    That the records, hearings, and physical precincts under the Bureau's care shall remain open to the citizens of Isfahan, save where conservation or safety requires temporary closure published in advance.
