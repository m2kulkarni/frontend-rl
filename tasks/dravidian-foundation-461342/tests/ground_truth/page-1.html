◆ Programme I · Conservation

The Srirangam Prakara Survey

Tiruchirappalli 2022 — 2026

A four-season structural and epigraphic survey of the seven concentric prakaras of the Ranganathaswamy temple at Srirangam, with particular attention to the Vellai, Akalankan and Chitra streets. Joint stewardship with the Archaeological Survey of India (Tiruchi Circle).

Cataloguing 1,247 inscriptions, mapping seepage damage in granite mandapas, and underwriting the cleaning of polychrome ceiling panels in the Sesharaya hall.

◆ Programme II · Manuscript

Chettinad Manuscript Recovery

Sivaganga & Pudukkottai 2021 — 2027

Recovery, deacidification and digital facsimile of palm-leaf and paper manuscripts held in the private agraharams and Nagarathar households of Karaikudi, Athangudi and Kothamangalam. Working in partnership with the École française d'Extrême-Orient.

To date, 11,400 folios have been conserved at the Trust's Tiruvanaikoil laboratory, including a complete Mahabhasya in seventeen bundles and a sixteenth-century Saiva agama copy.

◆ Programme III · Documentation

Hampi Pillared-Hall Records

Vijayanagara, Karnataka 2023 — 2028

Photogrammetric and measured documentation of the surviving thousand-pillar mandapas of the Vitthala, Achyutaraya and Krishna temple complexes at Hampi — including yali brackets, composite columns and the granite resonance pillars.

A reference corpus for the next generation of stapathis. The drawings will be deposited with the IGNCA at New Delhi and the Trust's library at Thanjavur, freely consultable.

◆ Our Mandate

What we have undertaken to keep

The Padmavati Heritage Trust was constituted in Thanjavur on the eleventh of April, 1987, under the Indian Trusts Act, with a mandate that is at once narrow and exacting: to underwrite the conservation of the granite, painted plaster and palm-leaf inheritance of the Dravidian temple, and to ensure that the guilds whose hands made it are not the last to do so.

We do not own monuments. We fund the silpis, stapathis, conservators, epigraphists and apprentices who work upon them — through field grants, multi-year stipends, the Stapathi Fellowship, and a small but adequate library and archive open to scholars by appointment.

◆ The 2024 — 2027 Endowment Drive

A corpus to outlive the trustees who raise it.

Over the next three years the Trust seeks to raise a permanent corpus of ₹ 18 crore, the income from which will fund three perpetual stapathi fellowships, a paid manuscript-conservator post at Tiruvanaikoil, and a standing emergency fund for cyclone-damaged temple structures along the Coromandel.
