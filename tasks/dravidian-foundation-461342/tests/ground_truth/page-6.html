Visit & Correspond

The Trust's reference library, photographic archive, and reading room are housed in the Madhavan Pillai Building at our Thanjavur office. Scholars, donors, and temple committees are warmly invited to call upon us by appointment.

I · The Three Facilities

Library, Archive & Reading Room

Bay I · Reference Holdings

The Reference Library

Approximately 11,400 volumes on Dravidian temple architecture, agamic ritual, sthapatya-veda, manuscript studies, and the iconography of the Chola, Pandya, and Vijayanagara periods. Includes the bequeathed libraries of K. R. Srinivasan (1996) and Pierre-Sylvain Filliozat (2014).

Open consultation; no reader's ticket required for printed holdings. Photocopying at ₹3 per page.

Bay II · Photographic Plates

The Veeraswami Archive

The Trust's photographic archive comprises 8,400 plates documenting active and ruined temple sites across Tamil Nadu, southern Karnataka, and Kerala. Begun in 1962 by V. Veeraswami Iyer and continued through field campaigns concluding in 2019.

8,400Plates · 1962–2019
312Sites Catalogued

High-resolution scans (600 dpi) supplied to bona-fide researchers at ₹240 per plate. Allow ten working days for fulfilment.

Bay III · Restricted Access

The Reading Room

A small panelled room of fourteen desks, reserved for credentialled researchers consulting palm-leaf manuscripts, original photographic plates, and the Trust's correspondence files. Access by appointment only.

Bay IV · Travel Directions

From Thanjavur Junction

  1. On exit from the railway station, bear left across the forecourt towards the auto-rickshaw stand at South Rampart Road.
  2. Engage a metered auto-rickshaw for the Brihadeeswarar Temple east gopuram; the standard fare is ₹80 to ₹110, journey roughly 9 minutes.
  3. From the east gopuram proceed north on East Main Street for 320 metres. The Madhavan Pillai Building stands on the right, opposite the Saraswathi Mahal Library compound wall.
  4. Enter through the blue-painted teak doorway bearing the lotus seal of the Trust. The reception is on the ground floor, the library on the first.
Please Note

On Correspondence Concerning Active Conservation Sites

Enquiries from temple committees regarding ongoing field programmes — including the Tiruchchirappalli Rockfort consolidation, the Darasuram bronze re-housing, and the Madurai stucco recovery — should be addressed directly to the relevant Project Convenor. The reception desk will route correspondence on request, but a delay of 7–10 working days should be anticipated during the festival months of Aippasi and Margazhi.

◆ Invitation to Correspond ◆
A Closing Bay

The Trust Welcomes Correspondence

The Padmavati Heritage Trust is, in its quiet daily working, an institution sustained by letters. We invite enquiry, opinion, and report from those whose interests run alongside our own — and we undertake to reply to every letter received, in the order in which it arrives, with such candour and detail as our office permits.

  • Scholars On readership applications, archive consultations, and proposals for joint publication.
  • Donors & Bequestors On endowed chairs, named fellowships, and the conditions attaching to charitable transfers.
  • Temple Committees On condition reports, conservation petitions, and the dispatch of stapathis from the guild rolls.
