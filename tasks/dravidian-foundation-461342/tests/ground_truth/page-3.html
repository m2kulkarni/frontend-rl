Stewards of stone, palm-leaf, and the unbroken hand

i. Founding & Continuity

A Half-Century of Quiet Stewardship

1971 — The Founding Bequest
1971

Dr. Krishnaswamy Iyer

The Trust was constituted on the third of October 1971 by deed of bequest executed at Thanjavur by Dr. M. Krishnaswamy Iyer (1921–2003), late Reader in Indian Antiquities, Presidency College, Madras. The founding corpus comprised his personal library of 4,118 palm-leaf manuscripts, the family house at East Main Street, and the sum of ₹3,75,000 then settled upon five trustees in perpetuity.

His instruction to the first board was a single line, copied still on every annual report: "That the hand which carved Brihadeeswara shall not, in our generation, fall idle."

1996 — The Ramaswamy Expansion
1996

V. Ramaswamy, ICS (retd.)

Under the chairmanship of Sri V. Ramaswamy, ICS (retd.), formerly of the Madras Presidency cadre, the Trust was reconstituted by supplementary deed on 14 February 1996. The board was enlarged from five to nine members; the field-grants programme was instituted; and the Trust took on its first conservation contracts at the Airavatesvara temple, Darasuram, and the Saraswathi Mahal manuscript library.

The 1996 deed introduced the present three-tier governance: a working board, an academic advisory panel, and a separate audit committee answerable directly to the chair.

2018 — The Present Board
2018

Prof. Anjali Subramaniam

Since the spring of 2018 the Trust has been chaired by Prof. Anjali Subramaniam, Directrice d'études at the École française d'Extrême-Orient, Pondicherry, and a former Fellow of the Institute of Advanced Study, Nantes. Under her direction the board was expanded to its present strength of eleven, and the Stapathi Fellowship Programme was placed on a permanent endowed footing in 2021.

The eleven-member composition is fixed by the 2018 supplemental deed; vacancies are filled by co-option, ratified at the annual general meeting each November at Thanjavur.

ii. The Eleven-Member Board

Trustees in Office, 2024–2025

The Board, in present session

Eleven trustees, drawn from the disciplines of epigraphy, structural conservation, manuscript studies, temple administration, and chartered accountancy. The chair, vice-chair, and treasurer are honorary; six trustees serve renewable five-year terms; two are nominated by the Saraswathi Mahal Library and one by the Tamil Nadu Hindu Religious & Charitable Endowments Department.

iii. Governance & Probity

The Order of Our Affairs

Tax Recognition

80G Status

The Trust holds permanent recognition under Section 80G(5)(vi) of the Income-Tax Act, 1961, vide order of the Commissioner of Income Tax (Exemptions), Chennai, dated 22 March 2002, renewed without lapse to date. All donations from Indian taxpayers are eligible for fifty per cent deduction.

For donations from foreign donors, the Trust holds active registration under the Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act, 2010 (Reg. No. 075890341), with State Bank of India, Thanjavur Main Branch, designated as the FCRA-Mandatory account.

Annual Audit

Brahmayya & Co.

The accounts of the Trust have been audited continuously since 1971 by M/s Brahmayya & Co., Chartered Accountants of Chennai, established 1933. The audited statement is laid before the trustees in November each year and published, unredacted, in the annual report.

  • Statutory audit completed by 30 September annually
  • Internal audit by rotation panel each quarter
  • FCRA returns filed by 31 December
  • Independent project audit for grants above ₹10 lakh
Procedures

Meetings & Quorum

The board meets four times in each financial year, traditionally in the months of March, July, September, and November, the November sitting being the annual general meeting at which the audit and accounts are received.

  • Quorum: six trustees, of whom one must be the chair or vice-chair
  • Decisions by simple majority; the chair holds a casting vote
  • All resolutions minuted in the trust register
  • Conflicts of interest declared at the head of each sitting

v. Published Code of Conduct

The Trust's Standing Rules for Site Interventions

First codified by Sri V. Ramaswamy in 1998 and revised at the 2019 sitting of the board, the following ten clauses bind every officer, fellow, and contracted craftsperson working under the Trust's name on any site, library, or workshop in our care.

  1. No intervention shall remove original fabric where consolidation in situ is structurally possible.
  2. All replacement stone shall be quarried within a hundred kilometres of the original quarry, where the source can be identified.
  3. Lime mortar shall be prepared on site, slaked for not less than ninety days, and tinted only with mineral pigments.
  4. Iron, cement, and synthetic resin are forbidden in all primary structural work; epoxies are permitted only for laboratory-grade manuscript repair.
  5. Inscriptions, even fragmentary, shall not be cleaned by abrasive method; consultation with the epigraphy trustee is mandatory.
  6. Living guild traditions shall be engaged in preference to outside contractors wherever the relevant guild remains active.
  7. The hereditary stapathi or silpi shall be paid not less than the union day-rate for skilled labour, plus a craft premium of one third.
  8. Every intervention shall be photographed before, during, and after; the record is deposited at the Saraswathi Mahal Library within ninety days.
  9. No object, fragment, or manuscript shall be removed from its place of origin except for treatment, and shall be returned within eighteen months.
  10. Where doubt exists between competing claims of conservation, the older practice shall prevail over the newer theory.
