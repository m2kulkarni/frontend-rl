A Living Apprenticeship

The Stapathi Fellowship Programme is the Trust's flagship human-capital intervention. Each cohort places seventeen young silpis, drawn by competitive examination from the temple-towns of Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh and southern Karnataka, into a three-year residential apprenticeship under the supervision of master craftsmen working in three traditional centres of practice — Mamallapuram on the coast, Kumbakonam in the Kaveri delta, and Swamimalai, the ancient seat of the bronze-casting guilds.

Fellows live within the workshop precincts. Days begin at 5.30 a.m. with the cleaning of tools and the sharpening of chisels, followed by recitation of the relevant agama text and four hours of supervised carving, casting, or pigment preparation. Afternoons are reserved for drawing from the Trust's tālamāna proportion-manuals and from rubbings taken in situ at Brihadeeswarar, Airavatesvara and Gangaikondacholapuram.

The programme is residential, fully funded, and confers no recognised university degree. What it confers, in the language of the guilds themselves, is the right to sign one's own work — the kai-mudra, the hand-mark, that no academy can issue.

The Four Curriculum Bands

A discipline divided in four hands

— I —

Granite Carving

Quarry assessment, the eight-stage shaping of krishna-shila, low-relief and high-relief figural work, and the cutting of vimana mouldings to historical proportion.

Led at Mamallapuram in the workshops of the Ganapathi Sthapati lineage.

36 months · 6 fellows
— II —

Bronze Cire-Perdue

Wax modelling under the Silpa Ratna proportions, mould preparation in clay-and-paddy-husk, panchaloha alloying, and the chasing and chiselling of cast figures after cooling.

Led at Swamimalai in association with the hereditary Sthapatis of Periya Vidhi.

36 months · 5 fellows
— III —

Mural Pigments

Lime-plaster preparation in three ground coats, the grinding of mineral pigments — haritala, lapis, lamp-black, geru — and brush-making from squirrel and palm fibre. Conservation cleaning of Nayaka-period murals.

Led at Kumbakonam under Sri V. Krishnamurthy.

36 months · 4 fellows
— IV —

Kolam Geometry

The mathematical study and field documentation of pulli kolam, sikku and chikku patterns; their proportional relation to temple vastu-purusha grids; and re-application in floor and ceiling ornament.

Itinerant; supervised by Smt. Lakshmi Subbaraman.

36 months · 2 fellows

Testimony · 2020 Graduate
I came to Swamimalai believing that bronze was a metal. I left understanding that it is a measure — a measure of patience, of breath held during the pour, of the prayers said the night before. The Trust did not teach me a craft. It returned me to one.
Smt. Anjali Sundaresan Fellow 2017–2020 · Bronze conservator on the Nataraja and Sivakami consort group, Brihadeeswarar precinct
The Three Resident Workshops

Where the fellows live and work

Mamallapuram
Sri P. Ganapathi Sthapati · Granite

The coastal stone-yard, set within a half-acre compound a short walk from the Shore Temple, has been a working silpa-shala since 1962. Fellows train here in figural and architectural stone-cutting under a hereditary line.

Workshop No. 4, Othavadai Cross Street,
Mamallapuram 603104, Tamil Nadu
Swamimalai
Sri R. Devasenapathi Sthapati · Bronze

The bronze-casters' quarter at Periya Vidhi maintains forty-two casting pits; the workshop is the senior-most of the active silpi houses, having cast for the Tirupati and Madurai precincts since the late nineteenth century.

14, Periya Vidhi, Swamimalai 612302,
Thanjavur District, Tamil Nadu
Kumbakonam
Sri V. Krishnamurthy · Pigment

The pigment workshop occupies the upper floor of a Chettiar house near the Mahamaham tank. Fellows here also receive instruction in the conservation cleaning of Nayaka-period murals at the Sarangapani temple complex.

2nd Floor, 88 Bazaar Street,
Kumbakonam 612001, Tamil Nadu
Application Instructions · 2025–2028 Cohort

How to Apply

  1. Eligibility Indian nationals between 17 and 24 years of age on 1 January 2025. Candidates must demonstrate basic literacy in Tamil and a working knowledge of one of the four curriculum disciplines, by family lineage or by prior practice.
  2. Written Submission A handwritten letter of intent in Tamil or English (max. 600 words), accompanied by three photographs of work executed by the candidate's own hand and a single character reference from a recognised sthapati, schoolmaster, or temple authority.
  3. Postal Address Submissions are received only by post. The Office of the Fellowship, The Padmavati Heritage Trust, 11 East Mada Street, Mylapore, Chennai 600004. Marked clearly: STAPATHI FELLOWSHIP 2025.
  4. Practical Examination Shortlisted candidates are summoned to Mamallapuram in early March for a two-day practical assessment: a stone-cutting test, a wax-modelling test, and an oral examination on the relevant agama text in the candidate's chosen band.
  5. Final Selection & Induction Final results are conveyed by registered post in the second week of May. Fellows are inducted in a ceremony held on the new-moon day of Ashadha at the Trust's library in Mylapore.
