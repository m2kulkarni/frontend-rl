Foundation & Mission

Charta Fundationis · MCMVII

"To gather, conserve, and set before the people of Rome the stones, bronzes, and inscriptions of the Imperial age, that the memory of the City may be preserved for ages without end."

The Charter of Foundation

XIV Martii MCMVII

The Antiquarium Traianum was constituted by royal decree on the fourteenth of March, nineteen hundred and seven, under the patronage of His Majesty King Vittorio Emanuele III and at the petition of the antiquarian society Cultores Vrbis, whose nine founding members had since 1894 collected funerary stelae, brick stamps, and architectural fragments lifted from the excavations of the Forum of Trajan and the slope of the Quirinal. The original deed assigns the new institution a fourfold mandate: to gather, to conserve, to study, and to display, without charge upon four days of each month, the material remains of the Imperial city to the citizens of Rome and to scholars of every nation.

— I —

War & Restoration

MCMXLIII · MCMLI

On the night of the nineteenth of July, 1943, an Allied incendiary bomb pierced the eastern roof of the original gallery, destroying seventeen plaster casts and damaging the Severan inscription CIL VI 1033. The collection, evacuated by Director Tullio Marconi to the cellars of the Castello Odescalchi at Bracciano, returned in stages from 1948. The Antiquarium reopened to the public on the second of June, 1951, with a restored façade by the architect Gaetano Minnucci and a new lapidary court paved in travertine recovered from the Via dei Fori Imperiali.

— II —

Palazzo Valentini

MCMLXXVIII

The expansion of 1978, negotiated under the directorship of Prof. Marcello Aurigemma and ratified by the Provincia di Roma on the eleventh of October of that year, joined the Antiquarium to the ground-floor halls of the adjacent Palazzo Valentini. The new galleries — some twenty-two hundred square metres — permitted the permanent display of the great equestrian bronze fragment from the Forum of Augustus and the reassembly of the Severan marble plan sections recovered in the Via Alessandrina excavations of 1932. A second entrance was opened upon the Foro Traiano, with portico restored after the manner of the Apollodoran original.

— III —

Conservation Lab

MMXIV

In 2014 the museum opened, in the vaulted substructures formerly assigned to storage, a fully equipped conservation laboratory for stone, bronze, and wall painting. The facility — directed by Ing. Roberto Salvi, Fellow of the Istituto Centrale per il Restauro — comprises a non-invasive imaging suite, a desalination tank of fourteen cubic metres, and a dedicated bench for the consolidation of opus sectile. Under his programme more than nine hundred objects have been treated, including the Trajanic frieze fragments returned in 2019 from the Glyptothek in Munich and the painted lararium recovered at Santa Maria Antiqua in 2016.
