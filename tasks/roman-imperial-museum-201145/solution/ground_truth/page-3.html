Mosaics of the Bay of Naples · 79 CE
VI Iunii — XIV Novembris  ·  6 June – 14 November
Curatores Dott.ssa Elena Vacca, Curator of Roman Pavements
Dott. Marco Pellegrini, Curator of Vesuvian Antiquities
in collaboratione cum Soprintendenza Archeologica di Pompei

On the Loan from Pompeii De Pavimentis Pompeianis Mutuo Datis

The present exhibition is realised under a triennial loan concord signed on the Kalends of February, MMXXIV, between the Antiquarium Traianum and the Soprintendenza Archeologica di Pompei. By the terms of this concord, eleven principal pavements and seven fragmentary panels are conveyed from the storerooms of the Casa di Bacco and the deposits at Boscoreale to the galleries of our museum, where they are displayed for the first time outside of Campania since their excavation.

Each pavement has been consolidated upon a fresh aluminium honeycomb support by the conservation atelier of Dott.ssa Vacca, removing the iron armatures imposed in the nineteenth century. The marine emblema from Herculaneum, in particular, required ninety-four working days of cleaning before its octopus and moray eel emerged from the calcium carbonate accretions of the Vesuvian flow. Visitors are reminded that the threshold inscription CAVE CANEM — once worn smooth by the feet of slaves and citizens of the Casa di Orfeo — is here viewed in its original orientation, set into the floor of Sala I as it would have greeted any guest crossing the fauces.

The accord further provides for an annual symposium of conservators, the publication of two volumes of technical findings, and the eventual return of all loaned material to Pompeii on the Ides of November, MMXXVI.

The Eleven Principal Pavements Pavimenta Praecipua · XI

The exhibition is built around eleven floors drawn from houses, baths, and a tomb chamber buried by the eruption of Mount Vesuvius on the twenty-fourth day of August, 79 CE. Each is presented at floor level on a low travertine plinth, lit obliquely so that the white-ground tesserae read as they did beneath the Italian sun.

  • Cave Canem — guard-dog threshold Casa di Orfeo (VI.14.20), fauces · black & white tesserae
  • Marine emblema with octopus and moray Herculaneum, Casa del Tramezzo di Legno · polychrome
  • Skeleton bearing wine jugs (memento mori) Pompeii, House V.6 · vermiculatum
  • Theatrical masks with garland border Casa del Fauno (VI.12.2), tablinum · 1st c. BCE
  • Nilotic scene with crocodile and ibis Casa del Medico (VIII.5.24), triclinium
  • Geometric scutulatum in red, black, white Villa di Diomede, peristyle · ca. 50 CE
  • Dove and bronze basin Casa delle Colombe a Mosaico (VIII.2.34)
  • Hercules and the Lion — figured panel Stabiae, Villa San Marco · polychrome emblema
  • Black-ground opus tessellatum with anchors Pompeii, Terme del Foro, frigidarium
  • Wreathed kantharos with flanking peacocks Oplontis, Villa Poppaea · room 14
  • Labyrinth with central Theseus & Minotaur Casa del Labirinto (VI.11.10) · cubiculum 42

Four Thematic Rooms Per Domum Romanam

The galleries are arranged after the plan of a Pompeian house, so that the visitor passes from the public threshold inwards toward the private garden, encountering the pavements in the spatial order for which they were designed. A printed leaflet, available in Italian, English, and Latin, accompanies each room.

SALA · I

Thresholdlimen

Apotropaic floors of the doorway: the watchful dog, the guarded entrance, and the magical inscription that addresses the arriving guest before they have spoken.

SALA · II

Domusatrium & tablinum

Public-facing floors of reception. Geometric scutulata and figured panels asserting the cultivation, the wealth, and the ancestry of the householder.

SALA · III

Tricliniumcenatio

Dining-room pavements: marine emblems, masks, and the famous asarotos oikos tradition of the unswept floor strewn with the remains of the feast.

SALA · IV

Gardenviridarium

Floors of the peristyle and the nymphaeum: doves at the basin, peacocks at the kantharos, and the polished black grounds of the bath complex.

Liber Catalogi

Pavimenta — The Exhibition Catalogue

Edited by Vacca & Pellegrini, with twelve essays on tessellation technique, eleven entries on the loaned pavements, a gazetteer of comparative floors from the Bay of Naples, and an introduction by Professor Ottavio Marchesi of the Università di Bologna. Hardbound, full colour, with three foldout plates of the marine emblema of Herculaneum at one-to-one scale.

288 pp. · 187 plates · 24 €
