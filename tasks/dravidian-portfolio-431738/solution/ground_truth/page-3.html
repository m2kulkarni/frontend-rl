Case Study · Commission III · Outer Mandapa

The Patteeswaram Mandapa

A record of forty-eight pillars in Salem black granite, raised for the outer mandapa of the Sri Patteeswaram Dhenupureeswarar Trust between Vaikasi of the year 2019 and Aippasi of 2022
✦ ◆ ✦ ◆ ✦ ◆ ✦

In the matter of the outer mandapa at Sri Patteeswaram, the trustees of the Dhenupureeswarar temple approached the atelier in the cool month of Margazhi 2018, with a brief drawn in measured cubits and the requirement that forty-eight pillars be carved from a single quarry of Salem black granite, dressed and erected within the duration of one full ritual cycle.

The work that follows records, in five sections, the conduct of that commission — from the first chisel laid against quarry-face to the morning of consecration when the chief priest poured holy water from the kalasha at sunrise.

Commission
№ III · MMXIX

The mandapa stands at the western prakara of the Dhenupureeswarar temple, where the rear procession path meets the cattle-shed shrine of Bhuminatheswarar. It receives the silver-cow utsavar on each Pradosham, and so the pillar arrangement was required to admit a clear processional aisle of nine cubits along the principal axis.

What follows is therefore not only a record of stone-craft but a record of accommodation — of liturgy, of footfall, and of the stubborn behaviour of granite under the hand. The drawings reproduced in section II were measured at the workshop in Kumbakonam and again on the temple ground, against the existing inner mandapa raised in 1672 by the Maratha mason Triambaka.

I · Quarry
Section I — Of the Quarry and the Selection of Stone

Salem Black at the Cauvery Bend

— Vaikasi to Aadi, 2019 · twelve weeks of selection at Kuppanur quarry

The trustees specified Salem black for its uniform grain and the carrying voice it gives the chisel; it is the stone of the great pillared halls at Darasuram and Tribhuvanam, and it answers the carver's hand without the brittle scaling one finds in the paler Karur stones. In Vaikasi 2019 the atelier despatched two men to the Kuppanur quarry above the Cauvery bend — V. Murugesan, the senior stoneworker, and the writer — to choose the forty-eight blocks.

Each block was rung with a brass mallet at six points along its length, and only those that returned a sustained tone, neither dull nor cracked, were marked with vermilion paste for cutting. Of one hundred and seventy blocks rung on the first morning, only fifty-three were judged sound; the surplus was retained against breakage in transit. The blocks were then reduced to rough cubits at the quarry — nominal length nine cubits and four angulas, square section one cubit and twelve angulas — and bound to bullock-carts for the journey of one hundred and ninety-three miles to Kumbakonam, which occupied seventeen days. The cost of stone, transport, and the customary offering at the quarry shrine of Kuppannan came to ₹ 14,82,400.

II · Plan
Section II — Of the Mandala-Based Plan and Bay Grid

An Ekashtaka of Eight by Six

— Aani 2019 · drawings prepared at the Kumbakonam workshop

The plan is a paramashayika mandala of eight bays in the principal direction and six in the cross, yielding forty-eight columns at the bay intersections and a covered area of forty-three by thirty-one cubits within the dripline. The four corner positions were assigned the heaviest shaft section and the deepest plinth, since they receive the diagonal thrust of the timber-and-tile roof; the inner field of twenty-four pillars was reduced by two angulas in girth to lighten the visual register and admit more light upon the navagraha shrine at the centre.

III · Capitals
Section III · Part A

The Yali Capital

— Designed and carved Maasi 2020 to Chittirai 2021

The Yali, the lion-bodied guardian of the southern temple, was set in pairs upon the eight intermediate pillars of the perimeter row. Each capital projects two cubits and four angulas from the abacus and bears a rider in the form of a small mounted warrior, drawn from the iconographic register of the Vijayanagara Hundred-Pillared Hall at Vellore.

The under-jaw of each Yali was carved hollow to a depth of six angulas, allowing the customary loose stone ball to be released from within — a test of the carver's judgement, since a single false stroke ruins the work of forty days.

Section III · Part B

The Rearing Horse

— Designed and carved Vaikasi 2021 to Margazhi 2021

For the eight cardinal pillars of the perimeter, the trustees requested the rearing horse motif particular to the Tanjavur mandalam, in the manner of the Sarangapani complex at Kumbakonam. Each horse was carved at the gallop, fore-hoof raised against an attacking foot-soldier, the rider a Maratha cavalryman in homage to the donor's lineage of Bhonsale stewards.

The horses were carved free of the shaft to a depth of fourteen angulas, with the supporting strut concealed within the warrior's lance — a device that has been the workshop's signature since the time of my grandfather, Sthapati R. Subramaniam.

A Short Note on the Sharpening of Tools

It is the practice of the atelier that no carver shall draw chisel against granite until the edge has been struck on the carborundum stone at the morning bell, examined against the lamp-flame for the absence of any reflected light along its line, and presented to the senior stoneworker for approval. The chief tools — the shulika, the tankam, the kunda, and the four sizes of point-chisel — are tempered each Friday afternoon at the smith's hearth in Sannadhi Street, where K. Ramaswamy has worked for the workshop since the year 1991.

A blunt chisel ruins the stone twice: once in the false stroke, and again in the carver's hand, which loses its trust. We hold to the old measure of the smiths of Swamimalai: iru-mukham, oru-vai — two faces, one mouth — meaning that a chisel struck honestly will eat the stone in a single clean line, and the carver should hear in that line the same note returned that the quarry-block first gave to the brass mallet. When the note is broken, the work is set aside and the tool re-tempered.

IV · Consecration

Kumbhabhishekam · The Consecration

— Aippasi Pournami, 18 October 2022 · the morning of the full moon, between Brahma Muhurtam and sunrise —

The consecration was performed by the chief priest Sri V. Sambasiva Sivachariar, in the presence of the trustees of the Sri Patteeswaram Dhenupureeswarar Devasthanam — Sri T. R. Krishnamurthy, Smt. Lakshmi Subramaniam, Sri P. K. Vaidyanathan, Sri R. Ganesan and Sri M. Doraiswamy — and an assembly of approximately four hundred and twenty devotees gathered upon the western prakara.

Eleven kalashas were prepared, of which the principal copper kalasha was poured upon the kumbam atop the central pillar of the navagraha shrine; the remaining ten were distributed to the corner pillars and to the cardinal Yali capitals. The mandapa was opened to the procession of the silver Bhuminatheswarar utsavar at the eighth hour, and the first Pradosham was observed within it on the second day following.
