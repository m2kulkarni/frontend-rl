In This Issue

Three featured essays on the long afterlife of Isfahan's craft

I

The Painted Heaven of Sheikh Lotfollah

Essay · Folio 14 · pp. 22–47

Beneath the lemon-dome of the queen's mosque, Farah Karimi reads the lattice of fired tile as a scored music — a ceiling that performs the night sky each afternoon at four o'clock.

II

Notes from the Workshop of Reza Abbasi

Essay · Folio 15 · pp. 48–71

Mehrdad Vaziri retraces the long line of a single brush — from Shah Abbas's royal library to a private album in Lisbon — and asks what survives when only the gesture remains.

III

A Plan of Water — The Gardens of Chehel Sotoun

Essay · Folio 16 · pp. 72–94

Bahar Sotoudeh walks the forty columns at dawn. The reflecting pool, she argues, is not a mirror but a second building — drawn in liquid, completed only when one is willing to see double.

From the Editor

A Letter, Folded Twice

Forty-seven issues on, the office of this quarterly is still small enough that we know which lamp is on by the angle of the light under the door. We measure our seasons by the visits of restorers from Yazd, by the proofs that arrive smelling of pressed paper, and by the long dispatches our contributing editor in London sends back, written, as ever, on the wrong side of British library cards.

This autumn we have gathered the issue under a single roof — the roof, more precisely, of the Sheikh Lotfollah Mosque, whose painted dome continues to govern our sense of what a built heaven might look like. To it we have added two further enquiries: a study of the workshop of Reza Abbasi, and a slow walk through the water-figures of Chehel Sotoun, where forty columns become eighty and the garden refuses to settle on a number.

We hope you will read it, as we made it, with the lamp set close and the windows open onto the maydan.

Nasrin Daryabandari
Editor in Chief · Tehran, the 14th of Mehr
