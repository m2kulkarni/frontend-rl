From the Desk of the Editor

Bunsei 6,
Eleventh Month —
a Winter Letter

Set down at the Nakagyō workshop with the brazier lit and the Kamo running grey; the kōzo deliveries from Imadate arrived on the seventh. — Ono Sumiko, Editor

The winter issue arrives, as is our custom, with the first frost on the Higashiyama tiles. Our concerns this quarter are three, and they are quiet ones. We return, first, to Hokusai in his old age — to the late Fuji prints of 1834 and to the spare, almost trembling line of One Hundred Views, where the master, by his own admission, had at last begun to learn how to draw.

We then take to the road. The contributor Yamashina Tōkichi has walked the Kisokaidō from Itabashi to Otsu in eleven days, eating badly and sleeping worse, and his notebook — passed to us at Kuwana — is reproduced here in full, with its observations on packhorse fees, post-station prices, and the snow at Magome.

Last, we sit a long afternoon with the Nishijin weaver Tatsumura Heizō at his loom in Kamigyō-ku; his notes on the karaori commission for the Yasaka shrine, and on the slow disappearance of certain indigo dyes, close the issue. May the gazette warm a tea-room hour.
