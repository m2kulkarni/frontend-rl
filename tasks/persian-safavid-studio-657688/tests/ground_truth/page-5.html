Programme of Study · Established 1387 SH

The Three-Year Apprenticeship

A binding covenant of hand and pattern, taken under the masters of the courtyard for thirty-six months from the autumnal equinox, in the manner of the Isfahan ateliers.

The Course of Years

Three Stations of the Apprentice

Each year carries its own master, its own discipline, and its own examination. Progression is not assumed — it is granted at the threshold of Mehr by the council of the workshop.

I
First Station

Cartoon & Pattern

under Roya Tabatabai

The opening twelvemonth is given wholly to the drawing-board. The novice copies historic cartoons from the Sheikh Lutfollah and the Imam Mosque, learning the geometric constructions of the eight-pointed and ten-pointed girih, the laying of palmette and saz on a knotted grid, and the discipline of the compass before the brush.

By the close of Esfand the apprentice must produce two full repeat-patterns at scale of 1:1, drawn in red ochre on linen-rag paper, ready for transfer to clay. No glaze is touched in this year.

II
Second Station

Glaze & Kiln

under Mehrdad Saffar

The second year removes the apprentice from paper to the firing yard. Mornings are spent grinding cobalt from Kashan ore, weighing copper for the turquoise, and learning the lead-alkaline frits proper to haft-rang and to underglaze. Afternoons are spent at the bisque kiln and the glaze kiln, charting cones and reductions in a bound day-book.

Each month the candidate fires a single tile of their own composition. The twelve tiles are bound at year's end into a personal palette — the apprentice's first patrimony.

III
Third Station

Independent Commission

under shared supervision

In the third year the apprentice is given over to a real commission of the house — a spandrel, a mihrab cartouche, a stair-riser, or a tomb-frieze — chosen from the standing book of orders. Both Roya khanum and Mehrdad agha attend the work, but the hand is the apprentice's own from cartoon to setting.

The completed work is signed in the lower-right cornerstone in the apprentice's own seal-name and installed in the patron's building. With its placement the apprentice is named ostad-shagerd and admitted to the workshop's roll.

Conditions of Entry

Requirements & Terms

The atelier admits a maximum of four apprentices each Mehr. Candidates are interviewed in person in the courtyard over two days; no decision is taken by post.

Required of the Candidate

Entry Requirements

  • A Drawing Portfolio Not fewer than twenty original sheets, of which at least eight must be measured architectural studies executed in pencil or ink. Photocopies are not received. The portfolio is returned at interview's end.
  • Working Knowledge of Persian Sufficient to follow instruction at the bench, to read the historical cartoon-books of the workshop, and to address the kiln-master. Calligraphic Persian is welcomed but not required at entry.
  • Fluency in Farsi or English Either tongue is accepted for the formal correspondence and the year-end examinations. Foreign candidates whose Persian is still developing may write their day-books in English for the first year only.
  • Sound of Hand and Eye A short bench-test is set on the second day of interview: the copying of a single quarter-repeat from a presented cartoon, in two hours, with compass and ruling pen.
