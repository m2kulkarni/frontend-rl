The Courtyard Tea-Room

Visitors are welcome at the courtyard tea-room beneath the plane trees, where pomegranate sherbet and small glasses of saffron tea are offered to those waiting to consult the tile-master or to inspect cartoons newly drawn for a commission. The room sits to the east of the firing kilns, behind a turquoise door inscribed with the workshop's mark; the bench cushions are stitched with the palmettes our apprentices learn to copy in their first season.

Photography of works in progress requires written permission from the master of the relevant atelier — calligraphy, gereh-cutting, or draughtsmanship — and is never granted for pieces under confidential commission. Sketching by hand, however, is encouraged for students of the craft, and notebooks may be left in the small cedar lockers by the entry vestibule.

۞  A note for those arriving from beyond Isfahan  ۞

The lane is too narrow for motor cars; visitors are kindly asked to leave taxis at the corner of Ostandari Street, beside the bookbinder's shop, and to walk the remaining hundred and twenty paces. The workshop's gate is the second on the left, marked by a cobalt tile-spandrel bearing the date 1398 H.Sh. and the studio's eight-pointed seal.
