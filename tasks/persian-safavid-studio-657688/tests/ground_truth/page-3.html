Commissions & Portfolio

A ledger of recent works carried out by the courtyard — restorations, dado panels, mihrab niches and calligraphic friezes set down between 1398 SH and 1403 SH, recorded here by year, place, and the hand that led each table.

On the Discipline of Recording Work

Each commission entered into this register has been measured, drawn, fired, and set under the supervision of a named ostād, whose hand bears responsibility for the work from the first cartoon to the final pointing of the joints. We do not subcontract; every haft-rang tile leaving the courtyard at Kucheh Joubāreh is signed on its reverse with the studio cipher and the year in the solar Hijri calendar. The works below are arranged by date of completion, eldest first, and represent only those projects whose patrons have given consent for public listing — a number of private residential commissions in Tehran, Shiraz, and Doha are by agreement omitted.

Spandrel Restoration

1398 SH ❋ 2019
Madrasa-ye Madar-e Shah, Isfahan

Replacement of one hundred forty-three failed mosaic-faience pieces in the south-east īwān spandrels, including reweaving of the cobalt vine-scroll lost to water ingress in 1392.

Original cartoons reconstructed from photographic plates of 1934 held by the Cultural Heritage Organisation; new tile fired in three campaigns to match the surviving Safavid palette.

Lead Artisan Ostād Mohammad-Reza Sehāti, kāshī-tarāsh

Private Courtyard Fountain

1400 SH ❋ 2021
Bāgh-e Dowlatābād Quarter, Yazd

Octagonal pool of four-and-a-quarter metres span with turquoise and oxblood seven-colour dado, commissioned by the Mehrābi family for a restored qājār courtyard house dated 1247 LH.

The basin floor carries a sixteen-pointed star drawn from a girih in the Topkapı Scroll, set in cuerda-seca on biscuit fired at our Kashan kiln to withstand the saline groundwater of the region.

Lead Artisan Ostāda Forough Naqdi, mosaic-faience

Calligraphic Frieze

1401 SH ❋ 2022
Qazvin Cultural Foundation, Sepāh St.

Continuous thuluth band of forty-two metres encircling the foundation's reading-hall, drawn from selected verses of Sa'di's Golestān and rendered in white interlace upon a cobalt ground.

Letterforms cut from imported Maragheh limestone and inlaid with glazed tile fragments; the project required eleven months in the courtyard and a further three on site, completed Mehr 1401.

Lead Artisan Ostād Hosayn-Ali Tabrizi, khoshnevis

Tile Dado Panels

1402 SH ❋ 2023
Hotel Abbasi, East Wing — Isfahan

Twenty-eight haft-rang dado panels at one-point-six metres height, replacing twentieth-century reproductions in the corridor connecting the east wing to the historic caravanserai courtyard.

Patterns drawn from surviving fragments of the 1085 LH Abbāsi scheme; commissioned by the hotel's heritage committee at a value of 4.2 milliard rial and delivered in two phases over fourteen months.

Lead Artisan Ostād Mohammad-Reza Sehāti, kāshī-tarāsh

Ablutions-Court Repair

1402 SH ❋ 2023
Masjed-e Hakim, Isfahan

Stabilisation of subsidence in the north-west corner of the ablutions court and re-laying of two hundred eleven square metres of seven-colour pavement disturbed by the works.

Carried out under joint supervision of the Endowments Office and our courtyard between Bahman 1401 and Khordād 1402; original glaze recipes recovered from kiln waste excavated on site by the survey team.

Lead Artisan Ostād Jalāl Pirniyā, master draughtsman

New Mihrab Niche

1403 SH ❋ 2024
Naderi Family Chapel, Natanz

An entirely new pointed-arch mihrab at two-point-four metres height, the first wholly original mihrab cut by the courtyard since the founding of the atelier; muqarnas hood of nineteen tiers in cobalt and gold.

Verse selection — the throne-verse encircling the niche — set in jali thuluth and finished in matt oxblood; consecrated on the fourteenth of Sha'bān 1445 LH in the presence of the patron family.

Lead Artisan Ostāda Forough Naqdi, mosaic-faience

On Receiving New Sefāresh

Inquiries regarding restoration, new mihrab work, courtyard pavements, or calligraphic friezes are received by letter or in person at the courtyard between Saturday and Wednesday, ten in the morning until four in the afternoon. A first cartoon and estimate are prepared within forty days of site survey.
