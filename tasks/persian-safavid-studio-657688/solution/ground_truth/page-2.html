Page Two ✦ The Workshop

Of Hand & House

کارگاه ما — a courtyard, a kiln, an unhurried hand

Behind a timber-columned façade off Chahar Bagh Avenue, eleven artisans practise the four ornamental disciplines of the Safavid masters: cut-tile mosaic, naskh and thuluth calligraphy, geometric draughtsmanship, and the patient gilding of borders.

A History in Three Stanzas

from a borrowed kiln in 2008 to a courtyard of eleven hands

I
MMVIII ✦ The Founding

A Borrowed Kiln

The atelier was founded in the spring of 2008 by master tile-cutter Mehrdad Saffar and the calligrapher Roya Tabatabai, who together leased a half-derelict timber-columned house in the lanes off Chahar Bagh Avenue and persuaded its octogenarian owner to let them clear the rear courtyard for a kiln.

For the first eighteen months they worked at two benches and shared a single firing chamber with a neighbouring potter. The earliest commission — a four-metre mihrab spandrel for a private oratory in Najafabad — was cut, glazed, and assembled almost entirely on the brick floor of that courtyard.

II
MMXV ✦ The Drawing Room

Measure & Compass

By 2015 the workshop's commissions had begun to require survey work and full-scale cartoons — particularly after the conservation of a Qajar bath-house in Kashan — and a measured-drawing room was added in the upper storey, fitted with eight inclined boards and a long zinc-topped table for unrolling tracings.

The draughtsman Bijan Esfandiari joined that autumn from the Faculty of Fine Arts at Tehran University, and the studio's discipline of tarrahi — the geometric setting-out that precedes any cut tile — was formally established as the third leg of the practice, beside cutting and writing.

III
Today ✦ Eleven Hands

The Present Courtyard

The atelier today numbers eleven artisans across four disciplines — five tile-cutters and glaziers, two calligraphers, two draughtsmen, and two gilders — supported by an apprentice intake of three each Mehr (October). Saffar and Tabatabai remain the principals; daily oversight of the kiln rests with master Hossein Mokhtari.

We accept between fourteen and eighteen commissions a year, ranging from a single carved cartouche to the full ornamental envelope of a small shrine. The original courtyard, the kiln, and the timber columns are unchanged; only the hands at the benches have grown more numerous.

Four Principles of the House

recited by every apprentice on the first morning

  • ❖ Axial Composition — every surface must declare a centre, and every ornament must answer to it. No motif is permitted to drift.
  • ❖ Mosaic Logic — colour is set by the cut of the tile, never by the brush. A line begun in cobalt ends in cobalt, or it ends.
  • ❖ Restrained Palette — cobalt, turquoise, gold, oxblood, ivory. Five voices, no chorus. Other pigments are kept in the cabinet for restoration only.
  • ❖ No Figurative Imagery in Religious Commissions — the geometry and the word suffice. Animal and human forms are reserved for secular, domestic, and garden work.
