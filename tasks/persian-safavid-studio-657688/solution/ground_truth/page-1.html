The Three Departments

Each branch of the atelier carries a distinct discipline of Safavid ornament, practised under one courtyard roof.

۱
Department I

Kāshi-kāri Mosaic

کاشی‌کاری

The cutting hall keeps eleven master and journeyman tile-cutters who reduce hand-fired faience into the small geometric units (mo'arraq) used in spandrel and dado work. Cobalt, turquoise, and oxblood glazes are fired in our two wood kilns at Bāgh-e Hasht-Behesht.

We dress walls from 6 to 240 square metres, working from a measured pounce on linen. Recent installations include the iwan of the Karbāsi residence and the qibla niche of Masjid-e Sharifiyya, Qom.

From 4,200,000 IRR / m²
۲
Department II

Naskh Banding

کتیبه‌نگاری

Under master calligrapher Hossein Mirzā-Ahmadi, the banding atelier composes thuluth and naskh-derived inscription friezes for cornices, drum bands, and mihrab borders. Each cartouche is ruled, gilded, and signed before transfer.

The studio holds four registered scripts and a working archive of 312 historical inscription rubbings taken between 1402 and 1404 SH from the monuments of Isfahan, Yazd, and Ardabil.

From 1,800,000 IRR / linear m
۳
Department III

Measured Drawing

نقشه‌برداری

Three architectural draughtsmen survey standing Safavid monuments at 1:20 and 1:50, producing inked plans, sections, and unfolded elevations on Fabriano paper. Our drawing room holds 1,140 sheets catalogued since 1391 SH.

Commissioned surveys are issued bound in cloth and hand-stamped; recent dossiers cover the Sheikh Lotfollāh dome interior and the chahār-bāgh pavilion at Farah-ābād.

Survey from 92,000,000 IRR

On the Continuity of the Craft

We do not restore Safavid ornament; we continue it. The drawings hung on our courtyard walls were ruled by the same compass that ruled the niches of Masjid-e Shāh, and the cobalt we grind in mortar No. 4 is bought from the same Qamsar pit that supplied the tile-makers of Shāh ‘Abbās.

A pattern is not a picture but an instruction held in a community of hands. So long as one cutter still chips the petal of a shamseh from a fired blank, and one scribe still rules a line of thuluth in iron-gall ink, the Safavid order remains a living craft — not a memory but a workshop, opening at eight each morning to the call of the muezzin from the Masjid-e Jāmi'.

— The Mu‘allim and the Twelve Workmen of the Atelier
