Crafts & Methods

Four disciplines practised under one courtyard sky — cut tile, painted glaze, brick set in calligraphy, and the measured hand that records what time has forgotten.

The Atelier accepts commissions in four hereditary practices, each carried forward from the workshops that adorned the Masjid-e Shāh and the Masjid-e Sheikh Lotfollāh between 1611 and 1629. Below, we describe the method, the material, and the rate at which our masters undertake new work in the year 1403 (2024–25).

Mo'arraq

معرّق — cut-tile mosaic

Mo'arraq

Cut-Tile Mosaic · Cartoon & Pincer

The cartoon (tarh) is laid full-scale on the workshop's plaster floor, then traced in red ochre. Each colour is fired separately — cobalt slabs at 980 °C, turquoise at 920 °C, oxblood manganese at 940 °C — and only after cooling are the tiles broken to shape with the curved-jaw kalbatān. A single spandrel of one square metre demands roughly 2,400 individual cuts. The fragments are then set face-down in the cartoon and a thin gypsum-and-lime backing is poured; when cured, the panel is reversed and the figure lives.

Our glazes follow the receipts kept by Ustad Ali-Reza of the Masjid-e Sheikh Lotfollāh atelier, refreshed only by modest substitutions of lead with bismuth-frit. The cobalt is still the Qamsar ore, ground in walnut oil; the turquoise still draws from copper-carbonate fluxed with potash from tamarisk ash.

Haft-Rang

هفت‌رنگ — seven-colour overglaze

Haft-Rang

Seven-Colour Overglaze · Painted Tile

Where mo'arraq cuts the figure from coloured ground, haft-rang paints it. The square biscuit-tile, dipped in opaque white tin-glaze, becomes the surface upon which the painter lays seven oxide colours in a single firing. The savings in labour are considerable; the hazards are equal — colours volatile at 880 °C will fight one another for the same space, and the muffle must rise and fall on a slow curve over thirty-six hours.

This method is suited to floral fields, garden scenes, and the figural medallions our patrons request for private rooms. The atelier's workhorse panel is 22 cm square, joined in grids of nine to eighty-one tiles. Cobalt, turquoise, manganese-purple, antimony-yellow, iron-red, copper-green, and a single black drawn from chromium make the seven.

Banna'i

بنّایی — brick-set calligraphy

Banna'i

Brick-Set Calligraphy · Geometric Kufic

The most architectural of the four crafts: words are written not by ink but by the alternation of glazed and unglazed brick across a wall. The script — square Kufic, called Banna'i after the masons themselves — must be planned on a strict square grid where every letter, ligature, and ground space resolves to whole bricks. Our standard course uses a 22 cm fired-clay brick, half-glazed turquoise on the visible face.

The discipline asks more of the architect than of the painter. The atelier composes the inscription on graph paper at 1:10, returns it to the patron for review, then cuts and glazes the bricks to order. Names of the Twelve Imams, the Shahāda, and Quranic phrases are the most often requested; we maintain forty-seven approved templates in our drawing-room.

Tarrahi

طرّاحی — measured drawing

Tarrahi

Measured Drawing · Heritage Survey

The fourth practice is the quietest. Before any restoration may begin, a building must be drawn — every dado, spandrel, and muqarnas pendant recorded at scale on hand-stretched paper. The Atelier's drawing-room offers this service to the Ministry of Cultural Heritage, to private waqf trusts, and to architects undertaking sympathetic new work. Three of our draughtsmen hold the Sazmān-e Mirās certification of 2019.

Survey drawings are issued in three sets: pencil original on Arches 300 gsm, ink fair-copy on linen, and a digital archive at 600 dpi. Field campaigns of up to four weeks are quoted by the day, including transport from Isfahan to anywhere within the historic provinces. We have surveyed in Yazd, Mashhad, Kāshān, and twice in Samarkand under joint commission.

A Note to Patrons

All quoted figures hold for the year 1403 and exclude shipping, scaffolding, and the architect's site-visits beyond Isfahan province. Deposits of one-third secure a place in the kiln calendar; the remainder falls due upon delivery of the finished panel to your masons. We do not undertake commissions whose iconography conflicts with the dignity of the building.
