A quarterly journal of the temple country — its stones, its plans, its processions, and the unbroken hands that keep them. This issue marks the millennium of consecration of Rajaraja's tower at Tanjavur.

The Margazhi Number

The Vimana & the Sky

For one thousand and eighteen years the granite finial of the Brihadeeswarar tower at Tanjavur has stood against the monsoon. Of the temples raised in its long shadow, none has matched its arithmetic; few have outlived it. We return to the foot of the sixty-six-metre vimana to ask, again, what permitted it.

In This Issue  ·  Six Features

Lead Essay  ·  pp. 14 – 41

How a Mountain Was Lifted: Granite, Ramps & the Logistics of the Great Tower

The eighty-tonne capstone of the Rajarajeswaram is the most often-cited figure in Tamil temple writing, and the least-examined. Drawing on the Thanjavur inscriptions, fresh quarry surveys at Tirumalaivasal, and a comparative reading of Chola wage-rolls, S. Ramanathan reconstructs how Perunthachan's masons moved an entire river-bed of granite forty-six kilometres inland — and why no king after Rajaraja attempted the feat at the same scale.

1018 years since the consecration of the Brihadeeswarar vimana, A.D. 1010 — Margazhi 2024

The essay closes on a tour of the lesser Chola foundations of the Kaveri delta — Tribhuvanam, Darasuram, Gangaikondacholapuram — where the same logistical genius produced towers of progressively diminishing ambition.

By S. Ramanathan, Tanjavur — forty illustrations

Photo Essay  ·  pp. 42 – 63

The Second Capital: A Quiet Walk Through Gangaikondacholapuram

Twenty-two photographs by Meera Subramaniam, taken at first light across three Margazhi mornings, record the surviving sculptural programme of Rajendra Chola's victory city — the Chandesa-Anugraha panel, the lion-well, and the long shadow of the unfinished gopuram.

Photographs & notes by Meera Subramaniam
