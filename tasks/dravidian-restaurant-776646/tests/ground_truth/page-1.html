The Kitchen

— Pots, Stones & Coal —

We grind our masala on granite each morning at six. Black pepper from Wayanad, kalpasi from the Kanjirapally hills, fennel and star anise weighed by hand. Eight kuzhambu pots simmer through service on coal fire. Every kari leaf is torn, never cut. The pestle, the iron pan, the slow flame — these are our only kitchen technology.

The Room

— Athangudi Tile, Lime Wash —

Forty-six covers under a temple-bright ceiling of lime wash and gilded beam. The floor is laid in cobalt and ochre Athangudi tile shipped from Karaikudi in 2017. Brass lamps, teak pillars rescued from a Madurai chettiar house, banana-leaf service at lunch, and peacock-blue linen at dinner. The room is meant to feel like a home, mid-feast.

The Reservation Book

— Two Sittings, Hand-Written —

The book opens fourteen days ahead. Lunch at 12.30 and 2.15; dinner at 7.30 and 9.15, Tuesday through Sunday. Sunday Karaikudi sappadu (₹1,450) is served on banana leaf and runs one sitting only, at 1 p.m. Walk-ins are kept for the eight bar seats facing the kitchen line. Telephone or write; we reply within the day.

My grandmother's kuzhambu pot in Karaikudi was older than her marriage — iron, blackened, never washed with soap, only wiped with rice water and set back on the hearth. Every dish I cook at Kalasha begins by remembering the weight of that pot in her hand.
— Meena Alagappan, Chef & Proprietor
