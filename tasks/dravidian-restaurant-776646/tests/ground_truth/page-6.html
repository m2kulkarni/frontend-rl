The Press & The Pantry

A small selection of writing about Kalasha — what critics, columnists, and a few generous readers have said about the kitchen since we opened our doors on Lavelle Road in September 2019.

Featured · Long Read

“The Most Quietly Ambitious Tamil Kitchen in the South”

Condé Nast Traveller India — March 2022 · by Nidhi Gupta
The most quietly ambitious Tamil kitchen in the south — a room that does not announce itself, but rewards the patient diner with sambar that tastes like a recovered memory.

Kalasha was profiled in CNT India's annual Hot List as part of a longer essay on heritage regional Indian cooking moving out of grandmothers' kitchens and into the urban dining room. The piece, photographed by Aman Makkar in our Lavelle Road dining hall, lingered on chef Alagappan Chettiar's decision to mill heritage seeraga samba rice on premises and serve it for the weekday meal at ₹680.

“What is striking,” Gupta writes, “is the restraint. There is no theatre of fire here, no smoke and no story-told-in-three-acts. There is, instead, a banana leaf, a ladle of vendhaya kuzhambu, and the sense that a hundred small decisions have been made correctly, in the dark, before you arrived.”

Column · Weekend

“Alagappan's Sambar is an Argument”

The Hindu Weekend — V. Sriram, July 17, 2021
Alagappan's sambar is an argument — a closely-reasoned defence of the small-batch toor dal, made daily, never reheated past one service.

V. Sriram, the paper's long-standing food columnist, devoted his Saturday page to a single bowl: the lunchtime vendhakkai sambar served with the ₹680 meal. He counted, by his own admission rather pedantically, fourteen distinct flavours and dedicated the closing third of his column to a defence of the kitchen's stubborn refusal to scale the recipe past forty portions.

“A larger pot,” he quotes Alagappan as saying, “is a different dish. We are not equipped, in temperament or in vessel, to make a different dish.”

Essay · Issue 14

An Inventory of the Chettinad Pantry

Goya Journal — Issue 14, “The Pantry”, 2023
To open Kalasha's spice store is to read a small biography of the Chettiar trade routes — Burmese star anise beside Ceylon cinnamon beside marathi mokku from a single grower in Karaikudi.

The journal's fourteenth issue, dedicated entirely to the working pantry, opened with a 4,200-word essay by writer Aysha Tanya on Kalasha's spice room. The essay catalogued forty-seven distinct ingredients, traced the supply chain of the kitchen's kalpasi (stone flower) back to a co-operative in Sivaganga district, and reproduced — with permission — the handwritten notebook page on which Alagappan's mother first recorded the family kuzhambu ratio in 1974.

The full essay, “What the Pantry Remembers”, runs across pages 22–41 of the print issue and is available at goya.in/issue-14.

❋ Three readers, reproduced with permission ❋

“ I came to Bangalore for a wedding in November and was taken to Kalasha by my cousin who, frankly, I don't always trust on these matters. The vendhaya kuzhambu at Sunday lunch tasted exactly like my paati's, only she has been gone fourteen years and I had begun to believe I was misremembering. I am writing to say: I was not misremembering. Thank you.
