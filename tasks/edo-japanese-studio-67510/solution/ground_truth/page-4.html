第四 · About the Bench

The Workshop —
Lineage & Tools

Four generations have stood at this bench in Kuramae. The room has been rebuilt twice — once after the great fire of Meiji 14, and once after the bombings of Shōwa 20 — but the bench itself, the Itō family bench, was carried out both times before the flames reached it. It remains the oldest tool in the shop.

I · Lineage of the Bench 四代の系譜

Itō Tōkichi opened the shop in Kuramae in the second year of Bunkyū. His great-grandson stands at the same bench today.

First Generation

Itō Tōkichi
伊藤 藤吉

b. Bunsei 9 (1826), Echizen d. Meiji 18 (1885), Tōkyō

Apprenticed at thirteen to the joiner Sakuma of Fukui. Walked to Edo in Ansei 4 and worked nine years for a tansu maker near Asakusa bridge before opening his own shop on Taishō-dōri in Bunkyū 2 (1862). The lacquered cherry plane stamped 藤 at the heel is his.

Second Generation

Itō Tōzaburō
伊藤 藤三郎

b. Keiō 1 (1865), Kuramae d. Shōwa 7 (1932), Kuramae

Inherited the shop at twenty. Survived the fire of Meiji 14 by carrying the bench, the four largest planes, and his father's chest of nomi out through the rear lane. Took the first commissions for the Mitsui household — six paulownia tea-utensil boxes, three extant.

Third Generation

Itō Kōzō
伊藤 幸三

b. Meiji 36 (1903), Kuramae d. Heisei 4 (1992), Kuramae

Trained under his father from age nine. Drafted in Shōwa 13; returned in Shōwa 21 to find the workshop burnt and the bench buried under a tin roof in the back garden, untouched. Rebuilt the shop in Shōwa 24 (1949) on the original footprint — eight tatami, north light.

Fourth · Present

Itō Kōichirō
伊藤 幸一郎

b. Shōwa 41 (1966), Kuramae Master since Heisei 14 (2002)

Began at the bench at fifteen under his father and grandfather both. Six years on stock preparation before being allowed to cut a single dovetail. Took commissions independently from Heisei 6, became shōnin in Heisei 14. Trains two apprentices: Sasaki Wataru (4th yr) and Mori Hisashi (2nd yr).

II · The Tools of the Bench 道具帳

Twelve named planes, nine chisels, two saws. Most inherited; the rest forged for this studio in Sanjō.

On the catalog

The list below is the working set kept on the bench, not the full inventory. Tools held in reserve, ceremonial planes, and the boxed collection of the second generation are not listed.

Each entry notes whether the tool was inherited (相伝) or forged for the studio (誂え). Inherited tools are not used for commissioned work; they are taken down once a year, in the first week of the new year, sharpened, and returned to their wrappings.

Funahiro of Sanjō 舟弘 — fourth-generation blacksmith of Sanjō, Niigata. Has forged irons for this studio since Heisei 9. Steel is white-paper No. 1; lamination, soft iron jigane sourced from a salvaged Meiji-era anchor chain.

III · Training Policy · 修業の規

Six years. No shortcuts.

The studio accepts one apprentice every three years. The term is six years; no exceptions have been made since Shōwa 24. The first three years are spent on stock preparation, sharpening, and the rebuilding of three of the studio's planes from rough castings. No commissioned work is touched.

In the fourth year the apprentice is permitted to cut joints in scrap for inspection. In the fifth, to rough-prepare components for the master's commissions. In the sixth, and only if the work merits, to complete a commission alone, signed jointly with the master's seal.

  • Stipend, room, and one set of tools provided. Tools remain studio property until the sixth year.
  • One day off per ten. No outside work permitted during the term.
  • Applications by handwritten letter only. No formal interview; a single day at the bench.
  • Next opening: Reiwa 9 (2027). Currently full.
— Itō Kōichirō, fourth master
