Letters to the Bench

On the writing of a first letter, the meeting at the workbench, and the slow setting of an estimate.

Reiwa 6 · Shōgetsu (the New-Year month)
Drafted at the bench, Kuramae
第 一 信

To the prospective client —

A commission begins with a letter. We ask only that you describe, in your own hand or in plain typed prose, the intended use of the piece — what it must hold, where it will stand, and the room's light at morning. Approximate dimensions in shaku or in millimetres are sufficient; we do not require drawings. A small tea-utensil box for a single chaire, a four-drawer kasane-dansu for a hallway in Setagaya, or a pair of shoji frames for a tatami room of four-and-a-half mats — each begins the same way, with one page.

Should the work be one we are able to undertake, we will reply by post and propose a meeting at the bench. You are received in the front room, given roasted hōjicha, and shown the woods then in stock — kiri from Aizu seasoned eleven winters, jindai-keyaki recovered from a storehouse beam in Niigata, hinoki cut on the Kiso slopes in Heisei 28. We discuss joinery openly: which corners are dovetailed, which are tongued-and-grooved, which are pinned with bamboo nail. Nothing is decided that afternoon. You are asked to sit with the proposal for one full month.

A written estimate follows, sealed and posted. It states price, wood, joinery, and a lead time of six to eighteen months, depending chiefly on the seasoning state of the timber and the order of the bench. Tea-utensil boxes in well-seasoned kiri may be delivered in the shorter season; tansu requiring fresh-cut zelkova will take the longer. Half the sum is requested upon agreement, the remainder upon delivery. We do not invoice for revisions made within the bench's own discretion.

We answer every letter we receive, including those from which no commission follows. The studio keeps no telephone hours after the seventh bell of evening, and the post is collected once each morning save Sundays and the three days of Bon.

— Itō Sōsuke, fifth-generation joiner Replies are written by hand and may take ten days.
