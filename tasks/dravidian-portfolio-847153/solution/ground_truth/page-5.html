Section V · Of Patronage and the Setting of Hands to Stone

Commissions & Terms of Engagement

By long usage and by the counsel of the Mānasāra, the right ordering of a commission is itself part of the work. Set down here are the three tiers in which this studio accepts engagements from temple trusts, private patrons, and conservation bodies — together with what is undertaken, what is required, and what, in conscience, must be declined.

Of the Three Tiers · त्रिविधं कर्म

Prathama · The First Tier

Drawings Only

From ₹ 1,85,000 · 4 to 7 months

  • Measured survey of existing fabric, conducted on site over 9–14 days
  • Plans, elevations and sections in ink on Daler-Rowney 220 gsm cartridge
  • Vāstu-puruṣa-maṇḍala plans drawn to the prescribed 64- or 81-pada grid
  • Ornament details at quarter-scale: kumbha, kapota, vyāla, makara-toraṇa
  • One bound portfolio (cloth, hand-stitched) and three loose blueprint sets
  • Consultation with the sthapati of record where one is appointed

No site work is undertaken at this tier.

Dvitīya · The Second Tier

Drawings With Supervision

From ₹ 6,40,000 · 9 to 14 months

  • All matter of the First Tier, prepared and bound as above
  • Site visits in the auspicious months of Cittirai, Āvaṇi, and Kārttikai
  • Inspection of granite blocks at the quarry (typically Tiruchirāpalli or Salem)
  • Marking of stones for the lead carvers; correction of proportion in situ
  • Written reports after each visit, in Tamil and English, to the trustees
  • Standing telephone counsel between visits, without further fee

A minimum of four supervisions, at intervals of eight to twelve weeks.

Tritīya · The Third Tier

Full Śilpi Commission

From ₹ 22,00,000 · 18 to 36 months

  • Drawings, supervision, and the carving itself by this studio's hands
  • Lineage carvers in granite, sandstone, or kṛṣṇa-śilā as the work demands
  • Selection and consecratory marking of all principal blocks
  • Workshop carving at 14, Sannidhi Street, with weekly correspondence
  • Transport, installation, and the seating of the work upon its adhiṣṭhāna
  • Attendance at the kumbhābhiṣekam where ritually proper

Eighteen months is the minimum; pillared maṇḍapas require longer.

On Materials · Of Conduct · Of Refusal

On Materials & the Question of Polychrome

The studio works principally in kṛṣṇa-śilā (the dark green-black granite of Tiruchirāpalli district), in the buff sandstone of Pāṇḍya country, and where the trust so directs, in seasoned teak for the carved struts and lintels of pavilion roofs. Kadappa and Tirumalai marble are accepted only for inscriptional slabs.

The matter of polychrome is the most-asked question of patrons. It is the firm view of this studio that the bare-stone aesthetic prized by collectors and museums since the late nineteenth century is itself a misreading: the Chola and Vijayanagara gopuras were finished in lime, ochre, and madder, and were never meant to stand naked.

Where a temple trust requests restoration of polychrome on figures already bearing pigment traces, the studio undertakes such work using lime-based grounds and mineral pigments prepared in the manner described in the Śilparatna. Where the stone has been cleaned of all pigment within living memory and the patron asks for cleaning only — this also we will do, but with a written record of what is lost.

What the Studio Declines

It must be said plainly. The studio does not accept commissions for identifiable mūrtis of named deities — Naṭarāja, Viṣṇu reclining, the Devī in any of Her recognised forms — when these are intended for unconsecrated display: hotel lobbies, private collections of the international art trade, the lawns of resort properties, or the cabinets of museums that purchase rather than receive.

An icon cut in the prescribed lakṣaṇas is meant to be opened by a priest and to live in worship. To send such a figure into a room where no lamp will be lit before it is, in our reading of the texts, a private dishonour to the carver and a public confusion of the form. Where doubt exists, the studio will ask to see the order of intended installation.

Decorative and architectural work — pillars, friezes, kapota mouldings, lion-bracket vyālas, even unconsecrated dvārapālas for garden gateways — these are accepted without hesitation. The line is drawn at the consecrable image alone.

The Order of an Engagement

I
Letter of Inquiry

Patron writes to the studio at Sannidhi Street, in Tamil or English, with site, scope, and the lunar month preferred for commencement.

II
Visit & Reading

The sthapati attends the site, reads the existing fabric, and prepares a written assessment within forty days. Travel borne by the patron.

III
Articles of Agreement

Drawn up bilingually, signed before two witnesses, with one-third on signing, one-third at the half-mark, the balance on installation.

IV
Drawing & Carving

Drawings precede stone by no less than four months. The carving room is open to the patron on the second Sunday of every month.

V
Seating of the Work

The work is set upon its place and the studio's responsibility ends with the priest's first arghya or the trustee's signed receipt.
