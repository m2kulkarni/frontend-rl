A Kyoto museum devoted to the daily life of the Edo period (1603–1868) — its machiya interiors, woodblock prints, and household objects. We work slowly, exhibit briefly, and present each season as it would have been kept inside the house.

Currently on view

Three things to see this season.

A current exhibition, a piece from the permanent collection, and a fresh return from restoration. Each is presented in its own room, with notes printed on the day.

Exhibition · 04 Oct 2024 – 14 Mar 2025

Hiroshige's Kisokaidō: Sixty-Nine Stations

The complete print series of the inland route between Edo and Kyoto, drawn from the Naitō and Kawabe holdings. Twenty-two impressions are shown on rotation; the full sequence is exhibited only in the closing fortnight.

Permanent collection · Gallery III

Nishijin Maru-obi, c. 1798

A double-sided Nishijin obi woven in Kyoto for a merchant's wife, with karakusa scrolling on indigo silk and a reverse face of plain bleached hemp. Acquired from the Yoshida family of Muromachi-dōri in 1981; rewoven supports added 2014.

Architecture · Reopened June 2024

The Doma Kitchen, Restored

After eighteen months closed for repair, the earthen-floored kitchen returns to view with its hearth, smoke shelf, and oki-kamado relaid by Saitō Sekkō of Kameoka. Visitors may stand in the doma during scheduled walk-throughs.
