The collection was assembled from the Yoshida family donation of 1962 — a Kyoto merchant household's three-generation accumulation of prints, dress, lacquer, and household goods — and has since grown through careful acquisition and bequest to 4,318 objects, of which roughly four hundred are on view at any moment across the machiya's six galleries.

i. Ukiyo-e

No. 01 / Five

Prints浮世絵 — UKIYO-E

Single-sheet nishiki-e and illustrated books from the late eighteenth century through the closing decades of the Edo period, with notable holdings in landscape, kabuki portraiture, and meisho-e of the Kyoto-Osaka region.

Acc. 1962.0014 — Yoshida bequest

Naruto no fūha — Whirlpools at Awa

Utagawa Hiroshigec. 1855Edo

Ōban triptych in nishiki-e, the central sheet bearing the censor's aratame seal of the third month. Acquired with the founding gift; a key sheet for the museum's Hiroshige holdings, exhibited in rotation every eighteen months.

ii. Senshoku

No. 02 / Five

Textiles染織 — SENSHOKU

Kosode, kimono, fukusa wrapping cloths, and obi from the Kyō-yūzen and Edo komon traditions, including a small group of sumptuary-restricted merchant-class garments characteristic of the Tenpō era.

Acc. 1962.0671

Kosode with pine, bamboo & plum

Yūzen-dyed silkc. 1780Kyoto

Outer kosode in lavender chirimen silk, the shoulders worked with shōchikubai motifs in resist-paste yūzen and couched gold thread. Worn by Yoshida Mine, daughter of the donor's great-grandfather, at her wedding in 1782.

Reversible coat of indigo-dyed cotton densely sashiko-stitched in concentric layers; interior painted with a dragon among waves. Acquired from the Kanda firefighters' brigade descendants association in 1994.

iii. Shikki

No. 03 / Five

Lacquerware漆器 — SHIKKI

Boxes, trays, sagemono, and tableware in maki-e and negoro techniques, with a particular concentration of inrō and writing boxes from the workshops of Kyoto and the Kii peninsula.

Acc. 1987.0214

Inrō with chrysanthemum & waves

Lacquer on wood, gold maki-ec. 1820Kyoto

Four-case inrō, the ground in roiro black with high-relief takamaki-e chrysanthemums riding stylized waves; ojime of carved coral, netsuke of stained ivory (signed Tomotada). Purchased at auction in Osaka, March 1987.

Acc. 1962.0291

Writing box (suzuribako) with autumn grasses

Hiramaki-e & mother-of-pearlc. 1750Kyoto

Rectangular suzuribako with sloping lid, decorated in low-relief gold maki-e and aogai shell inlay depicting susuki, kuzu, and bush clover at moonrise. Among the earliest objects in the Yoshida bequest, traditionally attributed to the Shiomi workshop.

Negoro footed tray

Cinnabar & black lacquer on woodc. 1670Kii province

Round tray with raised foot in negoro technique: vermilion lacquer worn through to underlying black at the rim by long temple use, the deliberate aesthetic of monastic wear. Acquired from Negoro-ji's deaccession of duplicates, 1978.

iv. Tōji

No. 04 / Five

Ceramics陶磁 — TŌJI

Tea-ceremony wares from Raku, Karatsu, Hagi, and Kyō kilns, alongside everyday Seto and Imari household porcelain that documents the spread of ceramic tableware into urban Edo-period kitchens.

Acc. 1969.0033

Black Raku tea bowl, named "Hatsushimo"

Raku ware, Sanyūc. 1760Kyoto

Hand-built black-glazed chawan, the foot trimmed with a single decisive cut and the rim slightly compressed at one side. Box inscribed by Omotesenke iemoto Sottakusai; gifted by the Imamiya tea family in 1969.

Acc. 1962.0809

Imari blue-and-white covered jar

Porcelain, sometsukec. 1810Hizen

Lidded household jar, decorated in underglaze cobalt with karakusa scroll on the body and a domestic kamon at the lid. Used for storing pickled plums in the Yoshida kitchen for at least four generations before its donation in 1962.

Set of seven Seto soba cups

Stoneware, ash glazec. 1850Owari

Seven (originally ten) cups in pale ash-yellow glaze with iron-painted bamboo, scaled for the standardised Edo soba portion. Acquired in 2011 from the surviving stock of the Yamashiro-ya noodle shop, Pontochō.

v. Kenchiku-zai

No. 05 / Five

Machiya Fragments町家建築材 — KENCHIKU-ZAI

Architectural members salvaged in situ from Kyoto townhouses demolished between 1968 and 2003 — ramma transoms, kōshi lattice panels, sliding-door pulls, and roof tiles — preserved with their address of origin and date of removal.

Acc. 1979.0204

Ramma transom with crane & pine

Carved keyaki woodc. 1845Nakagyō, Kyoto

Single-panel openwork transom, 1830 × 380 mm, salvaged from the Tominokōji-Sanjō merchant house "Sasaya" before its demolition in 1979. The crane is carved in three planes; traces of the original gofun whitening remain at the wing tips.

Acc. 1998.0082

Kōshi lattice façade panel

Sugi wood, sumi-stainedc. 1860Kamigyō, Kyoto

Full-height first-floor lattice from a textile-merchant's storefront, in the regular kyō-gōshi rhythm typical of weaving households (itoya-gōshi). Removed during a 1998 façade restoration on Nishijin's Ōmiya-dōri and replaced with a faithful copy.

Acc. 2003.0117

Onigawara end-tile with paulownia crest

Iburi-gawara fired tilec. 1810Fushimi, Kyoto

Smoked-grey ridge-end tile, 410 mm tall, the face moulded with a five-three paulownia kamon and a band of cloud scroll. Recovered from the Aburakōji-Shichijō teahouse roof during typhoon damage repairs in October 2003.
