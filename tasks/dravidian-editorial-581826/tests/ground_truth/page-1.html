The Lead Essay

The Granite Vimana
at Tanjavur

Nine Hundred Years of Shadow and Plumb-Line
By R. VENKATARAMAN, Reader in Architecture, Annamalai University

Standing two hundred and sixteen feet at its kalasha, the Brihadisvara's sixteen-tiered tower has cast its noon shadow inside its own prakara every Aippasi since the reign of Rajaraja the Great. Our correspondent measures the tower against its own myth — the apocryphal cap-stone, the eighty granite blocks of the sikhara, and the patient mathematics of the Chola sutragrahin who set them plumb in the year 1010 of the common era.

Field Report

The Outer Prakaras of Srirangam

By S. Kamala Ranganathan · Srirangam, Purattasi

A walking survey of the seventh prakara — the Chitirai Veedhi — where the temple gives way to the town and the town gives way again to the temple. Our writer traces the surviving Vijayanagara mandapas along its eastern stretch, notes the four extant gopuras of subsidiary shrines, and records the names of the seven sthapati families who still maintain the Garuda Vahana for the Vaikuntha Ekadasi processions.

On the Craft

Lime, Jaggery & the Patient Plaster

By Sthapati K. Muthiah · Mamallapuram

A working note on sudhai — the lime-and-jaggery plaster by which the Pallava and Chola figures on every gopuram have outlived their stone. The author's own workshop in Mamallapuram still grinds shell-lime with palm-jaggery, kadukkai, and the white of country-egg, in proportions handed down from his grandfather Ganapati Sthapati of the 1880s. The recipe, the curing, and the proper season for it.

From the Editor

A Letter from Mada Veedhi

By The Editor · Mylapore, Aippasi

Three months on from our independence, the question put to this journal grows louder: what is owed to the temple-builders' inheritance when the Republic must build schools, dams, and railway sheds before it builds vimanas? The editor argues that the plumb-line, the adi-tala, and the kanaka-shilpa belong as fully to the modern engineer as to the agama-pandit — and that to forget so is to forget the country.
