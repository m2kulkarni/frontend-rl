The Studio

— On the practice of cut-tile mosaic in the Safavid manner —

Atelier Naqsh-e Jahan was founded by Darius Farahani in Isfahan in 2011, a few hundred paces from the qibla iwan of the Masjed-e Shah whose seven-colour spandrels first set the course of his work. The studio is devoted to two living traditions of the Safavid period: haft-rang, the painted seven-colour cuerda-seca tile, and moarraq, the patient mosaic-faïence in which each shard of glazed earthenware is cut to its outline and set into plaster. We do not reproduce; we continue.

The workshop now occupies a vaulted ground floor on Chahar Bagh Street, where six craftspeople grind pigment, cut blanks against templates of pricked card, and fire the small kiln by night. Commissions arrive from conservators, architects of religious buildings, and a small number of private collectors. Darius works from a drawing board beside the south window; visitors are received on Wednesday afternoons by appointment, and tile is shipped, crated in straw, to nine countries.
