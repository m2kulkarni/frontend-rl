The Ardestan Hammam Spandrels

The Brief

❖  ❖  ❖

In the spring of 2018 the Ardestan Heritage Council, presided over by Dr. Mahnaz Khorasani, addressed a sealed letter to this atelier requesting that the four ruined spandrels of the Hammam-e Vakil — the small Vakil bathhouse on Kuche-ye Sangtarashan, west of the Friday Mosque — be returned to their original Safavid scheme. The bathhouse, in continuous use from 1668 until its closure in 1973, had suffered three successive whitewashings, a partial roof collapse in the winter of 1981, and an unfortunate cement infill in 1994. Of the original tilework only forty-one shards remained in situ, all of them in the lower register of the eastern wall.

The council's brief was unusually specific. We were not to invent. We were to recover. A budget of 1,420,000,000 rials was allocated against an eighteen-month schedule, with a clause permitting a three-month extension at the discretion of the supervising mason. The contract was signed at the council offices on 14 Khordad 1397 (4 June 2018) in the presence of Dr. Khorasani, the council's secretary Mr. Reza Tabatabai, and the master-mason Hossein Tabrizi, who would oversee installation.

The Cartoon

❖  ❖  ❖

Recovery began not at a drawing board but in the bathhouse archive itself — a low vaulted store-room tucked beside the cold chamber, where some twenty-three rolls of paper had lain undisturbed since the building's closure. Working with the archivist Soraya Esfahani over nine days in July 2018, we identified, among water-damaged ledgers and a 1924 inventory, a fragmentary cartoon on linen-backed paper, drawn in iron-gall ink and corrected in red ochre. It bore the partial signature of one ʿAli ibn Muhammad-Riza, tilemaker of Yazd, and a date which, by the surviving digits, can only be read as 1083 AH — that is, 1672 of the common era.

The cartoon described, in unbroken passages and in shorthand for the missing ones, the four spandrels of the central iwan: two cobalt grounds bearing a saz-leaf vine drawn in turquoise, with palmettes opening at the spring of the arch, and an inscription band running the inner curve in gold-leaf reserved on a deep oxblood field. The verse, identified by Dr. Khorasani as the seventh distich of Saʿdi's Bustan, had been partially erased; the missing characters were reconstructed by comparing extant sister hammams at Kashan and Naʾin.

“The hand that drew this cartoon in 1672 set down every joint between every shard. Three centuries later, the same joints met our blade.”

The Cutting

❖  ❖  ❖

Cutting commenced on 9 September 2018 and concluded on 22 July 2019. Across those eleven months the atelier produced 4,200 individual moʿaraq shards: 1,860 in cobalt, 1,290 in copper-turquoise, 760 in ivory, 220 in oxblood, and 70 reserved for the gold-leaf inlay of the inscription band. Each shard was struck from biscuit-fired tile blanks of 22 mm thickness, scored with a tungsten-tipped wheel, and cleaved over the edge of a carved boxwood block — the same technique recorded by Riyahi in his 1937 survey of the Yazd workshops, and the technique to which the original 1672 shards still bore witness.

The most exacting passage was the inner inscription band, where the gold-leaf inlay required that each oxblood shard be ground on its bevel to within 0.4 mm of the cartoon's intended kerf. Two apprentices, Niloufar Ranjbar and Behrouz Salimi, worked solely on the inscription for fourteen weeks. Of the 70 inscription shards, 64 survived the fire; the remaining six were re-cut and re-fired in February 2019.

Installation

❖  ❖  ❖

Installation was supervised on site by master-mason Hossein Tabrizi, then in his sixty-eighth year, with whom this atelier has now collaborated on five commissions. Tabrizi's method — a lime-gypsum bedding mortar at 3:1, struck flush and burnished with a leather pad before the shards are laid face-down into the cartoon and the back-bedding poured — is recorded in his own monograph of 2011 and was followed without alteration. The eastern spandrel was set first, on 14 August 2019, and the four were complete by the morning of 9 October 2019. The site was opened to the public, with a small ceremony attended by the council and by the surviving daughter of the bathhouse's last keeper, on 21 October 2019.

Coda

❖  ❖  ❖

The Ardestan spandrels are not a copy. They are a continuation: the same cartoon, the same kerf, the same fire. Whatever was lost in the three whitewashings has now been answered in cobalt, turquoise and gold, and the verse of Saʿdi runs once more across the inner curve of the arch. The ledger is closed. The bath is open again on Wednesdays and Fridays.

Materials

A reckoning of pigments, glazes and metals

Cobalt Oxide
Sourced from the historic deposits at Qamsar, near Kashan, ground in this atelier's mortar to 220 mesh. The cobalt yields the deep night-blue of the spandrel ground; 38 kg were consumed across the commission.
Copper-Turquoise Glaze
Compounded in the workshop from copper carbonate, soda ash and a quartz frit at a 6 : 19 : 75 ratio. Applied at 1.4 mm thickness and fired in a downdraft kiln to 940 °C, held for three hours, cooled over forty.
Ivory Slip
A tin-opacified white, drawn from the Yazd quarry tradition. Used for the calmed interior fields and for the highlights of the saz-leaf vine.
Gold-Leaf Inlay
Reserved exclusively for the inscription band along the inner curve of the arch. Beaten leaf at 22-carat, laid into oxblood ground, sealed beneath a low-fired transparent glaze. 70 shards in total; 7.4 grams of leaf consumed.
Oxblood Ground
An iron-rich slip stabilised with manganese, used only as a setting for the gold inscription and at one cartouche over the western spandrel — the single emphatic note in an otherwise restrained palette.
Lime-Gypsum Bedding
Mixed on site at 3 : 1 by Hossein Tabrizi, burnished before laying. Curing time observed at fourteen days per spandrel.

Chronology

Eleven months from cartoon to setting

    Contract signed in the council offices, Ardestan, by Dr. Khorasani, secretary Tabatabai, master-mason Tabrizi.
  2. 11 — 19 JULY 2018
    Recovery of the 1672 cartoon from the bathhouse archive with archivist Soraya Esfahani.
  3. 09 SEPT 2018
    First biscuit-firing of cobalt blanks. Cutting begins.
  4. FEBRUARY 2019
    Six inscription shards re-cut and re-fired after kiln loss.
  5. 22 JULY 2019
    Last shard cut. 4,200 pieces complete and racked for the journey south.

    Eastern spandrel set first by Hossein Tabrizi, lime-gypsum bedding at 3:1.
  7. 09 OCT 2019
    All four spandrels complete. Curing under canvas for fourteen days.
  8. 21 OCT 2019
    Hammam-e Vakil reopened to the public. Wednesdays and Fridays.
