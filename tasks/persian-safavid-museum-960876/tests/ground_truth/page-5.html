— III —

Getting There & Within

By rail, on foot, and by gentle ramp

By Metro & on Foot

RailThe simplest passage is by the Isfahan Metro, Line 1, alighting at Imam Hossein Station. Take the southern exit and turn onto Khiyābān-i Chahār Bāgh.

WalkingFrom the station the museum lies six minutes south, on the right side of the avenue, recognisable by its turquoise iwan and pair of cypress trees.

By CarriageTaxis from Naqsh-i Jahān and the bazaar quarters set down beside the porter's lodge.

Accessibility

Ramped AccessThe Dīvānkhāneh offers full ramped access to every public gallery, courtyard, and the tea-house. Thresholds have been levelled with stone wedges so that no passage requires steps.

WheelchairsTwo wheelchairs are kept ready at the porter's lodge at the front gate, available without charge on a first-come basis. A staff attendant may be requested at the same desk.

SeatingCushioned benches are placed at every other bay, and a quiet rest-room adjoins the manuscript room.

— IV —

The Tea-house & the Camera

Two further matters for the considerate visitor

Qahveh-khāneh-yi Chahār Bāgh

The museum's tea-house, Qahveh-khāneh-yi Chahār Bāgh, opens off the second courtyard beneath a vault of painted muqarnas. It serves Persian tea drawn from a samovar, saffron sherbet, fresh dates, and small pastries from a Yazd kitchen.

It keeps the same hours as the galleries and welcomes visitors with or without a museum ticket; a small terrace overlooks the reflecting pool.

Photography

Visitors may photograph freely without flash in all permanent galleries, the courtyards, the iwan, and the tea-house. Tripods and selfie-poles are kept at the lodge.

Photography of any kind is not permitted in the manuscript room, where the light levels are kept low to preserve the gold-illuminated folios. Notebooks and pencils are warmly encouraged in their place.

— A note for the visitor — Groups of more than ten persons are kindly asked to write ahead, that a docent of the museum may be appointed to accompany them through the galleries. The Dīvānkhāneh is, before all things, a quiet house; we ask that voices be kept low in the manuscript room and the chamber of the qiblah tiles.
