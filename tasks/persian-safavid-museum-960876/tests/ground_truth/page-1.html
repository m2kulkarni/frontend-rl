Tilework from the Maydān-i Naqsh-i Jahān

From the Directorship

A Word from the Director

It is the long custom of this house to greet visitors at the threshold and not at the gallery door; and so I greet you here, before you have set foot upon our marble. The Dīvānkhāneh was conceived as a quiet annex to the great square — a room in which the noisy magnificence of Isfahan might be slowed, sectioned, and read.

This season our three exhibitions answer one another. The tilework of the Maydān shows the Safavid eye trained upward; the painted veranda of Chehel Sotoun returns it to the human face and the diplomatic hour; and the grammar of ornament in Gallery III asks, simply, how any of it was drawn at all. Together they propose that style, in the seventeenth century at Isfahan, was not decoration laid upon things — it was the way things were thought.

We are honoured to receive you. Should you wish to linger, the upper hall is reserved each Friday for readers of the manuscripts; tea is served in the courtyard until the call of the maghrib.

— Dr. Roya Hashemi-Vance
Director, Dīvānkhāneh · Āzar 1403
