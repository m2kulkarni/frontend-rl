Maison Saint-Vrain · Anno MMXIX

The House & Its Cooks

A 14th-century cloth merchant's lodging restored to candlelight; a brigade of three at its hearth.

Long before the kitchen was a kitchen, the house at 14 rue des Drapiers was a counting-room. The first stones were laid in 1378 by Hugues le Mercier, a draper who traded fine Picard worsted as far as Bruges and the Hansa towns. The cellar — the same cellar in which our wines now rest — held bales of cloth then, sealed against damp by oak boards he is said to have cut himself. By 1426 his grandson had widened the street frontage and added the carved stone lintel, still in place above our door, bearing the merchant's mark and the words Deus providebit.

The house passed through six generations of cloth-merchants, two notaries, a wig-maker, and, between 1894 and 2017, a quiet ironmongery. When the last ironmonger closed his shutters, the building was structurally sound but exhausted: the timbers brown with coal-smoke, the plaster fallen in three rooms, the cellar half-filled with rusted hinges. The diocesan archives held the original 1378 notarial deed, and the front-elevation drawings from 1426 survived nearly intact. From these, the architect Élise Moreau drew the restoration that ran from October 2017 to the spring of 2019.

Nothing was added that could not have been there in 1426. The ceiling beams in the dining room are the originals, cleaned by hand over four months. The oak refectory table seats fourteen and was made in Amiens, by Atelier Verluise, from a single Picard oak felled in 2018. The candles are beeswax, set in iron stands forged at Compiègne. The diaper tile underfoot — a quatrefoil-in-square pattern — was cut to a 14th-century model held in the Musée de Picardie. Only the kitchen, hidden behind the rear wall, is wholly modern, and that by necessity.

IIThe Brigade

Aurélien Brescot

Chef de Cuisine · since 2019

Born in 1978 in Saint-Quentin, two streets from the Basilica, Aurélien Brescot grew up in the kitchen of his grandmother Renée — a Picard cook of the old persuasion, who kept her hutspot recipe in a notebook bound with cloth tape. He left for Paris at nineteen, and over the next two decades cooked his way through kitchens that taught him, in turn, restraint, ferocity, and silence.

At Maison Saint-Vrain he sets a single supper of seven courses each evening, six nights a week. The menu changes by the moon, never by the calendar. He cooks on a hearth and on induction; he writes the menu by hand on parchment paper each afternoon at four. He answers the telephone himself when the line is quiet.

Formerly chef de partie at L'Arpège (Paris, 2003–2008) under Alain Passard; sous-chef at Le Chapon Fin (Bordeaux, 2008–2014); head chef at the Hôtel de la Cathédrale, Amiens (2014–2018).

Margaux Vinet

Pâtissière · since 2020

Margaux Vinet came to the house in February 2020, four weeks before the world shut its doors, and stayed. She trained at the École Ferrandi in Paris and read history of medicine on the side; she keeps both shelves of books in the pantry, the herbal beside Lenôtre. Her sweets are the supper's quiet conclusion: walnut tarts steeped in vin de noix, rose-petal flummeries, a single pear in spiced wine, the medlar fool that has appeared on the menu in November every year since she arrived.

She bakes the suppers' bread before service — a sourdough miche of Picard blé de la Saint-Pierre, a rye for the cheese — and grinds her own spices on a small marble in the corner of the kitchen.

École Ferrandi, 2014. Pâtissière at La Grenouillère (La Madelaine-sous-Montreuil, 2015–2019). Stagiaire at Tortoreto, Rome, summer 2017.

Tobie Lemerre

Sommelier · Cellar-keeper · since 2019

Tobie Lemerre keeps the cellar — the same vaulted room that, six hundred years ago, held Hugues le Mercier's worsted. He came to the house from a small négoce in Beaune and brought with him a sense, unfashionable in his trade, that the bottle should stand back from the food. His list runs to four hundred and twelve labels, weighted toward the Île-de-France, the Champagne, and the mead of the abbeys; he buys, also, from three growers in the Aisne whom he visits every spring on a bicycle.

He decants every red older than fifteen years, on principle, and pours into pewter cups for the medieval pairings. He will, if asked, recite the cellar inventory from memory.

Diplôme de l'Université du Vin de Suze-la-Rousse, 2011. Caviste at Maison Champy, Beaune (2012–2019).

IIIThe House at Table

There are seats for fourteen at the long table and four at a smaller side table, eighteen suppers in all. Service begins at half past seven and ends, ordinarily, near midnight. We have no menu choice. The supper is what it is, and it changes with the season, the weather, the catch of M. Delavigne at the Saturday market. We ask that you come hungry, with three free hours, and with a willingness to be cooked for.

The brigade numbers three. There is no maître d'; Aurélien greets you at the door, Margaux pours the first wine, Tobie tells you what is in the glass. Plates come from the kitchen carried by whichever of the three is freest. We have found, over five years, that this is the way the house wishes to be run.

Aurélien Brescot
