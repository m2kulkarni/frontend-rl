Bill of the House  ·  Saint-Martin's Tide

The Supper

Souper de la Saint-Martin  ·  11 November — 23 December

Seven courses brought to one long table at half-past seven o'clock, in the manner of the cloth-merchants who once supped here by tallow-light. The bill turns with the season; the fires are lit at six. One hundred and forty-five euros the cover, wines apart.

I
Premier Service · Le Bouillon

Bouillon de Poule au Safran

An old broth of bone-hen and roasted leek, drawn slowly through a night and a morning until it carries the colour of amber. Saffron from the lower Somme is steeped at the last, with a single thread of preserved lemon and a crouton rubbed in goose fat.

Served in the small black bowls thrown by a potter at Marquaix, scarcely a half-litre, that the appetite may rise rather than be satisfied.

— Set at the Hour —
II
Deuxième Service · L'Entrée Chaude

Tarte aux Poireaux et Lard Fumé

A short crust of buckwheat and butter, blind-baked at first light, filled with poireaux sweated in cream and dotted with thumb-cut lardons smoked over apple-wood at La Bergerie de Vraignes.

Glazed at the salamander to a colour between honey and copper; cut into long wedges and served with a salt-cellar of grey fleur de Guérande set down between every two guests.

— Set at the Hour —
III
Troisième Service · Le Poisson

Brochet Poché, Sauce Verjus

A pike taken from the slow water of the Somme above Cappy, poached whole in a fumet of riesling and bay, then portioned at the sideboard. The sauce is mounted of verjus pressed in our own kitchen from green grapes of the Soissonnais, with shallot and a tail of butter.

Strewn with a thin coin of pickled radish and the pale inner leaves of celery; nothing more, that the freshwater taste be not muddied.

— Set at the Hour —
IV
Quatrième Service · Le Rôt

Chevreau Rôti aux Coings

A milk-fed kid from Ferme du Bois-Hallot, twelve weeks old, rubbed in the morning with thyme, juniper and pepper of the long sort, and turned slowly above embers of vine-cutting until the skin cracks like parchment. The shoulder is carved at the table; the loin rests under butter.

Beside it, quinces braised in red wine and a spoon of their own jelly; a single roast onion blackened in the ash, peeled at the edge of the plate.

— Set at the Hour —
V
Cinquième Service · Le Vert

Salade d'Herbes Amères

A plain dish in the medieval manner: dandelion, wild chicory, mâche, and the small bitter heart of treviso, dressed simply with hazelnut oil pressed in October at Domvast and a few drops of cider vinegar from Boves.

Set down between the meat and the cheese, as our forebears would have it, to refresh the palate before the long second half of the supper begins.

— Set at the Hour —
VI
Sixième Service · Le Fromage

Tomme de Picardie, Miel de Sarrasin

A young raw-milk tomme aged seven weeks in the cellars at Vraignes, washed with bière de garde so that the rind takes on the colour of damp brick. The paste is supple and faintly grassy, the smell ample but never vexed.

Served with a comb-spoon of dark buckwheat honey from Rucher Caudron, a slip of pickled walnut, and four oat-and-rye flatbreads baked at the back of the bread oven.

— Set at the Hour —
VII
Septième Service · L'Entremet

Rissoles aux Pommes et Cardamome

Small folded pastries of butter dough, filled with reinette apples cooked down with green cardamom, a spoon of fig molasses, and the merest grating of black pepper. Fried at the moment of service in clarified butter, drained on linen, and dusted with rough cane sugar.

Three to a guest, served upon a slate, with a small glass of warmed marc de Champagne for those who care to take it. With this the supper closes; coffee is brought into the small parlour after.

— Set at the Hour —

A Word on Provenance

Every supper at the Maison is built upon the labour of three houses, named here as a courtesy and a thanks. Their hands are in every plate set down upon our trestle.

Ferme du Bois-Hallot  ·  La Bergerie de Vraignes  ·  Rucher Caudron

The bill is fixed at €145 the cover, taken in one sitting at half-past seven o'clock, Wednesday through Saturday. The pairing of seven wines from the cellar is offered at €78 a head. Bread, butter, water of the table and coffee at the close are included. Service is settled.
