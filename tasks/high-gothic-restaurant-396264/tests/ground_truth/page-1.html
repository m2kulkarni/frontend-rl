A Word from the Kitchen

Welcome to the long table. We are Maison Saint-Vrain, a candlelit table d'hôte tucked beneath the gabled houses of the old cloth-merchants' quarter, where since the spring of 2019 we have served the slow suppers of medieval Picardy as I have come to remember them. Three nights each week — Thursday, Friday, and Saturday — at 19h30 sharp, twenty-six guests sit down to a single seven-course menu drawn from the cellar books of the abbey of Saint-Acheul, the market verses of Le Clerc, and the cooking my grandmother kept on a wood stove in Boves. There is no choosing, no à la carte, no second sitting; only what the season has sent us, dressed for the candle and the company at hand.

Aurélien Brescot, chef de maison

II · The Cellar

Vaults Beneath
rue des Drapiers

Two-hundred-and-forty bins under chalk: the dry whites of Cassel, the cidres bouchés of the Pays de Bray, claret of the Loire, and a small honest shelf of vins de paille kept aside for feast nights. Sommelière Margaux Pélégrin pours.
