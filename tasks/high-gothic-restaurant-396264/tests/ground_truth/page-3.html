Liber Vinorum · MMXXIV

The Cellar

Beneath the cloth-merchants' hall, in vaults dug under the rue des Drapiers in 1387, two hundred and ninety bottles wait their hour. Aurélien Brescot and our sommelière Inès Vaudremer keep the keys; what follows is a small register, faithful to the season.

On the Bottles in Our Keeping

Trois Maisons · Loire · Bourgogne · Picardie

The list is divided in three. To the left, the long, slate-rinsed wines of the Loire — Anjou and Touraine — which carry our river-fish and our white meats. In the middle, the elder Burgundies kept back from auction, decanted only at table. To the right, the meads and orchard ciders of the Somme and the Aisne, brewed in the manner of the abbey houses, served in pewter cups as a chaser to game and to the long puddings of Lent.

Domaine aux Moines

Savennières — La Roche aux Moines · 2018

Tessa Laroche, the third daughter to keep this priory vineyard above the Loire at Savennières, presses her chenin late and slow. The 2018 has begun to throw its yellow gold; on the nose, dried verbena, beeswax, the smoke of a snuffed candle. We pour it with the carp aux pruneaux and the salt-baked turbot, and beg the table not to chill it past cellar-cold.

Bouteille · €128

Clos Rougeard

Saumur-Champigny · Les Poyeux · 2014

Bottled by the late Foucault brothers and laid down by us in March of 2017. Cabernet franc of a gravity rare in the Loire — graphite, blackcurrant leaf, the tannin of an old breviary's binding. Decant a full hour. For the saddle of hare and the pigeon en croûte.

Domaine Faiveley

Mercurey 1er Cru · Clos des Myglands · 2015

Erwan Faiveley sent us six cases of the 2015 Mercurey in the late autumn of 2019; three remain. The wine has settled into its long-bone middle age — black cherry kirsch, woodsmoke, the iron of a pilgrim's spoon. It is the bottle we open most often for the venison stew with chestnuts and for the long evening in November when the candles last as the table.

Bouteille · €165

Domaine Comte Armand

Pommard 1er Cru · Clos des Epeneaux · 2009

From a magnum, drawn slowly over the course of a single supper. The Comte's monopole drinks now in the deepest autumn of itself: dried fig, leather, the church-incense of old burgundian glory. Reserved for tables of six and over.

Rucher Caudron

Hydromel élevé en fût de chêne · 2019

Henri Caudron keeps his bees on the chalk above Vaire-sous-Corbie, in sight of the cathedral at Amiens. This four-year mead, fermented from buckwheat honey and aged twenty months in old Calvados oak, drinks like a half-amber Tokaji that has seen weather. We pour it warm at the close of the meal with the fig tart and the walnut wafer; medieval suppers ended this way and ours do also.

Carafon 25cl · €34

Cidrerie du Vieux Bourg

Cidre fermier brut · Pays de Bray · 2022

A bottle-conditioned cider from Marie-Hélène Plouvier, made from twenty-three orchard varieties — Bedan, Mettais, Noël des Champs. Pale stone-fruit, salted apple skin, a finish like wet flint. Drunk in pewter, between courses, to clear the palate of butter.

The cellar is shown by appointment on Friday afternoons between three and five. Mademoiselle Vaudremer takes the visitors herself, six at a time, down the four flights of stone.

Accord du Soir · I

The Long Table — Six Cups

A flight of six pourings keyed to the supper of the day: Muscadet from the Briords with the oysters; Savennières with the carp; the Mercurey with the saddle of hare; the Pommard with the cheese-board; the Caudron mead with the fig tart; and the Groult Calvados to close.

Par convive · €92

Accord du Soir · II

The Cloister Flight — Four Cups

For Friday and the days of fast: a quieter procession of low-alcohol pourings — Bouzeron Aligoté, the Chablis Vaudésir, the Plouvier cider, and the buckwheat hydromel of Caudron. Held at cellar temperature throughout, served from glazed earthenware.

Par convive · €58
