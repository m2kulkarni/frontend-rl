About Rikuō  ·  History & Charter

Two centuries
of an open gate,
kept in Yanaka.

Rikuō began in 1825 as a private academy above a paper-merchant's storehouse, two streets west of Yanaka cemetery. It has, in the years since, twice been rebuilt — once in stone, once in cedar — and has not, in any year, closed its gate.

I. Institutional History

From shoin to chartered university.

Rikuō's record is preserved in the Hakuro Library as four bound volumes of ledgers, an unbroken series of attendance registers from 1827 onward, and the personal papers of its first three rectors. What follows is a brief reading of those documents.

1825

Founding of Rikuō Shoin

The merchant-scholar Tachibana Genshirō (1779–1851), retired from the Echigo paper trade, opened a small private academy on the upper floor of his Yanaka warehouse. The first roster lists eleven pupils — six sons of samurai, three of merchants, two of farmers — taught the Four Books, brush calligraphy, and elementary arithmetic. Tuition was paid, where it was paid at all, in rice or in labour at the binding bench.

1868

The Revised Charter

In the third month of Meiji 1, the second rector Tachibana Sōan revised the academy's founding statement, replacing its earlier Confucian preamble with a brief charter of seven articles. The document survives in two copies; the cleaner is held by the Yushima Seidō Bunko, the working copy in our own archive. Its opening clause is reproduced below.

1903

Reorganization and chartering

Under the Senmon-gakkō Ordinance, Rikuō Shoin was reorganized as a chartered university with three faculties — Letters, Historical Studies, and Industrial Craft — and its first formal entrance examinations were administered in the spring of 1904. The academy's Edo buildings were retained; a brick lecture hall was added at the eastern edge of the precinct.

1923

The Kantō earthquake; reconstruction

The brick lecture hall, the print workshop, and roughly 8,400 volumes of the library were lost in the September fires. Reconstruction, completed in 1928 to designs by Yorinaga Tetsujirō, returned the campus to timber framing and white plaster, with the present cedar-shingled main hall set on the original Edo foundations. The library's losses were partly recovered through donations from former pupils.

1949 — present

Postwar continuity

Rikuō was reaccredited under the new Education Law in 1949 with the addition of a fourth faculty, Translation. The institution remains small — by deliberate charter, never to exceed 1,500 enrolled — and continues to operate from the same Yanaka block on which it was founded.

II. The Revised Charter, 1868

The seven articles of Tachibana Sōan.

The charter is a brief document — fewer than four hundred characters in its working copy — and yet it is the longest-standing of the university's governing texts. Its first article remains, by tradition, recited at every spring matriculation. The vertical reading at left preserves Sōan's brush order; the gloss at right is the standard 1972 translation by Itō Yumiko, professor emerita of Letters.

學不問貴賤、門啓自旦至暮。

Learning shall not distinguish between high and low; the gate stands open from first light to last.

So opens the revised charter as Sōan set it down in the third month of 1868, in the small upstairs room above the warehouse where his grandfather had taught forty-three years earlier. The phrase ‘from first light to last’ is, in the original, the colloquial 自旦至暮 — and is taken at the academy quite literally: the south gate is unbarred at sunrise and not closed until the lamps are lit.

The remaining six articles concern matters of conduct, the upkeep of the library, the rector's stipend, and the obligation of every pupil to teach, in some form, before leaving.

Article II
The library shall be open to any who can read; entry shall not require the rector's leave.
Article III
No pupil shall pay for tuition what cannot be paid; what cannot be paid shall be entered as labour at the binding bench.
Article IV
The rector shall receive no stipend greater than the senior cook's, nor lesser than the junior cook's.
Article V
Every pupil, on completing four winters of study, shall in turn teach one season at the academy or another, before leaving.
Article VI
Books damaged shall be repaired at the binding bench; books lost shall be copied by hand and returned.
Article VII
That which is here written may be revised only by the assent of three rectors in succession; not otherwise.
Sealed at Yanaka, the third month of Meiji 1
Witnessed and re-affirmed, Reiwa 6 (2024)
