Hakuro Library  ·  白鷺文庫

The 白
White-Heron
Reading Room.

Hakuro Library and Archives is the principal repository of Rikuō University, housing 184,000 volumes across general circulation, rare books, and the university's growing collection of Edo-period maps, almanacs, and printed ephemera.

FOUNDED 1902  /  YANAKA, BUNKYŌ-ADJACENT  /  OPEN TUE–SAT
A note on the building The reading room occupies the second storey of a 1902 reinforced-concrete adaptation of a kura storehouse — one of the earliest such conversions in Tokyo. The shōji-screened mezzanine was added in 1957.

§ 01  /  HOLDINGS

Three pillars of the collection.

Beyond general circulation, three named gifts and one continuous acquisition define the character of the library. Each has its own catalog volume, available in printed form at the reference desk.

184,000

General Holdings

CIRC.  ·  OPEN STACKS

Across literature, history, philosophy, and the applied crafts — weighted toward Japanese-language scholarship of the long nineteenth century. Open stacks on floors one and two; closed stacks below grade.

312

Bunka–Bunsei Almanacs

WOODBLOCK  ·  1804–1830

A complete sequence of woodblock-printed daijō-goyomi covering the Bunka and Bunsei eras, including civic and farmers' calendars from Edo, Kyoto, and Osaka publishers. Cataloged 2011–2014.

山村

The Yamamura Collection

DONATED 1958  ·  1,470 SHEETS

Donated in the spring of 1958 by the family of the cartographer Yamamura Sōkichi (1881–1956), the collection comprises 1,470 sheet maps, fold-out plates, and ward-level ezu of Edo and the surrounding provinces, dating from the late seventeenth century to the eve of the Meiji Restoration. Highlights include three intact copies of the Bushū-toshima-gun-ezu (1745) and a hand-coloured presentation scroll of the Shitamachi waterways prepared for Tokugawa Ieyoshi. A bilingual finding aid is available; viewing is by appointment only.

江戸名所図会

Edo meisho zue

COMPLETE RUN  ·  ORIGINAL PRINTING

All seven books and twenty fascicles of Saitō Gesshin's topographical compendium of 1834–1836, in original printing with the Hasegawa Settan illustrations intact. Acquired piecemeal between 1923 and 1971.

04

Periodicals & Continuations

MEIJI  ·  TAISHŌ  ·  SHŌWA

Continuous runs of Taiyō (1895–1928), Bungakukai (1893–1898), and the Tōkyō nichi-nichi shimbun on microfilm and bound volume, supporting research in late-Edo and early-modern print culture.

§ 02  /  THE BUILDING

A kura, recast
in concrete.

The library occupies a free-standing block on the eastern edge of the Yanaka campus, completed in 1902 to plans by the Hokkaidō-trained engineer Yanagisawa Kichizō. The structure was conceived as a reinforced-concrete adaptation of a traditional kura storehouse — thick-walled, narrow-windowed, raised half a storey above grade for flood protection — and is among the earliest surviving examples of that hybrid in Tokyo.

The exterior plaster, refreshed in 1968 and again in 2004, retains the original namako-kabe lattice on the north and east elevations. Inside, the principal reading room runs the full length of the building's upper floor: forty paces north to south, with clerestory windows glazed in pale rolled glass and a pair of original lanterns, electrified in 1924, suspended from the central beam.

Below grade, three vault rooms hold the closed stacks at a controlled 18 °C and 52% relative humidity. The Yamamura maps are kept here in shallow cedar drawers commissioned from the Suzuki workshop of Asakusa in 1959, and rotated annually for inspection.

Visitors enter from the courtyard side, passing beneath a small paulownia carving by the sculptor Hirano Tarō — a gift from the class of 1934, and the only piece of figurative ornament permitted on the building.
