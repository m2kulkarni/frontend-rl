Section II · Academics

Faculties
& Schools 学部 ・ 大學院

— Editor's preface Rikuō teaches six disciplines under one tile-roofed quadrangle in Yanaka. Each faculty is small by design — fewer than two hundred students — and convenes around a single dean, a single workshop, and a single library wing. The list below records each faculty's founding year, its present dean, and the question that animates its research.
六 学部 Six Faculties
I · Faculty

Faculty of Letters

文 学 部

The original faculty of the university. Studies in classical Japanese, kanbun composition, the philology of waka, and the editorial culture of Edo-period woodblock prints — from kibyōshi pamphlets to Hiroshige's late series.

Dean
Prof. Tachibana Sugako
Founded
1887 (Meiji 20)
Emphasis
Edo print culture & the kanbun tradition
Students
184 enrolled
II · Faculty

Faculty of Fine Crafts

工 芸 学 部

Three workshops housed in the old Kanda annex: urushi lacquerware under Master Inoue, the woodblock-cutting atelier of the late Shimizu line, and the indigo-dye vats kept warm by the dyeing collective since 1923.

Dean
Prof. Inoue Hanjirō
Founded
1903 (Meiji 36)
Emphasis
Urushi · woodblock · aizome indigo
Students
96 enrolled
III · Faculty

Faculty of Architecture & Carpentry

建 築 ・ 大 工 学 部

Drawing tables and timber benches stand side by side. Curriculum spans sukiya-zukuri composition, traditional joinery without metal fasteners, and the mathematics of the kiwari proportional system as set down in eighteenth-century treatises.

Dean
Prof. Sakamoto Kenji
Founded
1921 (Taishō 10)
Emphasis
Sukiya-zukuri & traditional joinery
Students
142 enrolled
IV · Faculty

Faculty of History

史 学 部

A focused programme in Tokugawa-era political and economic history. Special emphasis is placed on the rice-stipend system, the bakufu-han balance of power, and the merchant ledgers of late-Edo Osaka, of which Hakuro Library holds 3,200 volumes.

Dean
Prof. Watanabe Mitsuko
Founded
1894 (Meiji 27)
Emphasis
Tokugawa political & economic history
Students
118 enrolled
V · Faculty

Faculty of Philosophy

哲 学 部

Reading-room culture rather than lecture culture. Confucian classics are read alongside Tendai and Zen Buddhist commentaries; the Faculty maintains a continuing seminar on the Hayashi school and on the writings of Itō Jinsai and Ogyū Sorai.

Dean
Prof. Ōmori Tetsurō
Founded
1899 (Meiji 32)
Emphasis
Confucian & Buddhist traditions
Students
74 enrolled
VI · Graduate School

Graduate School of Conservation

保 存 修 復 大 學 院

Two-year and four-year programmes in the conservation of paper, lacquer, polychrome wood, and silk-mounted hanging scrolls. Students work directly with objects on loan from temple collections in Ueno, Asakusa, and Nikkō.

Dean
Prof. Fujita Naoko
Founded
1968 (Shōwa 43)
Emphasis
Paper, lacquer & scroll conservation
Students
38 enrolled

A single course of study, six gates of entry 六 門 一 學

Every undergraduate at Rikuō, regardless of faculty, completes four common modules in the first year: classical reading, brushwork, the history of Edo Tokyo, and material observation. The intention is plain — that a future conservator should be able to read a carpenter's drawing, and a future historian should know the weight of an indigo-dyed thread.

Cross-faculty seminars convene each Tuesday afternoon in the Hakuro reading hall. Attendance is unfailing.

— The Office of the Provost, Yanaka, 2024
