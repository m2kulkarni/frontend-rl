Visiting the Yanaka Campus 谷中校舎案内 — A Guide for Guests

Founded grounds, retained. Four Meiji-era wooden lecture halls — Kō, Otsu, Hei, and Tei — stand as they were raised in 1889, set among gravel courts, a working indigo-dye garden, and the original Kiri Gate from which all public tours depart.
EST. 1889

The Four
Meiji Halls

Raised in 1889 by master carpenter Andō Kazuhei of Yanaka, the four wooden lecture halls — named in the old enumerative order Kō, Otsu, Hei, Tei — remain in continuous teaching use. Their sugi-bark roofs are recoursed every fourteen years; the next recoursing is scheduled for spring 2027.

甲

Kō Hall  — Letters

The eldest of the four. Houses the philology and classical Japanese seminars. North-facing tatami floor, original ranma transom carvings.

乙

Otsu Hall  — Crafts

Studios for woodblock cutting, paste-resist dyeing, and bookbinding. Adjoins the indigo garden through a covered roka.

丙

Hei Hall  — History

Lecture hall and seminar suite for the Faculty of History. Original 1889 blackboard, reglazed annually with sumi-ink.

丁

Tei Hall  — Music

Acoustically retuned in 1972 by Kawabata Iwao. Used for shōmyō recitation, gagaku rehearsal, and chamber recitals.

From Nippori Station

Exit through the West Exit of Nippori Station. Cross under the Sansaki-bashi bridge and ascend the Yūyake-dandan stone steps. At the top, turn left onto Sansaki-zaka and continue 180 metres until you reach a low cypress hedge.

The Kiri Gate stands at the far end, marked by a single dark-tiled gatehouse. The whole walk takes approximately five minutes at an unhurried pace.

Note — the lane through Yanaka Ginza is narrowest on weekend afternoons; weekday visits are quieter and recommended for guests using mobility aids.

藍The Indigo-Dye Garden

Behind Otsu Hall, a working aizome garden has been continuously cultivated by students of the Faculty of Fine Crafts since 1903. The garden raises Japanese indigo (tade-ai) from seed each April, with the leaves harvested between July and September and composted into sukumo through the autumn months.

Visitors on the Thursday tour may observe — but not handle — the fermentation vats. The garden is closed to the public during the harvest itself (typically the third week of August), when the entire faculty assembles for cutting and stacking.

Finished cloth from the previous year's vats is sold at the annual Kōbō-kai craft fair held on the first Saturday of November.
