Of the Two Aqueducts

The Two Great Lines of Drinking-Water

The City of Edo, set upon reclaimed marsh and brackish bay, draws its sweet water by two great conduits brought down from the western hills at the cost of much earth and timber. The elder is the Kanda Aqueduct (神田上水), sourced at Inokashira Pond in the Musashi uplands and conveyed by open ditch and covered wooden pipe through Sekiguchi, Koishikawa, and Yushima before issuing into the wells of Nihonbashi, Kanda, and the eastern Shitamachi.

The younger and longer is the Tamagawa Aqueduct (玉川上水), opened in Shōhō 4 by the brothers Shōuemon and Seiuemon of Hino, drawing from the Tama River at Hamura and running thirteen ri to Yotsuya Ōkido, whence wooden mains serve the western wards: Kōjimachi, Akasaka, Aoyama, Azabu, Shiba, and the lodgings of the daimyō.

Maintenance of conduit-stones, wooden joints, and intake-screens is reckoned a permanent charge upon the wards served. Each nanushi shall furnish able men in number set forth in the table below, on the days appointed by this Bureau, or pay commutation in mon at the standing rate of forty-eight per man per day.

Kanda Jōsui

神田上水 · Drinking-Water Line

The senior conduit, opened under Tokugawa Ieyasu in the closing years of the Tenshō era. Wooden mains of cypress and pine are inspected each spring at the joints in Sekiguchi.

Tamagawa Jōsui

玉川上水 · Western Aqueduct

Cut by the Tamagawa brothers of Hino in Shōhō 4 at the Shōgun's command. Beginning at Hamura intake, it falls a single shaku for every chō, by the careful sighting of the level-cord.

Onagigawa

小名木川 · Cargo Canal, East

The salt-canal cut in Tenshō 18 from the lower Sumida to Nakagawa, conveying the salt of Gyōtoku and the rice of Shimōsa to the granaries of Fukagawa. Dredged by the wards of Honjo and Fukagawa.

Where the silt rises a sun above the stake, the ward shall send men; where it rises a shaku, the ward shall send all men.
