Office,
Hours &
Petitions.

Practical instruction for ward elders (nanushi) and townspeople presenting written petitions to the Bureau. Read with care; the runner will not wait, and the gate-keeper at Tatsunokuchi will not admit any party without seal-paper in proper order.

Posted by order of the Bureau · For the Eight-Hundred-Eight Machi
I.

The Bureau Office

Paulownia seal of the Bureau — affixed only to documents bearing the Commissioner's mark.

Where to Present Yourself

The Bureau is housed within the outer wall at Tatsunokuchi, immediately east of the Wadakura gate, in the third building of the long row that fronts the moat. Enter by the small side-door marked with the paulownia seal; the main gate is reserved for officers of higher rank.

Bring your ward elder's written introduction. Arrive on foot. Palanquins must be set down at the corner stone before the bridge — the inner court has no room for them.

The waiting hall holds nine mats. When it is full, the gate-keeper will write your number on a slip of bamboo paper; return when called.

III.

Filing a Written Petition (Gansho)

Order of
Procedure
No petition is received except through the hand of one's own ward elder. Direct petition by townspeople is not admitted, save in cases of fire, flood, or grave injury.

  1. Speak first with your ward elder.

    Present your case to the nanushi of your machi. He will judge whether the matter falls within the Bureau's jurisdiction — wards, conduits, fire-watch posts, or the boundaries thereof. Matters of trade or marriage are not heard here.

  2. Have the gansho written upon proper seal-paper.

    The petition must be set down on stamped hōsho-gami bearing the official chop, in clean Mincho hand, with the date in the current era and the names and seals of two witnesses from your ward.

  3. Affix the ward elder's seal beside your own.

    The elder's vermilion seal stands at the upper right; the petitioner's seal at the lower left. Without both, the gate-keeper will refuse the document and return it folded thrice.

  4. Send the gansho by an authorized runner.

    The petition is carried to the Bureau by a registered ward-runner. The petitioner does not deliver it in person. A receipt of acknowledgement, marked with the paulownia, is returned by the runner the same day if filed before the Hour of the Sheep.

  5. Await summons.

    The Bureau replies within fifteen days for ordinary matters, within three days for matters touching fire-watch or the breach of a conduit. Summons is delivered to the ward elder, never directly to the petitioner.

IV.

Seal-Paper & Customary Stipend

— Hōsho-gami —

Seal-Paper

Petitions must be written on stamped hōsho-gami obtained from the licensed paper-houses at Asakusa-bashi or at the rear of the Nihonbashi fish-market. Three sheets are issued together, bound with a paper twist, and bear the year's official chop in indigo.

Sheets stamped in a previous year are not accepted. Folded sheets, sheets repaired with paste, or sheets with brush-blots beyond the inner ruling are returned without comment.

二十四文 per bound set of three sheets
— Hashiri-chin —

Stipend for the Runner

The customary stipend (hashiri-chin) for the registered ward-runner who carries the gansho to Tatsunokuchi and returns the receipt is fixed by long usage. It is paid in copper, in a folded paper twist, at the moment the runner takes the document.

A second twist of like amount is given upon return of the sealed receipt. Runners do not solicit further payment; offering more is considered improper and may be reported.

三十二文 per leg, paid in copper mon
