Commissions
& Works 仕事の記 ― しごとのき

Four works, drawn from twenty-three years of joinery, paper, and lacquer at the atelier on Aburanokōji-dōri. Each commission begins with a long conversation in the timber yard and ends, sometimes years later, with the quiet click of a wooden peg. We offer them here as records, not as advertisement.
作 品 譜
第 一 ・ I

住吉屋揚屋 Sumiya Ageya · Restoration of Lattice & Fittings

A two-year restoration of the surviving second-floor lattice screens, transom carvings, and tea-room joinery of the Sumiya ageya in Shimabara — the last extant Edo-period banquet house in Kyōto, designated an Important Cultural Property. Original eighteenth-century joints were reproduced in matched hinoki without recourse to glue or nail; the cinnabar lacquer of the Matsu-no-Ma transom was renewed by hand under the supervision of the family.

Year
1998 – 2000 (commenced spring, completed autumn)
Site
Nishi-Shinyashiki Ageya-chō, Shimoyō-ku, Kyōto
Materials
Kiso hinoki, aged sugi, urushi (kuro / shu), iron mokkō nails for hidden fixings
Lead artisan
Kōrin Tatsuhiko, second-generation master joiner
Scope
Eleven kōshi screens; Matsu-no-Ma ranma; tea-room ji-ita and tokobashira
第 二 ・ II

襖框・上京邸 Fusuma Frames for a Private Residence in Kamigyō

A set of fourteen fusuma frames commissioned for the principal rooms of a machiya residence near Demachiyanagi. Frames were jointed in straight-grained sugi from the family's own holdings in Kitayama, dressed only with raw tung oil. The kōzo paper was made within the atelier and brushed with a single field of gofun white; the proprietor requested no painted ornament, only the slow texture of the mulberry fibre against winter light.

Year
2007
Site
Private residence, Kamigyō-ku, Kyōto
Materials
Kitayama-sugi, hand-couched kōzo paper, hand-forged hikite, tung oil
Lead artisan
Kōrin Tatsuhiko (joinery) · Hatano Sumie (paper)
Quantity
14 fusuma · 4 maira-do interior screens
第 三 ・ III

祇園会・提灯七十張 Seventy Paper Lanterns for the Gion Matsuri

At the request of the Naginata-boko preservation society, a commission of seventy chōchin lanterns to replace those lost in storage damage of the previous year. Bamboo armatures were split and bent green; kōzo paper from Kurotani was washed in persimmon tannin to deepen the warm tone of evening; the iconography of the boko was inked by hand in sumi by an apprentice over the course of forty-three days. The full set hung along Shijō-dōri for the yoiyama nights of July 2014.

Year
2014 (delivered 12 July, in time for yoiyama)
Site
Naginata-boko, Shijō-Karasuma, Kyōto
Materials
Madake bamboo, Kurotani kōzo, persimmon-tannin wash, sumi ink
Lead artisan
Hatano Sumie (paper-making) · Mori Kentarō (bamboo)
Quantity
70 lanterns · 3 standard sizes (尺一・尺三・尺五)
第 四 ・ IV

大原・茶室再生 Tea-Room Renewal at Ōhara

A four-and-a-half mat tea-room in the hills above Ōhara, rebuilt around its surviving tokobashira of weathered akamatsu. The brief was contemplative rather than restorative: to keep what had aged honourably, replace what had failed quietly, and admit no flourish that the tea masters of the eighteenth century would not have recognised. The nijiriguchi was re-hung in a single board of two-hundred-year sugi salvaged from a demolished sake brewery in Fushimi.

Year
2021 (March – November)
Site
Private chashitsu, Ōhara Raikō-in district, Sakyō-ku, Kyōto
Materials
Reclaimed Fushimi sugi, akamatsu (existing), Yoshino-hinoki ceiling, urushi finish on tokobashira base
Lead artisan
Kōrin Naoko, third generation · with master plasterer Yamashita Tetsu
Scope
Full re-roof in kokerabuki shingle; tatami renewed; ro-bata cut anew
御依頼
Inquiries · Go-irai
The atelier accepts a small number of commissions each year, generally restoration of machiya elements, the making of fusuma and chōchin in series, and the rebuilding of tea-rooms. We meet first in the workshop, walk the timber yard, and only later discuss measure and cost. Letters reach us most reliably by post; our address is kept on the visit page.
