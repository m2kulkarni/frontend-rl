弟子入り — A seven-year covenant

七年の修業The Apprenticeship

Kōrin Mokkō accepts, when occasion warrants, a single apprentice into a seven-year course of joinery, paper, and finish. The discipline follows the rhythms of the Kyōto seasons — beginning with the broom in the genkan and, in the latter years, drawing toward an independent commission entrusted to the apprentice's own hand. The intake is not annual; the next is anticipated for the spring of 2027.

四つの段階The Four Phases

The course is divided into four uneven stages, each presupposing the last. No phase may be hurried; on rare occasions, a phase has been repeated. Tools, like the apprentice, are sharpened only by patience. What follows is the unalterable shape of the seven years.

Year One

Sweeping & Tool-Care

掃除と道具

The first year is given to the broom, the whetstone, and the lacquered wiping cloth. The apprentice rises before the master, opens the shutters of the workshop on Yanaginobaba-dōri, sweeps the genkan, fills the water-pail, and learns the names of forty-three planes and seventy-one chisels. No cutting is permitted of work-stock; only of waste cedar.

Hours: 06:00–18:00, six days. One day at Hōnen-in.

Years Two — Three

Planing & Chiseling

鉋と鑿

Now the kanna is taken up. The apprentice draws shavings — at first thick and torn, then, by the autumn of the second year, thin enough to read newsprint through. Chisel work begins on offcuts of hinoki and continues through the simpler housings. By the close of the third year, the apprentice grinds and re-handles their own tools without supervision.

Examination: a single katsura board planed to 0.04 mm shavings.

Years Four — Five

Joinery Proper

指物の本道

The apprentice is set to the binding of frames — first the simple kumiko grids of asa-no-ha, then sangi-kuzushi, and at length the compound goma-gara that demands no glue and no nail. Mortise-and-tenon, shihō-tsugi, and the blind dovetail are taught in turn. Restoration work on machiya fittings begins in the latter half of year five, under the master's eye.

Materials: hinoki, sugi, kuri; later kuwa and tagayasan.

Years Six — Seven

Independent Commission

独立の依頼

In the sixth year the apprentice is entrusted with a small commission of their own — a transom, a tea-room ranma, or a set of fusuma frames — drawn, costed, and built without intervention save in matters of safety. The seventh year permits a larger work and the journey to the lacquerers in Wajima or the paper-makers in Kurotani. At its close, the master may grant the apprentice's own seal.

Closing: presentation at the spring opening, fifth-of-fifth.

条件Terms of the Course

  • Lodging Room and board provided in the upper rear of the atelier — a six-mat room overlooking the inner garden, shared facilities, breakfast and supper at the master's table. The apprentice is expected to keep the room and to rise without summons.
  • Stipend No stipend is paid in years one and two. From the first month of year three, a modest monthly sum of ¥45,000 is paid, rising to ¥85,000 in year five and ¥140,000 in years six and seven. Tools are issued from the studio's reserve and become the apprentice's own at completion.
  • Holidays Three days at New Year, the week of Obon, and one day each fortnight. The full hottest week of August, when glue will not set, is given to rest or to the rice harvest if the family requires.
  • Departure An apprentice may withdraw at any time without obligation; the master will write a letter of introduction to another house if asked. Of nine apprentices accepted in the studio's history, six have completed the seven years.

志願の条件Criteria for Application

  1. Age between eighteen and twenty-eight at the date of intake. Earlier or later applications are considered only by personal introduction.
  2. Sufficient Japanese to follow daily instruction. Reading proficiency is not required at entry; it will be acquired in the course of the work.
  3. Sound health and undamaged hands. A medical certificate dated within ninety days is requested upon shortlisting.
  4. A letter, written by hand, describing one object the applicant has made and one object they have repaired. Photographs are welcomed but not required.
  5. Two letters of introduction — one from a teacher or employer, one from a person not related by family — sealed and unopened.
  6. Willingness to remain in residence for the seven years, with the brief exceptions noted above. The course cannot be undertaken in part.

The current apprentice — accepted spring 2019

Mori Yūta

of Sakai, Ōsaka — formerly of the Tominaga blade-house

Mori-san entered the atelier in the third week of March, 2019, having walked the Yamato-kaidō from Sakai with a single furoshiki. He completed the planing examination in the autumn of 2021 and is presently engaged on the latter half of his independent commission — a kumiko ranma for a teahouse in Kamigyō. He will conclude the course in the spring of 2026.

The next intake is anticipated for spring 2027. Letters of inquiry, written by hand, may be addressed to the master between the first day of the ninth month and the last day of the eleventh month, 2026.
