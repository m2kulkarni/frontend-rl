Campus & Library

Forty-seven granite acres in Tanjavur, riverside courtyards in Srirangam, and a pillared mandapam at Madurai — together holding 312,000 volumes and 4,800 palm-leaf manuscripts in trust for the next thousand years.

The Three Sthalas

Campuses of the Institute

Tanjavur

Main Campus · Est. 1948

The principal seat of the institute occupies 47 acres on East Main Street, a stone's stride from the Brihadeeswara temple. The granite-built Convocation Mandapam seats 1,200 beneath a 64-pillar hall modelled on Cholan vimana proportion.

House the Schools of Sthapatya-Veda, Agama, and Bharatanatyam, with workshops in stone, bronze, and wood arrayed along the western enclosure.

Madurai

Performing Arts Centre · 1971

Set near the Vaigai, the Madurai campus houses the School of Naṭya & Saṅgītam, with a 480-seat thousand-pillar style theatre, four music gurukula halls, and dedicated chambers for the Karnatic vocal, vīṇā, and mṛdaṅgam streams.

The campus collaborates with the Meenakshi Temple's office of arts in seasonal festival training each Chittirai.

Srirangam

Āgama Field Station · 1984

The riverside Srirangam campus sits on 11 acres alongside the Kāveri, devoted to Vaikhānasa and Pāñcarātra Āgama studies and to the textual study of temple ritual and pratiṣṭha procedure.

Faculty here maintain field-recordings of utsava rites at the Ranganātha temple, partnering with priestly lineages on a continuous archival programme.

The Saraswati Library

Saraswatī Granthālaya

Central Library · Tanjavur Campus

Founded with the institute in 1948 and housed since 1962 in a granite-faced two-storey building at the campus's eastern enclosure, the Saraswati Library holds 312,000 volumes across the disciplines of Sanskrit, Tamil classics, agamic studies, iconography, and architectural history.

Of these, 4,800 palm-leaf manuscripts form the spine of the collection, catalogued under the Sambandar Bequest of 1973 — donated by the heirs of the Madurai-Sambandar mathāḍhipati estate and indexed by Dr. K. R. Venkataraman across nine published volumes between 1976 and 1989.

Archive Access Policy

  • General Reader Photo identification at the gate; printed catalogue and 1947–present periodicals available without prior booking.
  • Sambandar Bequest Manuscripts Application by post or e-mail at least 14 days in advance, with letter from a recognised institution and statement of research purpose.
  • Visiting Scholars Renewable 90-day passes issued by the Office of the Librarian; ₹400 deposit, refundable on return.
  • Photography Permitted in the open stacks; manuscripts must be imaged only by the in-house digitisation desk (₹60 per folio).
श्री

Conservation & Digitisation Laboratory

Established in 2011 with the technical assistance of UNESCO under the Memory of the World programme, the conservation lab occupies the lower west wing of the library. It maintains climate-stabilised vaults at 20°C / 50% RH, a digitisation desk capable of 600 dpi colour-calibrated capture, and an ongoing programme to image the entire palm-leaf collection by 2031. To date, 2,140 manuscripts have been fully imaged and are accessible to credentialed scholars through the on-site terminals.
