Two Brushes, One Highway

Between 1835 and 1838, two of the great print designers of the late Edo period turned their attention inland — away from the coastal Tōkaidō that Hiroshige had already made famous, toward the mountainous Kisokaidō, the inner highway through the spine of central Honshū. The publisher Takenouchi Magohachi (Hōeidō) began the series; Iseya Rihei finished it. Of the seventy designs that compose the set — sixty-nine post stations and the starting point at Nihonbashi — Keisai Eisen produced twenty-four, and Utagawa Hiroshige forty-six.

Eisen's earlier plates carry the heavier line, the close-pressed crowds, the bold reds of the Bunsei generation. When Hiroshige takes up the brush at Mitake, the weather changes. Mist enters the page. The figures shrink against the slope of the Kiso valley, and the cedar forests begin to read as weather rather than scenery.

Mountain Weather

The Kiso road did not flatter the traveller. Where the Tōkaidō promised teahouses and inns, the Kisokaidō threatened landslip, river crossing, and the long climb over the Torii pass. Hiroshige seems to have welcomed this. His sheets at Suhara, Nojiri, and Tsumago are studies in the way snow softens architecture and the way rain flattens distance into a single grey wash.

Visitors should look, in particular, at the registration of the bokashi gradations on the early Hōeidō impressions on display in the Higashi-no-ma. The plate of Oi — moonlight on a sleeping post-town — is shown beside a later state from the Kinjudō reissue, so that the slow erosion of the cherry-block printing surface can be read directly off the paper.

Gallery Floor Plan

The exhibition occupies two adjoining halls on the second floor of the museum. The route is one-way, beginning at Nihonbashi in the Higashi-no-ma and concluding at Kusatsu in the Naka-no-ma; benches and the reading alcove are placed mid-route at the Tsumago wall.

  1. Higashi-no-ma — Plates 1–24 Eisen's contributions, from Nihonbashi to Honjō.
  2. Reading Alcove Hiroshige's travel diary in facsimile and the 1837 Iseya woodblocks.
  3. Tsumago Wall Six impressions of the Kiso valley sheets, comparative states.
  4. Naka-no-ma — Plates 47–69 Hiroshige's late Kisokaidō, ending at Kusatsu.
  5. Print Study Room Reserve viewing by appointment, Tuesdays and Fridays.
