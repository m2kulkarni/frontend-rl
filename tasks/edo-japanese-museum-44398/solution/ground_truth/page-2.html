The Four
Standing
Collections·

Four bodies of material, accreted over four decades from the houses of Yanaka, Nezu, and Sendagi, and from the Mitsui archive at Surugachō. Together they describe the surface of Edo: its roads, its rooms, its ledgers, its cloth.

Collection I · Prints

Ukiyo-e of the Tōkaidō Road

Hiroshige, Hokusai, and the road between two capitals

A working library of woodblock prints depicting the fifty-three stations between Nihonbashi and Sanjō Ōhashi, anchored by a complete Hoeidō-edition Hiroshige issued in 1834 — fifty-five sheets in untrimmed ōban tate-e, acquired in 1981 from the estate of Ishikawa Naotarō.

Collection II · Architecture

Machiya Fittings

Shōji, fusuma, and kōshi grilles from the merchant quarters

Salvaged carpentry from townhouses razed between 1962 and 2009 in Nishijin (Kyoto) and the slope-streets of Yanaka. Includes a complete bay of misé-gōshi shop-front lattice from a former kimono-jobber on Nezu-dōri, and four pairs of fusuma with under-paintings attributed to the studio of Maruyama Ōkyo.

Provenance

Collection III · Documents

Edo Ledgers & Receipts

The accounting hand of a great merchant house

A continuous run of daifukuchō (great-fortune ledgers), tally slips, and receipts donated in 1986 by the Mitsui house from their Surugachō counting-rooms. The earliest folio records cotton sales for the third month of Hōreki 11 (1761); the latest, an oil-credit dispute settled in Ansei 5 (1858).

Collection IV · Textile

Textile Wagara

Katazome stencils & shibori bolts of the late Edo years

Ninety bolts of indigo-dyed cotton and bast fibre, recording the stencil and tied-resist patterns worn by townspeople and farmers. The collection emphasises pattern-vocabulary — seigaiha, asanoha, shippō, kikkō — rather than ceremonial silks, and so reads as a quiet companion to the prints upstairs.

A note on
provenance

Every object on this page is held under a publication-and-research licence with the donating family or temple. Where prints retain the original publisher's seal — Hoeidō, Tsutaya, Marusei — the seal is reproduced beside the catalogue entry. Reproductions for academic publication are arranged through the curator's office; a fee of ¥4,200 per image applies for commercial use.
