Enkaku — A Brief Account

From a private
cabinet of prints
to a public
foundation.

Hakubutsukan Yanaka began in the back room of an antiquarian's house in Sendagi: two hundred and twenty ukiyo-e prints, forty-six lacquered objects, and a stack of carpenters' notebooks. Forty-seven years later, the same collection — now grown more than fivefold — anchors a public foundation devoted to the material culture of Edo.

I · The Founder
梅 ◇ 鶴
一

Ōkubo Ren'ichi and the First Two Hundred and Twenty

1908 — 1991

Ōkubo Ren'ichi was born in 1908 in the Negishi quarter of Shitaya, the second son of a paper merchant whose firm had supplied the Iwasaki estates since the late Bunkyū years. He read law at the Tokyo Imperial University, joined the Bank of Chōsen in 1932, and returned to Tokyo in 1946 to find his family's warehouse standing but its ledgers gone.

The collecting began the following winter, almost by accident. A neighbour brought him a bundle of forty-two Hiroshige prints rescued from a damp storeroom in Asakusa; he paid forty yen for the lot and spent the next three months drying, pressing, and cataloguing each sheet by hand. By 1953 he held over one hundred and ten impressions, all from the late Tokugawa landscape masters; by 1968 the count had reached two hundred and twenty, with the addition of forty-six lacquered domestic objects and an archive of carpenter's pattern-books.

He opened the collection to the public on the fourth Sunday of every month from the autumn of 1971, charging no admission but requiring an introduction from a previous visitor — a system he called kuchi-zoroe, "voices in agreement."

"I have not bought these things. I have only kept them company until they could be returned to the city that made them." — Ōkubo Ren'ichi, journal entry, 12 March 1978

On 4 October 1978, with the assistance of the architect Maekawa Kunio (then aged seventy-three) and the print scholar Narazaki Muneshige, Ōkubo formally constituted the Yanaka Townscape Cultural Trust — Yanaka Machinami Bunka Shintaku — and transferred the entire collection, together with a working endowment of ¥48,000,000, into its keeping.

He continued to live above the trust's first reading room on Yomise-dōri until his death on 19 January 1991, at the age of eighty-two. His desk, his copy-stand, and the small cinnabar seal he used to sign loan receipts remain on permanent display in Gallery III.

II · Chronology

Forty-seven
years, in
brief.

Principal events in the institutional life of the museum, from the original trust deed of 1978 to the bequest reform of 2022.

  1. 1978

    The Trust is constituted

    The Yanaka Townscape Cultural Trust is registered on 4 October. Founding documents list 220 ukiyo-e impressions, 46 lacquered objects, and 312 carpenter's pattern-sheets. Initial endowment: ¥48,000,000.

  2. 1984

    First public catalogue

    Narazaki Muneshige publishes the foundational checklist, Ōkubo Bunko Mokuroku, with the University of Tokyo Press. Annual visitorship reaches 4,200.

  3. 1991

    Death of the founder

    Ōkubo Ren'ichi dies on 19 January. His personal papers — 1,140 letters and 38 notebooks — are added to the holdings under the seal of his executor, the lawyer Kasuga Tomiko.

  4. 1996

    Reorganisation as a public foundation

    Under the 1995 reform of the Civil Code, the trust is reorganised as a kōeki zaidan hōjin — a public-interest incorporated foundation — and renamed Hakubutsukan Yanaka. First admission charge introduced: ¥600.

  5. 2008

    Conservation accord

    A ten-year conservation partnership is signed with the Tokyo University of the Arts (Geidai), establishing the joint paper-conservation laboratory at Ueno and an annual rotation of two graduate fellows.

  6. 2014

    The Yanaka building opens

    After three years of construction to the design of Aoki Jun, the present building opens at 3-14-7 Yanaka on 11 May, incorporating a reconstructed 1843 machiya frame from Kanda. Holdings now exceed 1,180 prints and 240 objects.

  7. 2022

    Bequest programme reformed

    The board introduces three named bequest tiers and the Ōkubo Fellowship for early-career conservators. The conservation accord with Geidai is renewed for a further fifteen years.

Board of Trustees

Eleven seats, three-year terms.

The foundation is governed by a board of eleven trustees, nine of whom are elected from the membership and two appointed by the City of Taitō. Quarterly meetings are held in the Reading Room and are open to members in good standing.

Conservation Partners

Geidai paper laboratory, est. 2008.

Since the original accord of 8 April 2008, every sheet entering the collection passes through the joint paper-conservation laboratory at the Tokyo University of the Arts in Ueno. Two graduate fellows are seconded to the museum each year.

IV · The Bequest Programme

Give a hand-press, a print, or a quiet sum.

Since 2022, the museum has accepted bequests under three named tiers, each acknowledged by a small ceremony held on the second Sunday of November in the Reading Room. All bequests are reviewed by the conservation laboratory before accession; objects deemed outside the collecting scope are referred — with the donor's permission — to partner institutions.

Donors who wish to discuss a planned gift may write to the Registrar, Hagiwara Mieko, at the museum address; an initial conversation is offered without obligation, and confidentiality is observed in every case.
