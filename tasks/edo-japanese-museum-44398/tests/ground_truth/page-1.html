谷中 Museum of Edo Townscape

A neighborhood museum devoted to the daily fabric of Edo — its lanes, lanterns, ledgers, and woodblock pages — kept on a quiet slope above the temple cemeteries of Yanaka.

Tsuru-no-maru — seal of the house
impressed in cinnabar, 1978
Director's
Greeting Aisatsu — 御挨拶

The Edo we study at Hakubutsukan Yanaka is not the Edo of castles and feudal pageantry, but the Edo of the lower town — of vegetable sellers calling at dawn, of paper-makers folding sheets of washi by lamplight, of ledgers kept in the back of a tea-warehouse and stacked floor to ceiling against the threat of fire.

Since taking up the directorship in 2019, I have tried to keep our rooms small and our questions large. What did a lantern cost in 1843? Where did the pigment for Hiroshige's blue come from, and at what price? How did a townhouse of three bays survive five fires and an earthquake, and still smell of cypress on a damp morning?

This year we exhibit the complete Sixty-Nine Stations of the Kisokaidō alongside the reconstructed machiya in our north wing. I invite you to take the long view, and the slow walk.

黒田 達雄 Kuroda Tatsuo · Director, since 2019

Current Exhibition

Hiroshige's Stations

12 Sept — 30 Nov 1867

The complete Sixty-Nine Stations of the Kisokaidō, drawn from the Takahashi family deposit, shown together for the first time in seventeen years. Includes proof prints, woodblock fragments, and the sumi-trial for Karuizawa.

Permanent · North Wing

The Reconstructed Machiya

Open Daily · 10:00 – 16:30

A three-bay townhouse from Shitaya-Hatchōbori, dismantled in 1971 and rebuilt timber by timber within our walls. Step inside the doma, examine the soot-dark beams, and hear how a household of seven worked an eight-mat room.

An Evening at the Stations

14 November 1867 · 18:00

A recreated lantern-lit talk in the manner of an Edo kōshaku, on the route of Hiroshige's Kisokaidō from Itabashi to Kusatsu. Reading by Tachibana Sumire; introduction by Director Kuroda. ¥1,200, machiya hall, 40 seats only.
