The Lead Essay

Lead Essay · Architecture & Cosmos

The Oculus and the Sky:
What the Pantheon Was For

By Helena Vespasia Ross
2,400 Words · Reading Time XX Minutes

Hadrian's rotunda has been called a temple, a tomb, a planetarium, and a problem. Standing beneath the twenty-seven-foot opening, on a clear October noon, the historian Helena Vespasia Ross argues that we have been asking the wrong question for nineteen centuries. The Pantheon was never simply a building dedicated to the gods; it was an instrument for measuring the relationship between Roman power and the visible heavens — and its silence has more to teach us than its inscription.
