De Proposito

· CAPVT II ·

On the purpose of the foundation, the bequest from which it springs, the seven trustees who govern its hand, and the motto by which all its labour is measured.

· Liber Primus ·

Of the Bequest, the Estate, & the Charge

Livia Marcellina Aurelia, born at Frascati on the seventeenth of April, nineteen hundred and thirty-two, and reposed at her villa above Tivoli on the ninth of November, two thousand and eight, charged her executors with a single instruction: that the whole of her estate — the orchard at Tivoli, the apartment in Via Giulia, and the securities held by the Banca di Sondrio — pass undivided into a perpetual trust whose only purpose should be the conservation of those Roman antiquities which the Italian state, by reason of scarcity or remoteness, cannot itself prioritise.

The trust was sealed at the office of Notary Bertelli on the twenty-third of February, two thousand and nine, valued at the time at €18,420,000, and registered with the Ministry of Cultural Heritage under file P-2009/0114. By a covenant of the deed, the principal may never be diminished; only the yield, after fees and the maintenance of the Tivoli house (which serves now as fieldwork residence and small library), may be expended upon the work.

The Foundation undertakes three classes of labour, and no others: the recording and consolidation of Roman epigraphy, with priority to inscriptions exposed to weather or theft; the survey and stabilisation of hydraulic infrastructure — aqueducts, cisterns, nymphaea, and the lesser conduits whose names are lost; and the lifting, cleaning, and re-laying of civic mosaics in the floors of basilicas, baths, and provincial fora across the territories that once answered to Rome.

No grant is given for new construction, nor for reproductions, nor for any work whose purpose is the entertainment of visitors. Where a site lies within a province now governed by another state, the Foundation works only by formal invitation of that state's antiquities authority, and only where local craftsmen are engaged at the prevailing rate.

The trustees serve six-year terms, renewable once, and convene three times each year — in March at Tivoli, in July at a working site, and in November in Rome. A simple majority binds; on questions of the principal, unanimity is required. Minutes are kept in the archive at the Tivoli house and are available, by appointment, to any scholar with bona-fide cause.

The Foundation employs no permanent staff beyond a single archivist, Sig. Pietro Cavalcanti, who has held that post since two thousand and eleven. All field labour is contracted; all academic counsel is honorary. By this discipline the operating overhead is held at, or below, four-and-one-half per centum of annual disbursement — a figure verified each spring by Studio Marchesi & Pavone of Rome.

What the Foundation seeks, finally, is not the rescue of any one stone, but the patient maintenance of a continuous record — that the inscriptions, the conduits, and the patterned floors of the Roman world should outlast the noise of our own brief century, and pass intact into the keeping of the next.

· FINIS ·

Domus Aurelia The Tivoli house, raised in 1781 on Roman foundations and acquired by the Aurelia family in 1908, serves as office, archive, and residence for visiting fieldworkers. It is not open to the public, save for two days each May.
Of the Founder Livia Marcellina Aurelia studied Latin epigraphy at the Sapienza under Margherita Guarducci, served the Soprintendenza of Lazio for thirty-one years, and wrote in private a five-volume manuscript on the inscriptions of the Via Tiburtina, deposited in the archive at her death.
