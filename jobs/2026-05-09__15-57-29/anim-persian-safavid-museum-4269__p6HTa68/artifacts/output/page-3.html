From the Curator

Among the painters who made up Shah ʿAbbasi's circle, Aqā Riza, called Riza ʿAbbasi, stands as the figure who, more than any, fixed the face of late Safavid drawing. The single-page work — drawn in line, sometimes lightly tinted, often only signed and dated — is his characteristic device. He gave the line of the body, the line of the cheek, the gradation of the dervish's robe a brevity not before seen in Iranian painting.

This exhibition gathers forty-two of his single-page works, drawn in nearly equal part from the holdings of this museum, the Boston Museum of Fine Arts, and the Iraq Library at Baghdad. They are arranged in three movements — the early dervishes; the seated and standing figures; and the late line drawings of the master himself.

The exhibition is open through 31 March 2025. A printed catalogue, edited by Ardashir Mostofi, is available from the Office of Research, with a long essay on Riza's signed works by Hossein Tabbatabaie.

A Brief on Conservation

The most pressing concern in the Riza sheets is foxing — the small russet spots of fungal origin which can, under unfavourable humidity, multiply across a leaf within months. Each work was examined under transmitted light and stabilised with a mild bath of calcium hydroxide before re-mounting upon Tibet paper.

Three sheets required additional aqueous treatment, two were lightly retouched at the corners, and one was returned in its original state under conservatorial recommendation. The work of the atelier was performed by Madame Yasmine al-Ghorab and her four assistants over the winter of 2023–2024.
