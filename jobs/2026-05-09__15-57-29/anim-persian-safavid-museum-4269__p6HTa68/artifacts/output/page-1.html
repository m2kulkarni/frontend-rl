An Address from the Director

Founded in 1977 in the gardens of Chahar Bagh, this museum was conceived as a centre for the study of the Safavid age — that brief, brilliant interval when Isfahan, raised by Shah ʿAbbas I to be the seat of empire, was named by its poets nesf-e jahān, the half of the world. Within these walls are gathered eleventh from the Mosque-e Shah, illuminated manuscripts from the royal kitabkhana, silks of Kashan and Yazd, and the courtly portraiture of Riza ʿAbbasi.

We are, before all else, a place of slow looking. The objects ask for it; the eye repays it.

Riza ʿAbbasi & the
Single-Page Painting

Forty-two loose drawings from Boston and Iraq, gathered for the first time outside the album archives, dervishes, and the long calligraphic line that defined the late Safavid hand.

Hall I — through 31 March 2025
Recent Conservation

The Ardabil-Style
Carpet Fragment

After three months in the textile atelier, the medallion fragment, attributed to the Tabriz workshop c.1539, returns to view. Note the silk warp, refastened thread by thread on a re-spun wool ground.
