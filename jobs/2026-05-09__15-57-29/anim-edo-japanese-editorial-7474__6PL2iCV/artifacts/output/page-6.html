A NOTE

On the matter
of why we print.

There was a quarterly journal devoted to the material culture of the Edo period — the Asahi Janarū·Edo, edited by Tatsugorō Kuwata's circle of writers in postwar Tokyo, which closed at its forty-third issue in 1973. There has not, since 1973, been another. The Tōkaidō Review is, in modest words, an attempt to pick that thread up again.

Our remit is narrow. The artisans who pulled blockprints to Kobe in the spring, the bakeries that have not changed in two centuries, the bath-houses, the indigo tradesmen whose practice goes unbroken — anyone who, in some real way, is still working in a manner that would be recognisable to a 1740 traveller. We publish quarterly, four issues per year, on uncoated paper from the Echizen mill.

Founded in 2019 by Naoko Sato (until recently of Asahi Brutus) and Kenji Akiyama (formerly of the publishing house Iwanami Shoten), the journal still operates as a six-person concern: two editors, a designer, two contributing editors, and a fact-checker who reads every word out loud before press.
