SCRIPTA

A short collected register of essays, fieldnotes, and lectures, written by L. Vennius and the studio's bench. Posted irregularly, in the order they happen, with a date and a setting.

— INDEX SCRIPTORUM —
AUGUST · MMXXIV
14AUG

"Concrete Without Apology"

A defence of opus caementicium as a structural choice. The studio's recent civic monument is dressed in travertine but stands, as Vitruvius would have it, on a foundation of pozzolana, lime, and rubble — assembled in the same proportion the engineers of the early Empire used at the Theatre of Marcellus.

02AUG

"The Threshold is the Argument"

On the rhetoric of entry. The threshold mosaic at the Villa Bartoli — laid this past spring — is read here as a small civic argument about what a guest is owed, and what a guest is permitted to bring under the lintel.

JULY · MMXXIV
22JUL

"On the Coffer, as Structural Honesty"

Reading the Pantheon's coffered ceiling as a structural form rather than a decorative one. Notes from a lecture given to the Scuola di Specializzazione in Beni Architettonici, March 2024.

08JUL

"Travertine is a Slow Material"

Sourcing notes from the Bagni di Tivoli quarry, where the studio purchased the eight blocks for the Aquileia cenotaph. On the porous, layered structure of the stone and the patience required of a mason who would cut it well.

01JUL

"As Walls Is Rome"

The Aurelian Wall, read as eight successive courses of building intention and a marker of the Roman idea of municipium. A short walk along the section between Porta Latina and Porta Ardeatina.

JUNE · MMXXIV
19JUN

"The Pediment Returns"

A short defence of the symmetrical pediment in the practice of building modest civic facades — including the studio's recent Forum Mercatorium plan in Ravenna, where the centre bay carries a low triangular gable in carved Carrara marble.

05JUN

"Against the Frameless Window"

A small polemic against the absence of a frame. The frame is, the studio holds, a contract between the wall and the world it filters: an articulate, weight-bearing membrane and not a luxury.

MAY · MMXXIV
22MAY

"Vesta, and the Patient Hand"

On the slow construction in the studio's bench. The mosaic Quattuor Tempora took 142 days; the studio's count is 142 days of two craftsmen. We log them, beneath each tessera.

11MAY

"A Catalogue of Beds"

A five-day fieldwork report: the studio inventoried the bedding mortars of forty-two surviving threshold mosaics in the Lazio region. Notes, preliminary sketches, and a forthcoming entry in the studio's stone register.

APRIL · MMXXIV
26APR

"Notes from a Closed City"

A field-note from the Aquileia ferment, where the studio came to install the cenotaph in the city's main square. Notes on the foot traffic, the stone, and the thirteen days of foul weather that delayed the lifting.

11APR

"Why We Still Draw the Order"

A defence of teaching the orders to first-year students of architecture at the Politecnico di Milano: not as the surface dress of a building, but as a structural language — the syllables in which a wall and a foot and a column eloquently speak.

MARCH · MMXXIV
28MAR

"Vitruvius at the Hardware Store"

A short note, written between the studio and the supplier of mineral pigments, on the small heroism of standing in the fluorescent aisle of a hardware store and saying "no, I want the natural one." A treatise of a kind on procurement.
