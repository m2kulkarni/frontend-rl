Hokurin's research culture is shaped by long horizons. We measure pigment migration in centimetres per century, listen to looms that have not changed since the Genroku era, and read books printed before electricity. Our smallness is deliberate.

The Tōrin Bunko was assembled across seven generations by the Tōrin family, Higashiyama merchants in tea, lacquer, and indigo from the Bunsei era onward. In October 1991, after the death of the eighth-generation head Tōrin Sōichi, the family conveyed the entire library — 14,210 volumes, 312 manuscript bundles, and 47 woodblocks — to Hokurin under a deed that specifies only one condition: the collection remain undivided and consultable in Kyoto.

The collection's strengths are Edo-period printed books on agriculture and dyestuffs (1693–1860), a deep run of merchant correspondence detailing the indigo trade between Awa and Kyoto, a 2018 audit identified twelve items not previously known to bibliographers, including a 1714 printing of the Kōeki kokusan-kō with marginalia in two hands.

研究 / RECENT PROJECTS
PIGMENT STABILITY

Beni Project

An eight-year longitudinal study of safflower-derived beni-zumi pigments under controlled humidity, in collaboration with the Kyoto National Museum.

RIVER ECOLOGY

Kamogawa Atlas

Five years of riverbed mapping across the Kamogawa from Kibune to Fushimi, paired with Edo-period flood records to model future watershed shifts.

PAPER STUDIES

Tōhoku Washi Survey

An ongoing record of fifty-six washi villages along the Tōhoku coast — fibre, sizing, beating times, and what each kiln chooses to keep.

Duplication Request Procedure

  • 01Submit a written request at the front desk indicating shelf-mark, folio range, and intended use.
  • 02Curatorial review within five working days; some fukuro-toji volumes are not photographed under any circumstance.
  • 03Capture is performed in-house on a 100MP camera mounted to a fixed cradle; flash and direct light are not used.
  • 04Image delivery by physical drive within four weeks for institutional requests, eight weeks for individuals.
  • 05Citation in any resulting publication includes the full shelf-mark and the form: Tōrin Bunko, Hokurin Daigaku.
客員 / VISITING SCHOLARS

Visiting Scholars

Each year the university convenes three visiting scholars to occupy the upper rooms of the East Annex for a residency of one to three terms. Applications open in May for the following academic year; selection is by the Provost in consultation with the relevant faculty chair.
