OF THE STONES

The building at 14 Rue des Lombards was raised in 1396 as the western annexe to the chapter house of Saint-Merri. After the Revolution it became a fish-monger's cellar; in the 19th century, a wine bar; in the 1920s, a pension; and after the second war, half-shuttered. My father Marcel, masonry-trained at Reims under his uncle, took the lease in 1982 and re-vaulted the cellar by hand, stone by stone, after the manner of the Romanesque churches he had served as apprentice.

After the augmentation of 1986 the stones poured through the foundation by a slow speed: a window, a lintel, a counter, a vaulted nesting of wine. Two of the kitchen's flagstones bear masons' marks from Saint-Merri itself. The trade goes by the building thus: stone takes care of bread, and bread of stone.

OF THE KITCHEN

Hélène opened the doors of the Maison de la Verrière on the eve of Saint-Martin, 1982, with a kitchen of three pots and a hand-bound copy of the Viandier de Taillevent which she had copied at the Bibliothèque de l'Arsenal. The cookery is drawn, line for line, from those sources: the chestnut soup of the workshops of Reims for the year of 1392; the partridge with quinces from Taillevent; and the tarts of verjus and crab-apple from a Loire register of 1409. We add nothing they would not know.

Nothing arrives on the plate that did not arrive in 1392 — except the bread, which is from a baker in the Marais. We are sorry. We are still asking.

THE DINING ROOM

One enters down the central archway from the rue de l'aux, on the strait of stone scuppered like that of the church of Saint-Julien-le-Pauvre. Hêtre, oak and a long single board of hooped ash — pegged, never glued — and a single lantern in the lock-bay where the keeper sat.

The cellar, cut as a herring of stone from the chalk-band shelves of Saint-Méen, was set up with two of the great drowsy beech-hours in 1986 above the long-cellar's vault. As of the dispatch of the Pasteur of XV February 1986, the wine of seven shires was tied with the second-flight binding for the priest's mark below the embroider.
