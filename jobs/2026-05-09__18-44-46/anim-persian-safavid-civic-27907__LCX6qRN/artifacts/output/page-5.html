Office of the Diwánbegi · Folio V

Petitions & Hearings

Concerning the proper submission of the arzeh-dasht — the written petition — and the conduct of public hearings before His Excellency the Diwánbegi, that justice may be sought without obstruction by any subject of the realm.

Be it known to all subjects

That petitions are received in the outer courtyard of the Diván each morning from after the dawn prayer until the second hour past noon, save upon the day of Friday and the festival of Nowruz. Petitions of greatest moment may be entered upon a Wednesday afternoon, from the third hour past noon until the call of sunset.

No petition shall be heard which lacks the seal of two witnesses, nor which is presented by an intermediary not entitled within its body.

Reception

Where to Present It

Bring the sealed petition to the outer courtyard of the Diván, between the Qaysarieh Gate and the Mosque of Sheikh Lotfollah, where the clerk of the registry sits beneath the northern arcade.

Each petition is numbered, sealed in turn, and entered to the great folio under the date of the third safar, Mírzá Mohammed Tāqī.

Hearing

Appearance Before the
Diwánbegi

Upon the Wednesday following acceptance, the petitioner shall present himself at the inner audience hall with both witnesses in attendance.

The Diwánbegi hears no more than twelve causes in a single afternoon; should yours not be reached this Wednesday, your matter is referred to the Wednesday next without re-submission.

The Order of Proceedings

  1. Composition. The petition is written in clear hand upon a single folio of paper, in Persian or, if the petitioner be Armenian, taken to dictation by a public scribe of the Maydán for a fee of one shahi.
  2. Witness & Seal. Two men of good repute, neither kin to the petitioner nor party to the cause, affix their seals beneath the text; their quarters and trades are entered alongside.
  3. Submission. The petition is presented to the outer courtyard, where it receives the registry stamp and a copy is retained in the great folio.
  4. Hearing. Upon the appointed Wednesday, the petitioner appears with witnesses; the Diwánbegi hears the cause, questions the parties, and pronounces his ruling.
  5. Decree. The ruling is set down in the register, sealed by the hand of the Diwánbegi, and a copy delivered to the petitioner without further charge.

Special Notice

Concerning New Jolfa

Petitions arising among the Christian community of New Jolfa — whether of trade, inheritance, marriage, or apostate — are first heard by their own kalāntar in the manner long established by His Majesty's gracious decree.

Only matters that cannot be so settled, or which touch upon a subject outside that community, shall pass thereafter to this Diván, bearing the seal of the kalāntar in attestation.
