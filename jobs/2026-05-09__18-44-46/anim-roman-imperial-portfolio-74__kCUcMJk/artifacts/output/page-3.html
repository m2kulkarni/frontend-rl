OPUS
TESSELLATUM

The art and craft of cutting, sorting, and laying tesserae — the patient grammar of stone. We work in opus tessellatum, opus vermiculatum, and opus signinum, in marble, smalti, and pebble.

THE SOURCING OF TESSERAE

I

MARBLE & LIMESTONE

Our principal stones are quarried in the Brescian Botticino, the Bergamasque Aurisina, the Verona red, and the cipollino verde of southern Tuscany. We use neither resin nor adhesive in laying.

II

SMALTI & GLASS

Coloured glass smalti are sourced from the Orsoni furnace in Cannaregio, founded in 1888. The atelier maintains a custodied palette of three hundred and twenty colours in the studio.

III

PEBBLE & SHELL

For opus signinum and the older Greek pebble work we collect from the Liguria coast and the Velino river bed; the latter yielding round forms in white, dove-grey, and slate-blue.

CONSERVATION METHOD

VII

Each restoration follows seven prescribed steps in order: dry-survey, photogrammetric record, lifting in sections by paper-faced consolidation, cataloguing of every individual tessera, repair of substrate, re-laying with a lime-pozzolana mortar of historic proportion, and final stitching of new and ancient tesserae where lacuna require it. Lacunae are honestly visible.

THREE CASE STUDIES

QUATTUOR TEMPORA

OPUS · TESSELLATUMSTONE · MARBLE & SMALTIRESTORATION · 2022

A III-century pavement of the four seasons, set on the threshold of a Roman domus near the Tiber. The atelier lifted two thirds of the surviving field, identified a missing autumn quadrant, and re-laid the work with a 4 mm lime-pozzolana bedding.

VILLA QUINTILIA · THRESHOLD

OPUS · VERMICULATUMSTONE · LIMESTONERESTORATION · 2021

A geometric guilloché threshold of red and ochre limestone, dated to the late II century, restored in situ. The damaged eastern field was reconstituted from photogrammetric record taken before the 1973 excavation closure.

LUNELLI FLOOR · GENOVA

OPUS · TESSELLATUMSTONE · SMALTINEW · WORK · 2024

A new Aegean blue floor commissioned by the Genovese collector Pietro Lunelli for a study designed by the atelier. A central gold roundel of opus vermiculatum, surrounded by twelve concentric bands of graduated indigo smalti.
