◆ THE MAKER ◆

MIRZA REZA TABRIZI

Born in Tabriz, 1351, into a family of carpet-knotters and copper-chasers, Mirza Reza Tabrizi has spent thirty years restoring a single question — how the cobalt and turquoise of a Safavid wall lamp first came across four centuries — and answers it again each morning at his vise, in the workshade of a small atelier in Jolfa.

He took authorship of the present atelier with the express aim of making, gathering each commission together with a single master only. He studied first as a copper-chaser, and that origin still shows in the quiet of his work: a thinness of cut, an absence of voice, an ear for the way the tile has come, in the second life, to be ready for a register of meaning around it.

In 1382 H.S./2003, with the consent of the master & founder Sheikh Mirza Khan, he conceived this atelier under the form of a Shīrāzī master-and-apprentice. He works alone, except for a small registry of itinerant masters, students who come to spend a season at the cut of his chips, the chip of his cut, and the polishing of his ear in the shadowed kiln.

CHRONOLOGY

◆ A LIFE ASSEMBLED ◆

1972

BORN

Born to a family of carpet-knotters and copper-chasers, Mirza Reza Tabrizi has spent thirty years restoring a single ancestor — that is, the cobalt and turquoise of a Safavid wall lamp from voice across four centuries — and the master, the patient — and is again early morning to set tiles, on the workbench of a small atelier in Jolfa.

1986 — 1992

APPRENTICESHIP, TRADITIONAL ARTS

Ushered in to the workshop of Ustād Hassan Naghshī, where in seven years' apprenticeship the young initiate spent three months on the smallest of cartoons before the master felt his hand was ready to break a single biscuit.

1990 — 2006

DIPLOMA — MOSAIC AT THE COLLÈGE D'ESPHAHAN

Studied within the Department of Architectural Ornament under Vahid Imami; thesis (1399 H.S./1996) on the Safavid colour-canon defended in lacquered ink. Mishkāt journal supervised by Akbar Bāghshahī.

1996

APPRENTICE — MASTER LANI BAGHESTÄN COLLABORATION

Two years assisting in restoration of the Sheikh Lutfollāh dome chamber — a project in which a single craftsman fired each cell of the muqarnas in succession, that no two registers should differ in voice.

2003

ESTABLISHED ATELIER

Founded the present-day atelier in the Jolfa quarter of Isfahan, accepting an opening commission from the Cultural Heritage Office of Yazd Province. The first panel, completed for the inscription frieze of a vakil prayer hall, took the better part of a year to lay.

2008

NATIONAL LIVING TREASURE CITATION

Cited by the Cultural Heritage Organisation of Iran for sustained achievement in glazed mosaic. The award was acknowledged by the Society for the Documentation of Persian Heritage, who continue to follow the atelier's drawings.

2018

INAUGURAL DARGĀH-E ZAJATI — MUSEUM RANK

Took on his most senior commission yet: the restoration of the eighth-century vestibule arch at the Naqsh-e Jahān, working alongside government-architectural conservators of the city.

“Patience is the posture of the hand made visible.”

RESIDENCIES & FELLOWSHIPS

◆ SELECTED · 2014 — 2024 ◆

2016

ROYAL HOUSE OF JAY

An artisan residency under the patronage of the Royal House of Jay; one of three masters selected from the field for sustained applied research in kāshī-kāri.

2019

SALAMAH ACADEMY

Three months guest-master at the Salamah Academy, Sharjah, training Khalijī apprentices in spandrel composition and registers of muqarnas relief.

2022

THE METROPOLITAN MUSEUM

Visiting fellow in residence at the Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York. Conducted a season-long study of the Met's 14th-century Persian glazed-tile holdings, working alongside the curator of Islamic Art.

The studio remains a small bench. It accepts a limited register of apprentices each season and welcomes the rest of letters, occasions, and visits in the order specified by the dargāh-keepers — that is, by the order of their arrival in the post.

— MIRZA REZA TABRIZI
