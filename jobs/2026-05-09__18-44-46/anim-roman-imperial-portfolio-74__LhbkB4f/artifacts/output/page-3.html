OPUS
TESSELLATUM

A study in the cutting and laying of stone tesserae — a survey of the materials, the methods, and three working examples drawn from the atelier's recent practice.

The Sourcing of Tesserae

I

Marble · Carrara

White, Statuario, Bardiglio. Cut to a 12 mm cube on the bench, hand-trimmed at the lay-in. Used for the lighter ranges of value: skin, cloud, plaster.

II

Smalti · Murano

Coloured glass enamels, made by the same Venetian furnaces that supplied the Vatican workshops. Saturated reds, deep ultramarines, leaf-greens.

III

Porphyry · Quarry of Egypt

Imperial purple, sourced through the licensed quarry near Mons Porphyrites. Reserved for the central medallions and the Christological figures.

Conservation Method

VII

Each restoration begins with a manual lift: every tessera is mapped, photographed, and transferred to a paper bed for cleaning, before any consolidation begins on the underlying mortar layer. We use a lime-based grout (calce idraulica naturale, NHL 3.5) for both bedding and pointing — Portland cement is permitted only in the modern bedding-slab beneath, where it is retained by lattice. The reintegration follows a strict no-falsification rule: lacunae are filled with a slightly recessed neutral tessera, never with a re-painted tile. The bench is the workshop, and the workshop is the building. The restoration is finished only when the floor walks under foot as it did before.

Three Case Studies

Helios Solis · Pavimentum Solis

Aquileia · MMXXII
I

A radial floor — sixteen rays alternating black and yellow, with a central rosette of porphyry and red smalto — recovered from a private domus near the Roman forum at Aquileia. Sixty per cent of the original surface had been lost to a 17th-century cellar dug through the floor; the reintegration drew the lost rays from a 1937 photograph in the Soprintendenza archive. Lifted in 2022, returned to the original site in 2023, and now visible to the public on the second Sunday of every month.

Opus Vermiculatum · Reticulum

Genova · MMXXIII
II

A textile-like ground in two colours of marble and one of smalto, conceived for the entrance hall of a 19th-century townhouse. The "thread" of the weave is a 5 mm tessera laid on the diagonal; warp and weft alternate every fifth row to create a slight shimmer underfoot. Designed to a 4 × 6 m field, on a herringbone subbase of pietra forte.

Vortex Aureus · Mosaicum Sphaericum

Privato · MMXXIV
III

A circular composition for a private oratory near Cremona — a spiral in six concentric bands of gold smalto on an aubergine ground, contracting to a single Carrara cube at the centre. The piece is laid in nine months on the bench and shipped in eleven sections, finished on site over two further weeks.
