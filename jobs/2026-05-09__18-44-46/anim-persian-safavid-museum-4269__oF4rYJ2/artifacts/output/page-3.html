Riza ʿAbbasi
& the Isfahan Style

Drawings from the Royal Library
1598 — 1635

From the Curator

A small, faithful, and slightly heretical exhibition. Forty-two folios of single-page drawings by Riza ʿAbbasi and the school he gathered around him in the kitabkhana of Shah ʿAbbas, presented for the first time outside the album form in which they have lived for four centuries.

The argument is not that the single page replaces the album, but that the drawing — the loose, calligraphic, almost casual notation of the courtly nude or the kneeling youth — is the Safavid contribution to the discipline of looking. Riza is the painter who decided not to paint.

— Faruq Najmuddin, Curator of Drawings

A Word on Conservation

Each folio has been re-mounted on Japanese kozo paper by the museum's atelier. The display is rotated every eight weeks: the drawings have rested four centuries in albums, and the museum is not in the business of cumulative light damage. If you are returning, you are not returning to the same exhibition.

A separate study cabinet of facsimiles is open to scholars by appointment with the Office of Research.

A small print catalogue accompanies the exhibition. Riza ʿAbbasi & the Isfahan Style, 88pp, museum press, 1.250.000 IRR.
