学部 ・ 課程

Hokurin's undergraduate body of about 1,640 students is organized into three intentionally small faculties, each rooted in the disciplines that have shaped Higashiyama for four centuries — letters, craft, and the watershed.

A four-year arc, with the third year spent away from the seminar room.

Every Hokurin undergraduate follows the same four-fold rhythm. The first year is a shared foundation — classical Japanese, English or a second European language, and a methodology core taught in the 1782 main hall. The second year is taken inside the chosen faculty, where students sit small reading and making seminars capped at twelve.

The third year is the fieldwork year, mandated since 1989: a minimum of twenty-four weeks of supervised work outside the campus — at a Mishima lacquer atelier, a Kumano river-management office, a Hawai‘i plantation archive, a Tōhoku washi village. The fourth year returns to Kyoto for the graduation thesis, defended in March before two faculty and one external reader.

YEAR ONEShared foundation in classical Japanese, languages, and method.
YEAR TWOFaculty seminars: twelve students per session, weekly.
YEAR THREEFieldwork year — off-campus, supervised.
YEAR FOURThe Hachiya tradition: thesis defence in March.
