RESEARCH & LIBRARY 研究と図書

Slow scholarship,
kept on shelves
of paulownia.

Hokurin's research culture is shaped by the Tōrin Bunko — a working library of Edo-period woodblocks and manuscripts kept onsite. Faculty are expected to publish slowly, often, and in dialog with the maker traditions they study.

— ACTIVE PROJECTS
— PROJECT 01

Pigment Atlas of Late Edo Ukiyo-e

A microspectrometric mapping of beni-e and ai prints across the Tōrin holdings, with conservation notes returned to lender institutions.

— PROJECT 02

Yura River Estuary Long Survey

Twenty-eight-year stream-quality study run with the Faculty of Environmental Studies and the Maizuru fisheries cooperative.

Documenting joinery patterns in surviving Higashiyama machiya, in cooperation with the City Heritage Office.

Duplication Request Procedure

Visiting researchers may request high-resolution scans of held items by lodging a one-page dossier with the Bunko's chief curator. Decisions are issued within fourteen days; physical reproduction is hand-collated.

A modest copying fee of ¥ 800 per sheet contributes to the conservation fund. Items in the Hachiya bequest may be photographed but not photocopied.

— VISITING SCHOLARS

Visiting Scholars

The university accepts six visiting scholars each year for terms of two to nine months. Successful applicants receive an unfurnished room in the South Annex and reading rights for the Tōrin Bunko. Applications are reviewed by a committee of four faculty in late September and again in late February. The university does not provide a per diem but does cover travel within Japan during the residency.
