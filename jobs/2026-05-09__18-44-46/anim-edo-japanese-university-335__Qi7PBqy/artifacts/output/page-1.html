— HOKURIN ・ 沿革

Founded in 1952.
Refounded on the
Higashiyama machiya in 1989.

Hokurin Daigaku was chartered in 1952 as a small public college for the humanities, occupying a former silk-merchant compound in northern Kyoto. In 1989, after a four-year restoration of its 1782 machiya complex along Sanjō-dōri, the university moved its main campus to the Higashiyama ward and recognized its faculties around three commitments: textual care, material craft, and the long ecology of the Kamo basin.

The university remains deliberately small — 1,640 undergraduates, 412 graduate students — and shares its central courtyard with the Tōrin Bunko, a working library of Edo-period woodblocks. Coursework is conducted in Japanese and English; admission is by interview and portfolio.
