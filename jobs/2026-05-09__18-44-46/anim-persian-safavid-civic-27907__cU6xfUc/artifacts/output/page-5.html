Bāb-i Pengjom · Folio V

Petitions & Hearings

Concerning the proper submission of the arzih dāsht — the written petition — and the conduct of public hearings before His Excellency the Dīvānbegi, that justice may be done without obstruction by any subject of the realm.

Be it known to all subjects

that the Dīvān sits in morning from after the dawn prayer until the call of sunset upon the days of Friday at the lofty house of His Majesty Shah Abbas; and the conduct of public hearings before the Dīvānbegi shall continue, in Wednesday afternoons, from the third hour of dawn until half of one.

No petition shall be heard which lacks the seal of attestation, nor which is presented by an intermediary not of the same kin or of the same household to body.

Reception

Where to Present It

Bring the carried petition to the outer courtyard of the Dīvān, between the Mosque of Shaykh Loṭfollāh, where the clerk of the supplications shall mark the petition with date upon arrival, and the date of its registry to the cause.

A petition unmarked in such wise, and entered through the lid of the Office of the side door, shall not be heard.

Hearings

Appearance Before the
Dīvānbegi

Upon the Wednesday following acceptance, the petitioner shall present himself in the outer audience hall, with such witnesses as are summoned.

The Dīvānbegi may receive his decision in writing in a single afternoon, but matters of weight may stand adjourned to the Wednesday next without ceremony.

The Order of Proceedings

  • I. The petitioner is rehearsed in the open hall, that none not of these doors shall give cause to grievance.
  • II. The Lord, the Dīvānbegi, having weighed the cause, shall settle by his own seal where the cause merits it.
  • III. Witnesses shall be sworn upon the public ground; the petitioner shall be heard at full length without prompting from the petitioner's quarter.
  • IV. Submission to the petition is permitted in the outer audience hall, where the registry mark is only a step in the process of the cause.
  • V. A petitioner so dismissed by the petition's intermediary, the Dīvānbegi, and a sitter of the conduct of the hearing, may yet adhere to the petition without further charge.
  • VI. The ruling so down in the register, sealed by the hand of the Dīvānbegi, with a copy delivered to the petitioner without further charge.

Special Notice

Concerning New Jolfa

Petitions arising out of the Armenian quarter of New Jolfa may continue to be borne to this Dīvān as before, with the kalāntar of that quarter affixing his seal in attestation. The Christian and the Hebrew may be answered alike. Hindu merchants of Cailay quarters shall the like form of writing, written in the customs of the Maydan and his lands.
