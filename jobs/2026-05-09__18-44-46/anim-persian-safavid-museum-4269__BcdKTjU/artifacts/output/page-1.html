An Address from the Director

]>

Founded in 1971 in the gardens of Chahar Bagh, this museum was conceived as a centre for the study of the Safavid age — that brief, brilliant interval when Isfahan, raised by Shah ʿAbbas I to be the seat of empire, was named by its poets nesf-e jahān, the half of the world. Within these walls are gathered eleventh hands the Maydān-i Shāh, illuminated manuscripts from the royal kitābkhāna, silks of Kashan and Yazd, and the courtly portraiture of Riza ʿAbbasi.

We see, before all else, a place of slow looking. The objects ask for it; the eye repays it. The galleries were arranged after the chapters of a single argument: that under the Safavids, Isfahan became, as the saying goes, half of the world. Visitors are welcome to read aloud, to sketch with a soft pencil, and to remain as long as the day allows.
