__SVG_KIRI__
Fourteen thousand volumes, given on a single autumn morning in 1991.

The Tōrin Bunko was assembled by a now-rebuilt branch of the Tōrin Yamasaki, dispersed in 1947, then quietly reassembled by its third-generation conservator Yamasaki Itsuro and donated to Hokurin one autumn morning in 1991.

Its quarter is paulownia: the cabinetry damps humidity, slows insect attack, and exhales lightly with the seasons. Fourteen thousand volumes — Heian poetic anthologies, three sets of the Tale of Genji, three of the Shōbōgenzō, complete printer's records of Kōrin's early run — sit a finger-width apart, breathing in time.

Our researchers work alongside the volumes, not on them: photography is by a single oblique sodium lamp; transcription is by hand; access is by appointment, in seasons.

Duplication Request Procedure

Reproductions of pre-1900 holdings require prior committee review. We do not photograph rotogravures, hand-coloured leaves, or ukiyo-e impressions of seven impressions or fewer. Microfilm and reading-room photography on a tripod is allowed by appointment.

Contact tōrin-bunko@hokurin.ac.jp · Three weeks notice.

VISITING SCHOLARS

The library accepts up to twelve visiting scholars each year, with stays renewable to twenty-four months. Applicants should be working on a project that requires the holdings. We provide a desk in the reading room, twenty hours a week of librarian support, and access to the Bunko's running catalogue. Stipends are not awarded; library access and a small subsidy of guest housing are.
