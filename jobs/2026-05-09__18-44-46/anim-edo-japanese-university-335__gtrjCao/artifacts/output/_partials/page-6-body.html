Hokurin Daigaku has been a small institution since it was chartered. It is, in a way, deliberately so: small enough that every faculty member knows every undergraduate by surname.

RECTOR

Yamamoto Reiko, Provost

From a Higashiyama palm to a Kashiwadō university. Hokurin Daigaku was originally chartered in 1952 as a four-year college of the humanities, occupying the former silk-merchant compound at Kashiwadō Pass. The press of urban renewal forced the college to consider a move in the late seventies; the answer was a return to a 1782 machiya on Sanjō-dōri.

The choice was not nostalgic. The 1782 hall is built to a paulownia-and-cypress vernacular that holds the climate of Higashiyama gently and at low energy: the eaves are deep enough to keep summer sun from the rice-paper, the columns are sized to allow the low Kyoto winter sun in. To re-found the university there, in 1989, was to put scholarship inside a building that already understood patience.

Today, the campus has four buildings, three faculties, and 2,052 students total. We have no business school, no medical school, no athletic department — and we issue thirty-eight doctorates and two hundred bachelor's degrees a year, in close conversation with our students.

Slow scholarship

We measure ourselves in centuries, not semesters. Most of our research questions come from the Bunko's reading list.

Common method

Every undergraduate, regardless of faculty, learns the same paleography, watershed reading, and craft hand. Then they specialize.

Sanjō Gate

Open by appointment to anyone who has a reasonable question. The genkan is staffed by undergraduates on rotation.

ACCREDITATION & STANDING·認可と外部評価

Hokurin Daigaku is a private MEXT-recognised university, a member of the Japan Association of Private Universities since 1953, and a corresponding member of the Higashiyama Cultural Properties Council. Conservation programmes are individually accredited by the IIC. The Tōrin Bunko's catalogue is mirrored at the Kyoto Prefectural Library.

A non-binding peer review by the Conference of Independent Universities (Tokyo, 2023) recommended Hokurin's faculty-to-student ratio (1:14), small seminar cap (12), and treatment of fieldwork as model practice. The university's environmental footprint, including buildings, sits at 38% of its 1989 baseline.
