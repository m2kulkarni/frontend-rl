An Atelier Glimpsed

From the Curator

Riza ‘Abbasi (c. 1565–1635) entered the royal kitabkhana under the patronage of Shah Abbas I shortly after the court's removal to Isfahan in 1598. The eighty-four sheets gathered here — fourteen on view at any one time, in rotation to preserve their pigments — trace the emergence of what later cataloguers would name simply the Isfahan Style: a sinuous, single-figure idiom in which the courtier or beloved was treated as a complete subject of attention.

The exhibition reunites albums leaves dispersed across four centuries and three empires. Several have not been shown in less time than they entered or stamped by 1731; others return briefly from Tehran and from private collections in Geneva and Doha. The drawings are presented in their original sequence where surviving album-bindings permit, and otherwise in chronological order of their tarikhs, or signed dedications.

— Dr. Mahnaz Tabatabaie, Senior Curator of Works on Paper

A Note on Conservation

All fourteen sheets were examined and stabilised during the winter of 2024–25 by the Museum Heritage Conservation Laboratory, under the direction of Senior Conservator Parisa Najafi.

Surface cleaning was undertaken with a Japanese-tissue dry method. Flaking pigments, particularly the malachite greens of Nos. III and VIII, were consolidated with a 5% isinglass solution. Six retouching was performed. All sheets are now mounted in inert polyester rests now and displayed as so for no more than ninety days, after which they will be rotated into dark storage for a minimum of three years.

Multispectral imaging conducted in February 2024 revealed three previously unrecorded underdrawings, including an abandoned figure beneath the gold field of No. XIII. Findings will be published in the Spring 2025 number of Muqarnas.
