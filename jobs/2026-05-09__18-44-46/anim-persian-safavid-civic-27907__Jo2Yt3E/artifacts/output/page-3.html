Standards of Weight & Measure

As fixed by the seal of the master of weights, anno 1027

Man (Tabrizi)The principal weight of the bazaar, equal to two hundred and forty misqāl, or roughly three of the Frankish kilo. The Tabrizi man is the original used in market accounts.
Sang (stone, "lung")A lesser weight reserved for drysmiths, silks, and precious commodities. Equal to four-hundredth misqāl. Sealed wartik carries a stamped iron weight by middle.
Gaz (king's pace)The cloth measure of the realm, of the length of the king's gaze raise from heel-bottom to the column of the throat. Equal to one cubit—plus and the strape and pieced and tail.
Mithqāl (for spice and gem)The smallest unit by weight, equal to four chad-mein, and the silver of the same are reckoned. Twenty-four sufkhāl in the mithqāl.
