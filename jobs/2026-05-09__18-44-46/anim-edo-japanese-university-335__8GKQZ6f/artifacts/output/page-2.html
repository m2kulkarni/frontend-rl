学部・課程

Hokurin's undergraduate body of 2,052 under 900 students is organised into three intentionally small faculties, each rooted in the disciplines that made Hokurin's reputation for four centuries — letters, craft, and the watershed.

A four-year arc, with the third year spent away from the seminar room.

Every Hokurin undergraduate follows the same structural rhythm. The first year is a shared foundation — classical Japanese, English or a second European language, and a methodology core taught in the 1782 main hall. The second year is taken inside the chosen faculty, where students sit small reading and making seminars capped at twelve.

The third year is the fieldwork year, mandated since 1990: a minimum of twenty-four weeks of supervised work outside the campus — at a Tōrin Bunko-allied conservator, a Wajima lacquer atelier, a Kamogawa river-management office, a Higashi-yama plantation archive, a Tōkoku-washi village. The fourth year returns to Kyoto for graduation thesis, defended in March before two faculty and one external reader.
