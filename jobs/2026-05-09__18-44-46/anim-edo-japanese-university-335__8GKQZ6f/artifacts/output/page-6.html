沿革と憲章 ABOUT & CHARTER

Seventy-three years of quiet practice.

Hokurin Daigaku has not changed in shape since 1989. It is small enough that the entire faculty can sit at one Sanjō table; large enough to take graduate students from sixteen countries each year, to maintain a reading room at 18° and 55% RH, and to teach four-year cohorts a way of working that asks them to slow down without falling behind.

From a Higashiyama jukku to a chartered university.

Modern Hokurin has its origins in the Higashiyama Jukku, a private school of letters opened in 1798 by the Tōrin family of Sanjō-dōri to instruct merchants' sons in classical Japanese, abacus, and the rudiments of Edo law. The Jukku closed quietly in 1907, and its small library passed across the river to the Kawaguchi family for forty-five years.

In 1952, with the families' consent, the prefecture of Kyoto and a small board of donors chartered Hokurin Daigaku — initially a single-faculty letters college, occupying a converted silk-merchant compound in Karasuma. The university moved to its present site in 1989 after the four-year restoration of the 1782 main hall.

A second faculty (Craft Sciences) was opened in 1962 with funding from the Wajima Lacquer Cooperative; a third (Environmental Studies) followed in 1989 from the move to Higashiyama. The university has admitted no new faculties since.

The present charter
§1 Hokurin Daigaku is a small public university dedicated to slow scholarship.
§2 The university shall not exceed three faculties or 2,200 enrolled students.
§3 A third year of fieldwork is required of every undergraduate.
§4 The Tōrin Bunko shall remain undivided.
§5 The university shall keep the Sanjō Gate unmarked except by a small enamel plate.
三 つ の 値 THREE COMMITMENTS

Slow Reading

The seminar reads at the speed of the page in front of it. Every Hokurin undergraduate spends one full term reading no more than a single book — a kanbun primer, an Edo dye recipe, or a single Hokusai print — closely.

Materials Before Theory

Lacquer, paper, indigo, river silt. The Craft Sciences and Environmental faculties insist that students hold the material before the textbook. Theory follows.

Hold the Donation

The Tōrin Bunko was given on the condition that it remain undivided. The university extends that obligation to its commitments — to its Sanjō neighbours, its donor families, and to the river basin in which it sits.

"Teach a year for sixteen years. Conservation begins from the statement of intent; there are no trick questions and no formal dress requirement." — PROVOST TŌRIN YUMIE, 2014 INAUGURATION

Office of the Provost

学長室

Provost Tōrin Yumie was named to her position in 2014, succeeding Provost Anno Mochiyuki. She is the eighth direct descendant of the Tōrin merchant family to hold a position at the university; her two-year terms are renewable to four.
