VI · I
On the matter of why we print.
編集方針

Tōkaidō Review is a quarterly journal devoted to the material culture of the Edo period — the years between Tokugawa Ieyasu's first arrival on the eastern marsh and the abdication of the eleventh shogun. We publish slowly, four issues a year, on uncoated paper from the Echizen mill.

Our remit is narrow. The artisans who pulled indigo from vats in their kitchens. The post-towns whose innkeepers preserved twelve-tatami rooms because no one tore down the cedar beams to put up a hotel. The printers, woodblock cutters, and stencilled lacquer-trailers who carried Japan's eye into the modern world.

Founded in 2019 by Shōko Sato and a group of former curators from the Edo-Tokyo Museum, the Review remains independent, reader-funded, and unaffiliated with any university press.
