H

okurin Daigaku was chartered in 1952 as a small public college for the humanities, occupying a former silk-merchant compound in northern Kyoto. In 1989, after a four-year restoration of its 1782 machiya complex along Sanjō-dōri, the university moved its main campus to the Higashiyama ward and recognized its faculties around three commitments: textual care, material craft, and the long ecology of the Kamo basin.

The university remains deliberately small — 1,840 undergraduates, 412 graduate students — and shares its central courtyard with the Tōrin Bunko, a working library of Edo-period woodblocks. Coursework is conducted in Japanese and English; admission is by interview and portfolio.

SYMPOSIUM · CONSERVATION SCIENCES

04 — Winter Symposium on Ukiyo-e Pigment Stability

A two-day gathering convened by the Tōrin Bunko on the lightfastness of beni- and sumi-pigment encaustic across one hundred years of woodblock impressions. Open to graduate students and visiting conservators.

APPOINTMENT · OFFICE OF THE PRESIDENT

03 — Provost Yamamoto Reiko named to MEXT Heritage Council

Provost Yamamoto, a specialist in lacquerware restoration, will serve a three-year term advising the Ministry on the conservation of Heian-period properties in private and temple collections.

CALL · ADMISSIONS

02 — Applications open: MA in Conservation

The two-year MA in Woodblock & Paper Conservation admits a cohort of fourteen students each autumn. Portfolio and a letter from a working conservator are required; interviews held in early March.
