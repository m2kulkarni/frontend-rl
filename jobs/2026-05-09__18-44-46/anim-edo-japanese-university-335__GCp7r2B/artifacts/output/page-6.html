From a Higashiyama plot to a Sanjō machiya, by way of a chartered university.

Hokurin Daigaku was chartered in 1952 by a city committee of textile merchants and three retired professors of Heian literature, with a charge to keep a small humanities school in Kyoto. The first home was a former silk-merchant compound in northern Kyoto.

In 1989, after a four-year restoration of an 1782 machiya complex along Sanjō-dōri, the university moved its main campus to the present Higashiyama address. The Tōrin Bunko was given by an unnamed collector in 1991 and rests on its original paulownia shelves.

In 2008 a small library annex was added on the southeast corner; in 2018, a single-room timber craft workshop replaced an old kura along the eastern edge. No further building is planned.

三つの方針 ・ THREE PRINCIPLES
ONE · 小ささ

Stay small

The smallness of Hokurin is not an interim measure. It is the condition under which we work. We will not grow past 2,500 enrolled students, and we will not open a satellite campus.

TWO · 文脈を持つ

Context-bound

We work on the materials of this place and this basin. The Kamo, the timber economies of Hieizan, the woodblock trades of the lower city. Our research belongs to its setting.

THREE · 言葉を選ぶ

Words last

We write slowly. The university press publishes one short monograph per faculty per year and no journal. We expect each book to be read aloud to its author before going to the printer.

"Teach at a human pace, in a room that smells of cedar. The rest will keep."
— Founder Inaba Hisako, 1958
学長より — A NOTE FROM THE PRESIDENT

Inaba No.18 · Higashiyama

If you are reading this, you have already taken the trouble to walk past the Sanjō gate or to find this page in the small grey type beneath our seal. Hokurin will give you a small thing — a slow place to read for four years, two pots of tea each afternoon, and a library that smells of paulownia and old paper.

If that is the right thing for you, you are very welcome. The application sits on the corner of the Sanjō desk; we open it on Mondays.

— Yamamoto Reiko, President · 学長
