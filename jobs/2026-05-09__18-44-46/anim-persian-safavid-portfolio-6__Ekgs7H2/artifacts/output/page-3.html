THE QUIET DISCIPLINE
OF KĀSHĪ-KĀRI

A note from the workshop on what is kept secret, what is taught — from a practice that begins with sand and lime, and ends in carbon black, gold, and the Persian sky.

I   ·   PREMISE

A CRAFT HELD IN THE HAND

The atelier, in the Sheikh manner as practiced in Isfahan to-day, is not a decoration with finishes architecture but a discipline that begins with measured paper and lime carbonate, then becomes the slow choosing of every glaze and tile-cutter to make. We follow firstly the traditional rendering of the four arts: kāshī, panj-ka kāshī, shamsa, and the muqarnas-vault. Each art is built from inheritance, taught by stitch and substitution: from a master to one apprentice at a time, with no chart or specification beyond the chalk drawings of the Tabrizi workshop.

The atelier was founded by Mirza Reza in 1386 in continuation of his grandfather's school. Two assistants are admitted as apprentices each spring, having paid no fees but having committed to seven years of practice and four years of journeyman labour. We accept no further hands than this; no looking is done; no commission is undertaken without the master's hand on each cut tile.

What follows is a working description of each stage, our materials and our daily rotations, with a few notes on the lore of various firms and the seven days that hold our colour, our hands, and the kiln. Inheritors of Mirza Reza, when they arrive, will find no other notation. Our remembered words and what is set down in this little book are all there is.

UNIQUE TO MIRZA REZA

  • SEVEN-RUNNED KILN-FIRING

    A pinion-paced kiln cycle drawn from the Tabrizi master's notes c. 1308.

  • QĀJĀR-PERIOD COBALT GLAZE

    Mixed in iron oxide and copper, spent over four uninterrupted firings.

  • CARRIED MEASURE TO PANEL

    Designs are gridded by a mechanical proportional rod, never by computer.

  • HAND-CUT TURBĒ-T

    Each tile is cut on a knee-high block; never by machine, never by jig.

THE FOUR STAGES

I   ·   KĀSHĪKĀR   ·   THE PAPER

ṬARRĀĪ

The paper is drawn at full size in graphite-clean ink — a heavy paper of Tabriz mill. The pattern is then drawn on the under-skin of the tile by hand and the cuts gridded by mechanical proportion.

II   ·   KĀSHĪBORĪ   ·   THE WHEEL

BORĪDEN-KĀRĪ

Glazed clay tile blanks, fired between 940° and 990°C, are cut on the knee-block with the small hand-snipper. The cut tessera will be from a few millimetres to a few centimetres on a side.

III   ·   CHĪDEMĀN   ·   THE LAY

CHĪDEMĀN

The cut pieces are laid face-down on the master pattern, the under-side glazed with lime mortar, and fired in the kiln. The piece is reversed once the curing is complete.

IV   ·   NASB   ·   THE FIXING

BAND-KESHĪ

The piece is set on the wall by the master itself, with each tile laid into a still-wet bed of binding mortar. The lines are then re-pointed by hand with a fine bone tool until ‎they shine.

◆   THE SEVEN COLORS · HAFT-RANG   ◆

Each glaze is mixed in a master mineral and one of seven kiln-aged compositions. Of which the chamber's first heat is for cobalt, and the seventh is the cooled white. We rotate using lime mineral salts that have been impregnated for full periods of forty days from the bench at lower mountains.
