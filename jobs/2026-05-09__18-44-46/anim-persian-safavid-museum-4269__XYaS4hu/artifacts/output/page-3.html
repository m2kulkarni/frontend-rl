From the Curator

Riza ʿAbbasi (c. 1565–1635) entered the royal kitabkhana under the patronage of Shah ʿAbbas I shortly after the court's removal to Isfahan in 1598. The eighty-four sheets gathered here — fourteen on view at any one time, in rotation to preserve their pigments — trace the emergence of what later catalogues would name simply the Isfahan Style: a minute, single-figure idiom in which the brush is lifted, set, and pivoted in three or four passes, leaving the page as much white as inscribed.

The body of work falls in two registers. In the first, dervishes, scribes, and youthful princes are caught in instants of repose; in the second, dating to the 1620s, the line falls quieter still, and the figures bend toward the picture-plane as if listening.

That distinct phase is on view in this gallery for the first time outside the Royal Library.

— Dr. Mahnaz Tabatabai, Curator

Fourteen Works on View

No. 117 · Single-Figure Study

Youth in a Lavender Coat with a Cup

Brush and ink with gold heightening, on burnished cream paper. Inscribed, lower right.

c. 1610 · 27.4 × 14.8 cm

No. 119 · Single-Figure Study

Falconer with Hooded Saker

Brush and ink with gold heightening, with two albums assembled by Sayyid Mahdi.

c. 1614 · 26.1 × 17.2 cm

No. 122 · Single-Figure Study

Dervish at Rest, Reading

Brush and ink, with washes of madder and indigo. Annotated in nastaʿliq, lower border.

c. 1614 · 22.0 × 13.4 cm

No. 124 · Single-Figure Study

Reclining Lady with Wine Cup

Opaque watercolours on paper, c.1622. The figure's robe a single field of unmodulated rose; after the Yāzami fashion.

c. 1622 · 26.9 × 16.0 cm

No. 127 · Calligraphic Study

Verses by Saʿdi, Bordered with Birds

Calligraphy by Ali Riza ʿAbbasi (no relation), 1622. Marginal birds and palmettes by Riza Abbasi.

c. 1622 · 23.7 × 14.1 cm

No. 130 · Single-Figure Study

Old Man with Staff and Gourd

Ink and tinted wash, 1626. The artist's last manner — a sinuous, near-curved long line.

c. 1626 · 31.8 × 15.5 cm

No. 133 · Single-Figure Study

Two Camels Fighting

After Mīrak's lost gold and opaque watercolours, 1628. A demonstration piece copied from a treasured ancestor.

c. 1628 · 19.5 × 26.0 cm

No. 135 · Single-Figure Study

Young Scribe at His Writing Box

Brush and ink, 1630. Identified by some scholars as the artist's son, Muhammad Shafiʿ.

c. 1630 · 22.8 × 15.1 cm

No. 138 · Calligraphic Study

Invocation of the Twelve Imams

Gold and Lapis on indigo-dyed paper, 1633. The artist's only known invocation of this kind.

c. 1633 · 27.8 × 14.5 cm

No. 141 · Calligraphic Study

Self-Portrait of the Artist, Drawn from a Mirror

Ink, gold, and slight colour, 1634. Inscribed in the artist's hand: 'this faqir, in the sixty-ninth year.'

c. 1634 · 18.8 × 11.2 cm

A Note on Conservation

Nine of the fourteen sheets have been newly stabilised.

All fourteen sheets were examined and stabilised during the winter of 2023–24 by the Hassan Khoubdar Conservation Atelier under the supervision of Dr. Iraj Khalifeh. Surface cleaning was followed by alkalinity-buffered re-mounting on Korean kozo.

One sheet, No. 122, has been remounted from the verso, restoring an inscription by Sayyid Mahdi visible since 1731.

– Ṣadiqi Beg Afshar, Qanun al-Suvar, c. 1597
