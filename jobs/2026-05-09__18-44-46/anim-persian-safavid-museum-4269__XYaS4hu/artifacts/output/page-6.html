Museum & Society

Beneath this small vault of bricks, scholars, vault, and patrons gather for the surviving fragments of the Safavid age — its tiles, its manuscripts, its silks — and offered them, since 1971, to scholar and citizen alike.

A Brief Chronicle of the Museum

1971
Foundation

Founded by the Pahlavi-era Society for National Monuments as the Centre of Safavid Studies in the Chahar Sotoun pavilion, the museum opened with eleven cases of tilework rescued from the demolition of the maydan.

1983
Reconsecration

Reorganised under the new Ministry of Cultural Heritage; the institution was given its present scholarly mandate and absorbed the Safavid holdings of the former Imperial Library.

2008
Expansion

The Maydān Wing, designed by the architect Nader Ardalan, doubled the gallery floor and added the Iwan of Manuscripts, the Tilework Court, and the present scholar's reading room.

Charter of the Museum

The Museum of Isfahan exists, plainly, to keep this small fragment of the Safavid age within the city that made it. Its work is to gather, conserve, study, and exhibit the material culture of the years 1501 to 1736, and to do so as a working institution: collegial, slow, hospitable to the common visitor and the candidate scholar alike.

Three premises govern the work. The first is that an object held in the city of its making is not the same as one held abroad: the patina of place, the company of cognate objects, the common air of dust and citrons in spring — these are part of the work's meaning, not adornments to it.

The second is that a museum is a place of slow looking. Wall texts are kept brief; benches abundant. The galleries close half an hour before the doors do, that visitors may see them empty.

The third is that scholarship is the marrow of curatorial work, not a footnote to it. The Royal Atelier Library is open year-round; the Museum Press publishes four titles a year; one fellowship is held in residence at any moment.

Office of the Director

Dr. Hooshang Mowsavi

Director, since 2019

Trained at the Sorbonne and the University of Tehran, Dr. Mowsavi held curatorial posts at the National Museum of Iran and the Aga Khan Museum before her appointment in 2019. Her present scholarship treats the Maydān-i Naqsh-i Jahan as a single decorative argument.

Dr. Parisa Karbalāʾi

Deputy Director · Office of Research

Co-author of An Atlas of Safavid Isfahan (2022) and editor of the journal Maydān; oversees the Royal Atelier Library, the Museum Press, and the resident fellowships. Reads four scripts and writes in three.
