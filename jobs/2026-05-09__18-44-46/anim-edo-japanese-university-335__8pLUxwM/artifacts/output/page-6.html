沿革・歴史· FROM A HIGASHIYAMA JUKU

From a Higashiyama juku to a chartered university

Hokurin began as a private juku in the rented backroom of a Sanjō kimono shop, run on Saturday afternoons by three young scholars who had returned to Kyoto after the war. By 1952 the juku had been chartered as a four-year college for the humanities. The 1989 move to its present machiya site, after a four-year restoration of a 1762 kimono compound, established the present three-faculty arrangement and the joint custody of the Tōrin Bunko.

The university takes its name from the small grove of paulownia trees that once stood in the Higashiyama courtyard — gone now, except for the kamon and the timber on which the Bunko's shelves were planed in 1991. The motto is from a 1738 letter held in the Bunko: gakumon wa te no shigoto de aru — scholarship is a work of the hand.

Governance is by senate of the three faculty heads, the chief librarian, and the head of the conservation studio. The provost is elected for a five-year term and may not stand for re-election. The current provost, Yamamoto Reiko, is the seventeenth.
