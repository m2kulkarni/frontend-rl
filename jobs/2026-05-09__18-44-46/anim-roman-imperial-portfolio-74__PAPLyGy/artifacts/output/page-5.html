SCRIPTA

A chronological registry of essays, lectures, and notebook entries from the studio — drafted in the first language the author had at hand. Translated by request only.

I.

"Concrete Without Apology"

A defence of fair-cement work, on a project in the Seralico for an Aurelian patron whose engineer asked us to "imitate stone" with an industrial poured surface. The right surface was the surface — left as cast.

II.

"The Threshold is the Argument"

Notes from a lecture given at the Università di Trento. The threshold of a building is its only true ornament; everything else is a consequence. Borrowed from a fragment of Aulus Gellius which I cannot now find again.

III.

"On the Coffer, as Structural Honesty"

Reading my friend's first volume on Borromini, I came around to a position I had refused for a decade — the coffered ceiling is not décor but a confession of its own load path. We must hold the slab proud.

MMXXIII
IV.

"Travertine is Slow Material"

A short essay for the studio's MMXXIII annual: travertine cures, breathes, and continues to be carved by atmosphere long after we lay it. We design as if the building has not yet finished arriving.

V.

"As Walls is Rome"

A walking essay in seven parts, each begun at a city gate and concluded at the next. Read aloud at the academy in Trastevere on the spring equinox of 2023, and reproduced (in part) in Domus of June MMXXIII.

VI.

"The Pediment Returns"

An essay on the symbolic burden of the pediment in modern civic work. The reader will note that I argue against the pediment in private domestic work but for it where the building is a public thing, owned by no one.

MMXXII
VII.

"Against the Frameless Window"

A complaint against the contemporary cult of the picture-window: the unmuntined plate of glass, made for selling rooms in photographs, which dissolves the wall it fakes to honour. Frame the view, do not erase it.

VIII.

"Mosaic and the Patient Hand"

Notes from the bench. On the cure of the hand by repetitive, paid-by-time work — and the patience of the master mosaicist who will not cut a tessera small enough to disappear before its colour answers the next tessera at the seam.

IX.

"A Catalogue of Bolts"

A short, eccentric inventory of every iron bolt, chain, and hinge held in the studio's salvage cabinet — provenance, weight, and the date it left a building. Reprinted (with adjustments) in Pietra e Acqua, Anno III.

X.

"Notes from a Closed City"

Twelve laconic observations on the practice of architecture during the long quiet of MMXX-MMXXI. Mostly about light, the absent footfalls of others, and the discovery (re-) that doors are essential precisely because we know how to close them.

JANUARII
MMXXI
XI.

"Why We Still Draw the Order"

A defence of teaching the orders by hand to junior staff. The exercise is not nostalgia — it is the reading of a measure-system devised across two thousand years, in front of which the architect of the present is a pupil.

XII.

"Vitruvius at the Hardware Store"

An affectionate essay on the persistence of antique categories of stone, lime, and timber inside the apparently modern industry of building. Bring Vitruvius to the supply yard on Via Casilina; he will not be confused.
