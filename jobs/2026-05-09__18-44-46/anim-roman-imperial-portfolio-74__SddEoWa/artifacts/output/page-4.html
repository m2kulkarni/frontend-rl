Lucius Vennius was born in Ravenna in the spring of 1976, four blocks from the Mausoleum of Galla Placidia, and grew up taking the long way to school in order to pass beneath its star vault. He studied architecture at the Politecnico di Milano under Pier Carlo Bontempi and afterward took the two-year diploma at the Scuola di Restauro di Spoleto, where he learned to set tesserae by hand on the dorsa di Murano della Scuola di Restauro di Spoleto, where he learned to set tesserae by hand.

From 2007 to 2011 he apprenticed at Bottega Caratelli di Trastevere, working on the re-laying of the fourth-century floors at Santa Pudenziana and on three private commissions for villas in the Castelli Romani. The atelier was founded in 2011 in his own small house in via dei Coronari, where it has remained.

The practice is small and deliberately so — Vennius, two associates, and a rotating bench of three or four mosaicists drawn from Spoleto, Aquileia, and Ravenna. Every commission, whether a private chapel or a fifteen-metre apse, is drawn entirely by hand on the long oak table at the back of the studio. Nothing leaves the door without first being walked across at full scale.

Three instruments govern the studio. None of them is a screen. Each is older than the practice that uses it, and each is slower than the patron expects.
INSTRUMENTUM I

AXIAL PLANS, DRAWN BY HAND

Every project begins with a single set drawing on Fabriano 220 grams, drawn along the cards and descendance of the Roman pes. The proportions are derived first by armature, then by ratio, never by computer.

INSTRUMENTUM II

FULL-SCALE MOSAIC CARTOONS

Every mosaic is first produced 1:1 in charcoal on kraft paper, pinned to the long studio wall. The pattern is walked across, criticised at ten or fifteen feet of distance, then redrawn before a single tessera is cut.

INSTRUMENTUM III

PLASTER MODELS FOR VAULTS

Domes, groin vaults, and apsidal half-domes are first built at 1:20 in hydraulic plaster on a turned wooden formwork. The model is the proof — it is the first test of the line of thrust before the order of marble cutting begins.

STRUCTURAL ENGINEER

MARCO FENOGLIO

Ingegnere strutturale · Torino · b. 1971
◊

Trained at the Politecnico di Torino and previously of Favero & Milan, Marco has been the studio's structural conscience since 2015. He calculates the masonry footprint for every job in hand, draws — with him, only by certification, in finite-element software. He tests the limits of an arch by stacking lump sugar on lengths of dressed beech, and has never declined a job.

MOSAIC MASTER

GIULIA TREVI

Capomastra · Tivoli · b. 1979
◊

Third-generation cutter of the Trevi family of Tivoli, Giulia leads the cubicular cuts on every Atelier project from foundation to keystone. Her speciality is opus minutum in proportion, but her crew of seven has placed two large—and many real-and-every-stone field worked under each of half a dozen mid-Imperial floor projects across Italy. She has not laid a single tessera since 2009. She does not permit power tools above the studio table.
