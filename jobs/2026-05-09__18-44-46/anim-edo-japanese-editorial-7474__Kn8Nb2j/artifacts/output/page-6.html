第一節I   COLOPHON

On the matter of why we print.

Tōkaidō Review is a quarterly journal devoted to the material culture of the Edo period — the years between Tokugawa Ieyasu's first arrival on the eastern marsh and the abolition of the shōen-shogun. We publish slowly, four issues a year, on uncoated paper from the Echizen mills.

Our remit is narrow. The artisans who pulled lodge from a tube of skies in the morning, the women in the woodblock workshops, the marketplace economists working printers' inks and the railroad inspectors. We are committed to: real research, no opinion-piece writing, and unaffiliated with any university press.

Founded in 2019 by Mariko Sato and a group of former curators from the Edo-Tokyo Museum, the Review remains independent: reader-funded, ad-unaffiliated and not university-press.

The masthead.

Six permanent editors and a rotating roster of ten field contributors.

EDITOR-IN-CHIEF

Mariko Sato

佐藤 真理子

Founder. Formerly senior curator of woodblock art at the Edo-Tokyo Museum. Author of The Wood-block & the Wayside.

ART DIRECTOR

Hisao Tanabe

田辺 久夫

Designs every page of the Review. Studied with Kohei Sugiura. Twenty-year veteran of independent type design in Tokyo.

MANAGING EDITOR · CRAFTS

Kenji Arakawa

荒川 健治

Edits the textile and lacquer beats. Born in Niigata, school of Mingei. Coordinates studio visits across Western Japan.

MANAGING EDITOR · ROUTES

Yūki Hoshino

星野 結衣

Born in the foothills of Mt. Ontake. Reports on routes, post-towns, and pre-Meiji walking culture. Walked the Nakasendō twice.

PRINT & PRODUCTION

Daniel Ferrar

ダニエル・フェラー

English-language editor; correspondence. Brooklyn, now Hongō. He has set every issue of the Review on a 1968 Heidelberg.

CONTRIBUTING EDITOR

Reiko Iwata

岩田 玲子

Writes on archival ephemera and the materials of the printers' guild. Author of Kabuki Programs & Their Survivors.
