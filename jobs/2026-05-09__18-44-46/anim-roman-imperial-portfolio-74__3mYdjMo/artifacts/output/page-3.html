TESSELLATUM
Pavimenta · Parietes · Restauratio

Within the atelier the mosaic bench has stood since 2008 — a long oak table, raked north light, and trays of marble cut to four millimetres. From this room Lucius Vennius works as both designer and conservator, setting new pavements for civic patrons and consulting on the recovery of antique surfaces from Vesuvius to Pompeii.

THE SOURCING OF TESSERAE

BIANCO DI CARRARA

Tuscania · Stone Quoq.

Cut from the Polvaccio quarry. In 30 kg billets and reduced by hand on the medicine to 8 mm cubes. Vennius reserves the lightest grades for the high-key fields of restoration work.

ROSSO DI VERONA

Veneto · East emendat.

A modular ammonitic limestone whose iron-rich matrix gives the deep burnt-coral the Romans called rubrum. Used solely for the field of accents.

PORFIDO EGIZIANO

Mons Porphyrites · MMXVII

Imperial purple porphyry, quarried only in the Eastern desert. Each fragment is sourced through restoration and re-cut into ten-mm cubes, never larger.

CONSERVATION METHOD

VII

The seven-step conservation method developed at this bench begins with a survey by reflected light — never by raking or photogrammetric — and proceeds through consolidation of the bedding, careful detachment by sectioned plaster, archival cataloguing, micro-cleaning by quartz dental tools, anastylosis with tinted lime mortar, and final reburial of redundant fragments. No tessera leaves the building unrecorded; no fragment leaves the city without licence.

THREE CASE STUDIES

Case IAquileiaMMXXII

Inaugural commission, undertaken on a north transept floor of the Basilica Patriarcale. The atelier reset 184,000 tesserae across a 24-square-metre field of original IVth century work, reintegrating eleven pictorial fragments without ghosting or contour. The completed surface was rededicated in October 2022 in the presence of the conservator and the bishopric.

Case IIPompeiiMMXXIII

Reconstruction of a banded threshold in the House of the Vestals, drawing on figurative fragments cleared during the 1907 excavations. The treatment leaves the deteriorated wall undisturbed but resets a band of red-and-cream chevrons in cleansed mortar, anchored to the original opus signinum substrate. Documentation is held in the local Soprintendenza archive.

Case IIIGenovaMMXXIV

An entirely new pavement, designed for a private chapel on the Via di Casaregis. The composition spirals from a central gold-leaf disc through nine bands of indigo, lapis, and porphyry — an emblematic plan derived from the Greek chrismon and adapted to the patron's wish for a centred meditative field. Completion: spring 2024.
