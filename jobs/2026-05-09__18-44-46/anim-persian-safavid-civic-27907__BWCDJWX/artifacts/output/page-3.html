Weights & Measures

Standards of Weight & Measure

Man (weight) — The principal weight of the bazaar, equal to two hundred and forty mithqals, or roughly three of the Frankish kilo. The man itself is twice the weight used in the warden's office.

Sang — A lesser weight reserved for drysmiths, silks, and precious commodities. Equal to four hundred mithqals' bowl. Each mithqal carries a stamped iron weight by certification.

Gaz (length) — The cloth-measure of the realm, of the length of the king's gaz at boot. And chained to the column of the Qaysarieh gate. No piece of cloth may sell at any other gaz.

Mithqāl (for spices & saffron) — The smallest coin weight, by which spices, saffron and the silver of the mints are reckoned. Twenty-four added to the mithqāl.

Office of the Muhtasib

Inspections of the Muhtasib

By appointment of the Dīvānbegi, conducted in person.

The honoured Muhtasib, accompanied by two scribes and four attendants of the watch, shall pass through the arcades on the appointed day, weighing the merchant's stock against the stamped iron of the Office.
