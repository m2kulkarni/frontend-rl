学部・課程
Hokurin's undergraduate body of 1,640 under 90 sensei is organised into three intentionally small faculties, each rooted in the disciplines that have shaped Higashiyama for four centuries — letters, craft, and the watershed.

A four-year arc, with the third year spent away from the seminar room.

Every Hokurin undergraduate follows the same four-year structural rhythm. The first year is a shared foundation — classical Japanese, English or a second European language, and a methodology core taught in the 1762 main hall. The second year is taken inside the chosen faculty, where students sit small reading and making seminars capped at twelve.

The third year is the fieldwork year, mandated since 1989: a minimum of twenty-four weeks of supervised work outside the campus — at a Wajima lacquer atelier, a Kumano-river management office, a Hawaii plantation archive, a Tōhoku washi village. The fourth year returns to Kyoto for the graduation thesis, defended in March before two faculty and one external reader.

YEAR ONE
Shared foundation in classical Japanese, languages, and method.
YEAR TWO
Faculty seminars: twelve students per cohort.
YEAR THREE
Fieldwork off-campus, 24 weeks supervised.
YEAR FOUR
Thesis defence in March before two faculty.
FACULTY I

Humanities

人文学部 — Bungakubu

Classical Japanese letters, premodern Chinese, and intellectual history of the Edo academy. The faculty maintains the Tōrin Bunko reading room.

  • Classical Japanese (waka, kanbun)
  • Edo intellectual history
  • Comparative manuscript studies
  • Buddhist textual care
FACULTY II

Craft Sciences

工芸科学部 — Kōgeikagakubu

Material chemistry, conservation science, and the workshop tradition of Kyoto's lacquer, paper, and pigment trades. Studio time is required from year two.

  • Pigment & binder chemistry
  • Washi paper & binding
  • Urushi lacquer substrates
  • Woodblock conservation
FACULTY III

Environmental Studies

環境学部 — Kankyōgakubu

The Kamo basin as a four-century coupled human-watershed system. Coursework joins hydrology, agronomy, and land-tenure history.

  • Kamo basin hydrology
  • Edo land-tenure archives
  • Riparian forest stewardship
  • Climate & the machiya block

The third year is when the seminar table walks out of the room. A student who has not stood to teach, to lacquer black, or to mountain archive has not yet read.
