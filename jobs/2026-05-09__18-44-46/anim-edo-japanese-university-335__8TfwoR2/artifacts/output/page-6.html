沿革・由緒·ABOUT & HISTORY

Seventy-three
years of quiet
practice.

Hokurin Daigaku has, since its 1952 charter, kept itself deliberately small — three faculties, four buildings, one library — and turned its energy toward students rather than scale. We are a public university; tuition is held below the prefectural average, and admissions are by interview.

From a Higashiyama jukku to a chartered university.

Hokurin began in 1888 as a private juku — a back-of-the-shop reading society — kept by the silk merchant Inoue Tōsui in the Higashiyama district. Tōsui's grandson Inoue Banzan turned the room into a free school for sons of conservators in 1912. After the war, the prefecture chartered the school in 1952 as a small public college for the humanities; it received university status in 1989 after the four-year machiya restoration.

The university operates under a board of seven trustees, four of them current or retired faculty. Provost: Yamamoto Reiko, since 2021. President of the Board: Sasaki Toranosuke, since 2024.

一

Small classes

No undergraduate seminar above twelve students; no graduate one above eight. Faculty teach every cohort in person.

二

Touchwork

Every student spends a third year off-campus, doing the work that books are otherwise about — paper, pigment, watershed.

三

Slow review

Faculty publish at the cadence of the materials. There are no annual research metrics; the next book is the next book.
