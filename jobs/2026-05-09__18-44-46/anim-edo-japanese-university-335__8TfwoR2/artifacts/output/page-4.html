Research at Hokurin is bound to its library — the Tōrin Bunko, a working collection of 2,341 Edo-period woodblocks, manuscripts, and pigment samples. Faculty publishing follows the cadence of the materials, not the calendar.

The Tōrin Bunko

An open-stack reading room behind the seminar wing, supervised by a curator and one MA student each term. Visiting scholars accepted three windows per year.

蔵書概要·TŌRIN BUNKO

Fourteen thousand volumes, given on a single autumn morning in 1991.

The Tōrin Bunko was bequeathed by a now-defunct branch of the Higashi-Honganji book-keepers' guild, packed for transit in twenty-three lacquer crates and registered into the catalogue over the following six years.

研究計画·ACTIVE PROJECTS
PROJECT 一

Pigment Stability across the Beni Years

Five-year study of beni (safflower) and ai (indigo) lightfastness in 1820–1850 ukiyo-e impressions held in the Bunko. Lead: Y. Yamamoto.

PROJECT 二

Kamo Basin Riparian Forests, 1610–2025

A coupled history of forestry policy, charcoal demand, and dam construction. Co-led with Kyoto University and the Kamo River Association.

PROJECT 三

Lacquer Substrate Microflora

Microbial colonisation of historical urushi grounds; collaboration with the Wajima Atelier and Tōhoku Bunko Archives.

客員研究員·VISITING SCHOLARS

Visiting Scholars

Two visiting scholar windows are open each year — a six-week winter window and a fourteen-week summer one. Visiting scholars are given a desk in the seminar wing, full library privileges, and a teaching invitation worth one undergraduate seminar. Applicants are reviewed by the Provost's office and announced two months before each window opens.
