FACULTIES · 学部

Three faculties,
one machiya.

三学部 · 一町家

Hokurin teaches three undergraduate faculties under one roof. Coursework moves between studio, kiln, and field site; the daily rhythm is set by the workshop bell at the south gate.

A four-year arc, with the third year spent away from the seminar room.

人文学部

HUMANITIES

Bunko studies, classical Japanese philology, and translation between Heian-period registers and modern English.

工芸科学部

CRAFT SCIENCES

Material physics for paper, lacquer, indigo, and pigment; conservation chemistry for Edo-period objects.

環境学部

ENVIRONMENTAL STUDIES

River-basin ecology of the Kamogawa, urban hydrology of Kyoto, and the long historical climate of the Tamba uplands.

An old-fashioned humanities department that reads slowly. First-year students share the genkan reading room with the Tōrin Bunko archivists; third-years take a residency at a partnered temple library.

  • Classical Japanese Philology
  • Buddhist & Shintō Texts
  • Edo Print Culture
  • Translation Studies

A studio-laboratory faculty: half a working bench, half a spectrometer. Pigment chemistry sits next to wood-block carving; the third year goes to a working atelier in Kanazawa or Wajima.

  • Pigment & Dye Chemistry
  • Paper & Substrate Studies
  • Lacquer Conservation
  • Woodblock Reprographics

A small department with long instruments. Most coursework is done outdoors: stream gauging in the upper Kamo, soil cores from the Tamba foothills, and a four-month field season in the Kii peninsula.

  • River-Basin Ecology
  • Urban Hydrology
  • Historical Climatology
  • Forest Management

The third year is when the seminar table walks out of the room. A student who has not stood in mud, in lacquer dust, or in a mountain archive has not yet read.
