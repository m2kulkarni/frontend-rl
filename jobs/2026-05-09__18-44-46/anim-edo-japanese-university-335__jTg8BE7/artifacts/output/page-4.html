研究と東林文庫

Slow scholarship,
kept on shelves
of paulownia.

Hokurin's research culture is shaped by long horizons. We measure pigment migration in centimeters per century, listen to looms that have not changed since the Genroku era, and read books printed before electricity. Our smallness is deliberate.

autumn morning
in 1991.

The Tōrin Bunko was assembled by a private collector across the late Edo and early Meiji eras, then donated to the university by his descendant in 1991. We catalogue at the slowest reasonable pace — at present, an average of 220 items each year — to preserve the integrity of bindings, sequences, and provenance notes that the cataloguers of an earlier century would have stripped.

主な研究 · Main Projects

An ongoing study of beni-pigment migration on Edo-period ukiyo-e impressions. Optical microscopy with paired Raman spectroscopy.

Re-creation of a working Genroku indigo vat using locally fermented sukumo. Documentation in collaboration with the Aizome guild of Tokushima.

The Kamogawa Hydrology Field Project — a long-record monitoring of mid-river chemistry, run with the City Office of Kyoto since 1996.

複写について

Duplication Request Procedure

  1. Submit a written request listing item shelf-marks at least seven business days in advance.
  2. A Bunko archivist will assess preservation condition and approve the format of duplication.
  3. Photography is by appointment; flash and tripods are not permitted in the reading room.
  4. For external publication, please write to research@hokurin.ac.jp with the proposed citation.
VISITING SCHOLARS · 客員研究員

Visiting Scholars

The Bunko hosts a small number of visiting scholars across the year. Stipends and lodging are arranged in coordination with the host faculty; scholars are expected to give one informal talk in the genkan reading room and to leave behind a working notebook of their stay. Applications open each January.
