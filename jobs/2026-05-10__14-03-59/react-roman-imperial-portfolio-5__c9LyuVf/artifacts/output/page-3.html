MOSAIC PAVEMENT,
VILLA AURELIA

PERIODLATE ANTONINE, c. 180 CE
SCOPE43.6 M²
AMERICAN ACADEMY IN ROME

SITE & FINDINGS

Rediscovery of a pavement first lifted in 1894

The Villa Aurelia pavement was first documented in October 1894 by Rodolfo Lanciani during the cutting of a service trench along the eastern garden wall. His field notebook, now bound as Vat. Lat. 13041, records "tessellatum opus, geometrici composuit, integrum per pedes circiter centum quadraginta," and includes a hasty graphite sketch of the central nine-square field.

When the floor was reopened by our team on 14 March 2022, the condition assessment was sober. Of an estimated original 51.4 m², roughly 42.8 m² survived in situ; a further 3.1 m² had been lifted in 1894 and was rediscovered in nine pine crates in the storage vaults of the Musei Nazionali Romani. The remainder is presumed lost to a 1944 trench.

Three principal pathologies were recorded: bowing of the bedding course across the southern quadrant (max. deflection 38 mm), salt-driven detachment of approximately 1,200 individual tesserae, and a fracture line running NNE–SSW, almost certainly seismic, datable on stratigraphic grounds to the early sixteenth century.

GEOMETRY

An opus sectile module of 0.382 metres

On a clean morning of measurement — 6 April 2022, with the floor washed and the joints chalked — we recovered the underlying module. The pavement is set on a square grid of 0.382 m, very close to one and a quarter Roman feet (1.291 pedes), repeated thirty-six times along the principal axis and twenty-four across.

TESSERAE

The central nine-square field articulates a quincunx of inscribed roundels, each one struck from a radius of 0.171 m — exactly nine-twentieths of the module — and each enclosing a related swastika-meander in bardiglio against a giallo ground. A continuous Greek key runs the perimeter at a stride matching the module precisely; nothing in the design is approximate.

MATERIAL

Three quarried marbles, sourced for sympathetic replacement

Fewer than four percent of the original tesserae required replacement, and in every case the stone was matched by quarry, bed, and cutting protocols. The reset campaign drew on three principal marbles, each obtained from documented sources and held under conservation invoice.

  • GIALLO ANTICO
    Numidian yellow, sourced from antique spolia held by the Soprintendenza Speciale di Roma, deposit at Centrale Montemartini · 18.3 kg released under permit 339-2022-T8.
  • PAVONAZZETTO
    Phrygian, from a single column drum recovered at Villa dei Quintili, 1976 inventory · Cutting overseen by Marmi Cave Michelangelo, Carrara, May 2022.
  • BARDIGLIO
    Bardiglio imperiale, freshly quarried at Cave di Seravezza, Tuscany, best selected for blue-grey saturation matching the 1894-lifted fragments.

METHOD

Lifting, consolidation, and reset on lime mortar
I
FACING & LIFTING
The southern quadrant — 11.4 m² — was faced with Japanese tissue and Paraloid B-72 at 5% in acetone, gridded into 92 panels of approximately 0.124 m², and lifted in five working days (12–16 September 2022) against rigid backing boards of phenolic plywood.
II
CONSOLIDATION OF TESSERAE
Detached tesserae — 1,217 individually catalogued — were cleaned of carbonate crusts in sequential baths of demineralised water and ammonium carbonate poultice, then consolidated with nano lime in suspension (CaLoSiL E25, 7% wv applications).
III
NEW BEDDING COURSE
A bedding of hydraulic lime (NHL 3.5) gauged with washed pozzolana from Bacoli was laid to a finished depth of 64 mm over a geotextile separation layer. The mix was specified at 1:2.5 by volume, slump 110 mm, on the recommendation of the conservator.
IV
RESET & FINAL SURFACE
Panels were reset working from the central quincunx outward over twenty-two working days (3–28 February 2023). Joints were closed with a fine lime-marble dust grout tinted to 0.4 % iron oxide. Final surface was dressed by hand with bone tools and dry-brushed.
