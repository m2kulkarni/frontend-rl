Galleries &
Permanent Collection

Seven galleries housing the cathedral arts of northern France, arranged along a single processional axis from Lancet Hall to the open Mason's Yard.

The Cloister Museum's permanent collection comprises one thousand four hundred and sixty-two catalogued objects, of which two hundred and ninety are on continuous public display across the seven galleries described below.

The remaining works are accessible to credentialled researchers through the Reading Room by appointment, and a rotating selection appears in the museum's biennial loan exhibition. All accession numbers follow the museum standard adopted in 1962: prefix CMS, gallery code, and acquisition sequence.

The Seven Galleries

In processional order, west to east

[motif: arch-pointed]
I

The Lancet Hall

c. 1140 – c. 1300

Twenty-four panels of thirteenth-century French stained glass, arranged in three superposed registers across the east wall and lit from behind by a calibrated daylight rig held at 2,700 K.

Provenance
Long-term loan from the Diocese of Soissons, contracted 1932, renewed 2014 for a further fifteen years. Removed from the dismembered Maison de Sainte Hildegarde in 1923, attested 1856.
Conservation
Lead cames replaced 2019 (Atelier Lecot, Chartres). Vidimus film applied 2024. Annual deflexion survey; current bow at panel L8-04 measured 2.5 mm, within tolerance.
[motif: arch-pointed]
II

The Tympanum Court

c. 1170 – c. 1260

A single carved Last Judgement fragment, limestone, 4.6 m across, depicting the weighting of souls and the mouth of Leviathan. Mounted at original portal height on a steel armature designed by Forme + Pickering.

Provenance
Auteyne workshop, c. 1184. Recovered from the rubble of a demolished parish church near Reims in 1908, held by the Musée de Picardie until permanent transfer to the Cloister Museum in 1956.
Conservation
Consolidated with ethyl silicate (Wacker OH 100), 2003. No biological growth recorded since installation in climate-controlled court (RH 50 ± 2%, 18 °C).
[motif: arch-pointed]
III

The Scriptorium

c. XII – XIV

Twelve illuminated leaves displayed under reduced lighting at 50 lux, including a Beatus initial in deep ultramarine and minium from the Abbey of Saint-Avoir (folio 13r), Apocalypse cycle.

Provenance
Mixed: Lecot bequest (Avoir 1947) and Cluny stock acquired 1962 by the Cloister Boudry chairholder. Chasse psalter fragment from the Westhoff family, 1991.
Conservation
Leaves rotated on a 90-day cycle to limit cumulative light exposure (target ≤ 50,000 lux-hours per annum). Mylar interleaving renewed 2022.
[motif: arch-pointed]
IV

The Reliquary Chamber

c. XII – XV

Seventeen reliquaries in champlevé enamel, gilt copper, and rock crystal. The centrepiece is a Limoges chasse, c. 1200, depicting the martyrdom of Saint Thomas Becket.

Provenance
Bequest of Mme Eleanor Whittembre, 1974, with subsequent acquisitions from the De Saux collection (1995) and the Vatican's programme of restitution to civilian custodians (2007).
Conservation
Enamel surfaces examined under raking light biennially. The Becket chasse underwent gold-leaf reintegration at the V&A conservation studios, 2018.
[motif: arch-pointed]
V

The Capitals Cloister

c. 1115 – c. 1240

Forty-nine carved capitals on freestanding limestone columns, arranged in a square plan around a central well. Subjects include foliate, zoomorphic, and historiated programmes.

Provenance
Drawn from the demolished cloister of the Priory of Saint Yves de Thouvérat (Berault), dispersed 1791, reassembled through nine separate acquisitions between 1923 and 2010.
Conservation
Salt efflorescence on capitals 12 (S2) and 21 (W7) monitored quarterly. Dendrochronic poultices applied 2017; recurrence rate halved.
[motif: arch-pointed]
VI

The Rose Window Study

c. 1240 – c. 1400

A scholarly gallery of six full-scale cartoon reconstructions of the great rose windows of Chartres, Notre Dame de Paris, and Reims, prepared by the Atelier Simon Marq, 1994 – 2014.

Provenance
Commissioned directly by the Cloister under Charvel as a pedagogical resource. The cartoons are themselves now considered conservation reference documents.
Conservation
Pigment stability tested annually. The Notre Dame de Paris cartoon was photographed at high resolution in 2019 and now serves the post-fire restoration committee.
[motif: arch-pointed]
VII

The Mason's Yard

c. XII – XX

An open-air gallery of eighty-eight architectural fragments — voussoirs, finials, gargoyles, rosary elements, and one full surviving spire pinnacle — laid out as a working archaeological grid.

Provenance
Assembled from the salvage records of nineteen French and English cathedral fabric offices, 1894 onward. Each piece bears its original site mark in chalk or red lead.
Conservation
Exposed to ambient weather by curatorial intent; lichen growth recorded as part of the long-term study (begun in Open Air 1994 – present).
[motif: rose-window-notre-dame]
