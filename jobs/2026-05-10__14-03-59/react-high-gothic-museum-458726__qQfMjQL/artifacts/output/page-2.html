Galleries &
Permanent
Collection

Seven galleries housing the cathedral arts of northern France, arranged along a single processional axis from the Lancet Hall to the open Mason’s Yard.

The Cloister Museum’s permanent collection comprises one thousand four hundred and seventy-two catalogued objects, of which two hundred and sixteen are on continuous public display across the seven galleries described below. The remaining works are accessible to credentialled researchers through the Reading Room by appointment, and a rotating selection appears in the museum’s biennial loan exhibitions. All accession numbers follow the museum standard adopted in 1962: prefix CMS, gallery code, and acquisition sequence.

The Seven Galleries

in processional order, west to east

I[motif: arch-pointed]

The Lancet Hall

circa AD 1190 – 1310

Twenty-four panels of thirteenth-century French stained glass, arranged in three superimposed registers across the east wall and lit from below by a calibrated daylight rig held at 2,700 K.

Provenance
Long-term loan from the Dioceses of Soissons, restored 2012, returned 2023 for a further fifteen years. Removed from the dismembrance of Saint Jean-aux-Vignes during the 1972 motorway campaign.
Conservation
Lead crown replaced 1989 (Stahler-Lutz, Chartres). Pâte-de-verre fills applied 2014. Annual deflection survey: ceramic loss in pixel 5.8–B6 measured 1.2 mm, within tolerance.
II[motif: arch-pointed]

The Tympanum Court

circa AD 1140 – 1200

A single carved Last-Judgment fragment, limestone, 2.4 m across, depicting the weighing of souls and the mouth of Leviathan. Mounted at original portal height on a steel armature designed by Foster + Pickering.

Provenance
Anima workshop, circa 1184. Recovered from the cobble of a demolished parish church near Reims in 1908, held by the Musée de Picardie until permanent transfer to the Cloister Museum in 1994.
Conservation
Consolidated with ethyl silicate (Wacker OH 100), 2003. No biological growth recorded since installation in climate-controlled court (RH 50 ± 5 %, 18 °C).
III[motif: arch-pointed]

The Scriptorium

circa AD 1230 – 1450

Twelve illuminated leaves displayed under reduced lighting at 50 lux, including a Beatus initial in deep ultramarine and minium from the Abbey of Saint-Avoir (folio 12r), Apocalypse cycle.

Provenance
Mixed: leases here had acquired in the holder’s sale of the Chevin Beatry duplicate, 1968. Eleven psalter fragments returned by the Maxwell family, 1999.
Conservation
Loaves rotated on a 90-day cycle to limit cumulative light exposure (target ≤ 35,000 lux-hours per annum). Mylar interleaving introduced 2022.
IV[motif: arch-pointed]

The Reliquary Chamber

circa AD 950 – 1450

Seventeen reliquaries in champlevé enamel, gilt copper, and rock crystal. The centerpiece is a Limoges chasse, c. 1200, depicting the martyrdom of Saint Thomas Becket.

Provenance
Bequest of Mr. Eleanor Whitcombe, 1974, with subsequent acquisitions from the Sa Beat collection (1995) and the Vatican’s programme of restitution to civilian custodians (2007).
Conservation
Enamel surfaces examined under raking light biennially. The Becket chasse was attested and gold-leaf reintegration on the Vital connection studio, 2018.
V[motif: arch-pointed]

The Capitals Cloister

circa AD 1100 – 1240

Forty-two carved capitals on freestanding limestone columns, arranged in a square plan around a central well. Subjects include foliate, zoomorphic, and historiated programmes.

Provenance
Drawn from the demolished cloister of the Priory of Saint Pierre des Châtillons (Roussel), departed 1791, reassembled through nine separate acquisitions between 1903 and 2019.
Conservation
Salt efflorescences on capitals C-12 and C-13F monitored quarterly. Desalination poultices applied 2017; recurrence rate halved.
VI[motif: arch-pointed]

The Rose Window Study

circa AD 1200 – 1450

A scholarly gallery of six full-scale armature reconstructions of the great rose windows of Chartres, Notre-Dame de Paris, and Reims, prepared by the Atelier Simon-Marq, 1998–2014.

Provenance
Commissioned directly by the museum and the Centre André-Chastel as a pedagogical resource. The armatures are themselves cast in glass.
Conservation
Pigment stability tested annually. The Notre-Dame de Paris armature was photographed at high resolution in 2019 and now serves the post-fire restoration commission.
VII[motif: arch-pointed]

The Mason’s Yard

circa AD 1100 – 1300

An open-air gallery of eighty-eight architectural fragments — ornaments, finials, gargoyles, rosary elements, and one full surviving spire pinnacle — laid out as a working archaeological grid.

Provenance
Assembled from the salvage records of nineteen French and English cathedral fabric offices, 1994 onward. Each piece bears its origin site mark in chalk on the tail.
Conservation
Exposed to ambient weather by curatorial intent; lichen growth recorded as part of the long-term study (Status to Open AC 1995 – present).
