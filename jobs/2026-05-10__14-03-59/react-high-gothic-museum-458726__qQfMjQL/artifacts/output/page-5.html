Plate Tracery and the Geometry of the Quatrefoil

Delivered by Dr. Hélène Marquand, Maître de conférences, Université Saint-Anthème

Drawing on a decade of fieldwork at Chartres, Reims, and the lesser-known chapter house of Saint-Quentin, Dr. Marquand traces the evolution of the quatrefoil from its origins as a pierced circle in plate tracery to its full flowering as the governing geometry of the rose window. The lecture will be illustrated with original-site photography and reconstructed compass diagrams.

A reception of mulled wine and almond cakes will follow in the West Cloister.

For Apprentices & Adepts

Workshops in the medieval crafts, for the young and the not-so-young

A Mason’s Apprenticeship

Saturdays · MMXXIV–MMXXV · ages 8–12

Children take up the chisel and the set-square in the hands-on workshop. They will dress a small block of soft Limestone, learn to read a mason’s mark, and assemble a model portal arch using the same wedge-and-keystone principles that raised the great cathedrals. Each apprentice receives a leather-bound logbook and a numbered mason’s mark of their own.

Six PUPILS · TUITION · MASONS PROVIDED

The Scriptorium Workshop

Adv–Adst · IV Sundays · adult

Under the guidance of conservator Frère Anselm Vandrelin, participants prepare gesso, lay bone-leaf gold on vellum, and burnish to a mirror finish using the agate tooth. The course concludes with the gilding of a personal initial in the manner of a thirteenth-century Beatus page. All materials — including 24-karat leaf — are supplied.

Vellum, pigments, materials provided

The Glaziers’ Hand

Half-time intensive · 22–28 October · ages 16+

A three-day introduction to the medieval glazier’s craft. Students cut coloured glass with a grozing iron, paint trace lines with iron-oxide pigment, and lead a small panel of their own design. The intensive culminates in a kiln-fired modular each participant carries home.
