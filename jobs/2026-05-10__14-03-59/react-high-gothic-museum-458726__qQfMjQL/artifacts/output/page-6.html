About the Museum &
History of the
Cloister

A century and a quarter of stewardship over the sacred arts of the cathedral building age — glass, vellum, stone, and stones gathered in the ruins of the Priory of Saint-Hilaire-des-Aulnes.

[motif: quatrefoil-in-square]

Three Foundations

1897

The Antiquarian’s Vow

Edouard de Saint-Pierre, antiquarian of Reims and student of Viollet-le-Duc’s later disciples, purchased the dissolved grounds of the Carmelite Priory of Saint-Hilaire-des-Aulnes for 64,000 francs. His purpose, recorded in his deed of foundation of 14 October 1897, was the perpetual care of "fragments rescued from the cathedral fires of the Revolution and after."

1923

The Glass Bequest

Upon the antiquarian’s death, his heirs entrusted the collection to a board of trustees. The museum was reorganised around the de Saint-Pierre stained-glass holdings — eighty-one panels of 12th- and 13th-century French work. The first public galleries opened on the feast of Saint Denis, 9 October 1923, with admission set at one franc fifty.

1956–1962

The Rebuilding

After incendiary damage in the war years, the cloister roofs and the lancet hall were rebuilt to designs prepared by the atelier of Eugenia Forrer in the years 1956 through 1962, retaining the original Cistercian foundation lines in poured concrete faced with Caen limestone.

[motif: quatrefoil]

An Account of the Cloister

The Priory of Saint-Hilaire-des-Aulnes stood empty from the suppressions of 1791 until Édouard de Saint-Pierre, then fifty-four years of age and lately retired from his commerce in ecclesiastical antiquities at Reims, took possession of its ruined chapter house and west cloister in the autumn of 1897. He installed his private collection — manuscript leaves, capital fragments, and a set of three rose window medallions attributed to the workshops of Saint-Denis — in the surviving south range, opening one room to scholars by appointment. Following the bequest of 1923 the trust acquired contiguous parcels to the north and east, regularising the precinct to its present 4.6 hectares. The new lancet hall, completed under the Forrer atelier in 1962, runs ninety-three metres along the former ceremonial axis: its twenty-two paired lancets borrow the rose of Bardes Saint-Pierre at the original full height. The Rose Window Study wing, dedicated in May 1959, completes the north-west angle of the cloister and provides climate-controlled vitrines for the medallion fragments and a small chapel for the conservation of pigments and lead came.
