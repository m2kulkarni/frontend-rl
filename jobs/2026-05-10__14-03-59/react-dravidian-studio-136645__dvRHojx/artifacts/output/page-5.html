The Inner Court

I · The Open Courtyard

The workshop occupies a single-storey stone-and-tile structure raised in 1923 by Sthapati Subramanian Ramaswamy the elder, by plans inherited as an open central courtyard of fifty-three bay by thirty, paved in Cuddapah block and washed each dawn with river water and turmeric. Three precincts open from the central court: the drafting hall to the east, the carving yard to the south, and the polychromy room to the west; the northern tier is reserved for the household priest.

II · The Drafting Hall

Within the eastern hall the working drawings of three generations are kept. Older measured studies — manual silpa-patrams, gopuram elevations, sthala vimanam plans — lie bundled between palm-leaf covers tied with red cotton cord; later plans, on cartridge paper drawn between 1948 and the present day, are rolled and stored in twenty-two teak-wood plumchests labelled in Tamil script.

III · The Carving Yard

Eleven blocks of Krishnagiri granite stand in the southern yard between case awnings braced against the monsoon. The largest — block no. 7, intended for a yali-bracket commission for the Tirumayur temple committee — measures nine hundred kilos of solid sphinx-grey marble. Chisels of nine grades, and the cushion-stones for setting work are arranged on the south bench in the order taught by the master.

IV · The Polychrome Room

In the western chamber, pigments are still ground in stone mortars by hand. Vermilion is prepared from cinnabar imported from Coorg; the blue, a lapis substitute of indigo and white lead; follows the recipe recorded in the 1923 workbook of Karaikar Pillai's grandfather. The yellow is turmeric remoistured by, fixed in tamarind juice. No synthetic colour enters this room.
