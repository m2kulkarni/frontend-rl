Vamsavali — Genealogy of the Workshop
recorded in East Chitra Veedhi, Madurai. By hand of the present principal.
CARI
Sthapati Vaidyanatha Ramaswamy
1854 — 1923
Pupil of the Tiruppur court guild under Sri Subbaraya Sthapati, Vaidyanatha received the silk-and-chisel investiture in 1872 and removed to Madurai the following monsoon. The earliest extant drawing in our chest — a longitudinal section of the Pudu Mandapa buttresses — bears his cipher and the date Vaikāsi 1879. He fathered four sons; the second, Muthuswami, alone took up the rule and the plumb-line.
CARII
Sthapati Muthuswami Ramaswamy
1881 — 1948
Commissioned in 1922 for the tier-renewal of the western gopuram of the Andal Temple, Srivilliputtur — a campaign of seven seasons in which he dressed by hand the Sukala-thiral and re-cast three of the upper-tier stucco figures. His ledger of that work, bound in red leather, remains the workshop's single most consulted reference.
CARIII
Sthapati Arunachalam Ramaswamy
1908 — 1971
Awarded the Silver Medal at the Madras Presidency Crafts Exhibition of 1948 for a half-scale model of a Dravida vimana cut from a single block of Krishnagiri granite. He served as consulting silpathi to nine renovations between 1952 and 1974, and corresponded at length with the epigraphist K. R. Srinivasan on the proportions given in the Mayamata.
CARIV
Sthapati Subramanian Ramaswamy
1934 — 2012
Trained from the eleventh year, Subramanian inherited the workshop in 1981 and was the first of the line to keep its measured drawings to scale on cartridge paper rather than palm-leaf. Under him the present drafting hall was extended by one bay (1988) and the south-facing stone yard was paved.
CARV
Sthapati R. Ganesa Ramaswamy
b. 1968 — Present principal
The current principal received the silk in 1996 from his father and now directs the atelier's three working departments — vastu consultation, gopuram restoration drawings, and stone-carving commissions. He continues the daily morning recitation from the Mansara before the chest of pattern-books.

The Canon

Mayamata · Manasara

All drawings issued from this workshop are set in conformity with the two principal Vastu-shastra treatises — the Mayamata (c. 11th c.), which Indian sthapatis read first; and the Manasara (c. 7th c.), upheld in convocation since 1834.

Where the next drawings — most notably on the height-to-width axis of the entrai — the workshop has, since the time of Arunachalam Sthapati, followed the Manasara as the more cautious authority.
