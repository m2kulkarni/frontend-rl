Collection

Seven galleries housing the cathedral arts of northern France, arranged along a single processional axis from Lancet Hall to the open Mason's Yard.

T

he Cloister Museum's permanent collection comprises one thousand four hundred and seventy-two catalogued objects, of which two hundred and sixteen are on continuous public display across the seven galleries described below.

The remaining works are accessible to credentialed researchers through the Reading Room by appointment, and a rotating selection appears in the museum's biennial loan exhibition. All accessions number from 1924.

[motif: quatrefoil]

The Seven Galleries

In processional order, west to east

[motif: arch-pointed]
I

The Lancet Hall

CA. XIII–XV CENT.

Twenty-four panels of thirteenth-century French stained glass, arranged in three superposed registers across the east wall and lit from behind by a calibrated daylight rig held at 2,700 K.

Provenance
Long-term loan from the Diocese of Soissons, contracted 1922; renewed 2014 for a further fifteen years. Removed from the dismantlement of Saint-Jean-des-Vignes during the 1972 restoration campaign.
Conservation
Lead cames replaced 1989 (Atelier Lorin, Chartres); Tikhon film applied 2011. Annual deflection survey; counter-bow at panel III-IIa measured 5.2 mm, within tolerance.
[motif: arch-pointed]
II

The Tympanum Court

CA. XII–XIV CENT.

A single carved Last Judgment fragment, limestone, 2.4 m across, depicting the weighing of souls and the mouth of Leviathan. Mounted at original portal height on a steel armature designed by Foster + Pickering.

Provenance
Animex workshop, circa 1180. Recovered from the rubble of a demolished parish church near Beruz in 1908, held by the Musée de Picardie until permanent transfer to the Cloister Museum in 1994.
Conservation
Consolidated with ethyl silicate (Wacker OH 100), 2003. No biological growth recorded since installation in climate-controlled vault (RH 55 ± 2%, 18 °C).
[motif: arch-pointed]
III

The Scriptorium

CA. XI–XV CENT.

Twelve illuminated leaves displayed under reduced lighting at 50 lux, including a Beatus initial in deep ultramarine and minium from the Abbey of Saint-Sever (folio 218r, Apocalypse cycle).

Provenance
Mixed: bistre fonts had acquired in the bibliothèque sale of the Chenin-Beatty duplicates, 1968. Chenin psalter fragment acquired in MacGowell family sale 2002.
Conservation
Leaves rotated on a 90-day cycle to limit cumulative light exposure (target ≤ 50,000 lux-hours per annum). Mylar interleaving renewed 2022.
[motif: arch-pointed]
IV

The Reliquary Chamber

CA. XII–XVI CENT.

Seventeen reliquaries in champlevé enamel, gilt copper, and rock crystal. The centrepiece is a Limoges chasse, c.1200, depicting the martyrdom of Saint Thomas Becket.

Provenance
Bequest of Mme. Eleanor Whitcombe, 1976, with subsequent acquisitions from the De Bost collection (1992) and the Vatican's programme of restitution to civilian custodians (2007).
Conservation
Enamel surfaces examined under raking light biennially. The Becket chasse underwent gold-leaf reintegration at the V&A conservation studios, 2018.
[motif: arch-pointed]
V

The Capitals Cloister

CA. XII–XIII CENT.

Forty-one carved capitals on freestanding limestone columns, arranged in a square plan around a central well. Subjects include foliate, zoomorphic, and historiated programmes.

Provenance
Drawn from the demolished cloister of the Priory of Saint-Pons-de-Thomières (Hérault), dispersed 1791, reassembled through nine separate acquisitions between 1907 and 2019.
Conservation
Salt efflorescences on capitals XII-XXI and XXII-XXVII monitored quarterly. Desalination poultices applied 2017; recurrence rate halved.
[motif: arch-pointed]
VI

The Rose Window Study

CA. XIII–XV CENT.

A scholarly gallery of six full-scale cartoon reconstructions of the great rose windows of Chartres, Notre-Dame de Paris, and Reims, prepared by the Atelier Simon-Marq, 1998–2014.

Provenance
Commissioned directly by the museum under the Conrad-Saint-Médard II patronage; the cartoons are themselves now considered conservation reference documents.
Conservation
Pigment stability tested annually. The Notre-Dame de Paris cartoon was photographed at high resolution in 2019 and now serves the post-fire restoration commission.
[motif: arch-pointed]
VII

The Mason's Yard

CA. XII–XV CENT.

An open-air gallery of eighty-eight unfinished fragments — moments, finials, gargoyles, tracery elements, and one full surviving spire pinnacle — laid out as a working archaeological grid.

Provenance
Assembled from the salvage records of nineteen French and English cathedral fabric offices, 1894 onward. Each piece bears its original site mark in chalk or red iron.
Conservation
Exposed to ambient weather by curatorial intent; lichen growth recorded as part of the long-term study (born to Open Air 1994–present).
[motif: rose-window-notre-dame]
