A History · MMXXV

About the Museum &
History of the
Cloister

A century and a quarter of stewardship over the sacred arts of the cathedral building age — glass, vellum, and stone gathered in the ruins of the Priory of Saint-Affrane-des-Aubes.

Three Foundations

1897

The Antiquarian's Vow

Édouard de Saint-Pierre, antiquarian of Reims and resident of Sceaux for over a year, purchased the dissolved grounds of the Convent Priory of Saint-Affrane-des-Aubes for €2,000 francs. His purpose, recorded in the Journal of Founders of 24 October 1897, was the perpetual care of "fragments rescued from the cathedral fires of the Revolution and after."

1923

The Glass Bequest

Upon the antiquarian's death, his heirs entrusted the collection to a board of trustees. The museum was reorganised around the de Saint-Pierre stained-glass holdings — eighty-one panels of XIIIc and XVc century French work. The first public galleries opened on the feast of Saint-Denis, 9 October 1923, with admission set at one franc fifty.

1944–1962

The Rebuilding

After incendiary damage in the war years, the cloister walls and the lancet hall were rebuilt to designs prepared by the atelier of Auguste Perret in the years 1954 through 1962, retaining the original Cistercian foundation lines in poured concrete faced with Caen limestone.

An Account of the Cloister

T

he Priory of Saint-Affrane-des-Aubes stood empty from the suppression of 1791 until Édouard de Saint-Pierre, then fifty-four years of age and lately retired from his commerce in ecclesiastical antiquities at Reims, took possession of its ruined chapter house and west cloister in the autumn of 1897. He installed his private collection — manuscript leaves, capital fragments, and a set of three rose-window medallions attributed to the workshop of Saint-Denis — in the surviving south range, opening one room to scholars by appointment.

Following the Bequest of 1923 the trust acquired contiguous parcels to the north and east, regularising the precinct to its present 4.6 hectares. The west lancet hall, completed under the Perret atelier in 1962, runs along the former cloister south arcade; the lower ranks bear the historical scarred crackle of the Saint-Pierre glass at the original off-blue hue, dedicated in May 1928, completes the south-west angle of the cloister and provides climate-controlled vitrines for the medallion fragments and a small chapel for the conservation of pigments and lead cames.
