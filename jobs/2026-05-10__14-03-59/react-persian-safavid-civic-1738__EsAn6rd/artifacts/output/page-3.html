Petitions & Public Audience

ʿARZ-NĀMA  ·  The Right of Approach

On the Lodging of an ʿArīḍa

Procedure and Forms of Address by the Petitioner

By order of His Excellency the Kalāntar of Iṣfahān, every resident of the Naqsh-e Jahān quarter — whether of Bīdābād, Khwāju, Maydān-Mihr, Jūbāra, Dardasht, or the watered districts of Jūlfā — may bring before this audience whatsoever may concern the welfare of his household, the security of his shop, the equity of his measure, or the restoration of just usage of water and well. Audiences are heard upon the Maydānof Chahār Bāgh through the four-thousandth-and-eight petition placed in the brass coffer of the Pasha's audience hall.

Notice — Petitions delivered without the correct seal's chancery's stamp shall be set aside and not read. The stamp is renewed each Friday after the noon prayer.

Days & Hours of Audience

Saturday (Shanba)2nd – 4th hour

Festival days— closed —

Where to Present Yourself

The Bureau of Records occupies the eastern wing of the dīvān-khāna, behind the cypress court of the Naqsh-e Jahān. Enter by the inner gate (dar-i dākhilī) facing the workshops of the gilders, ascend the seven steps of grey marble, and present your stamped paper to the doorkeeper. Your name, quarter, and gabled affiliation will be entered into the day-register (rūznāma) before you are admitted to the rotunda.

Drafting Conventions

  1. Open with the formula Huwa al-walī & Hū is the Helper at the head of the folio.
  2. Name yourself, your father, your quarter, and your guild — in that order.
  3. State the matter plainly, in not more than seven lines of cursive nastʿaliq.
  4. Avoid figurative speech and complaints against named persons of higher rank.
  5. Close with the date in the Hijrī reckoning and your seal, or, failing seal, the impress of your right thumb in lampblack.
  6. Leave a margin of three fingers' breadth at the foot of the folio for the customary endorsement.

Order of Precedence

  1. Where the remedy is certain, petitions are called in the following order, by guild and by quarter, as established by the edict of the late Sayyid 1.
  2. Sayyids and ʿulamāʾ of the Friday Mosque, and elders of the Sufi orders of Iṣfahān.
  3. Guilds of the goldsmiths (zargarān) and the silk merchants of the Qaysariyya bāzār.
  4. Tūyas, masters, and gildam-bāshī of soul workshops, on matters of wages and apprenticeship.
  5. Residents of Jūlfā and Dardasht, in matters of water-rights and the rents of dwellings.
  6. Strangers, merchants of Kabul, in lodging or visa transit matters.
  7. All others; residents of the city in the order of their arrival at the gate.
