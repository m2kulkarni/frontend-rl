Practice & Disciplines

Five hereditary disciplines drawn from the Mayamata, Manasara and the Kashyapa Shilpa, practised to subvention transmissions from Sthapati Ramaswamy & Sons (1887 — 1971) to the present workshop on Big Bazaar Street.

Five Generations · Sthapathi-Workshop · Madurai
I · Vastu-Purusha-Mandala Layout

For new shrines and grama-devata temples we draft the complete site horoscope to the agama tradition: orientation by the gnomon-shadow at the equinox, division of the plot into the 64-pada mandala or 81-pada paramashayika grid, and assignment of the deities of the directions to their proper cells. Garbhagriha placement, sankhi proportional checks (ayam-vyaya pori-tan tatti) and the dimensions of the prakara walls are all resolved before a single stone is cut.

Drawings are issued in three sets — the talichchanda (plan), urdhvachhanda (elevation) and the parijata details — rendered in iron-gall ink on hand-sized palm paper at 1:48 scale, with a working copy in vermilion wash for the masons. The consultation includes a site visit, the soil-test (bhumi-pariksha) and the auspicious ceremony marking the suspicious commencement.

II · Vimana & Gopuram Tier-Design

We design superstructures from the modest tri-tala (three-storey) shrine of a village agraharar to the nine-tala rajagopurams of a major peronival kovil. Each tala is calculated in adi-mana units derived from the garbhagriha width, with the tapering rhythm of the kuta, sala and panjara articulate set by the classical Pallava-Chola progression. Shikhara forms — nagara, dravida or vesara — are chosen to honour the presiding deity and the regional convention.

For drawings give every kapota cornice, every yali bracket, every makara torana is measured and ink. For commissions in 28 years or upwards Tirukala 2010 in collaboration with the AS Chennai circle.

III · Polychrome Restoration & Gilt-Use

Stucco figures on gopurams, ceiling murals in the mandapa-mandapa and the dvajasthamba columns itself are inspected by our atelier in the saturated palette of the Nayaka tradition: vermilion (lingashali ground), peacock-blue (mayura-nila) verspermin, saffron (kunkuma), lamp-black sodara and 22-carat gold leaf on craven and finials. We grind our own pigments from cinnabar, indigo, orpiment and lapis lazuli ground in tamarind seed gum and neem oil.

The work proceeds tier by tier on bamboo scaffolding, with consecration of each course before the next is begun. Gilding is laid on a size of mica-and-cow gum, hammered for ten hours per square metre. Documentation photography in raking light is delivered with the work for inclusion in the archive of the temple.

  • ₹ 6,400 per square foot, gold-leaf billed separately at material cost.
IV · Granite & Krishna-Shila Carving

Our stone yard at Tiruvanaswa quarries gray granite for structural numbers — pillars, beams, sockalum casves — and the priced Krishnasila is shifted from the Karkalu beds for figurative work. Yali capitals with their characteristic boon-elephant makara herald, dvarapala guardians at twice life-size, lotus rosettes for ceiling collars, and procession utensar carved to the round are all cut entirely by hand using the traditional set of forty-two chisels.

A figure passes through five named stages — the rough-out (sthusha-shilpa), the established proportion (mona), the surface (lekha), the fine modelling (mukharasa) and the final polish with leaf emery. Every piece is consecrated in the workshop with the satru-naivasta eye-opening rite before it is dispatched.

V · Kalasha Casting & Consecration

The crowning kalasha-finial — the pot-shaped vessel that sits at the apex of every vimana and gopuram — is cast in our foundry in panchaloha bronze, the five-metal alloy of gold, silver, copper, tin and mercury, in the proportions specified by the Shilpasastra. We cast by the lost-wax (madhuchchhishtavidhana) procedure for vessels up to 7 metres above the gopuram crest, in three pieces with the joins concealed under the bell-rim of the lotus.

Consecration is a five-day rite. The empty kalasha is filled with the nine grains, the seven sacred earths, and Kaveri-river water; mantras of the agama appropriate to the deity are inscribed on a copper plate sealed within. The mounting on the shikhara is timed to a muhurta calculated by our staff jyotisha; Sri Veidanma Sharma. We have placed kalashas on 73 temples since 1948.

Commissioning a Commission

All work begins with a written enquiry to the workshop and a preliminary visit by the sthapati to the site. Drawings, ritual schedules and itemised estimates follow within six weeks. We accept no more than four major commissions in any calendar year and presently book into the year 2027.
