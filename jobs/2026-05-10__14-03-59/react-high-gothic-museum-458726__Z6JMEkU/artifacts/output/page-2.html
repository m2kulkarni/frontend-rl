Galleries &
Permanent Collection

Seven galleries housing the cathedral arts of northern France, arranged along a single processional axis from Lancet Hall to the open Mason's Yard.

The Cloister Museum's permanent collection comprises one thousand four hundred and ninety-two catalogued objects, of which two hundred and sixteen are on continuous public display across the seven galleries described below.

The remaining works are accessible to credentialed researchers through the Reading Room by appointment, and a rotating selection appears in the museum's biennial loan exhibitions. All accession numbers follow the museum standard adopted in 1962: prefix CMS, gallery code, and acquisition sequence.

The Seven Galleries

In processional order, west to east

[motif: arch-pointed]I

The Lancet Hall

C. 1190 – C. 1230

Twenty-four panels of thirteenth-century French stained glass, arranged in three superimposed registers across the east wall and lit from behind by a calibrated daylight rig held at 2,700 K.

Provenance

Long-term loan from the Diocese of Soissons, contracted MCMXII; received MCMXIV for a further fifteen years. Restored from the dismemberment of Saint-Jean-des-Vignes during the MCMXIV campaign.

Conservation

Lead cames replaced MMV (Atelier Lorin, Chartres). Vidimus film applied MXIV. Annual deflectivity-survey; counter-lens at panel LIV-XII measured 0.5 mm, within tolerance.

[motif: arch-pointed]II

The Tympanum Court

C. 1145 – C. 1240

A single carved Last Judgement fragment, limestone, 2.4 m across, depicting the weighing of souls and the mouth of Leviathan. Mounted at original portal height on a steel armature designed by Foster + Pickering.

Provenance

Atelier workshop, circa 1160. Recovered from the rubble of a demolished parish church near Reims in 1908, held by the Musée de Picardie and permanent transfer to the Cloister Museum in 1944.

Conservation

Consolidated with ethyl silicate (Wacker OH 100) MMVI. Re-biological growth recorded since installation in climate-controlled vault (RH 50 ± 3%, 18 °C).

[motif: arch-pointed]III

The Scriptorium

C. XII–XV CC.

Twelve illuminated leaves displayed under reduced lighting at 50 lux, including a Beatus initial in deep ultramarine and minium from the Abbey of Saint-Sever (Folio CXII, Apocalypse cycle).

Provenance

Mixed: leaves loan-pool acquired at the Atelier's estate of the Chenu-Brusly duplicates, 1968. Chenu-poolse Engravers donated for the Beauvais bench, 1985.

Conservation

Leaves rotated on a 90-day cycle to limit cumulative light exposure (target 4 000.000 lux-hour per annum). Mylar interleaving renewed 2022.

[motif: arch-pointed]IV

The Reliquary Chamber

C. XII–XV CC.

Seventeen reliquaries in champlevé enamel, gilt-copper, and rock crystal. The centrepiece is a Limoges chasse, c. 1200, depicting the martyrdom of Saint Thomas Becket.

Provenance

Bequest of Mme Eleanor Whitcombe, 1974, with subsequent acquisitions from the De Boer collection (1991) and the Vatican's programme of restitution to civilian custodians (2017).

Conservation

Enamel surfaces examined under raking light biennially. The Becket chasse underwent gold-leaf reintegration on the chevet plaque in 2017 (atelier Goudji).

[motif: arch-pointed]V

The Capitals Cloister

C. XII–XIII CC.

Forty-one carved capitals on freestanding limestone columns, arranged in a square plan around a central well. Subjects include foliate, zoomorphic, and historiated programmes.

Provenance

Drawn from the demolished cloister of the Priory of Saint-Yrieix-de-Theumère (Hérault), dispersed 1791, reassembled through nine separate acquisitions between 1903 and 2019.

Conservation

Salt efflorescences on capitals LV-VIII and XV-XII monitored quarterly. Desalination poultices applied 2021; recurrence rate halved.

[motif: arch-pointed]VI

The Rose Window Study

C. XIII CC.

A scholarly gallery of six full-scale cartoon reconstructions of the great rose windows of Chartres, Notre-Dame de Paris, and Reims, prepared by the Atelier Simon-Marq, 1996–2004.

Provenance

Commissioned directly by the museum under Charter under the patronage of M. Vandoel as a pedagogical resource. The cartoons are themselves now considered conservation reference documents.

Conservation

Pigment stability tested annually. The Notre-Dame de Paris cartoon was photographed at high resolution in 2009 and now serves the post-fire restoration commission.

[motif: arch-pointed]VII

The Mason's Yard

C. XII–XX CC.

An open-air gallery of eighty-eight architectural fragments — voussoirs, finials, gargoyles, rosary elements, and one full-surviving spire pinnacle — laid out on a working archaeological grid.

Provenance

Assembled from the salvage records of nineteen French and English cathedral fabric offices, 19th onward. Each piece bears its origin-site mark in chalk on the underside.

Conservation

Exposed to ambient weather by curatorial intent. Lichen growth recorded as part of the long-term study (Sister 39 (1994 – present).
