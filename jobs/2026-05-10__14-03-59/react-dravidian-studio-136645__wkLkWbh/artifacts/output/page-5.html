Sthapati Ramaswamy Workshop

The Drafting Hall
& Stone Yard

No. 14, East Chitra Veedhi, Madurai — a 1955 stone-and-tile vihara of working artisans, opened daily save the new moon.

The Inner Court

I · The Open Courtyard

The workshop occupies a single-storey stone-and-tile structure raised in 1955 by Sthapati Ramaswamy the elder. Its plan ordered around an open central courtyard fifty-two feet by thirty, paved in Cuddapah black and washed each dawn with river water and turmeric. Three precincts open from the four cardinal sides: the drafting hall to the east, the carving yard to the south, and the polychromy room to the west; the northern wing is reserved for the household shrine.

II · The Drafting Hall

Within the eastern hall the working drawings of three generations are kept. Older measured studies — manai sūl proportions, gopuram elevations, śīlpa-vinyasam plates — lie bundled between palm-leaf covers tied with red cotton cord; later sheets, on cartridge paper drawn between 1968 and the present day, are rolled and stored in twenty-two muhamuri pigeonholes labelled in Tamil script.

III · The Carving Yard

Eleven blocks of Krishnagiri granite stand in the southern yard beneath cane awnings braced against the monsoon. The largest — block no. 7, intended for a yali-bracket commission for the Tirumayur temple committee — measures four feet eight inches at its base. Hammers, chisels of nine gauges, and the cushion-stones for setting work are arranged on the south bench in the order taught by the master.

IV · The Polychrom Room

In the western chamber, pigments are still ground in stone mortars by hand. Vermilion is prepared from cinnabar imported from Coorg; the blue, a lapis substitute of indigo and white lead, follows the recipe recorded in the 1922 workbook of Karuna Pillai's grandfather. The yellow is turmeric-mordanted lac, fixed in tamarind gum. No synthetic colour enters this room.
