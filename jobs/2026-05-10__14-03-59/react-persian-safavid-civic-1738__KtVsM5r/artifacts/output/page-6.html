Petitioners, scribes, and curious travellers are received at the western arcade of the Maydān-i Naqsh-e Jahān.

Notes for Those Who Come

  1. On the manner of dress

    The chamarchi is a place of public record. Visitors of all stations are asked to come modestly attired — long sleeves, covered head for women in the inner hall, and clean footwear left at the second threshold. Robes of state are not required.

  2. For the elderly & infirm

    A shaded resting bench of carved walnut stands in the inner courtyard, beside the cypress. Petitioners who cannot stand for the queue may signal the doorkeeper, who will summon a clerk to attend them where they sit.

  3. Public scribes

    Directly opposite our entrance, beneath the eastern arcade, sit the registered public scribes (kātibān-i ʿumūmī). They draft petitions, certify translations, and read documents aloud for a head fee posted on their boards.

  4. The afternoon recess

    At the noon call for ẓuhr the gates close and the porter Ḥasan ʿAlī carries his keys to the Maydān. Matters arriving late will be received the following morning; no exception is made save for the Mayor’s own warrant.

Approaching the Bureau

Enter the Maydān from its southern arch and keep the great pool to your right. The western arcade runs the length of the square; our doors are the third you will pass after the Qaysariyya Gate, marked by twin cobalt tiles bearing the city’s cypress device. Should you lose your bearing, any of the kabāb-merchants under the arcade will point you to the Dīvān-khāna without charge — it is a kindness customary to the quarter.

Travellers arriving from the caravanserai may find it shorter to approach by the Chahār Bāgh and turn east at the Madrasa of the Mother of the Shāh; the walk takes roughly the time of two-thirds of an azan. Mounts and pack-animals are tethered at the public picket-line on the square’s northern flank, never within the arcade itself.
