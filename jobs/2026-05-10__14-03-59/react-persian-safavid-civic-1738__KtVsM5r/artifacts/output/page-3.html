On the Lodging of an ʿArīḍa

The proper procedure as observed by the customs of the Dīvān

y order of His Excellency the Kalāntar of Iṣfahān, every resident of the Naqsh-e Jahān quarters — whether of Bāzār-i Buzurg, Maydān-i Shāh, Jūbāra, Dardasht, or the Armenian suburb of Julfā — may bring forward a written petition before the city secretariat. ʿArīḍas is held to the columned arcade-tower of the Bureau on the morning of Saturday and Tuesday, between the second and fifth hours after sunrise. Petitioners shall present themselves with two witnesses of good standing and with a sheet of stamped chancery paper (kāghaz-e mohrshudah) drawn that same week from the office of the Muḥtasib at Maydān.

Note: Petitions delivered without the written seal’s chancery stamp shall be set aside and not read. The stamp is renewed each Friday after the noon prayer.

Days & Hours of Audience

The columned rotunda is opened by the porter (darbān) at the call of the second hour. Petitioners are received in the order of their arrival, modified only by the precedence named below.

Saturday (Shanbe)2nd — 5th hour
Tuesday (Sih-shanbe)2nd — 5th hour
Friday— closed —
Festival days— closed —

Where to Present Yourself

The Bureau of Records occupies the eastern wing of the dīvān-khāna, behind the cypress court of the Naqsh-e Jahān. Enter by the inner gate (jubbah-darb), traverse the rendering of the pillars, mount the staircase of grey marble, and on the household courtyard turn to the doorkeeper. Your name, quarter, and guild affiliation will be entered into the day- register (rūznāma) before you are admitted to the rotunda.

Drafting Conventions

Petitions are read down to the secretariat; their form is therefore prescribed. The sworn scribes of the secretariat will compose on your behalf if requested:

  1. Open with the formula Bāsmaʿa al-uṣūl ki bi-Hujjat-i at the head of the folio.
  2. Name yourself, your father, your quarter, and your guild — in that order.
  3. State the matter plainly, in not more than seven lines of cursive nastaʿlīq.
  4. Avoid figurative speech and complaint against named persons of higher rank.
  5. Close with the date in the Hijrī reckoning and your seal, or, lacking a seal, the imprint of your right thumb in lampblack.
  6. Leave a margin of three fingers' breadth at the foot of the folio for the secretariat’s endorsement.

Order of Precedence

When the rotunda is crowded, petitioners are called in the following order, by guild and by quarter, as established by old custom of the city:

  1. Sayyids and ʿulamāʾ of the Friday Mosque, and elders of the half-orders of Iṣfahān.
  2. Guild of the goldsmiths (zargarān) and the silk-merchants of the Qaysariyya bāzār.
  3. Tilers, masons, and gilders of the royal workshops, on matters of wages and apprenticeship.
  4. Residents of Jūbāra and Dardasht, in matters of water-rights and the quanāt division.
  5. Armenians, both Christians of Julfā in their kalāntar, in commercial-and-inheritance affairs.
  6. All other residents of the city in the order of their arrival at the gate.
