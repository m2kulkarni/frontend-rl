Tome IV · Folio & Public Audience

Petitions & Public
Audience

Chapter I — Mode of arīḍa, an order, an enquiry

On the Lodging of an Arīḍa

Procedure laid out for the inhabitants of the Royal City

[motif: arabesque-filet-divider]

Apetition of His Excellency the Kalāntar of Iṣfahān, every resident of the Naqsh-e Jahān quarters — whether of Bāzār-i Buzurg, Maydān-i Shāh, Jūbāra, Dardasht, or the Armenian suburb of Julfā — may bring forward a written petition before the city secretariat. Audience is held in the columned arcade (ṭalār) of the Bureau on the mornings of Saturday and Tuesday, between the second and fifth-hours after sunrise. Petitioners shall present themselves with one witness of good standing and with a sheet of stamped chancery-paper (kāghaz-i muhrdār) drawn that same week from the office of the Munshi al-Mamālik.

Notes — Petitions delivered without the written seal’s chancery stamp shall be set aside and not read. The seal is renewed each Friday after the noon prayer.

Days & Hours of Audience

The columned ṭalār is opened by the porter (kalīddār) at the call of the second loud. Petitioners are received in the order of their arrival, modified only by the precedence noted below.

Saturday (Shanbe)2nd–4th hour
Tuesday (Sih-shanbe)2nd–4th hour
Friday— closed —
Festival days— closed —

Where to Present Yourself

The Bureau of Records occupies the centre wing of the dīvān-khāna, behind the cypress court of the Naqsh-e Jahān. Enter by the laser gate (dar-i Khwāja) by the workshop of the gilders, ascend the seven steps of grey marble, and present your stamped paper to the doorkeeper. Your name, quarter, and guild affiliation will be entered into the day-register (rūznāma) ere you are admitted to the verandah.

Drafting Conventions

Petitions are read aloud in the secretariat, their form is therefore prescribed. The seven scribes of the munshi-khāna will compose on your behalf if requested.

  1. Open with the formula Bism Allāh al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm, then in the head of the folio.
  2. Name yourself, your father, your quarter, and your guild — in that order.
  3. State the matter plainly, in not more than seven lines of cursive nastaʿlīq.
  4. Avoid figurative speech and complaint against named persons of higher rank.
  5. Close with the date in the Hijrī reckoning and your seal, or, lacking a seal, the impress of your right thumb in lampblack.
  6. Leave a margin of three fingers’ breadth at the foot of the folio for the secretariat’s endorsement.

Order of Precedence

When the matter is crowded, petitioners are called in the following order, by guild and by quarter, as established by the old custom of the city:

  1. Sayyids and ʿulamā of the Friday Mosque, and elders of the four orders of Sufism.
  2. Guild of the qubbahbāfān (saqqāza) and the silk merchants of the Qaysariyya bāzār.
  3. Tilers, masons, and gilders of the royal workshops, in matters of wages and apprenticeship.
  4. Residents of Jūbāra and Dardasht, in matters of water-rights and the qanāt division.
  5. Armenians from Sufflej of Julfā, by their kalāntar, in commercial and inheritance affairs.
  6. All other residents of the city in the order of their arrival at the gate.
