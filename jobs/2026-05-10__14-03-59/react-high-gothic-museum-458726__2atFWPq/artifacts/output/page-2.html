Galleries &
Permanent
Collection

Seven galleries housing the cathedral arts of northern France, arranged along a single processional axis from Lancet Hall to the open Mason's Yard.

The Cloister Museum's permanent collection comprises one thousand four hundred and seventy-two catalogued objects, of which two hundred and eleven are on continuous public display across the seven galleries described below. The remaining objects are accessible to credentialled researchers through the Reading Room by appointment, or by writing to the museum's bursarial office. All accessions transferred from the museum's bureau before 1962 prefix CMS, gallery code, and acquisition sequence.

The Lancet Hall opened in 1898 and was followed by the Tympanum Court (1903), the Scriptorium (1911), and the Reliquary Chamber (1924). The four newer galleries — the Capitals Cloister, the Rose Window Study, the Mason's Yard, and the present North Wing — were added between 1962 and 2024 in a programme of measured expansion described in our publications. Each gallery is arranged so that the visitor passes from glass to stone to manuscript and back again, a circuit which the founding curator, Édouard de Saint-Pierre, called "le pèlerinage minéral".

The Seven Galleries

In processional order, west to east

I
[motif: arch-pointed]

The Lancet Hall

CMS I · 1872 – 2014

Twenty-four panels of thirteenth-century French stained glass, arranged in three superposed registers across the east wall and lit from behind by a calibrated daylight rig held at 2,700 K.

Provenance
Loan-trust from the Diocese of Soissons, contracted MCMI; renewed MCMII for a further fifteen years. Removed from the dembolitions of Saint-Jean-des-Vignes during the MCM restoration campaign.
Conservation
Lead caries replaced MMV (Atelier Loire, Chartres). Vidimus film applied 2012. Annual deflection survey; counter-bow at panel B-3IV measured ±2 mm; within tolerance.
II
[motif: arch-pointed]

The Tympanum Court

CMS II · 1872 – 2014

A single carved Last Judgment fragment, limestone, 2.4 m across, depicting the weighing of souls and the mouth of Leviathan. Mounted at original portal height on a steel armature designed by Forme + Pickering.

Provenance
Anima workshop, circa 1180. Recovered from the rubble of a demolished parish church near Reims in 1898, held by the Musée de Picardie until permanent transfer to the Cloister Museum in 1944.
Conservation
Consolidated with ethyl silicate (Wacker OH 100), 2003. No biological growth recorded since installation in climate-controlled court (RH 50 ± 3%, 18 °C).
III
[motif: arch-pointed]

The Scriptorium

CMS III · 1872 – 2014

Twelve illuminated leaves displayed under reduced lighting at 50 lux, including a Beatus initial in deep ultramarine and minium from the Abbey of Saint Sever (folio 138r, Apocalypse cycle).

Provenance
Mixed: leaves loan-loaned at the lender's wish to the Cluny family duplicate, 1968. Eleven psalter Eugénie donated by the family; folio 138r private deposit.
Conservation
Leaves rotated on a 90-day cycle to limit cumulative light exposure (target ≤ 50,000 lux-hours per annum). Mylar interleaving renewed 2022.
IV
[motif: arch-pointed]

The Reliquary Chamber

CMS IV · 1872 – 2014

Seventeen reliquaries in champlevé enamel, gilt copper, and rock crystal. The centerpiece is a Limoges chasse, c. 1190, depicting the martyrdom of Saint Thomas Becket.

Provenance
Bequest of Mrs Eleanor Whitcombe, 1976, with subsequent acquisitions from the Sir Ronan Collection (1992) and the Vatican's programme of restitution to civilian custodians (2007).
Conservation
Enamel surfaces examined under raking light biennially; the Becket chasse underwent gold-leaf reintegration in the 1948 restoration studios, 1996.
V
[motif: arch-pointed]

The Capitals Cloister

CMS V · 1872 – 2014

Forty-one carved capitals on freestanding limestone columns, arranged in a square plan around a central well. Subjects include foliate, monstrous, and historiated programmes.

Provenance
Drawn from the demolished cloister of the Priory of Saint-Jean-de-Thorières (Hérault), dispersed 1791, reassembled through nine separate acquisitions between 1903 and 2019.
Conservation
Salt efflorescence on capitals 23-24 and 31-32V monitored quarterly; desalination poultices applied 2017; recurrence rate halved.
VI
[motif: arch-pointed]

The Rose Window Study

CMS VI · 1872 – 2014

A scholarly gallery of six full-scale cartoon reconstructions of the great rose windows of Chartres, Notre-Dame de Paris, and Reims, prepared by the Atelier Simon-Marq, 1998–2014.

Provenance
Commissioned directly by the museum under the Cordon Auriol Charitable Trust in a pedagogical reverse. The cartoons are themselves 1:1 considered cardboard conservation reference documents.
Conservation
Pigment stability tested annually. The Notre-Dame de Paris cartoon was photographed at high resolution in 2009 and now serves the post-fire restoration researchers.
VII
[motif: arch-pointed]

The Mason's Yard

CMS VII · 1872 – 2014

An open-air gallery of eighty-eight architectural fragments — moments, finials, gargoyles, rosary elements, and one full surviving spire pinnacle — laid out as a working archaeological grid.

Provenance
Assembled from the salvage records of nineteen French and English cathedral fabric offices, 1846 onward. Each piece bears its original site-mark in chalk or paint.
Conservation
Exposed to ambient weather by curatorial intent; lichen growth recorded as part of the long-term study borne in (open-air 1996–present).
