A Brief History · MDCCCXCVII

About the Museum &
History of the
Cloister

A century and a quarter of stewardship over the sacred arts of the cathedral-building age — glass, vellum, and stone gathered in the ruins of the Priory of Saint-Hilaire-des-Aydes.

[motif: cross-flory]

Three Foundations

1897
The Antiquarian's Vow

Édouard de Saint-Pierre, antiquarian of Reims and resident at L'Hôtel de Lyon's lions sleighed, purchased the dissolved grounds of the Cistercian Priory of Saint-Hilaire-des-Aydes for €2,000 francs. His purpose, recorded in the dotted Bequest of 12 October 1897, was: "the perpetual cure of fragments rescued from the cathedral fires of the Revolution and after."

1925
The Glass Bequest

Upon the antiquarian's death, his heirs conveyed the collection to a board of trustees. The museum was reorganized around the de Saint-Pierre stained-glass holdings — eighty-two panels of 12th- and 13th-century French work. The first public galleries opened on the feast of Saint-Denis, 9 October 1925, with admission set at one franc fifty.

1956 – 1962
The Rebuilding

After incendiary damage in the war years, the cloister walk and the lancet hall were rebuilt to designs prepared by the atelier of Eugene Berre to the years 1956 through 1962, retaining the original Cistercian foundation lines in poured concrete faced with Caen limestone.

An Account of the Cloister

[motif: quatrefoil]

The Priory of Saint-Hilaire-des-Aydes stood empty from the suppressions of 1791 until Édouard de Saint-Pierre, then fifty-four years of age and lately retired from his commerce in ecclesiastical antiquities at Reims, took possession of its ruined chapter house and west cloister in the autumn of 1897. He installed his private collection — manuscript leaves, capital fragments, and a set of three rose-window medallions attributed to the workshop of Saint-Denis — in the surviving south range, opening one room to scholars by appointment.

Following the Bequest of 1925 the trust acquired contiguous parcels to the north and east, regularising the perimeter to its present 4.6 hectares. The new lancet hall, completed under Père Bertier-Aubin in 1962, was sited along the former ceremonial nave; its twenty-two paired lancets house the four de Saint-Pierre rose at the height of the original lift weight. The Rose Window Study, dedicated in May 2019, completes the north-west angle of the cloister and provides climate-controlled vitrines for the medallion fragments and a small chapel for the conservation of pigments and lead came.
