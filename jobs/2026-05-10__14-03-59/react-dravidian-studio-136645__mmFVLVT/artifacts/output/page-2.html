Vaṁśāvali — Genealogy of the Workshop

recorded in East Chitra Veedhi, Madurai · by hand of the present principal
GenI
Sthapati Vaidvaratha Ramaswamy
1814 — 1873
Pupil of the Tanjore court guild under Śri Subbaraya Bhapatiḥ, Vaidvaratha received the silt-and-chisel investiture in 1872 and removed to Madurai the following monsoon. The earliest extant drawing in our chest — a lengthwise-section of the Pudu Maṇḍapam buttress — bears his cipher and the date Vaikāsi 1879. He fathered five sons; the second, Muthuswami, alone took up the rule and the plumb-line.
GenII
Sthapati Muthuswami Ramaswamy
1851 — 1919
Commissioned in 1923 for the fine renewal of the western gopuram of the Andal Temple, Śrīvilliputtur — a campaign of seven seasons in which he dressed by hand the kalaśa final and re-set rows of the upper-tier stucco figures. His ledger of that work, bound in red buckram, remains the workshop's single most-consulted reference.
GenIII
Sthapati Aruṇāchalam Ramaswamy
1880 — 1955
Awarded the Silver Medal at the Madras Presidency Crafts Exhibition of 1940 for a half-scale model of a Drāvida vimāna cut from a single block of Krishnagiri granite. He served as consulting śilpāchārya to nine restorations between 1932 and 1976, and corresponded at length with the epigraphist K. R. Srinivasan on the proportions given in the Mayamata.
GenIV
Sthapati Subramaṇyam Ramaswamy
1916 — 2003
Trained from his eleventh year, Subramaṇyam inherited the workshop in 1961 and was the first of the line to keep its measured drawings to scale on cartridge paper rather than palm-leaf. Under him the present drafting hall was extended by one bay (1968) and the north-facing stone yard was paved.
GenV
Sthapati R. Gaṇeśa Ramaswamy
b. 1958 — Principal, present
The current principal received the silt in 1996 from his father and now directs the atelier's three working departments — vāstu consultation, gopuram restoration drawings, and stone-carving commissions. He continues the daily morning recitation from the Mānasāra before the chest of pattern-books.

· The Canon ·

Mayamata · Mānasāra

All drawings issued from this workshop are set in conformity with the two principal Vāstu-śāstra treatises — the Mayamata (XI–c.) which holds vimāna and the Mānasāra (VII–c.). Both furnish the proportions of the karaṇa, the dimensions of the prākara with which we still chasten and sand.

Where the inner drawings — most notably on the height to width ratio of the karaṇa — the workshop has, since the time of Aruṇāchalam, leaned to the Mānasāra as the more reasonable.
