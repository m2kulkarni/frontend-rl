A fifth-generation atelier of śilpaśāstra on East Chitra Veedhi, Madurai — founded by Sthapati Vaidvaratha Ramaswamy in 1887 within sight of the Meenakshi Amman gopuram. We draw, consult, and carve in the Drāvida tradition: tools for new mandapas, polychromy for restored gopurams, and granite figuration cut by hand.

The Threefold Practice

From the drafting hall on East Chitra Veedhi the workshop attends to three disciplines, each governed by the Mānasāra and the Mānasāra-śilpa-śāstra, each prized by hand-day and carried in Krishnagiri black granite or Sadarahalli grey.

I · Vāstu Consecration
New Mandapas
Site orientation, axial calculation, and full ground-plan drawings for kalyāṇa mandapas, navagraha shrines, and village āraṇya temples. Each commission begins with a three-day visit to read the soil, the water-table, and the cardinal aṣṭas.

Drawings are inked on cotton paper at 1:48 and accompanied by a written vāstu-mañca properties table.
From₹ 1,40,000
II · Gopuram Restoration
Polychromy & Stucco
Survey, condition report, and full-scale colour cartoons for the kuda, śāla, and stucco figures of storied gopurams. We work in lime and jaggery render and the twelve mineral pigments — vermilion, peacock, orpiment, ultramarine — fixed with archival sizing.

Scaffold engineering and bell-metal kalaśa replacement are carried out with allied workshops in Swāmimalai.
From₹ 4,40,000
III · Granite Carving
Figurative Commissions
Hand-carved mūlavaras and utsava figures, pillared yāli-brackets, and door-jamb dvārapālakas, cut to tāḷamāna proportions and to the canon of the silt for the Mauttilai. We accept commissions for master-craftsman K. Murugesan and his fine apprentices.

Standard size for a seated Ganeśa in Krishnagiri black is thirty-six aṅguḷa, delivery to fourteen months from the day of stone selection.
From₹ 8,25,000

Eleven-tier polychromy renewal for the western Rajagopuram of the Aruṇāchaleśwara temple — 1,890 stucco figures inventoried, pigments mixed on site in lime and jaggery, scaffold raised through the monsoon shadow.

A new shoḍaśa-stambha mandapa beside the Saraṅgapāṇi tank, in Sadarahalli grey granite. Sixteen yāli-bracketed pillars, a central lotus-ceiling medallion, and four corner dvārapālakas drawn from our 1933 pattern-book.
