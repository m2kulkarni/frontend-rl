Register IV · Of the Bureau

[motif: arch-ogee]

The Bureau & Its Officers

Established by royal firmān in the year 1006 of the Hijra, the Dīvān-i Shahr keeps the tally of the residences of Iṣfahān, the seven quarters of the Naqsh-e Jahān, the measures of the markets, and the courtesy of the canals.

Bunyād · Founding

Of the Founding under Shāh ʿAbbās

In the year 1006 A.H. — being the year of the Maintenance of the royal seat from Qazvīn to Iṣfahān — His Majesty Shāh ʿAbbās, called the Great, did command that a Bureau be raised within the new city, fronting the maydān of Naqsh-e Jahān, that no measure should be false in the bazaars, no canal stopped against its neighbour, and no household unrecorded upon the rolls. The seal was struck in cobalt and gold and pressed upon the founding ledger by the hand of the wazīr Iʿtimād Beg.

From that day forward the Dīvān has kept its watch from the chamber beside the Qaysariyya gate, its officers four in number, its scribes nine, its couriers six, and its lamps trimmed each evening by the porter Ḥasan ʿAlī, son of Murād, in the manner appointed.

The Present Roster of Officers

As they are seated in the chamber, in the year 1108 of the Hijra

[motif: arabesque-filet-divider]

KALĀNTAR · MAYOR

Mīrzā Ḥusayn Khān

OF THE CITY

[motif: arabesque-filet-divider]

Lord Mayor of the City and chief warden of the Bureau. He sits in council each Saturday after the dawn prayer, and receives petitioners between the second and fourth hours.

MUNSHĪ · CHIEF SECRETARY

Āqā Ṣādiq Tabrīzī

TO THE DĪVĀN

[motif: arabesque-filet-divider]

Keeper of the past weight and the rose press. He walks the bāzār daily, the pad of his neck a band of three. The kalām of the writs and the silver lacquer is on his desk.

NĀẒIR · MASTER OF WATERS

Ūstād Ibrāhīm of Yazd

[motif: arabesque-filet-divider]

Apportioner of the Zāyandeh-rūd among the seven mādī-canals. He keeps the schedule of the rains-water (āb-i bārān) and oversees the repair of the bridges of Allāhverdī Khān and Khājū.

[motif: islamic-star]

MUḤTASIB · INSPECTOR

OF MARKETS & MORALS

Mīr Muḥammad-Bāqir

Chief of the writing-chamber, keeper of the great register and the seal of wax. Under his hand pass all decrees of the Bureau; nine scribes copy fair under his watch.

Maxim · The Charge

The Threefold Duty of the Bureau

The keeping of true records — that no name be lost, no birth uncounted, no deed unattested.

The just measure of bread — that the loaf weigh full, the price be just, and the poor not be wronged at the oven.

The proper repair of canals and bridges — that water reach every garden in its turn, and the spans of the river stand firm against flood.

Sealed at Iṣfahān, in the chamber beside the Qaysariyya gate · On the Register of Quarters · Volume A · Numbering II.
