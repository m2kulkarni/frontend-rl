The Cobalt of Chartres

Lectern of the Master Workshops · 1170 – 1217

Forty-two panels and roundels, including those known to have survived after the fire of 1836, set against the workshop's signature deep cobalt — the pigment whose recipe was, for a generation, kept under oath.

Ribs & Buttresses

Drawings of the Master Masons of Reims

Thirty-one parchment elevations and a vast full-scale tracing-floor fragment, drawn from the lectern of Antoine Vinciot, master mason. A flying buttress that was never built.

Exhibition · III

Folios from the Très Riches Heures

An East from the Book Closet · 1410-1485

Six original folios of the Limbourg brothers' calendar cycle, displayed for ninety days only under controlled lux. The April folio — the betrothal in the green orchard — overlooks any of its companions: an extraordinary illumination painted in 1414.

A Word from the Director

Mlle Geneviève d'Aspremont · Directrice, The Cloister Museum

It is my joy to welcome you, in this hour-month of Michaelmas, MMXIV, to the opening of our north wing — a chamber long imagined and at last built, whose seven lancets face the same compass-point as those at Reims and Chartres, that the morning light might pour first through coloured glass before it touches any visitor's eye.

We are a small institution, and an unfashionable one. We collect cathedrals in fragments — a medallion, a quatrefoil, a single foot of cobalt sky — and we ask of our visitors only what the cathedrals themselves once asked: patience, attention, and the willingness to look upward.

The new wing was made possible by a bequest from the Confraternity of Saint Bernard and by twelve years of stonework by the apprentices of the Compagnons du Devoir. To them, and to you, our thanks.

— Geneviève d'Aspremont
