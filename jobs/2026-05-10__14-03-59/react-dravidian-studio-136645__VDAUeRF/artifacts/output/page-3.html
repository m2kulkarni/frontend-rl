Practice & Disciplines

Five hereditary disciplines drawn from the Manasara, Manasara and the Kashyapa Shilpa, practised in unbroken transmission from Sthapati Ramaswamy Achari (1854 — 1923) to the present workshop on Big Bazaar Street.

I. Vastu-Purusha-Mandala Layout

For new shrines and grama-devata temples we draft the complete site horoscope to the vastu mandala — orientation by the gnomon shadow at the equinox, division of the plot into the 64-pada mandala or 81-pada paramashayika grid, and assignment of the deities of the directions to their proper cells. Garbhagriha placement, ayadi proportional checks (ayam-vyayam-yoni-vara-titi) and the dimensions of the prakara walls are all resolved before a single stone is cut.

Drawings are issued in three sets — the talachhanda (plan), urdhvachhanda (elevation) and the punjara details — rendered in iron-gall ink on hand-sized paper at 1:48 scale, with a working copy in vermilion-wash for the masons. The consultation includes a site visit, the soil test (bhumi-pariksha) and the ashvarudana ceremony marking the auspicious commencement.

II. Vimana & Gopuram Tier-Design

We design superstructures from the modest tri-tala (three-storey) shrine of a village agraman to the nine-tala rajagopuram of a major personal kovil. Each tala is calculated in adi-mana units derived from the garbhagriha width, with the tapering rhythm of the kuta, sala and panjara salisikas set by the classical Pallavar-Chola progression. Shikhara forms — sugata, dravida or vesara — are chosen to honour the presiding deity and the regional convention.

Our drawings give every kapota cornice, every yali bracket, every makara torana its measured detail. For the more ambitious gopuram, the workshop maintains in the modeling shop a small block of Krishnagiri granite. He served as consulting sthapati to nine renovations between 1952 and 1974, and corresponded at length with the epigraphist M. R. Krishnaswami of the Madras Presidency Crafts Society.

III. Polychrome Restoration & Gold-Leaf

Stucco figures on gopurams, ceiling murals in the vimana mandapa, and the dwarapalaka colours itself are repainted by our atelier in the seasoned palette of the Mayuka tradition: vermilion (kinguli ground), peacock-blue (mayura nila) sweets, saffron (ksoma-pita) drapery, lamp-black outlines and 22-carat gold leaf in cornice and finials. We grind our own pigments from cinnabar, indigo, orpiment and arsenic.

The work proceeds tier by tier on bamboo scaffolding, with consecration of each course before the next is begun. Gilding is laid on a ten-mil glue-and-clay bole burnished with agate; this finish has been documented to last 60 to 80 years in coastal Tamil air, the documentation photography in raking light is delivered within four weeks of completion.

IV. Granite & Krishna-Shila Carving

Our stone yard at Tiruvayyaru quarries grey granite for structural members — pillars, beams, adishana stones — and the prized black Krishna-shila from the Karkala beds for figurative work. Yali capitals with their characteristic lion-elephant-makara fusion, dwarapalakas guardians at twice life-size, lotus rosettes for ceiling coffers, and processional vimana morsels in the round are all cut entirely by hand using the traditional set of forty-two chisels.

A figure passes through five named stages — the rough-out (kebabra-shila), the established proportion (mana), the surface (lekha), the fine modelling (sukshma) and the final polish with leaf emery (vam-pra) — each stage is consecrated to the workshop with the nava-mantras opening before it is dispatched.

V. Kalasha-Casting & Consecration

The crowning kalasha finial — the pot-shaped final tier of the agres of every vimana and gopuram — is cast in our foundry in panchaloha alloy, the five sacred metals of copper, brass, lead, gold and silver in proportions specified by the Shilparatna. We let the cock breed the wax model, sift the clay coupons received and replaced with a beauty bask of an open-bale fire. We will consecrate the model with our ten gold marks set with rishi yantra, jasper, beryl, garnet, sapphire and emerald.

C onsecration is a five-day rite. The image kalasha is filled with the nine grains, the seven sacred earths, and Kaveri river water; mantras of the agama appropriate to the deity are inscribed on a copper plate sealed within. The mounting on the shikhara is timed to a muhurta calculated by our staff jyothisha, Sri Venkatesha Sharma. We have placed kalashas on 75 temples since 1948.
