Vamsavali — Genealogy of the Workshop

recorded in East Chitra Veedhi, Madurai · By hand of the present principal

I
1854 — 1925

Sthapati Vaidvarutha Ramaswamy

Founder · 1854 — 1925

Pupil of the Tanjore court guild under Sri Subbarama Sthapati, Vaidvarutha received the silla and chisel inheritance in 1872 and removed to Madurai the following monsoon. The earliest stone drawing in our chest — a longitudinal section of the Pudu Mandapam buttress — bears his cipher and the date Vaikasi 1879. He fathered four sons; the second, Muthuswami, alone took up the silla and the plumb-line.

II
1879 — 1957

Sthapati Muthuswami Ramaswamy

1879 — 1957

Commissioned in 1923 for the tier-renewal of the western gopuram of the Andal Temple, Sirivilliputhur — a campaign of seven seasons in which he dressed by hand the kalasha-finial and re-set three of the upper-tier stucco figures. His ledger of that work, bound in red buckram, remains the workshop's single most consulted reference.

III
1908 — 1991

Sthapati Arumachalam Ramaswamy

1908 — 1991

Awarded the Silver Medal at the Madras Presidency Crafts Exhibition of 1948 for a half-scale model of a Dravida vimana cut from a single block of Krishnagiri granite. He served as consulting sthapati to nine restorations between 1932 and 1974, and corresponded at length with the epigraphist K. R. Srinivasan on the proportions given in the Mayamata.

IV
1939 — 2011

Sthapati Subramahniah Ramaswamy

1939 — 2011

Trained from his eleventh year, Subramahniah inherited the workshop in 1981 and was the first of the four to insist its measured drawings be issue as cartridge paper rather than palm-leaf. Under him the present drafting hall was extended by one bay (1985) and the north-facing stone yard was paved.

V
b. 1968

Sthapati R. Gahela Ramaswamy

b. 1968 · Principal, present

The current principal received the silla in 1996 from his father and now directs the atelier's three working departments — vastu consultation, gopuram restoration drawings, and stone-carving commissions. He continues the daily morning recitation from the Manasara before the chest of pattern-books.

The Canon

MAYAMATA · MANASARA

All drawings issued from this workshop are set in conformity with the two principal Vastu shastra treatises — the Mayamata's 12-pada nidhi-vinyasa and the Manasara's 7th, 8th, and 9th adhikaras. Where the two diverge — most notably on the height of the suvla over the kut-vyasa — the workshop has, since the time of Arumachalam Sthapati, followed the Manasara as the more conservative authority.
