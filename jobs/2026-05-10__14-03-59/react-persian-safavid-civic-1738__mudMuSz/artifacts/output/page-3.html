Petitions & Public Audience

Chapter III · Mā‘rūḍāt · ‘arā‘iḍ va shikāyāt

[motif: arabesque-filet-divider]

On the Lodging of an ‘Arīḍa

Procedure observed at the chamber-beside-the-gate of the Dīwān

By order of His Excellency the Kalāntar of Iṣfahān, every resident of the Naqsh-e Jahān quarters — whether of Bāzār-i Buzurg, Maydān-i Shāh, Jūbāra, Darbastīn, or the Armenian suburb of Julfā — may bring forward a written petition before the city secretariat. Audiences are held on the cobalt-tessera tables of the Bureau on the morning of Saturday and Tuesday, between the second and fifth hours after sunrise. Petitioners shall present themselves with two witnesses of good standing and with a brace of stamped chancery papers (kāghaz-i mukhtūm) drawn fresh from the office of the Mustaufī al-Mamālik.

Note — Petitions delivered without the correct seal or chancery stamp shall be set aside and not read. The stamp is renewed each Friday after the noon prayer.

Days & Hours of Audience

The cobalt-tessera tables are opened by the great Kalāntar in the call of the second hour. Petitioners are received in the order of their arrival, modified only by the precedence noted below.

Saturday (Shanba)2nd – 5th hour
Tuesday (Sih-shanba)2nd – 5th hour
Friday— closed —
Festival days— closed —

Where to Present Yourself

The Bureau of Records occupies the eastern wing of the Dīwān-khāna, behind the cypress court of the Naqsh-e Jahān. Enter by the lower gate (dar-i kūchak) marked along the windings of the gillaba, ascend the seven steps of grey marble, and present your stamped paper to the doorkeeper. Your name, quarter, and guild affiliation will be entered into the day-register (rūznāma) before you are admitted to the veranda.

Drafting Conventions

Petitions are read from the secretariat in three letters in the matter of the same hand. The letters of the secretariat will compose on your behalf if requested:

  1. Open with the formula Ḥuwa al-mu‘īn fī 'l-ḥasanāt in the hand of the kātib.
  2. Name yourself, your father, your quarter, and your guild — in that order.
  3. State the matter plainly, in not more than seven lines of cursive nasta‘līq.
  4. Avoid figurative speech and complaint against natural powers of higher rank.
  5. Close with the date in the Hijrī reckoning and your seal, or, lacking a seal, the impress of your right thumb in lampblack.
  6. Leave a margin of three fingers' breadth at the foot of the folio for the secretariat's endorsement.

Order of Precedence

When the matters or crowded, petitioners are called in the following order, by guild and by quarter, as established by old custom of the city:

  1. Sayyids and ‘ālamīs of the Friday Mosque, and elders of the bait al-fuqarā.
  2. Guild of the qaṣīrīn-bāshīs (caravan), saqaf, and the merchants of the Qaysariyya bāzār.
  3. Tilers, masters, and qālīcha of the wool workshops, on matters of wages and apprenticeship.
  4. Residents of Jūbāra and Darbastīn, on matters of water-rights and the qanāt division.
  5. Armenians and Christians of Julfā, by their kalāntar, in commercial and inheritance affairs.
  6. All others: residents of the city in the order of their arrival at the gate.

Sealed in the Dīwān-khāna of Iṣfahān, in the cobalt-tessera tribunal, on the morning of an ordinary Saturday.
