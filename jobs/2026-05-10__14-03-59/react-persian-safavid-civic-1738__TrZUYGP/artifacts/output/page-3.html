Register III ✶ ʿArżdāsht u Sōmārē

Petitions & Public Audience

Chapter 3 · Ḥuk-i ḳ · at-Ḳāḍī va Tāḳīr

On the Lodging of an ʿArīḍa

— Procedures observed at the audiences of the Dīvān

[motif: arabesque-filet-divider]

By order of His Excellency the Kalāntar of Iṣfahān, every resident of the Naqsh-e Jahān quarters — whether of Bāzār-i Buzurg, Maydān-i Shāh, Jūbāra, Dardasht, or the Caravanserai-Junubī — may bring forward a written petition before the city secretariat. Audience is held in the columned ārshahr-ī of the Bureau, on the morning of Saturday and Tuesday, between the second and fifth hours after sunrise. Petitioners shall present themselves with two witnesses of good standing and with a sheaf of stamped chancery paper (kāghaz-i muhrshudah) drawn that same week from the office of the Marshal-of- Stamps.

Note — Petitions delivered without the customary chancery stamp shall be set aside and read aloud only. The notepad is reckoned each Friday after the noon prayer.

Where to Present Yourself

The Bureau of Audience occupies the eastern wing of the dīvān-khāna, behind the cypress court of the Naqsh-e Jahān. Enter by the upper gate (dar-i kūshk), abreast of the booksellers, then bear left at the second threshold. Each petitioner is met at the third quarter on the second day above, and a clerk shall direct him to the proper bench.

Drafting Conventions

  1. Open with the formula of bismi'llāh, every member of the audience here held aloft.
  2. Name yourself, the family of yours, your quarter, and your guild — in that order.
  3. Avoid hyperbole and complaint against neighbours of higher rank.
  4. Cite, with the date in the hijrī reckoning and not in the rūmī reckoning, every prior decree.
  5. Leave a margin of three fingers' breadth at the foot of the folio for the pronouncement.

Order of Precedence

  1. Sayyids and aʿyān of the Friday Mosque, and members of the Royal Court.
  2. Kalāntars of the noble guilds (juhūdīn) and members of the Qaysariyya merchants.
  3. Tūʾar, runners, and aṭilla of the rural landings; or in the company of the Mīrzās.
  4. Residents of Jūbāra and Dardasht, in person and reckoned upon time as appointed.
  5. Strangers and friends of public, by their leave; their petitions are read on the four afternoons reserved.
