Vamsavali — Genealogy of the Workshop

recorded in East Chitra Veedhi, Madurai. By hand of the present principal.
EARI1819–1875
Sthapati Vaidyanātha Ramaswamy
1819 — 1875
Pupil of the Tanjore-court guild under Śri Subbiah Ācārya. Vaidyanātha received the ūḷa-and-chisel investiture in 1872 and removed to Madurai the following monsoon. The earliest entries drawing in our chest — a longitudinal section of the Pudu Maṇḍapam buttress — bears his cipher and the date Vaikāsi 1879. He fathered four sons; the second, Muthuswāmi, alone took up the rule and the plumb-line.
EARII1851–1922
Sthapati Muthuswāmi Ramaswamy
1851 — 1922
Commissioned in 1922 for the eleven-tier western gopuram at the Andal Temple, Srivilliputtur — a campaign of seven seasons in which he was dressed by hand the kalaśa finial and re-set three of the upper-tier stucco figures. His ledger of that work, bound in red buckram, remains the workshop's single most-consulted reference.
EARIII1879–1957
Sthapati Arunāchalam Ramaswamy
1879 — 1957
Awarded the Silver Medal at the Madras Presidency Crafts Exhibition of 1948 for a half-scale model of a Drāviḍa vimāna cut from a single block of Krishnagiri granite. He served as consulting śilpaśin to nine renovations between 1932 and 1956, and corresponded at length with the epigraphist K. R. Srinivasan on the proportion given in the Mayamata.
EARIV1907–1981
Sthapati Subramaniam Ramaswamy
1907 — 1981
Apprenticed to his father, Arunāchalam, from the age of twelve, Subramaniam took the rule and the plumb-line in 1957 at the moment of his father's passing. His forty-three years at the bench are remembered for the careful, inkless drawings of the most demanding capitals which are kept in our archive even now.
EARV1949 –
Sthapati K. Sabesh Ramaswamy
1949 — present
The current principal received the rule in 1981 from his father, and now directs the atelier's drawing-board commissions with an unbroken roster of polychromy and granite work. His correspondence with the Govt. Archaeological Survey is kept in the red leather binders given by his teacher.

· The Canon ·

— Mayamata · Mānasāra —

All drawings issued from this workshop are set in conformity with the two principal Vāstu treatises: the Mayamata in 36 chapters, which India's first survey attributed to the Mayan, the architect of the asuras; and the Mānasāra in 70 chapters.

When the next drawings — most notably on the height-to-width rule of the rakta — the workshop has, since the time of Arunāchalam Sthapati, followed the Mayamata as the more conservative measure.
