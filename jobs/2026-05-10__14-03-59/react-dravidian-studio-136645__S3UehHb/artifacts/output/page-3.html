Practice & Disciplines

Five hereditary disciplines drawn from the Mayamata, Manasara and the Kashyapa Shilpa, practised in unbroken transmission from Sthapati Ramaswamy Achari (1814 — 1879) to the present workshop on Big Bazaar Street.

I.Vastu Purusha · Mandala Layout

For new shrines and grove dharmas temples we draft the complete site horoscope in the squares-tradition: orientation by the gnomon shadow at the equinox, division of the plot into the 64 padu mandala or 81-pada paramasāyikā grid, and assignment of the deities of the directions to their proper cells. Garbhagrha placement, śankhā proportional dimensions, paksha-sutra masonic-yantra rules and the dimensions of the prakara walls are all resolved before a single stone is cut.

Drawings are issued in three sets — the talāchhanda (plan), urdhvachhanda (elevation) and the panjara dētalu — rendered in iron-gall ink on hand-sized palm paper at 1:48 scale, with a working copy in vermillion mash for the masons. The consultation includes a site visit, the soil-test (bhumi-pariksha) and the ankurarpana ceremony marking the auspicious commencement.

Total Drawing Time

14 to 22 weeks from site visit to issued drawings.

II.Vimana & Gopuram Tier Design

A design superstructures from the modest tri-tala (three-storey) shrine of a village agraver to the nine-tala rajagopuram of a major perennial kovil. Each tala is calculated in adi-mana units derived from the garbhagrha width, with the tapering rhythm of the karu, sala and panjara articulate set by the classical Pallava-Chola progression. Shikhara forms — saugha, dravida or vesara — are chosen to honour the presiding deity and the regional convention.

A drawing-set proper begins three stages — the rough-out (kalasa-shilpa), the established proportion (manasi, the surface jhānki), the line modelling (mukhalavyāsa) and the final polish with leaf-motiff fillet jhānki tracings on a mosquito-net cartridge.

Tiers Drawn

Three tala, 8 months drawing · 12 months execution. Nine tala, 30 months drawing & 6+ years execution.

III.Polychrome Restoration & Gold Leaf

Stucco figures on gopurams, ceiling murals in the mantapa-vasalu and the dvaja-stambha column itself are repainted by our atelier in the seasoned palette of the Nayaka tradition: vermillion (lingaral-ground), peacock blue (vasanthva-nila) ramavarma, kāñcakśa kānaīrik and 22-carat gold leaf on woven Indian khadi. We grind our own pigments from cinnabar, indigo, orpiment and lac-cha­ shell-burned into tamarind-seed glue, and water-set the lac-tha-sub Ramamithora prescription.

The work proceeds tier by tier on bamboo scaffolding, with consecration of each course before the next is begun, taking it as a no cost gone of curd shell hue burnished with agate, this final has been documented to last 80 to 120 years in coastal Tamil air. Documentation photography in raking light is delivered as a separate folio.

IV.Granite & Krishna Shila Carving

Or rom yard at Tiruvanyam quarries grey granite for structural members — pillars, beams, adishan trabaeers — and the prized black Krishna-shila from the Karkala beds for figurative work. Yali capitals with their characteristic lion-elephant-makara forms, dvarapala guardians at twice life-size, lotus runners for ceiling coffers, and processional utsava-mūrti to be moved out at the cult ovenly by hand using the brakemoorings of forty-two chisels.

A figured passes through five named stages — the rough-out (kalasha-shilpa), the established proportion (manasi, the surface jhānki), the line modelling (rusphenkalava) and the final polish with leaf emery before the latter is dispatched.

Yali Capital

Yali capital: 14 weeks. Life-size dvarapala: 22 to 26 months.

V.Kalasha Casting & Consecration

The crowning kalasha-finial — the pot-shaped vessel that sits at the apex of every vimana and gopuram — is cast in our foundry in pure-lota brass, in the proportion specified by the Shilparatna. We do not solder the body of the kalasha; the half-shells are joined by a cireperdue rim and brought to a copper plate sealed within. The mounting-on the shikhara is timed to a muhurta calculated by our chief purōhita, Sri Venkatanam Sharma. We have placed kalashas on 75 temples since 1948.

Consecration is a five-day rite. The empty kalasha is filled with the nine grains, the seven sacred earths, and Kaveri river-water; mantras of the agama appropriate to the deity are inscribed on a copper plate sealed within. The mounting on the shikhara is timed to a muhurta calculated by our chief purōhita, Sri Venkatanam Sharma. We have placed kalashas on 75 temples since 1948.

Casting

Casting 6 to 14 months. Consecration five ritual days in situ.
