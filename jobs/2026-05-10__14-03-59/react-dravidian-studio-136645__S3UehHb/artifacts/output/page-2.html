Vamsavali — Genealogy of the Workshop

recorded in East Chitra Veedhi, Madurai. By hand of the present principal.

I1814

Sthapati Vaidyanatha Ramaswamy

1814 — 1879

Pupil of the Tanjore court guild under Sri Subbarama Bhupathi, Vaidyanatha received the silā-and-chisel investiture in 1872 and removed to Madurai the following monsoon. The earliest extant drawing in our chest — a longitudinal section of the Pudu Mandapa buttresses — bears his cipher and the date Vaikāśi 1879. He fathered four sons; the second, Muthuswami, alone took up the rule and the plumb line.

II1857

Sthapati Muthuswami Ramaswamy

1857 — 1916

Commissioned in 1923 for the rim renewal of the western gopuram of the Andal Temple, Sirivilliputhur — a campaign of seven seasons in which he dressed by hand the kalasa-finial and re-cast three of the upper-tier stucco figures. His ledger of that work, bound in red buckram, remains the workshop's single most-consulted reference.

III1908

Sthapati Arumachalam Ramaswamy

1908 — 1968

Awarded the Silver Medal at the Madras Presidency Crafts Exhibition of 1948 for a half-scale model of a Dravida vimana cut from a single block of Krishnagiri granite. He served as consulting silpathi to nine renovations between 1952 and 1976, and corresponded at length with the epigraphist K. R. Srinivasan on the proportions given in the Mayamata.

IV1934

Sthapati Subramaniam Ramaswamy

1934 — 2011

Trained from his eleventh year, Subramaniam inherited the workshop in 1981 and was the first of the line to keep its measured drawings in true-to-cartridge paper rather than palm-leaf. Under him the present drafting hall was extended by one bay (1988) and the south-facing stone yard was paved.

V1968

Sthapati R. Gahela-Ramaswamy

1968 — Principal Present

The current principal received the silā in 1996 from his father and now directs the atelier's three working departments — vastu consultation, gopuram restoration drawings, and stone-carving commissions. He continues the daily morning recitation from the Manasara before the chest of pattern-books.

The Cabinet

Mayamata · Manasara

All drawings issued from this workshop are set out in conformity with the two principal silpa shastric treatises — the Mayamata of the southern school and the Manasara in the Vidya I. M. Iyer recension. A second-hand octavo edition of the Manasara, published Madras 1934, is consulted at the founding of every commission.

Where the texts diverge — most notably on the height-to-width ratio of the kalasha — the workshop has, since the days of Arumachalam Sthapati, followed the Manasara as the more conservative.
