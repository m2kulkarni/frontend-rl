— Sthapati Ramaswamy Workshop —

The Drafting Hall
& Stone Yard

No. 14, Karaikkattu Street, Kumbakonam — a 1903 stone-and-tile vihara of working artisans, opened daily save the new moon.

The Inner Court

I · The Open Courtyard

The workshop occupies a single-storey stone-and-tile structure raised in 1903 by Sthapati Subramaniam the elder. Its plan ordered around an open central courtyard fifty-two feet by thirty, paved in Cuddapah black and washed each dawn with river-water and turmeric. Three precincts open from the four cardinal sides: the drafting hall to the east, the carving yard to the south, and the polychrome room to the west; the northern wing is reserved for the household shrine.

II · The Drafting Hall

Within the eastern hall the working draughtsmen of three generations are kept. Older measured studies — manual and proportional, gopuram elevations, sthala vimanam plans — are bundled between palm-leaf covers tied with red cotton cord. Later sheets, on cartridge paper drawn between 1948 and the present day, are rolled and stored in twenty-two teakwood pigeonholes labelled in Tamil script.

III · The Carving Yard

Eleven blocks of Krishnagiri granite stand in the southern yard beneath cane awnings braced against the monsoon. The largest — block no. 7, intended for a yali-bracket commission for the Tirumayur temple cornice — measures four feet eight inches in at base. Hammers, chisels of nine gauges, and the cushion-stones for setting work are arranged on the south bench in the order taught by the master.

IV · The Polychrome Room

In the western chamber, pigments are still ground in stone mortars by hand. Vermillion is prepared from cinnabar imported from Coorg; the blue, a lapis substitute of indigo and white lead, follows the recipe recorded in the 1921 workbook of Kuman Pillai\'s grandfather. The yellow is turmeric mordanted lac, fixed in tamarind glue. No synthetic colour enters this room.
