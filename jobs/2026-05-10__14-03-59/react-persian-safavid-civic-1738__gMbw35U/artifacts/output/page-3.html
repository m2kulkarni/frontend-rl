Chapter I · Bāb iṣ — al-ʿaḍr al-Maydān

[motif: arabesque-filet-divider]

On the Lodging of an ʿArīḍa

Procedures observed in the chamber-vestibule of the Dīvān

By order of His Excellency the Kalāntar of Iṣfahān, every resident of the Naqsh-e Jahān quarters — whether of Banān-i Buzurg, Maydān-i Shāh, Jūbāra, Dardasht, or the Armenian suburb of Julfā — may bring forward a written petition before the city secretariat. Audience is held in the columned vestibule (rāhrū) of the Bureau, on the morning of Saturday and Tuesday, between the second and fifth hours after sunrise. Petitioners shall present themselves with two witnesses of good standing and with a sheet of stamped chancery paper (kāghaz-i mahabbat) drawn that same week from the office of the Munshī al-Mamālik.

Note — Petitions delivered without the written seal of a chamber-aamīr shall be set aside and read aloud. The aamīr is summoned each Friday after the noon prayer.

Days & Hours of Audience

The columned vestibule is opened by the porter (darbān) at the call of the second hour. Petitioners are received in the order of their arrival, modified only by the precedence noted below.

Saturday (Shanba)2nd – 5th hour
Tuesday (Sih-shanba)2nd – 5th hour
Friday— closed —
Festival days— closed —

Where to Present Yourself

The Bureau of Records occupies the eastern wing of the Dīvān-khāna, behind the cypress court of the Naqsh-e Jahān. Enter by the lesser gate (dar-i kūchak) facing the workshops of the gilders; ascend the seven steps of grey marble, and present your stamped paper to the doorkeeper. Your name, quarter, and guild affiliation will be entered into the day register (rūznāma) before you are admitted to the rotunda.

Drafting Conventions

Petitions are read direct to the secretariat; their form is therefore prescribed. The seven scribes of the antechamber will compose on your behalf, if requested:

  1. Open with the formula Ḥuwa-l-naṣīr al-Mūʿīn; the head of the folio.
  2. Name yourself, your father, your quarter, and your guild — in that order.
  3. State the matter plainly, in not more than seven lines of cursive nastaʿlīq.
  4. Avoid figurative speech and complaint against named persons of higher rank.
  5. Close with the date in the Hijrī reckoning and your seal, or, lacking a seal, the impress of your right thumb in lampblack.
  6. Leave a margin of three fingers' breadth in the foot of the folio for the secretariat's countersignature.

Order of Precedence

When the rotunda is crowded, petitioners are called in the following order, by guild and by quarter, as established by old custom of the city:

  1. Sayyids and ʿulamāʾ of the Friday Mosque, and elders of the Sufi orders of Jūbāra.
  2. Guild of the qaṣṣāb-bāshī (saqaṭīn) and the silk-merchants of the Qaysariyya bāzār.
  3. Tūrs, masons, and gilders of the royal workshops, on matters of wages and apprentices.
  4. Residents of Jūbāra and Dardasht, in matters of water-rights and the qanāt-divisions.
  5. Armenians from Julfā, in their kalānsar of commercial and inheritance affairs.
  6. All other residents of the city in the order of their arrival at the gate.
Sealed at the Dīvān-khāna of Iṣfahān, in the columned vestibule, on the morning of an ordinary Saturday.
