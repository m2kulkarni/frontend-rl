Practice & Disciplines

Five hereditary disciplines drawn from the Manasara, Mayamata and the Kashyapa Shilpa, practised to subordinate transmission from Sthapati Ramaswamy Achari (1842 — 1875) to the present workshop on the Big Bazaar Street.

Five Disciplines·Vastu·Vimana & Gopuram·Polychromy·Granite·Kalasa & Consecration·Tamil-Sanskrit Concordance
I. Vastu-Purusha-Mandala Layout

For new shrines and grama-devata temples we draft the complete site horoscope to the agama tradition: orientation by the gnomon-shadow at the equinox, division of the plot into the 64 pada-mandala or 81 pada paramashayika grid, and assignment of the deities of the directions to their proper cells. Garbhāgrha placement, axial proportional checks (upon request poet-vata-bīkti) and the dimensions of the prākara walls are all resolved before a single stone is cut.

Drawings are issued in three sets — the talachhanda (plan), urdhvachhanda (elevation) and the panjara details — rendered in iron-gall ink on hand-laid palm paper at 1:48 scale, with a working copy in vermillion wash for the masons. The consultation includes a site visit, the soil and bhumi prāṇāyāma rite, and the auspicious ceremony marking the auspicious commencement.

Typical Duration

14 to 20 weeks from site visit to issued drawings

II. Vimana & Gopuram Tier-Design

We design superstructures from the modest tri-tala (three-storey) shrine of a village ayapurar to the nine-tala rajagopuram of a major personal motif. Each tala is calculated in adi-mana units derived from the garbhagriha width, with the tapering rhythm of the kuta, sala and panjara articulated set by the classical Pallava-Chola progression. Shikhara forms — nagara, dravida or vesara — are chosen to honour the presiding deity and the regional convention.

For drawings give every kapota cornice, every yali bracket, every makara torana to measured detail. For restoration projects we begin with a photogrammetric survey, mounted to a square 60 mm grid for the masons. Recent work includes the seven-tala east gopuram of the Ranganatha temple, completed 2019 in collaboration with the ASI Chennai circle.

Typical Duration

Three tala: 4 months drawing · 22 months execution. Nine tala: 11 months drawing · 6 to 9 years execution.

III. Polychrome Restoration & Gold-Leaf

Stucco figures on gopurams, ceiling murals in the maṇḍapas, and the dharmaśālika column itself are repainted by our atelier in the seasoned palette of the Nayaka tradition: vermillion (lingulai), peacock blue (mauvāra-nīla) tinted with iron oxide, jaggery-yellow, lamp-black soft-binding cured glue, and 23-carat leaf on creamy and śilājit. We grind our own pigments from cinnabar, indigo, orpiment and lamp-soot, bound in tamarind-seed gum and neem oil to the Mūlaśāstrākātma prescription.

The work proceeds tier by tier on bamboo scaffolding, with consecration of each course before the next is begun. Gilding is laid on a six-coat gesso of conch-shell lime burnished with agate; this finish has been documented to last 60 to 110 years in coastal Tamil air. Documentation photography in raking light is delivered at completion.

Typical Duration

One full gopuram tier: 7 to 11 months including monsoon pause

Recent Engagement

₹ 5,48,000 per square foot, gold-leaf billed separately at material cost.

IV. Granite & Krishna-Shila Carving

Our stone-yard at Tiruvayāra quarries grey granite for structural members — pillars, beams, jamb stones — and the hand black Krishna-shila from the Karkala beds for figurative work. Yali capitals with their characteristic lion-elephant-makara fusion, dvarapāla guardians at twice life-size, lotus rosettes for ceiling cofers, and processional utsava-mūrti in the round are all cut entirely by hand using the traditional set of forty-two chisels.

A figure passes through five named stages — the rough-out (kāmakhya śilīpa), the established proportion (mānā), the surface (kelvar), the fine modelling (mukhima) and the final polish with tied beeswax (haraharam). Every piece is consecrated in the workshop with the navadhānya ritual before it is despatched.

Typical Duration

Yali capital: 18 weeks · Life-size dvarapāla: pair to 14 months

V. Kalasa Casting & Consecration

The crowning kalasha-finial — the pot-shaped vessel that sits at the apex of every vimana and gopuram — is cast in our foundry in panchaloha, the five-metal alloy of gold, copper, tin, silver and lead in proportions specified by the Shilpaśāstra. We cast by the lost-wax bhuhmkhi sthāli technique, which permits a watertight bell-cast even in tower-finials weighing thirty kilograms or more.

Consecration is a five-day rite. The empty kalasha is filled with the nine grains, the seven sacred earths, and Kāveri river-water, anointed of the agama appropriate to the deity, set on the inscribed on a copper plate sealed within. The mounting on the shikhara is timed to a muhurta calculated by our staff jyotisha, Sri Venkatesa Sharma. We have placed kalashas on 73 temples since 1944.

Typical Duration

Casting: 6 to 14 months. Consecration: five ritual days at site

Recent Engagement

Three foot kalasha ₹ 1,40,000 · Pancha-loha at material weight + 14% labour.

Commencing a Commission

All work begins with a written enquiry to the workshop and a preliminary visit by the sthapati to the site. Drawings, ritual schedules and itemised estimates follow within six weeks. We accept no more than four major commissions in any calendar year and presently book into the year 2027.
