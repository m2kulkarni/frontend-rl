Register IV · Of the Bureau

The Bureau & Its Officers

Established by royal firmān in the year 1006 of the Hijra, the Dīvān-i Shahr keeps the rolls of the city subdued of Naqsh-e Jahān, the treasure of the bazaars, and the records of the canals.

Bureau · Founding

Of the Founding under Shāh ʿAbbās

[motif: arabesque-filet-divider]

In the year 1006 A.H. — being the year of the elevation of the royal seat from Qazvīn to Iṣfahān — His Majesty Shāh ʿAbbās, called the Great, did command that a Bureau be raised within the new city, fronting the maydān of Naqsh-e Jahān, that no measure should be done in the bazaars, no canal stopped against its neighbour, and no household unrecorded upon the rolls. The seal was struck in cobalt and gold and pressed upon the founding ledger by the hand of the wazīr Hātim Beg Urdūbādī.

From that day forward the Dīvān has kept its watch from the chamber beside the Qayṣariyya gate, its officers four in number, its scribes nine, its couriers six, and its lamps trimmed each evening by the porter Ḥasan ʿAlī, son of Murād, in the manner appointed.

The Present Roster of Officers

Bureau · The Pillars of Office

Kalāntar · Mayor

Mīrzā Ḥusayn Khān

[motif: arabesque-filet-divider]

Lord-Mayor of the City and chief warden of the Bureau. Sits in council each Saturday after the dawn prayer, and receives petitioners between the second and fourth hours.

Mušrif · Inspector of Markets

Āqā Ṣādiq Tabrīzī

[motif: arabesque-filet-divider]

Keeper of the just weight and the true price. He walks the bazaar daily, the loaf of bread is fixed at one dīnār of bran-bread, and his vigilant eye is fixed at the works.

Mīr-āb · Master of Waters

Ustād Ibrāhīm of Yazd

[motif: arabesque-filet-divider]

Apportioner of the Zāyande-rūd among the seven mādīs of the city. Keeps the schedule of the canals, and receives complaints of farmer or miller, of the bridges of Allāhverdī Khān and Khājū.

Munshī al-Mamalik · Chief Scribe

Mīr Muḥammad-Bāqir

[motif: arabesque-filet-divider]

Chief of the writing chamber, keeper of the great register and of the seal of state. Under his hand pass all decrees of the Bureau, none orderly may take undue counsel from before its proper face.

Maxim · The Charge

The Threefold Duty of the Bureau

[motif: arabesque-filet-divider]

The keeping of true records — that no name be lost, no birth uncertain, no deed unwitnessed.

The just measure of bread — that the loaf weigh full, the price hold steady, the poor not be wronged at the oven.

The proper repair of canals and bridges — that the water reach every garden in its turn, and the spans of the river stand firm against flood.

[motif: arabesque-filet-divider]

Sealed in Iṣfahān, in the chamber beside the Qayṣariyya gate · On the Register of Quarters: the Edition in Bearings.
